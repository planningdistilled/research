Script: scratchpad `sdcocr/ocr.py`. Page numbers cited in the cases are OCR page markers, which equal PDF pages.
5. **Appeals.** The API `appealDetails` on all SDC apps with `hasAppeal` showed **no SDC appeal decisions dated on or after 17 Aug** in the portal (the latest was 14 Aug, 6001556). The portal probably lags PINS, so the appeals agents should use ACP directly.

## How SDC officers are handling the key issues

- **5YHLS.** Every housing report uses the same template: 2.21 years at 31 Mar 2025, LHN 1,112 dpa, a five-year requirement of 5,560, a gap of at least 3,261 homes, and "may be … 4,000–5,000". Officers treat this as the S5(1)(j) and GB7(1)(g)(ii) "evidenced unmet need" automatically, with no type-of-need analysis, and give housing **substantial weight** under HO7, even for 1 or 2 homes. When objectors raised the HDT (published 17 Aug 2026), officers declined to report it and stuck with the 31 Mar 2025 figure (Welford update sheet).
- **Washed-over villages and "settlement".** Snitterfield, Tanworth and Earlswood are washed over by Green Belt; only Alcester, Henley and Studley are inset (SWLP GB Topic Paper ¶¶169–176, which proposes to inset Claverdon, Snitterfield, Tanworth and others). **No report engages with the Annex B exclusion of washed-over villages from "settlement".** Officers run S5(1)(j) first ("well-related to the settlement of …"), then note S5(5) and route through GB7(1)(g). They call the villages "settlements" throughout. Grey belt is found with a stock paragraph: "X is a village, some distance from large built-up areas and towns", so no strong contribution to (a), (b) or (d). Officers also test (c) and (e) for the "fundamentally undermine" limb. In villages with no BUAB (Priors Hardwick), "physical confines" is judged case by case.
- **Transitional(2).** A standard paragraph holds CS.15, CS.16 and AS.10 (and CS.10 for housing, CS.20, CS.26, and NDP settlement-boundary policies such as H1, H3, HLU1 and HE5) "materially inconsistent" with the 2026 decision-making policies, so **very limited weight**. The reasoning is that S5(1)(j) permits what they would not. CS.26 fails because it lacks TR6's "severe" test. CS.8 and NDP heritage policies are also called "materially inconsistent" with HE5 because HE5 has "new criteria". Yet refusals still cite CS.8 (Ilmington). Design, landscape, flood, ecology and amenity policies (CS.4, CS.5, CS.6, CS.9, CS.12) are "materially consistent". CS.10 is called consistent for householder Green Belt cases (26/01310) but inconsistent for housing: its weight depends on the type of development.
- **S6 and NDPs.** S6 was held not engaged for NDPs made more than five years ago or without allocations (Snitterfield 2018, Welford 2017, Tanworth 2022 with no allocations, Stratford 2018). The **IPPS (Cabinet 1 Jun 2026)** is used to decide whether an NDP is "in date". Ilmington (2021, with allocations) is treated as in date, but Southam (2023, with allocations) as out of date. Long Marston is described as in date with sufficient allocations. The IPPS also supplies **parish housing-need figures** that officers cite as a positive (Tanworth: about 133 homes over five years).
- **SWLP (Reg 19, 21 Jul – 8 Sep 2026).** DS.8, DS.11 and DS.12 conflicts are given **very limited weight** under DM4 in every report. Draft allocations are a "very limited but positive" factor (Southam SOU.1), and HO13(4) is used to confirm consistency with them.
- **Transition mechanics.** Reports finished before 17 Aug were closed out by (a) an update sheet saying the 2026 NPPF "does not affect the substantive matters" (committee), or (b) a one-paragraph "Procedural Issue" note that the balance "remains generally consistent" (delegated: Kineton 26/01764, Oxhill 26/01393). At least one notice dated 17 Aug (25/01271) and one dated 18 Aug (26/01514, Fenny Compton, report cites NPPF 2024) applied the old Framework outright.

## OBSERVED PATTERNS

1. **The "substantially outweighed" tilt is decisive in SDC's hands.** Harm given *substantial* weight lost to substantial housing weight in three approvals: character harm at Snitterfield (stratford-26-00617-PIP), landscape harm at Earlswood (stratford-26-01458-FUL) and listed-building harm at Long Marston (stratford-26-01906-PIP). No housing scheme reaching the S4 or S5 balance was refused on the balance alone.
2. **Refusals come only through "should be refused" policies or a standalone heritage test.** F7, F5 and F6 were used via S4(2)(c) at Alcester (stratford-26-01376-FUL). HE6(4) was run as a free-standing test at Ilmington (stratford-26-01399-PIP), Quinton (stratford-26-00922-FUL) and Trinity College (stratford-26-01387-LBC). **DP3(3) ("should be refused if …") was cited but not used as an S5(2) trigger** at Snitterfield, and L2(1)(d) was not used as an S4(2)(a)(ii) trigger in the Pillerton Priors recommendation (lead).
3. **The heritage outcome depends on method.** Ilmington ran HE6(4) first and refused. Long Marston folded heritage harm into S4 and granted (stratford-26-01399-PIP vs stratford-26-01906-PIP), on similar one-dwelling facts, a month apart.
4. **GB7(1)(g)(iii) is passed easily at LSV edges.** Pass factors: a frontage footway plus about 400 m to services, with unfooted village-core sections accepted (stratford-26-00918-PIP); at Snitterfield (stratford-26-00617-PIP) the report first gave limited weight to 100 m of walking in a 30 mph carriageway, but the committee Update Report corrected this: the proposed Park Lane footpath means the whole route is on made footpath; and location within the BUAB alone, with no TR3 analysis (stratford-26-01458-FUL). "The car trip is only 5–10 minutes" is used as mitigation (Snitterfield, Welford). No SDC report uses the Connectivity Tool.
5. **Grey belt is found by stock paragraph** for any village-edge field ("a village, some distance from large built-up areas"), including an undeveloped Ecosite paddock (Snitterfield) and a site where a 2019 appeal had found significant character harm (stratford-26-00617-PIP).
6. **The Annex B "settlement" definition is not being applied.** Washed-over villages are treated as settlements (Tanworth, Snitterfield, Earlswood). Priors Hardwick is treated as a settlement in one report and as countryside in another (stratford-26-01211-FUL vs stratford-26-00898-FUL). Gaydon considered, but did not adopt, the allocated-land limb (stratford-25-01765-FUL).
7. **Transitional(2) is used broadly.** The locational, Green Belt (housing), heritage, highway and NDP boundary policies are all called "materially inconsistent", so very limited weight (all housing cases). The same CS.10 is "aligned" for householder refusals (stratford-26-01310-FUL) and CS.8 is still relied on in refusals. This is internally inconsistent and exploitable on appeal by both sides.
8. **S5(1)(d) and (c) are read generously.** Rural residential curtilage counts as PDL, and (d) has "no restrictions relating to scale", so a 3.4× replacement house was approved despite failing (c) (stratford-26-01558-FUL). Subdivision fits (c) (stratford-26-00898-FUL). On the other side, the 26/01608 benchmark is +16% volume = "not materially larger" under GB7(1)(b).
9. **The switch to the 2026 Framework was handled by assertion.** Update sheets and "Procedural Issue" notes say the result is unchanged, with no re-consultation (stratford-25-00346-OUT, stratford-24-03145-FUL, stratford-26-01764-PIP, stratford-26-01393-FUL), and notices dated 17–18 Aug applied the 2024 Framework (stratford-25-01271-FUL). Objectors' "you applied a revoked test" points were answered in one line (stratford-26-00772-PIP).
10. **PIPs are the main vehicle for edge-of-village housing.** 7 of the housing cases are PIPs. Officers treat detail-dependent harms (ecology, access, drainage, trees) as TDC-stage matters. Mitigation is written into the description of development, as with the crossing at Bordon Hill (stratford-26-01588-PIP) and the footpath link at Snitterfield.
11. **Energy-efficiency benefit under HE6(4) and CC2(2) is weak against listed-building harm** where a less harmful alternative exists (stratford-26-01387-LBC). It was ignored altogether where the windows replaced were modern (stratford-26-00922-FUL).

### Observed patterns

1. **Refusals on the "substantially outweighed" balance have started.** Members refused Pillerton Priors on the S4(1) balance against a recommendation to grant (stratford-26-01894-PIP), and an officer refused a single dwelling at Earlswood on the S5(5) balance (stratford-26-01542-FUL). Both rest on backland character harm graded "substantial". In the first pass no housing scheme was refused on the balance alone.
2. **L2(1)(d) is now reasoned in terms, but S4(2)(a)(ii) is still not applied.** Both garden schemes quote S4(2)(a)(ii) and L2(1)(d) in full. At Pillerton Priors the officer found limb (iii) failed and read it down ("should", not "must"); the members' reason cites "L2" and "S4" without limbs (stratford-26-01894-PIP). At Earlswood limbs (i) and (ii) became a refusal reason in their own right (stratford-26-01542-FUL). Neither report makes the "substantial adverse impact" finding that S4(2)(a)(ii) asks for.
3. **SDC officers have begun to apply the Annex B definition of settlement, but not consistently.** One report holds that a site in Earlswood "is not considered to be within a settlement because it lies within the Green Belt" (stratford-26-01614-FUL). Another, eight days later in the same village, applies S4 and L2(1)(d) as if it were a settlement (stratford-26-01542-FUL). A third uses the definition to bring a village with no boundary inside S4 (stratford-26-01687-FUL).
4. **GB7(1)(c) village infill was passed for a backland plot** because there was built form on all sides (stratford-26-01542-FUL). CS.10 is still "materially consistent" for householder cases (stratford-26-01614-FUL) and "materially inconsistent" for housing and commercial cases (stratford-26-01542-FUL, stratford-26-01141-FUL, stratford-26-01447-FUL).
5. **Fallbacks are tested more closely than in the first pass.** An extant outline permission and a Class Q conversion under way were accepted as real (stratford-26-01660-OUT, stratford-26-01801-FUL); a permitted development fallback was cut back to what could be built together and given limited weight (stratford-26-01614-FUL).
6. **Housing weight is no longer always substantial.** It is still the default (stratford-26-01894-PIP, stratford-26-01660-OUT, stratford-26-01687-FUL), but it was qualified as "limited in scale" for one dwelling (stratford-26-01542-FUL), neutral where a fallback already gives the dwelling (stratford-26-01801-FUL), and neutral for a net loss of two dwellings (stratford-26-01141-FUL). The loss of 5 self-build plots was a moderate disbenefit (stratford-26-01660-OUT).
7. **Green Belt reasoning is thin where nobody objects.** Grey belt was found in one sentence beside the district's main town, and (g) and (e) were run together with no S5(5) balance (stratford-26-01141-FUL). Very special circumstances were accepted with no openness assessment and without "clearly" in the conclusion (stratford-26-01447-FUL).
8. **Template slips persist.** Two report headers cite "NPPF 2024 (Updated 2026)" (stratford-26-01542-FUL, stratford-26-01141-FUL); the Golden Rules are cited by 2024 paragraph numbers (stratford-26-01141-FUL); "TR 4" stands for TR6(4) and "CS.2 (2)" for CC2(2) (stratford-26-01660-OUT); one balance sentence is inverted (stratford-26-01542-FUL).
