# Do other councils make the four Stratford errors? A cross-council check, and an audit of the method

Note of 18 October 2026. The Stratford-on-Avon District Council (SDC) analysis `stratford-officer-reports-four-errors.md` scores 44 SDC officer reports written under the National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF) of 17 August 2026 against four errors. This note asks two questions. First, do officers at other councils make the same errors at the same rate, scored the same way? Second, is the Stratford analysis itself sound: are the readings of the Framework right, and are the codes fair? Not legal advice.

The table is `other-councils-four-errors-2026-10.tsv` (99 rows, same columns as the Stratford table plus `scored_in`). Every adverse code carries a PDF page and a verbatim quotation that the scoring agent machine-checked against the `pdftotext` text; every "not mentioned" is a grep with zero hits. The reports are in the private sources checkout: the 50 already held in `sources:council/`, and 49 fetched on 18 October into `sources:council/other-councils-2026-10/` (with each set's scoring summary). The quotations were checked by the agent that scored each set, not re-checked independently for this note.

### What the best reports elsewhere do that Stratford's do not

- **The Green Belt route stated and followed.** Bromsgrove's template quotes the S3 split and then "S5 does not apply to development proposals in the Green Belt and that these proposals should be determined in accordance with NDMP GB6 and GB7" (Beoley 26/00889/PIP p.2), runs GB7(1)(a) to (g) as a list, and at Rubery closes with the S5(5) balance in terms. Dacorum's Chesham Road update: "Policy S5 does not provide an alternative route for approval. S5(5) provides that S5 does not apply to development proposals in the Green Belt" (p.7). Elmbridge Moore Place: "policy S5(5) of the NPPF is of relevance" (p.23), then GB7(1)(g)(i) to (iv) and S5(2) at the balance.
- **GB7(1)(g)(iii) answered with the route, in TR3's words.** Bromsgrove Beoley: footway side, 40 mph limit, lighting, distances to each facility, a once-a-week bus, the Bournheath appeal on the movement threshold, and "the site is not considered to offer a genuine choice of transport modes for future residents who are likely to be reliant on use of the private car" (p.4). Bromsgrove Dodford: "Criteria iii requires that the development would be in a sustainable location having regard to policy TR3 of the 2026 Framework" (p.4), bus "on a Tuesday and Friday only", so not sustainable, so inappropriate, so very special circumstances.
- **The Connectivity Tool in an ordinary delegated report.** Wychavon's Offenham reports quote TR3(1)(a) in full, give a score ("The application site has a Connectivity Score of 23", New End W/26/00329 p.6; 37 and 34 at Offenham) and decide on footway, speed limit, bus distance and frequency, with the Category 1 label as context: "connectivity could be better, on this occasion the proposal is considered to be acceptable" (W/26/01940 p.8). Cheshire East Shavington: "Local Authority Band B" (p.11). Bath Press: "a Department for Transport connectivity score of 80-82, categorised as 'very high' nationally" (p.116).
- **DP3(3) quoted as a refusal rule.** Wychavon in three reports: "NPPF page 67 is clear that development proposals should be refused, if, without clear justification, they conflict with Paragraph 1 of Policy DP3" (W/25/02384 p.5), with the design conflict then carried as a refusal reason rather than a weight. Elmbridge Hersham 2026/0036 finds a DP3 conflict, quotes DP3(3), and says that under S4 "it is necessary to consider whether the benefits of approving development are likely to be substantially outweighed by adverse effects" (p.61). Cheshire East Bollington asks the S4(2)(c) question in terms: "There is no conflict with any of the national decision-making policies which state that development proposals should be refused in specific circumstances" (25/4697 p.12).
- **Annex A(2) applied policy by policy.** Dacorum Polehanger declines to cut saved Policy 108 because it "is not considered to be so materially inconsistent with the NPPF to the extent it should be afforded very limited weight" (p.61). Worcester 26/00541: "No such material inconsistency has been identified in respect of the policies relied upon in this assessment" (p.8). Tonbridge and Malling Town Hill ties its cut to a part: CP14 "attracts reduced weight insofar as it seeks to apply a restrictive approach to development outside settlements" (p.6).
- **The Annex B settlement test applied to a cluster.** Wychavon Pinvin quotes the definition and concludes "it is not considered within a settlement as defined within the NPPF. Therefore, in this case the proposal needs to be assessed against Policy S5" (p.6). Every South Worcestershire officer facing a cluster with no boundary (Sinton Green, Shoulton Lane, two Offenham ribbons) held it not to be a settlement and ran S5, the opposite of Stratford's Ardens Grafton.

### Where other councils repeat Stratford

- **Warwick** is Stratford's twin. Norton Lindsey W/26/0066 (committee, approved) describes the site as "washed over by Green Belt" (p.1), lists and quotes S4 as the principle policy (p.3), never mentions S5(5) or Annex B, and passes GB7(1)(g)(iii) in one sentence: "The application site is located within the settlement boundary of Norton Lindsey, which includes a number of facilities for day to day living and therefore the proposal is considered to be in compliance with Policy TR3 of the Framework" (p.5). Warwick's own Local Plan "TR3 - Parking" makes every Warwick report mention TR3 while the NPPF policy goes unreasoned. Cubbington W/25/1088 was issued on 7 October 2026 on an April 2026 report with no 2026 re-anchoring.
- **North Hertfordshire** Pirton (50 homes, granted) is the tier shorthand in full: "a logical extension of the village" (p.28), a Category A village, with no bus stop distance, frequency, footway or walking distance anywhere, and the conversion update maps 2024 paragraphs 110, 115 and 116 to TR6 alone, dropping TR3. New Pound Farm runs S5(1)(j) on a Green Belt plot "by Policy S4 (j. i.)" (p.6).
- **Basildon** Pound Lane opens "The principle of development within settlements is set out in policy S4" (p.22) for a washed-over Plotlands site, and two urban reports still run "the Tilted Balance of NPPF paragraph 11" under an August 2026 heading.
- **Malvern Hills** assesses routes candidly and then approves against its own finding: Brookfield, Hallow M/26/01143 finds the site 1.3 km from the school with no footpath or lighting, a Highway Authority objection that "the site occupies an inherently unsustainable location" (p.2), then approves on a "reasonable case that it constitutes previously developed land" (p.12) without running S5(1)(d)'s test. The rubric scores that `ok` on tier because it tests method, not result; a separate "approved against the officer's own route finding" column would catch it.
- **Member refusals against officer advice** are frequent everywhere (eight in the held committee set, three in three weeks on the October agendas, Pillerton Priors at Stratford), usually on Green Belt openness or character, and the reasons rarely name DP3(3) or S4(2)(c).

## 3. Audit of the method: is the Stratford analysis itself flawed?

### 3.1 Readings of the Framework that hold

- **Annex B and washed-over villages.** The text excludes "villages which lie within and are defined as part of the Green Belt in the development plan". The government's consultation response says the definition was amended "to address how the definition applies to villages within the Green Belt" (Q35, p.30) and GB4 changed "to recognise that villages lying in the Green Belt should not be identified as settlements for the purpose of the spatial strategy" (Q132, p.90). Stratford's Core Strategy treats its Green Belt Local Service Villages as lying within the Green Belt, with Built-Up Area Boundaries defined only "to identify where limited infilling might be appropriate" (para 4.1.7) and housing there "wholly in accordance with the provisions of Policy CS.10" (CS.16). The appeals review found one letter in 137 treating a washed-over village as a settlement. No officer outside Stratford and Warwick applied S4 in a washed-over village. The reading stands.
- **S5(5) and the Green Belt route.** Bromsgrove, Dacorum, Elmbridge, Three Rivers, Bath and Lichfield templates all state it. Stands.
- **DP3(3) as a "should be refused" policy.** The text says "should be refused"; S4(2)(c) and S5(2) pick up "national decision-making policies which state that development proposals should be refused in specific circumstances". Commentary (Landmark Chambers, the Local Government Association briefing, practice guides) describes DP3 as one of the few design policies that ground refusal; 108 inspector letters apply it by name; Wychavon, Elmbridge, Cheshire East and Tonbridge and Malling officers quote or paraphrase it. Stands.
- **Annex A(1), no transition for decisions.** "must be taken into account in decision-making from the day of its publication". Every council in the sample converted or re-anchored its reports; the late unconverted notices at Warwick and Wychavon are the same defect Stratford's table records. Stands.
- **The Connectivity Tool is usable.** The GOV.UK page offers a public Lite version and automatic full access to gov.uk email addresses, updated 3 July 2026 with guidance on interpreting scores. "Used in none of the 44" is a fair fact, and eight reports elsewhere show it fits in a delegated report.
- **Annex A(2) cuts parts, not policies.** The government response confirms the change was made "to address concerns that entire policies could be given limited weight based on partial inconsistency" (Q192, p.132). The template paragraph remains a real point. Caveat: Matthew Reed KC's view that S5(1)(j) "will likely override all other settlement-based constraint policies in existing LPs" is a reputable contrary reading, so the paragraph is defensible as a position even if it skips the step Annex A(2) describes.

### 3.2 Where the coding overstates, or needs a split

1. **TR3 "not mentioned" counts small schemes that officers and inspectors alike treat as below the TR3(1)(a) threshold.** Of the 12 inspector letters in the corpus that engage the "significant amount of movement" wording in terms, six hold that a single dwelling or small scheme does not reach it (6003001, 6005162, 6008848, 6009593, 6010422, 6004899); three find it reached (28 homes at Hatton Station 6006637, five at Newchapel 6010313, a freight use 6006761). Bromsgrove's Fairfield report says one dwelling "would not generate significant amounts of movement". In the other-council sample, 20 of the 34 "not mentioned" rows are five homes or fewer, and five more are estate or town-centre schemes inside a built-up area (Basildon 8 and 19, Chorley 14, Cheshire East Bollington 77, Bath co-living 272); the remaining nine are the real misses by any reading (five Basildon Green Belt outlines converted by a one-paragraph addendum, East Devon 105, North Hertfordshire Pirton 50, Dacorum Marchmont 350, Wychavon Hartlebury 12 on a 2025 report). Stratford's "dodged" code for Ravenstone (three homes) labels a mainstream position as an evasion; it should be `threshold-not-met`, and the table should report TR3 for schemes of six or more homes, and for every GB7(1)(g)(iii) case, separately from the rest. On that cut Stratford's real misses are Home Farm (217), Alcester Road (65), Harbury (38), Checketts Abattoir (7), Kineton (6), Oxhill (5 on the edge), Stockton (75, thin) and the Bearley training centre.
2. **"Not mentioned" also ignores 2024 equivalents reasoned in substance.** Chorley's addendum-converted report contains the fullest route reasoning in the held committee set ("sustainable transport options such as walking, the bus and the train offer genuine alternatives to the car", p.13) and codes `cited-only`; Cotswold's Aldi report quotes TR3(1) in full and reasons nothing, and codes the same. The code measures whether the limbs are run against TR3 by name. The Stratford table applies it the same way (Pillerton Hersey is noted as "2024 equivalents applied in substance"), but the headline should say so.
3. **DP3(3) `error` should be split three ways.** (a) Refusals: four of Stratford's 18 (Malthouse Lane, Ardens Grafton, Gorcott Hill, Pillerton Priors) and five of the other councils' eight are refusals where the conflict was itself a refusal reason; the missing step is formal and could not change the outcome. The appeals review already treats "dismiss by another route" as consistent with the policy; the two registers should use the same rule. (b) Landscape-character conflicts: Elmbridge and Dacorum treat a conflict with a Core Strategy countryside or landscape policy as harm to weigh under N2, not as a DP3 design conflict; the Stratford table takes the broad reading (Lucy's Farm, Stockton, Bordon Hill). Either reading is arguable; the table should say which it uses and give the count on both. (c) Approvals after a design conflict proper: this is the real finding, and it survives. Stratford has 15 approvals or recommendations to grant after a found conflict with no clear-justification question; the other 22 councils together have three. Part of that gap is composition (other officers more often find no conflict, or refuse), but Snitterfield, Ladbroke, Pillerton Priors and Stockton have no counterpart elsewhere except Leigh Sinton and Moore Place.
4. **On the Didcot reading, a weighing can be a clear-justification finding.** At Didcot 6009340 ¶22 "clear justification" was satisfied because the benefits outweighed the harm on a level balance. An officer who finds "limited" or "moderate" design harm and "substantial" benefits has, in substance, done that; what is missing is the trigger (S4(2)(c) or S5(2)) and the threshold word. The cases where the omission could change the result are those where the harm was found "significant" or "substantial" and the scheme was still approved on the tilted test: Snitterfield ("significant harm", "substantial weight"), Ladbroke ("significant weight"), Pillerton Priors ("significant weight", recommended for approval). The note should say that the 18 are not all of the same weight.
5. **Annex B errors are of two kinds and two of them are tier errors in Green Belt dress.** Route-changing errors apply S4, S5(1)(j) or L2(1)(d) as the operative test in a washed-over village or hamlet: Ravenstone, Malthouse Lane, Snitterfield (S5(1)(j) run), Ardens Grafton; Norton Lindsey, Pound Lane, New Pound Farm elsewhere. Belt-and-braces runs test the S5(1) categories and then apply S5(5) and GB7 correctly: Bromsgrove Wythall and Crabmill Lane, Bath Clutton, Basildon Glebe Road. Tithe Barn Lane and Butts Lane are coded `error` because the Built-Up Area Boundary or the tier is used as the GB7(1)(g)(iii) test, which is the tier error again. The Stratford count of six should be presented as "four route errors, two of which also pass (g)(iii) on the boundary or tier" to avoid double counting across columns.
6. **`partial` is a wide band.** It covers Tredington ("a local bus service", no stop or frequency) and Tonbridge and Malling Town Hill (bus stops adjacent, 700 m to the High Street, 1.4 km to the station). A split into `partial-thin` (label plus a bare transport remark) and `partial-route` (label plus distances or frequencies) would let the Stratford rate be compared fairly; on that split most of Stratford's 12 are thin and most of the other councils' 18 are route.
7. **The tier `error` label is stronger than the inspector evidence.** The earlier adversarial review (`service-village-page-adversarial-review.md`) found inspectors citing a tier inside allowed location findings in at least eight appeals, always alongside a daily bus, lit footways or an urban setting. The published page was corrected to that wording on 1 October; the four-errors note still defines error 1 as if the tier were irrelevant. Keep the code, because the Stratford reports in question give no route fact at all, but define the error as "tier or facilities list as the whole location finding", which is what the table in fact scores.
8. **The Connectivity Tool is a shared absence, not a Stratford fault.** Eight uses in 99 reports, and seven of those are three teams' templates. The memory rule already in the project (TR3(2) is one input; never a standalone gap) is right, and the four-errors note's "used in none of the 44" should carry the comparison.
9. **"Mixed" and "2024+note" are not the "2024-unconverted" defect.** Ten "mixed" reports elsewhere apply 2026 policies with a stale heading, a stale condition reason or a "tilted balance" sentence; none changes the test. The Stratford table's three "mixed" rows are the same. Only the unconverted rows (four at Stratford, three elsewhere) apply the wrong test.
10. **The template-weight comparison is partly a supply-position artefact.** The South Worcestershire councils claimed a five-year supply until 9 October 2026 and give the hierarchy "full weight"; the councils that cut weight (Stratford, West Northamptonshire, Maldon, East Devon, North Hertfordshire) are all short of supply. Wychavon's reports after mid-October, when appeal 6006301 found 4.49 years, are the natural test of whether the paragraph appears once S5(1)(j) opens.

### 3.3 Composition caveats

- Stratford's 44 are mostly delegated reports on one to seven homes; the held committee set is major schemes, where design is often reserved and location is argued from a transport assessment. The fair comparators for the delegated habit are Warwick, Bromsgrove and South Worcestershire: Warwick repeats the Stratford pattern, Bromsgrove and Wychavon do not.
- Washed-over villages are rare outside Stratford and Warwick in the sample (Norton Lindsey, Burton Green, Pound Lane), so the Annex B comparison rests on few cases.
- Five agents scored five sets with one rubric; each checked its own quotations. Cross-agent consistency was tested only by the shared rules and by the `(c)` sections of each summary, which raise the same points independently (the TR3 threshold, the refusal DP3(3) cases, the width of `partial`).
- Outcomes are missing for Elmbridge 13 October and Dacorum 15 October (minutes not yet published), and three Bromsgrove and Warwick files had no notice or no report.

## 4. Verdict

- **The Stratford findings are not an artefact of the method.** Scored identically, 22 other councils make the tier error a third as often, the Annex B route error less than half as often, and approve after an unaddressed design conflict a fifth as often per report. The template weight paragraph is almost unique to Stratford in form. Warwick is the one council that reproduces the Stratford pattern, and it shares Stratford's plan structure and Green Belt.
- **Three of the Stratford codes are harder than the evidence supports** and should be re-presented rather than withdrawn: TR3 "not mentioned" for schemes of five homes or fewer (a threshold point officers and inspectors both take), DP3(3) "error" on refusals (formal), and the Connectivity Tool (a universal absence).
- **Two readings need stating.** Whether a landscape-character conflict is a DP3 conflict (the table's broad reading is arguable, Elmbridge and Dacorum read it narrowly), and that the `error`/`partial` tier codes measure drafting (no route fact at all) rather than the inspectors' rule (tier plus a daily bus or lit footway can suffice).
- **One finding is strengthened.** Approvals after a found design conflict with no clear-justification question: 15 at Stratford against three across 22 councils.

## 5. Changes to make to the Stratford note and table

1. In `stratford-officer-reports-four-errors.md`, add this note's headline table after "Counts" and cite the TSV.
2. Recode Ravenstone's TR3 cell from `dodged` to `threshold-not-met`; add a `homes>=6 or GB7(1)(g)(iii)` filter to the TR3 count and report both numbers.
3. Report DP3(3) as approvals and refusals separately (15 and 3 at Stratford), and state the landscape-character reading.
4. Report Annex B as route errors (4) and (g)(iii)-on-boundary errors (2), and note that Tithe Barn Lane and Butts Lane also appear in the tier column.
5. Split `partial` into thin and route, or at least say in the counts paragraph that it is a wide band.
6. Add the Connectivity Tool comparison (8 of 99) beside "used in none of the 44".
7. Redefine error 1 as "tier or facilities list as the whole location finding", matching the corrected service-village page.
8. Align the refusal rule with the appeals review: a refusal that carries the design conflict as a reason is consistent with DP3(3) in outcome.
