# Batch 1: candidate examples for the NPPF Navigator

Appeal decisions dated 23 to 30 September 2026. Each entry names the navigator node file and the point, the case and paragraph, a verbatim quote (machine-checked against the letter text), and why it is stronger than what the node cites now, or how it contradicts the node. All are Planning Inspectorate (PINS) appeal decisions. "Hearing" marks the higher-authority ones; the rest are written representations.

## Contradicts a point the graph makes

### 1. S5(1)(j)(i) "physically well-related" read to include character (countryside.ts, `j` closeCall)
The node says: "On the text it is a physical test; character, design and access have their own policies, so a site can pass here and still fail those (6008785 ¶37, ¶39)." Three letters in this batch, two after hearings, read the limb to include character and setting harm, and fail it on that ground.

- Case: PINS-6007133 ¶40 (Bilsborrow, hearing, dismissed)
  Quote: "I take compliance against S5(1)(j)(i) to be not just the site’s own spatial relationship to the settlement of Bilsborrow, against which is it physically well-related due to the extent and positioning of the built form around it, but also how the proposal itself would relate to the surrounding character."
- Case: PINS-6008253 ¶80 (Aston Clinton, hearing, allowed under S5(4))
  Quote: "However, I have found that not only would the proposal be harmful to the character and appearance of the area, it would also harm the setting and context of the village. Albeit only causing limited harm. Even so, in my view, it is not physically well related to the settlement and therefore does not comply with criterion j of Policy S5."
- Case: PINS-6010422 ¶42 (Harpley, dismissed), which reaches the same result through the "nature of the development" proviso and adds highway safety
  Quote: "given the harm I have found in respect of character and appearance and the highway safety implications, the nature of the development would be inappropriate in this location and therefore criterion j. would not be satisfied."

Why it matters: this is a third reading alongside the physical-only and strict-form readings the node lists. It is now held at two hearings, so the closeCall should present it as a contested reading rather than state the physical reading as the text's meaning.

### 2. TR3(1)(a) not engaged, yet moderate (not limited) weight to car reliance (tr3.ts, `tr3Engaged`)
The node says that if TR3(1)(a) is not engaged, "any reliance on the car can still carry limited weight", and its effect records the harm as limited.

- Case: PINS-6010422 ¶49 (Harpley, one self-build home, dismissed under S5(4))
  Quote: "In this case, the development would not generate a significant amount of movement, given its small scale."
  Quote: "I find moderate negative weight should be attributed to this matter in this case."

Why it matters: an inspector found TR3(1)(a) not engaged for one home but still gave moderate weight to the poor walking route (600 m, no footway, unlit; ¶25) in an S5(4) balance. The node's fixed "limited" weight is narrower than practice.

### 3. The TR3 "impractical" proviso applied to a change of use (tr3.ts, `tr3Engaged` / `tr3` closeCall)
The node does not deal with the TR3(1)(a) proviso "unless the nature of the development would make this impractical".

- Case: PINS-6009619 ¶18 (Dalwood, holiday let to unrestricted dwelling, allowed)
  Quote: "I also find that the change of use nature of the proposal means that a genuine choice of transport modes for future occupiers would be impractical."

Why it matters: the inspector walked the route and found car reliance (¶5, ¶7), then treated a change of use of an existing building as making a genuine choice "impractical". This is a novel and contestable reading that an applicant could cite. The node should say how to answer it: the proviso concerns the nature of the development, not the fact that the building already exists.

## Stronger or gap-filling illustrations

### 4. DP3(3) "clear justification": both readings, each after a hearing (triggers.ts, `dp3` contested readings)
The node cites Polegate 6008314 ¶43 (necessity, allowed, trees) and Didcot 6009340 ¶22 (level balance, allowed, energy).

- Necessity, used to refuse character harm. Case: PINS-6007133 ¶59 (Bilsborrow, hearing, dismissed)
  Quote: "I do not find the specific and significant harm to the character and appearance of the countryside to be necessary to achieve the substantial public benefits"
- Level balance, for housing, after the S5(4) test. Case: PINS-6008253 ¶86 (Aston Clinton, hearing, allowed)
  Quote: "I consider that the substantial benefits are sufficient to provide clear justification for the conflict with Policy DP3 of the Framework, which would only cause limited harm."

Why stronger: both are hearings, both deal with countryside character harm rather than tree loss or energy, and 6007133 shows the necessity reading used to refuse, which neither current example does. 6008253 also shows the sequence: DP3(3) answered after the S5(4) balance (¶85–86).

### 5. A village absorbed into a town is a "large built-up area" for purpose (a) (greenbelt.ts, grey-belt purposes step)
The node cites Mangotsfield 3378284 ¶36 for "unless it has in effect been absorbed into a larger conurbation".

- Case: PINS-6008723 ¶14 (Lydiate, Sefton, dismissed)
  Quote: "Whilst Lydiate may have originated as a distinct village, the evidence before me indicates that development has resulted in a continuous urban form with Maghull. Having regard to the way in which the Local Plan identifies and treats the settlements, I find that Lydiate forms part of the same large built-up area as Maghull."

Why stronger: grey belt fails on this point alone, and the letter names the two tests used (continuous built form, and how the Local Plan treats the settlements). It also rejects a parish council and neighbourhood plan as evidence of separate identity (¶12), and the ¶18 "extended finger" finding completes the Annex E(3) reasoning.

### 6. S5(4) passed at appeal (balance.ts, `exceptional`, "yes" pointers)
The "yes" pointers cite no case.

- Case: PINS-6008253 ¶85 (Aston Clinton, 66 homes, hearing, allowed)
  Quote: "In my view therefore taken together the substantial benefits of the proposal would substantially outweigh the moderate weight I have applied to the adverse effects to the character of the countryside."

Why it fills a gap: a rare S5(4) pass after a hearing. The facts: a substantial supply shortfall, 25% affordable housing secured, and the Council's own emerging allocation covering the site (¶73–78). Caution: accessibility was agreed rather than tested, and plan weight was cut on 2024 lines (¶39).

### 7. HE6(4)'s named benefits filtered by necessity (heritage.ts, public-benefits step, `reuse` / `energy` options)
The options cite no case.

- Case: PINS-6006266 ¶23 (The Stables, Funtington, listed building consent, dismissed)
  Quote: "it is not demonstrated that this conversion scheme is the only way to secure the long-term reuse of the vacant/underused building nor enable energy efficiency and low carbon heating measures to be employed."

Why it fills a gap: it applies both named HE6(4) benefits and discounts them because the harmful scheme is not shown to be needed to deliver them.

### 8. Long-term structural survival as a public heritage benefit that outweighs harm (heritage.ts, HE6(4) "outweighed" pointer)
The "outweighed" pointer cites no case.

- Case: PINS-6010276 ¶33 (76 Andover Road, Cheltenham, listed building consent, allowed)
  Quote: "The proposed works would secure the long-term structural stability of the garden wall, thereby supporting its continued contribution to the significance of the Grade II listed building, Nos. 72-80 Andover Road and CCA. This is a meaningful public heritage benefit."

Why it fills a gap: a clear allowed HE6(4) balance. HE6(1) and HE6(3) are stated correctly (¶34), and the less harmful buttress alternative is rejected for lack of evidence (¶35).

### 9. Walking route tested for all users (tr3.ts, `tr3` method step "Test the route for all users")
The step cites no case.

- Case: PINS-6010422 ¶25 (Harpley, dismissed)
  Quote: "some may struggle to do that, including those with limited mobility or those with young children."
- Case: PINS-6009691 ¶10 (Hurst Green, dismissed), which also covers the walk to the bus
  Quote: "There is a bus stop within Hurst Green for Route 5 which provides a link to Clitheroe for access to a wider range of facilities. However, this would also require walking along an unlit road."

Why it fills a gap: direct wording for the all-users test, and for the point that a bus only helps if the walk to the stop is usable.

### 10. GB7(1)(e): a bigger replacement building still below "substantial" harm (greenbelt.ts, `gb7e` pass pointers)
The pass pointer lists "Similar or reduced volume and height compared with what is there".

- Case: PINS-6007335 ¶7 (Southend, commercial rebuild, allowed)
  Quote: "It is likely to be taller than that structure, with an unbroken ridge line that contributes to a significant increase in scale and massing by comparison."
- Case: PINS-6007335 ¶8
  Quote: "Although the proposal would have a moderate harmful effect on openness of the Green Belt overall, this would not meet the threshold of substantial harm to openness set out in Framework policy GB7 paragraph 1(e)."

Why it matters: a larger building plus new parking still passed, because screening confined the visual effect. The pointer implies similar or reduced size is needed; it is not.

### 11. S5(1)(d): lifting a holiday-occupancy restriction is a PDL change of use (countryside.ts, `s5d`)
The pass pointer "Material change of use of lawfully developed land to residential" cites no case.

- Case: PINS-6009619 ¶15 (Dalwood, allowed)
  Quote: "The appeal property accords with the definition of PDL in the glossary in Annex B of the Framework."

Why it fills a gap: removing a holiday restriction from a converted building is treated as a material change of use of previously developed land (PDL) to residential under S5(1)(d). This is relevant to holiday-let conversions generally. Pair it with item 3, which covers how the same letter handled TR3.

### 12. Annex A(2): the 2024 "out-of-date" route in allowed appeals (triggers.ts, development-plan contested reading 3)
The node cites two dismissals (6008785 ¶38, 6007466 ¶25).

- Case: PINS-6008253 ¶39 (Aston Clinton, hearing, allowed)
  Quote: "given that the housing land supply shortfall is substantial, I give conflict with the relevant policies limited weight."
- Case: PINS-6009849 ¶37 (Navestock, allowed)
  Quote: "The overall housing land supply position indicates that the development plan is failing to meet its strategic challenges and the settlement strategy is not working effectively."

Why stronger: in both, the reduced weight helped the appeal succeed. At Navestock it is the only reason a car-reliant location did not count against the scheme, because TR3 was never applied. That makes them better warnings than the two dismissals.

## Not recommended
- Towan Cross 6009030 ¶27 paraphrases DP3(3) without its clear-justification qualifier. It is a misstatement, not a reading worth adding to the graph, and the result was a dismissal.
- The Lawn Cottage 6007335 ¶9 and Navestock 6009849 ¶12 openness wording repeats what 6011330 ¶10 already gives.
