# Batch 5: candidate examples for the NPPF Navigator

Cases from batch 5 (incoming 30 Sep 2026) that are clearer, fill a gap, or contradict a point in `pages/england/nppf-navigator/graph/nodes/*.ts`. All are inspector decisions on written representations unless noted. Quotes were machine-checked against the letter text and the cited paragraph.

## Fill a gap

### 1. triggers.ts, `f7` node: the F5 exemption and access routes
- **Point.** Help step says "Note any exemption in F5(2)" with no example.
- **Case.** PINS-6004952, Tarleton, West Lancashire (9 homes, dismissed).
  - ¶8: "The FRADS identifies part of the access serving the site within Flood Zone 2 and the more recent flood modelling identifies a greater extent of flood risk than was previously understood."
  - ¶9: "A proportionate approach to an assessment does not displace the requirement in Policy F5 for a sequential test where the development is in an area known to be at risk from flooding and does not fall within one of the identified exceptions."
- **Why stronger.** Only example of the F5 exemption being tested: dwellings outside the flood zone do not qualify if the access or escape route is at risk, and an absent EA objection does not establish the exemption (¶9).

### 2. tr3.ts, `tr3Lit` and `tr3Speed`: lighting and speed on the route
- **Point.** Neither question has a case in its help text.
- **Case.** PINS-6012304, Marton, Cheshire West and Chester (2 homes, dismissed).
  - ¶9: "There is a 40mph speed limit along Dalefords Lane meaning there would be relatively fast-moving traffic."
  - ¶9: "this route would not be a realistic for many future residents especially a parent with young children or those with mobility issues."
  - ¶10: "The absence of lighting combined with the isolated nature of the routes would deter regular use by future occupiers of the proposed development, especially anyone with limited mobility or travelling alone."
  - ¶11: "the proximity of other built form is not a measure of sustainability."
- **Why stronger.** Deals with the alternative-footpath argument: unlit, secluded public footpaths fail even where they avoid the road. It also tests the route for named user groups (children, mobility, lone travellers). ¶11 is a crisp answer to the argument that the site is next to other houses.

### 3. tr3.ts, `tr3Services`: distance to everyday services
- **Point.** No case in the services question.
- **Case.** PINS-6008791, Colney, South Norfolk (1 home, dismissed).
  - ¶33: "However, other day-to-day facilities, such as food shops and primary education, are located beyond a convenient walking distance from the site."
- **Why stronger.** Shows that footways, lighting, frequent buses and a signal crossing (¶32) do not help when everyday services are beyond walking distance. The employment and hospital destinations nearby did not count as everyday services. Location was not decisive here (N6 was), so treat it as supporting only.

### 4. greenbelt.ts, GB7(1)(g)(ii): unmet need for non-housing uses
- **Point.** The node treats (g)(ii) as a housing and footnote 41 question only.
- **Case.** PINS-6010709, Nazeing, Epping Forest (B8 vehicle storage, dismissed).
  - ¶20: "In my view, Policy GB7.g.ii requires an assessment of the need for the specific type of development proposed, rather than whether it is required at this particular location."
  - ¶22: "there is no substantive evidence to demonstrate that the overall supply of B8 land is insufficient to meet current demand."
- **Why stronger.** For commercial grey belt it links (g)(ii) to E2(2) market signals (¶19). An operator's failed site search is not evidence of area-wide unmet need.

## Contradict or qualify a point in the graph

### 5. greenbelt.ts, GB7(1)(c) closeCall: "No 2026 appeal in the decisions reviewed analyses GB7(1)(c) in any depth"
- **Case.** PINS-6010709, Nazeing (as above).
  - ¶9: "when viewed ‘from the ground’ the nearest residential properties are significantly dispersed and there is not a discernible group of closely associated residential buildings, which would be expected within a village or rural community."
  - ¶11: "Given its significant size and potential to accommodate a significant number of light goods and public carrier vehicles, in my view, the proposal cannot be considered to constitute limited infilling."
- **Why.** It applies Wood v SSCLG (the on-the-ground village test) and the limited element (¶8–11). The closeCall statement is now out of date. Also PINS-6010537 ¶16 (not a gap in a built-up frontage).

### 6. heritage.ts, HE6(4) public benefits: "Long-term reuse of a vacant or underused listed building — secured"
- **Case.** PINS-6006903, Whitlees bastle, Northumberland National Park (LBC conditions, dismissed).
  - ¶22: "This benefit could be provided through a more sensitive scheme, as evidenced by the existence of the extant approvals."
- **Why.** Adds a filter to the named benefit: reuse carries little weight where an extant, less harmful consent already delivers it. It fits the "less harmful design would deliver the same benefits" fail factor.

### 7. tr3.ts closeCall: rural allowance and a lower threshold
- **Point.** The graph says TR3(1)(e) "does not qualify the (a) test", and gives 6009632 and 6010973 as allowals that accepted less.
- **Cases (counter-examples to cite as differing).**
  - PINS-6008987 ¶39 (dismissed on design): "Given the location of the site adjacent to a settlement, which is supported in principle under Framework Policy S5 in certain circumstances, I find limited weight should be attributed to this matter in this case." The same paragraph reads TR3(1)(e) as a rural allowance, although the letter found no pavements, unlit roads and two buses a weekday (¶8–9).
  - PINS-6009837 ¶13 and PINS-6011410 ¶15 (both allowed, Coventry Green Belt, same inspector): "such that day-to-day travel would not be wholly dependent on the private car." This passes (g)(iii) on a test of whether travel is wholly car dependent, with no distances or bus frequencies.
  - PINS-6010537 ¶28 (allowed, Green Belt PIP): "the appeal site is in a suitable location with convenient access to public transport and opportunities for walking to a range of services and facilities." A one-sentence (g)(iii) pass.
- **Why.** These are further examples of the lenient reading, useful for the "Some inspectors have accepted less" sentence. None is a better authority for the correct test.

### 8. countryside.ts, S5(1)(j)(i) step: "Keep accessibility separate"
- **Case.** PINS-6009990, Wiswell, Ribble Valley (PIP, appeal planning officer, dismissed).
  - ¶15: "Barrow is not a significant distance to the south of the appeal site, but given my findings on its accessibility in regard thereto, I would not consider it to be physically well related."
- **Why.** It applies the accessibility reading of "physically well-related" (one of the four readings in DISTILLATION-GUIDE §10, item 14), which the node tells users to keep separate. It is low authority (appeal planning officer), so cite it only as an example of the divergence.

## Comparable or additional (not clearly stronger)

- **countryside.ts, S5(1)(j)(i) fail factor "Near only a hamlet or scattered houses".** PINS-6012304 ¶29: "The adjacent ribbon development is not defined as a settlement within the development plan and is no more than a scattered group of houses outside a predominantly built-up area." It tracks the Annex B wording closely and could be added alongside 6009593 ¶13.
- **greenbelt.ts, grey belt "Assess the site, not the wider parcel" (6010471 ¶15).** PINS-6010537 ¶21: "The Council has not provided any compelling evidence that the contribution the appeal site makes to any of the purposes of the Green Belt has been assessed, separate to the wider parcel of land in which it is included in the Green Belt Study." It puts the evidence burden on the council, but the same letter's (g)(iii) finding is weak (see 7).
- **greenbelt.ts, GB7(1)(e) pass factor "Similar or reduced volume and height".** PINS-6008176 (allowed): a replacement house with a 441 m² footprint (against 118 m²) passed (e) because the whole farmstead was PDL and the outbuildings were removed. ¶11: "The proposal therefore meets the exception in Policy GB 7 (1) (e) of the Framework." This qualifies the pass factor: the comparison is with all PDL on the site, not the house alone.
- **greenbelt.ts, footnote 41.** PINS-6009837 ¶9 (and 6011410 ¶11): "the HDT remains the published measure for the purposes of applying national policy at the time of this decision." A council's challenge to the ONS figures behind the HDT did not displace it.
- **DP3(3) explicit clear-justification findings.** PINS-6004363 ¶28 ("Based on my findings, there are no clear justifications for the harm.") and PINS-6008987 ¶40 are further examples. They are no stronger than the cited 6008167 ¶21 and 6006720 ¶34.
