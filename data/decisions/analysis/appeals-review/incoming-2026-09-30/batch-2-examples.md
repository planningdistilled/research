# Batch 2 (incoming 30 Sep 2026): candidate examples for the NPPF Navigator

These are cases in batch 2 that illustrate a Navigator point better than the case it currently cites, fill a point that has no example, or contradict a point the graph makes. Quotes are verbatim from the letter text, and each has been machine-checked against the cited paragraph. The graph's own wording is paraphrased here, not quoted.

Top three: the first two (Chaldon, on the Connectivity Tool and on bus frequency) and the DP3(2)(d) gap (North Weald).

### 1. tr3.ts, `tr3Tool` help (Connectivity Tool): PINS-6006497 ¶42-44
- **Point in graph:** the same score of 52 has gone both ways (failed at 6011736, passed at 6010471), and the tool does not measure footway width or lighting.
- **Case:** Chaldon, Tandridge. Inquiry, allowed 25 Sep 2026. PINS-6006497 ¶42-44.
- **Quote:** "is a relative measure not an absolute measure of connectivity and it shows how places compare to other places and should always be interpreted comparatively"
- **Why stronger:** It comes from an inquiry, and it says how to read the score, not just that results vary. At ¶43-44 the inspector reads 52 against Tandridge's own bands (band A) and against named settlements where development is accepted (Woldingham 56, Godstone 55, Nutfield 52). It explains the 6011736 and 6010471 split and gives a method. Suggest adding a step: read the score against the authority's bands and against comparator settlements.

### 2. tr3.ts, `tr3Bus` options and the `tr3` pass pointer (bus frequency): PINS-6006497 ¶39-40
- **Point in graph:** a pass pointer lists an evidenced bus at least hourly, running when people travel to work and school. The option labelled "limited" covers a few journeys a day.
- **Case:** Chaldon, inquiry, allowed. PINS-6006497 ¶39-40 (with ¶37 on term-time school buses and ¶45 on the conclusion).
- **Quote:** "the 411 bus service does provide a genuine sustainable transport mode to reach other facilities and services throughout the day, including for grocery shopping at several supermarkets"
- **Why it matters (contradicts, in part):** A bus about every two hours, with no commuter-time service, plus term-time school buses, was accepted at inquiry as part of a genuine choice. The inspector accepted that commuting would mostly be by car (¶39). This is the highest-authority pass on a sub-hourly service in the database. The graph's close-call note treats infrequent buses as a lenient-rural outlier (6010973). Chaldon sits on a town edge in band A, so it is better read as the "local trips" reading of TR3 than as a rural concession. Worth adding as a contested reading: a genuine choice for local and school trips, with commuting by car accepted.

### 3. triggers.ts, DP3(3) conflict options, `movement` (DP3(2)(d)), which has no example: PINS-6009621 ¶40
- **Point in graph:** DP3(2)(d) (walking, wheeling, cycling and public-transport connections) is listed as a conflict that can engage DP3(3), with no case cited.
- **Case:** North Weald Bassett, Epping Forest. Written representations, dismissed 24 Sep 2026. PINS-6009621 ¶40.
- **Quote:** "Policy DP3.2.d requires proposals to reflect principles such as providing good connections to nearby settlements and prioritising walking, wheeling, cycling and public transport"
- **Why stronger:** It fills the gap. Poor accessibility (unlit lanes without footways, low connectivity) was treated as a DP3(2)(d) conflict, making DP3 a should-be-refused policy under S5(2), on a Green Belt site that had passed GB7(1)(e). It shows accessibility can bite through DP3 even when TR3(1)(a) is not the gate. Caveat: the letter does not separately reason about clear justification.

### 4. tr3.ts, `tr3` method step on testing the route for all users (no case cited): PINS-6005652 ¶12
- **Point in graph:** test the route for all users (children, older people, wheelchair and pushchair users), after dark and in bad weather. Only TR4 text is cited.
- **Case:** Upton St Leonards, Stroud. Written representations, dismissed 29 Sep 2026. PINS-6005652 ¶12.
- **Quote:** "this uphill walk, however short, would be unappealing to most pedestrians and probably not feasible for those with restricted mobility or young children, particularly in winter months"
- **Why stronger:** A direct inspector finding for this step, covering all users, season, gradient and "however short". It pairs with 6007428 ¶15-16 (a short distance does not excuse walking in the carriageway). TR3 is not named; the letter applies local policy CP13 and national policy on movement (¶37, ¶40).

### 5. greenbelt.ts, `greyBeltUndermine` method, "in a meaningful way": PINS-6006497 ¶28
- **Point in graph:** ask whether the rest of the Green Belt could still serve its purposes in a meaningful way, citing 3378284 ¶41 (Mangotsfield, written representations, itself flagged borderline in issue 4).
- **Case:** Chaldon, inquiry, allowed. PINS-6006497 ¶28.
- **Quote:** "the proposal would not materially affect the ability of the remaining Green Belt across the plan area to serve all five Green Belt purposes in a meaningful way"
- **Why stronger:** Same test in the same words, from an inquiry. At ¶27 the letter also confirms that the assessment is not confined to the site or a single purpose, following the PPG.

### 6. heritage.ts, public benefits option `energy` (no case cited): PINS-6009857 ¶22
- **Point in graph:** energy efficiency and low-carbon heating are named HE6(4) benefits; measures that only meet Building Regulations are neutral.
- **Case:** Marlborough Avenue, Hull. Written representations, dismissed 23 Sep 2026. PINS-6009857 ¶22.
- **Quote:** "the actual thermal improvements by the insulation and environmental benefits have not been quantified, and it is not clear if similar or greater benefits could not have been found from alternative sustainable technologies in a less harmful manner"
- **Why stronger:** It fills the gap with a clear reason for discounting a named HE6(4) benefit: the gain is unquantified and less harmful alternatives were not excluded. The insulation got modest weight. Caveat: the same letter gives CC2 substantial weight to the same insulation in the overall balance (¶33), so cite ¶22 for the HE6(4) filter only.

### 7. heritage.ts, HE4(3) deliberate neglect (not covered): PINS-6008732 ¶21
- **Point in graph:** none. The heritage node does not deal with HE4(3), under which a deteriorated state caused by deliberate neglect is disregarded.
- **Case:** Seamer, North Yorkshire (listed building consent). Written representations, dismissed 24 Sep 2026. PINS-6008732 ¶21.
- **Quote:** "the evidence before me is insufficient to establish that the neglect in this case was deliberate. I have therefore taken the present condition of the outbuildings into account"
- **Why it helps:** It fills the gap. HE4(3) needs evidence that the neglect was deliberate; without it, the actual condition counts. At ¶20 the letter adds that a condition report by a joiner, not a structural engineer, carries limited weight. That is useful for a pointer on demolition justified by condition.

### 8. core.ts, S3(2) split-site option (no case cited): PINS-6011227 ¶25
- **Point in graph:** where a site is partly inside and partly outside a settlement, S4 applies to the part inside and S5 to the rest, then an overall view is taken. No case is cited.
- **Case:** Uffington, South Kesteven. Written representations, dismissed 25 Sep 2026. PINS-6011227 ¶25 (and ¶26).
- **Quote:** "Where a development proposal falls partly within and partly outside a settlement, policies S4 and S5 of the Framework should be applied to the relevant parts which are inside or outside of the settlement boundary"
- **Why it helps:** It fills the gap with an appeal example. The split was made within one plot: a walled former garden read as inside the village, a paddock as outside (¶26). The guide's example is a council case (maldon-26-00066-OUTM).

### 9. countryside.ts, S5(1)(c) method (a new home still goes through S5(3) and HO11): PINS-6005652 ¶30-31
- **Point in graph:** if the scheme creates a new home outside any settlement or group of houses, inspectors have still applied S5(3) and HO11 (6008115 ¶26, 6009718 ¶61).
- **Case:** Upton St Leonards, Stroud. PINS-6005652 ¶30-31.
- **Quote:** "The requirements of S5(1) c appear satisfied in these respects"
- **Why stronger:** It is the clearest sequence in the database. The letter finds (c) apparently met (¶30), then says the home is isolated under S5(3) (¶31), then fails HO11 because the scheme would not enhance its immediate setting (¶33). It shows that a (c) pass does not rescue an isolated home.

### 10. triggers.ts, `devPlan` method (give very limited weight only to the inconsistent part), for a Green Belt policy: PINS-6009621 ¶9
- **Point in graph:** give very limited weight only to the inconsistent part (6005809 ¶76, a spatial-strategy example).
- **Case:** North Weald Bassett, Epping Forest. PINS-6009621 ¶9 (and ¶17).
- **Quote:** "Consequently, the wording of Policy DM4, insofar as it relates to openness, is not consistent with the current Framework."
- **Why it helps:** A crisp Green Belt example: an older no-greater-impact openness test against GB7(1)(e)'s substantial-harm threshold, cut only "insofar as it relates to openness". Chaldon PINS-6006497 ¶17 does the same for a policy that does not recognise grey belt, and Uffington PINS-6011227 ¶24 for an infill-location clause.

### 11. Contradiction (weak authority; do not cite as an example): tr3.ts, count planned improvements only where secured: PINS-6004780 ¶33-34
- **Point in graph:** count planned improvements only where they are secured.
- **Case:** Hempnall Green, South Norfolk. PIP, allowed 25 Sep 2026. PINS-6004780 ¶33-34.
- **Quote:** "future occupiers would have a convenient, clear, and comfortable cycle route to the village"
- **Why noted:** The inspector allowed the PIP partly on a footway to a bus stop left to technical details consent (¶34), and accepted a 1.6 km cycle along unlit roads without a footway. TR3 and S5 were never applied. This is a shortcut to flag, not a contrary authority.

### 12. Contradiction (weak authority; do not cite as an example): tr3.ts fail pointers on short car trips: PINS-6008864 ¶13
- **Point in graph:** relying on residents' car use, or on short car trips, is not a genuine choice of transport modes.
- **Case:** Somerford, Cheshire East. PIP, dismissed on heritage 30 Sep 2026. PINS-6008864 ¶13.
- **Quote:** "the development would retain a reasonable and overall sustainable relationship with the services and facilities available within the nearby settlement of Congleton"
- **Why noted:** A short car journey was treated as a sustainable relationship, and the location harm was found limited. It is an example of the shortcut the graph warns against, not authority for it.
