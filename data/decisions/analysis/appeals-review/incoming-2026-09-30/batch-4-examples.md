# Batch 4: candidate navigator examples

Cases from batch 4 (incoming 2026-09-30) that are clearer, more on-point, or fill a gap compared with the cases cited in `pages/england/nppf-navigator/graph/nodes/*.ts`, or that contradict a point the graph makes.

Letter quotes are in double quotes and cite the letter paragraph. Every one was machine-checked against `data/open-sources/pins-corpus/<ref>.txt` and matched the cited paragraph. Wording from the navigator graph is in single quotes.

## 1. triggers.ts, dp3Conflicts: the ‘DP3(2)(d) Movement’ option has no case cited

- Case: PINS-6007677 (Brenzett, Folkestone and Hythe; one self-build dwelling; dismissed 30 Sep 2026; written representations)
- Quote: "It would also conflict with paragraph 2(d) of Policy DP3 as walking, wheeling, cycling and public transport is not prioritised through the introduction of a car dependent development. There is no clear justification for the conflict with these paragraphs." (PINS-6007677 ¶27)
- Why stronger: This fills a gap. It is the first dataset letter to route car dependence through DP3(2)(d), make the explicit clear-justification finding, and then apply the S5(2) trigger, all in one paragraph. It did so for a single dwelling on previously developed land that passed S5(1)(d).

## 2. tr3.ts, tr3Engaged: ‘If it is not engaged ... any reliance on the car can still carry limited weight’ (qualifies this)

- Case: PINS-6008773 (Singleborough, Buckinghamshire; PIP for one dwelling; dismissed 29 Sep 2026)
- Quote: "whilst the proposal is only for one dwelling, it also includes more general principles about sustainable patterns of movement" (PINS-6008773 ¶30)
- Why stronger: This qualifies the graph's point. For one dwelling, the inspector held that TR3's general principles still apply, paired them with DP3(2)(d) (¶31), and counted car reliance among the adverse effects that substantially outweighed the benefits: the site "would rely almost entirely on the private car to meet its day-to-day needs" (PINS-6008773 ¶32). Read with item 1, car dependence for a single home can be decisive through DP3(2)(d) and DP3(3), not only a limited weight. The help text could note that an unengaged TR3(1)(a) does not cap car reliance at limited weight where there is a DP3(2)(d) conflict without clear justification.

## 3. tr3.ts, contested reading on existing residents walking the route: the ‘Accepted’ side cites only a council report

- Case: PINS-6010706 (Stibb Cross, Torridge; condition appeal, one self-build dwelling; allowed 30 Sep 2026)
- Quote: "The unchallenged evidence before me is that no pedestrian injury collisions have been recorded hereabouts in recent years, despite the reliance of these other properties on use of the verge." (PINS-6010706 ¶9)
- Why stronger: The ‘Accepted’ reading currently rests on a council decision (stratford-26-00918-PIP). This is an inspector accepting a verge-only route beside the A388, relying on existing residents' use and a nil collision record. The inspector also accepted that removing the footway "may well result in access not being suitable for all people" (PINS-6010706 ¶13). It is higher authority for the accepted side, and a direct contrast with Hatton Station 6006637 ¶23-26. Caveat: it is a DM6 proportionality decision on a condition for one dwelling, and the location had already been accepted by the Council.

## 4. tr3.ts, tr3 closeCall: ‘Some inspectors have accepted less in rural areas’ (6009632, 6010973), now at the Green Belt gate

- Case: PINS-6007484 (Tarleton, West Lancashire; Green Belt PIP for 6 to 9 homes; allowed 23 Sep 2026)
- Quote: "Future occupants would be likely to rely upon the private car for a proportion of journeys. However, that is not unusual in a rural context and does not, in itself, render the location unsustainable." (PINS-6007484 ¶14)
- Why stronger: This is the same weak rural reading applied at the GB7(1)(g)(iii) gate, where the graph says a sustainable-location finding is required whatever the scale. The letter relies on the 2024 idea that sustainable transport solutions "will vary between urban and rural areas" (PINS-6007484 ¶13) and gives no route facts (distances, lighting, speed, bus frequency). It is a clearer counter-example than 6009632 because it is a Green Belt allowal of up to 9 homes.

## 5. heritage.ts, heEffect: ‘Less than substantial harm is a 2024 term’; name the degree (no case cited)

- Case: PINS-6003226 (Kennington Park Road, Southwark; LBC; dismissed 29 Sep 2026)
- Quote: "In my judgement and utilising the terminology of the 2026 version of the Framework, the works have resulted in harm to the significance of this designated heritage asset. Given the cumulative effect of the appeal scheme works, the degree of that harm would be high." (PINS-6003226 ¶22)
- Why stronger: This fills a gap. In the same paragraph the inspector notes that the appellant called the harm "less-than-substantial" (PINS-6003226 ¶22), then switches to the 2026 method and grades the harm high. It is the cleanest illustration of the closeCall instruction.

## 6. heritage.ts, he64 benefits: the ‘Energy efficiency or low-carbon heating, beyond the regulatory minimum’ row (no case cited)

- Case: PINS-6003226 (Kennington Park Road, Southwark)
- Quote: "It is feasible that the works could have improved the energy efficiency of the building, which would amount to an important public benefit. However, there is no evidence before me to demonstrate the energy efficiency gains that have occurred; and how they have been achieved." (PINS-6003226 ¶27)
- Why stronger: This fills a gap. It shows both halves of the row: the benefit is recognised as important, but carries no weight without evidence of the gains, and it was also discounted because "there could have been other, potentially less harmful ways, in which the gains could have been realised" (PINS-6003226 ¶27). Grove House is the companion for the regulatory-minimum wording (in the dataset but not cited): "there is little evidence that it would be outstanding design which promotes high levels of sustainability or that it would go beyond compliance with the requirements of national and local planning policy" (PINS-6006023 ¶35).

## 7. greenbelt.ts, GB7(1)(e): ‘A garden outside a built-up area can be PDL (6012162 ¶8–9)’

- Case: PINS-6010198 (Heronsgate, Three Rivers; replacement self-build dwelling; allowed 24 Sep 2026)
- Quote: "It does not have the attributes of a built-up area including a lack of street lighting, pedestrian footways or any apparent or well-defined building lines." (PINS-6010198 ¶12)
- Why stronger: It gives the factors for a built-up area (lighting, footways, building lines, lane-like roads, low density) in a developed Green Belt settlement. It also shows the GB7 categories working as alternatives. A replacement that failed GB7(1)(b) as materially larger passed GB7(1)(e), because size "is not the test of this element of the policy of the Framework which requires that a proposal should not cause substantial harm to the openness of the Green Belt" (PINS-6010198 ¶14).

## 8. triggers.ts, dp3 contested ‘clear justification’ readings: the necessity side cites only Polegate 6008314 ¶43, where justification was found

- Case: PINS-6012293 (Greenham, Somerset; barn conversion, NDHA; dismissed 24 Sep 2026)
- Quote: "While the proposal would retain and expose elements of the historic structure and provide it with a viable use, this does not clearly justify the presented design." (PINS-6012293 ¶24)
- Why stronger: This is a dismissal applying a necessity or less-harmful-alternative reading. The inspector added that "any such benefits could conceivably be achieved through a less harmful" scheme (PINS-6012293 ¶24). Polegate is an allowal on tree loss, so this adds the refusal side. For the level-balance reading, Brocton is a dismissal counterpart to Didcot 6009340 ¶22: "I have not been provided with any clear justification for the proposal that would outweigh conflict with design policies in the Framework." (PINS-6011888 ¶24).

## 9. triggers.ts, devPlan (Annex A(2)): the step says to name the national policy the inconsistent part conflicts with

- Case: PINS-6008773 (Singleborough, Buckinghamshire)
- Quote: "Though the proposal conflicts with policies S1, S2 and S3 of the LP, they are materially inconsistent with policy S5 of the Framework, which provides more broad support for limiting infilling outside of settlements. As a result, this conflict is given very limited weight in accordance with the Annexe A of the Framework." (PINS-6008773 ¶27)
- Why stronger: It names the national policy and the specific S5 category (limited infilling) that the local restriction bars, and applies exactly the very limited weight Annex A(2) prescribes. The current examples are 6001260 ¶28 and 6005809 ¶76, and 6005809 gave limited rather than very limited weight.

## 10. triggers.ts, devPlan contested readings: a pattern not yet listed (contradicts Annex A(2))

- Case: PINS-6007352 (Melksham, Wiltshire; dismissed 30 Sep 2026)
- Quote: "For this reason, I afford the conflict with the development strategy only moderate weight." (PINS-6007352 ¶25)
- Why it matters: The same paragraph finds CP1, CP2 and CP15 "materially inconsistent with the national decision-making policies of the Framework, particularly Policy S5" (PINS-6007352 ¶25) and then gives moderate, not very limited, weight, contrary to the text of Annex A(2). Folkestone is a related variant that reaches very limited weight but reasons from housing delivery, the 2024 route: "The Council’s spatial strategy is therefore not meeting the local housing needs of the area" (PINS-6007677 ¶20). The same inspector as Melksham relied on CP1/CP2 with no Annex A(2) step in Semington: "the development conflicts with Policies CP1, CP2 and CP48 of the WCS and therefore the development plan taken as a whole" (PINS-6008970 ¶27). Useful as a caution under the ‘Consistent, but reduced for housing supply’ reading, or as a fourth reading.

## 11. countryside.ts, S5(1)(j) closeCall: ‘On the text it is a physical test; character, design and access have their own policies’ (contrary reading)

- Case: PINS-6007352 (Melksham, Wiltshire)
- Quote: "For the reasons set out above, I have found that the site is not physically well-related to the settlement both in terms of accessibility to services and character and appearance." (PINS-6007352 ¶27)
- Why it matters: A clear, recent example of the broad reading the graph advises against: the physical relationship is failed on accessibility and character together. The step already says inspectors differ; this is a crisp quote for the strict side.

None of the other batch-4 letters is a clearly better illustration than what is cited. Seamer PINS-6009092 ¶29-35 is a model HE5(2) to HE6(4) to HE4(2) sequence, comparable to, not better than, the cited heritage examples.
