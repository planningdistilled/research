# Batch 6 (incoming 30 Sep 2026): candidate examples for the NPPF Navigator

These are cases in batch 6 that illustrate a Navigator point better than the case it currently cites, fill a point that has no example, or contradict a point the graph makes. Quotes are verbatim from the letter text, and each has been machine-checked against the cited paragraph. The graph's own wording is paraphrased here, not quoted.

Top three: the Buntingford inquiry on the reach of DP3(1) (a contradiction), Ware Park on measuring distance to services, not the settlement edge (a gap), and Preston on DP3(2)(a) liveability (a gap).

### 1. triggers.ts, `dp3Conflicts` option `context` and the landscape node (which quotes DP3(1) with N2(1)(a)): PINS-6008238 ¶123-124, ¶168-169
- **Point in graph:** the DP3(1) context option includes urbanising a rural edge, and the landscape node pairs N2(1)(a) with DP3(1). This treats harm to countryside character as a possible DP3(1) conflict that can engage DP3(3).
- **Case:** Buntingford, East Hertfordshire. Inquiry, allowed 24 Sep 2026 (600 homes).
- **Quote (PINS-6008238 ¶124):** "national decision-making policy DP3(1) relates to matters such as on-site scale, layout, landscaping and appearance rather than to the effect of a proposal on the landscape character and visual attributes of the countryside"
- **Why it matters (contradicts):** An inquiry inspector held that landscape-character harm belongs to N2, not DP3(1), so DP3(3) is not engaged by it. In the alternative (¶168-169) he ran only the S5(2) and substantially-outweighed test, calling DP3(1) the refusal policy, and asked no clear-justification question. This is the highest-authority statement on DP3(1)'s reach so far, and it cuts against the graph's rural-edge reading. Suggest a contested-reading note on the `context` option: DP3(1)'s own text refers to the site's setting, but this inquiry confined it to on-site design. Its alternative reasoning is also a fourth allowed example of the issue-1 pattern (DP3 conflict with no clear-justification step).

### 2. tr3.ts, `tr3Services` question and method step on walking the actual route (no case on where to measure to): PINS-6006224 ¶13
- **Point in graph:** the services question asks for the walk to everyday services. No case shows the common appellant move of quoting distance to the settlement edge.
- **Case:** Ware Park, East Hertfordshire. Written representations, dismissed 28 Sep 2026 (Green Belt PIP, limb (iii) failed).
- **Quote (PINS-6006224 ¶13):** "these distances are to the edge of these settlements and the distance to key services, facilities and public transport would be significantly further"
- **Why stronger:** It fills the gap: 0.72 km and 1.15 km to two town edges did not count, because the trip is to services. The same letter at ¶11 reads a Connectivity Tool score of 49 (90th percentile within "Rural hamlets and isolated dwellings") as relative only, which pairs with the batch-2 Chaldon point for the `tr3Tool` help. Quote for that: see item 3.

### 3. tr3.ts, `tr3Tool` help (the score is relative): PINS-6006224 ¶11
- **Point in graph:** the same score has gone both ways; the tool does not measure footway width or lighting.
- **Quote (PINS-6006224 ¶11):** "The score is a relative measure which shows how places compare to other places rather than whether its connectivity is “good” or “bad” in isolation"
- **Why stronger:** It adds a specific trap: a high percentile *within a rural class* was still a fail on the route facts (¶13, ¶17). It complements Chaldon (batch 2), which read a score against district bands and passed.

### 4. triggers.ts, `dp3Conflicts` option `liveability` (DP3(2)(a)), which has no example: PINS-6009669 ¶13, ¶22
- **Point in graph:** DP3(2)(a) liveability is listed as a conflict that can engage DP3(3), with no case cited.
- **Case:** Broadgate, Preston. Written representations, dismissed 28 Sep 2026 (HMO).
- **Quote (PINS-6009669 ¶13):** "These policies are consistent with Policy DP3 of the Framework, which seeks, amongst other things, to create well designed places which achieve liveability, support healthy, mixed, vibrant and integrated communities and function well over time"
- **Why stronger:** It fills the gap. HMO overconcentration (five HMOs clustered in one short terrace) was treated as a DP3 liveability conflict. At ¶22 that failure decided S4 as a should-be-refused policy. Caveat: the letter omits the clear-justification words. Contrast PINS-6013087 (same inspector, no overconcentration, allowed).

### 5. countryside.ts, `s5j` closeCall (which reading of physically well-related): PINS-6008238 ¶22 and PINS-6008693 ¶21
- **Point in graph:** state which reading you apply; on the text it is a physical test, and access has its own policies.
- **Quote (PINS-6008238 ¶22):** "it constitutes a soft connection bridging the settlement of Buntingford with the proposed development rather than being perceived as being a hard boundary, or a prominent landscape break"
- **Quote (PINS-6008693 ¶21):** "the indicative access from the site would be at the furthest point away from the village and along a section of the road that is narrow and in the absence of a footpath. The development would thus not be physically well-related to the existing settlement"
- **Why it matters:** Buntingford (inquiry, allowed) applies a physical reading: a trunk road in cutting, bridged by new links, with intervisibility, is not a hard edge. Pickwell (written representations, dismissed) reads the same words through access and the lack of a footpath, which is the accessibility reading the closeCall warns against. Both are useful as named examples of the two readings; Buntingford supports the graph's preferred reading at higher authority.

### 6. tr3.ts, `tr3` pass pointers and method step on counting secured improvements: PINS-6008238 ¶92, ¶77
- **Point in graph:** a pass needs a continuous footway, services within a comfortable walk, an evidenced bus at least hourly, and secured improvements. The contested passes cited are all written representations or hearings.
- **Quote (PINS-6008238 ¶92):** "With the proposals in place, the location of the development would limit the need to travel by private car, and it would offer a genuine choice of sustainable, quality and safe transport modes for residents and users"
- **Why stronger:** An inquiry pass built on secured improvements: lit and widened PROWs, a new footbridge and active-travel bridge, a toucan crossing, and bus services routed into the site at 30-minute frequency. At ¶77 it also answers the after-dark test: a less-surveilled footpath is acceptable where other lit routes exist for people who avoid it at night. It is a good illustration of the can-be-made-so limb of TR3(1)(a).

### 7. tr3.ts, `tr3` closeCall (rural context): PINS-6008693 ¶12 (fail) and PINS-6010020 ¶15 (lenient)
- **Point in graph:** some inspectors accepted less in rural areas (6009632, 6010973, both allowed); others failed despite the rural context (6008688, 6009718).
- **Quote (PINS-6008693 ¶12):** "Whilst I acknowledge that in rural areas greater dependency on the private car is a reality, this site is not well connected to the immediate village or services and facilities in the wider area"
- **Quote (PINS-6010020 ¶15):** "Consequently, given its rural context, the proposal would maximise the ability of people to make trips by public transport and other sustainable modes of travel"
- **Why it matters:** Pickwell is the clearest single sentence yet for failure despite the rural context. Poundstock is another lenient example: two-hourly buses and short car trips accepted under plan policies with no TR3 reference, beside an unlit A39 with no pavement. It was dismissed on character, so it shows the lenient reading is not confined to allowals.

### 8. triggers.ts, `tr64` unacceptable pointers: PINS-6009517 ¶8, ¶28
- **Point in graph:** the safety limb stands alone; failing splays and collision data are cited.
- **Quote (PINS-6009517 ¶8):** "the fact that a proposal would improve an existing arrangement does not necessarily demonstrate that the resulting arrangement would be safe and suitable"
- **Quote (PINS-6009517 ¶28):** "I agree that the structure of national policy has changed. However, I do not agree that this materially alters the assessment of the appeal"
- **Why stronger:** It fills two small gaps. (a) Improving a substandard existing access (visibility from under 50 m to about 80 m, against 116 m needed at 43 mph and over 21,000 vehicles a day) is not enough. (b) It answers the argument that the 2024 "safe and suitable access" test has gone: TR3(1)(c) and TR6(4) together do the same work.

### 9. triggers.ts, `devPlan` method step naming the inconsistent part: PINS-6010911 ¶31
- **Point in graph:** name the national policy a plan restriction conflicts with, for example a boundary restriction against S5 (6001260 ¶28).
- **Quote (PINS-6010911 ¶31):** "These policies are materially inconsistent with Policy SP5 of the Framework with regards to its broader support for limited infilling outside of settlements. As a result, the conflict with these policies is given very limited weight"
- **Why stronger:** It is more specific than a general boundary restriction. Plan policies confining infill to named settlements are inconsistent with S5(1)(e), so they get very limited weight. The letter miscites S5 as SP5. It pairs with the countryside `s5e` node: at ¶29-30 a gap between two houses in a 15-house hamlet with no facilities passed S5(1)(e) though the hamlet is not a settlement (this corroborates 6009593).

### 10. benefits.ts, `otherBenefits` typical weights (affordable and self-build at significant weight): PINS-6008238 ¶139
- **Point in graph:** secured affordable housing and secured self-build are shown with typical significant weight, separate from HO7(1) substantial weight.
- **Quote (PINS-6008238 ¶139):** "I find that the appellant has correctly disaggregated these benefits rather than considering them as one single benefit"
- **Why it matters (contradicts, in part):** At inquiry, market, affordable, elderly and self-build housing each got substantial weight under HO7, linked to HO1 (¶139). Separately, a single custom-build home secured by unilateral undertaking got substantial weight at PINS-6010020 ¶23. The graph's default of significant weight may understate what inspectors give. Worth a note that some decisions give substantial weight to each type.

### 11. heritage.ts, public benefits option `reuse`: PINS-6006905 ¶22 (same letter as PINS-6006903)
- **Point in graph:** reuse of a vacant listed building is a named HE6(4) benefit, which must be genuine and secured.
- **Quote (PINS-6006905 ¶22):** "there is little evidence before me that this benefit is entirely dependent upon the provision of the windows that have resulted in the harm"
- **Why stronger:** It adds a discount the option does not mention: the named benefit carries little weight where an extant, less harmful consent already delivers the reuse. If batch 5 has proposed 6006903 for this, cite either ref, since it is one letter.
