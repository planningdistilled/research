# Batch 3: candidate examples for the NPPF Navigator

Cases in batch 3 (decided 23 to 30 September 2026) that are better illustrations than what the graph now cites, fill a point that has no example, or cut against a point the graph makes. Every quote below was machine-checked against the letter text. All are written-representations appeals.

## Strongest three

### 1. Washed-over village is not a settlement: fills an explicit gap
- **Node:** `core.ts`, `washedOverSettlement`. The contested summary says: "No appeal decision since August 2026 has ruled squarely on the Annex B exclusion."
- **Case:** PINS-6009919, Ewen House, Fobbing (Thurrock), 29 Sep 2026, dismissed. ¶49.
- **Quote:** "villages which lie within and are defined as part of the Green Belt, as is the case with Fobbing, are not settlements for the purposes of the Framework, and consequently Policy S4 is not engaged. As the proposal is inappropriate development in the Green Belt, Policy S5 does not apply to it either" (¶49).
- **Why stronger:** This is the squarely-on-point appeal ruling the node says does not exist. It applies the Annex B exclusion to a named washed-over village and then routes the case through GB6/GB7. The "Annex B: not a settlement" reading could cite it, and the sentence saying there is no such appeal decision needs updating.

### 2. TR3 route, bus hours and highway authority "no objection": fills two gaps
- **Node:** `tr3.ts`, `tr3` judgement: the help line on highway authority "no objection" has no case; the `fail` pointer on buses cites only 6009966 ¶22 (no evidence of hours); the `tr3Bus` help cites a twice-weekly bus (6006637 ¶15).
- **Case:** PINS-6007668, Upper Farm, Shut Lane Head (Newcastle-under-Lyme), 29 Sep 2026, dismissed (Green Belt, GB7(1)(g)(iii) failed).
- **Quotes:**
  - The lane: "being a no-through road, traffic would most likely be light and for local residents and visitors, and with farm traffic or occasional delivery vehicles. As such walking or cycling along the lane would not be unduly difficult or unsafe" (¶14); but "having walked some 800 metres and on reaching the A53 there is no footpath and nowhere to walk, cycle or wheel to" (¶14).
  - Bus, with a timetable in evidence (6 a day Monday to Saturday): "there is no service on a Sunday or bank holidays. Anyone working shifts or wanting to access other services, such as leisure, in the evenings, on Sundays or bank holidays would not be able to do so" (¶15). "the presence of a bus stop at the bottom of the lane does not, of itself, make the location suitable or sustainable" (¶19).
  - Highway authority: "I understand that the Highway Authority reached a similar conclusion but nevertheless raised no objection. While I have had regard to that opinion, in my view the appeal site is in an unsustainable location which would not support sustainable patterns of movement or enable good accessibility for different users" (¶19).
- **Why stronger:** The timetable was produced, so this is a failure on hours and days, not on missing evidence. It is a more demanding and more realistic bus example than a twice-weekly service. ¶19 is a direct appeal example for the help line that says a highway authority's "no objection" does not answer TR3(1)(a). ¶14 also shows the route test done section by section: a quiet unlit lane was acceptable, and the failure was where it met the main road.

### 3. S5(1)(j)(i) "physically well-related" read through accessibility: contradicts the graph's reading
- **Node:** `countryside.ts`, method `j`: "Keep accessibility separate" and "Passing this limb does not answer TR3", and closeCall "On the text it is a physical test".
- **Case:** PINS-6007158, Brishing Court Farm, Boughton Monchelsea (Maidstone), 28 Sep 2026, dismissed. ¶44.
- **Quote:** "Given the site’s countryside location and accessibility constraints in terms of walking, cycling and access to public transport, I find that the proposal would not be physically well-related to an existing settlement" (¶44).
- **Why it matters:** A 2026-Framework appeal that fails (j)(i) on walking, cycling and public transport, which is the accessibility reading the graph advises against. Add it to the competing readings in step 2 of method `j` (with 6006893 ¶69, 6009838 ¶15), rather than as support for the physical reading. The same letter is also a good example for the `tr3` method step on testing the route "after dark and in bad weather", which has no case: "narrow and unlit country lanes without formal footpaths, which would likely discourage future residents from walking or cycling, particularly outside daylight hours and in inclement weather" (¶23).

## Other candidates

### DP3(3): the level-balance reading of "clear justification" in a dismissal
- **Node:** `triggers.ts`, `dp3` contested readings. The level-balance reading cites only Didcot 6009340 ¶22, an allowed appeal.
- **Case:** PINS-6004873, 37 Eastern Esplanade, Southend-on-Sea, 25 Sep 2026, dismissed. ¶27-28.
- **Quote:** "Even if the appeal shopfront has enhanced the appeal property’s contribution to the economy, including the vitality and viability of the city centre, those benefits would be modest and they do not, in my judgement, amount to clear justification for the development’s harm to the character and appearance of the host building and area" (¶27).
- **Why stronger:** It shows the same benefits-based reading failing, and then runs the full route: DP3(1) conflict, clear-justification question, S4(2) trigger (¶28). That gives the level-balance reading an example on each side.

### F4(2)(c): flood risk assessment in Flood Zone 1
- **Node:** `triggers.ts`, `f7` method step 2 ("the listed cases in Flood Zone 1"), which has no Flood Zone 1 example.
- **Case:** PINS-6011882, Merienda, Elton (Huntingdonshire), 25 Sep 2026, dismissed. ¶6-7.
- **Quote:** "regardless of whether a FRA was required for validation purposes, it remains necessary to provide adequate evidence addressing these matters" (¶6); "Conflict also arises with the Framework F4 2c) which requires the submission of a site Specific flood risk assessment for sites in Flood Zone 1 where land has been identified in a SFRA or the Flood Map for Planning as being at risk of flooding from any source" (¶7).
- **Why stronger:** Fills the Flood Zone 1 gap. It also answers the common "not a validation requirement" argument.

### F5 sequential test: search area and Environment Agency "no objection"
- **Node:** `triggers.ts`, `f7` method step 3 (deal separately with the sequential test), which has no case.
- **Case:** PINS-6011235, 132 Cock Bank, Turves (Fenland), 23 Sep 2026, dismissed. ¶13-14.
- **Quote:** "I am not satisfied that the area of search should be limited to the appeal site" (¶13); "Although the Environment Agency raises no objection, responsibility for applying the sequential and exceptions tests rests ultimately rests with the decision-maker" (¶14).
- **Why stronger:** Fills a gap. It also shows that an S5(1)(d) pass does not survive a sequential-test failure (¶23, ¶30-31).

### F7: FRA without levels, and why a condition cannot fix it
- **Node:** `triggers.ts`, `f7` "not-demonstrated" pointers.
- **Case:** PINS-6009631, Stratton House Hotel, Cirencester (Cotswold), 30 Sep 2026, dismissed. ¶13, ¶19-20.
- **Quote:** "conditions would not be reasonable as any mitigation measures found necessary to reduce flood risk and incorporate sustainable drainage systems could lead to a substantial redesign of the scheme that is currently before me" (¶19).
- **Why useful:** A new pointer: the FRA gave no ground levels, finished floor levels or design flood level (¶13), and a condition was refused because raising floor levels could change the scheme and the setting of a listed building (¶20). It also ran F7 through S4(2)(c) (¶44).

### HE6(4): informal, unsecured public benefit
- **Node:** `heritage.ts`, HE6(4) help ("Reduce the weight of any benefit that is not secured"), with no case for the unsecured limb.
- **Case:** PINS-6001939, Adsdean House (South Downs NP), 30 Sep 2026, dismissed (listed building consent). ¶14.
- **Quote:** "this is done on an informal ad-hoc basis, and as such, there is no guarantee of this continuing in any formal manner. Therefore, even if there is a direct link between the proposed works and this public benefit, I can only give it modest weight" (¶14).
- **Why useful:** A clear, short example of the unsecured limb for public access and community use.

### HE6(4): a less harmful location, and benefits another scheme could supply
- **Node:** `heritage.ts`, HE6(4) fail pointers (6007466 ¶23 "not specific to this site").
- **Case:** PINS-6010811, 100 High Street, Bromley (BT Street Hub), 25 Sep 2026, dismissed. ¶17.
- **Quote:** "it has not been explained why the hub could not be located in a different, less harmful position, whilst still delivering the same benefits" (¶17).
- **Why useful:** Puts the "achievable with less harm" limb in so many words, and also discounts benefits that nearby hubs may duplicate (¶17).

### Annex A(2), part by part: a local requirement S5(1)(a) does not impose
- **Node:** `triggers.ts`, `devPlan` "Part by part" reading.
- **Case:** PINS-6009410, land rear of 53 Beresford Avenue, Chapel-en-le-Frith (High Peak), 30 Sep 2026, allowed. ¶22-23.
- **Quote:** "Policy S5 of the Framework supports development for agriculture outside settlements and does not require such development to support an existing agricultural enterprise" (¶22).
- **Why useful:** A non-housing example of the plan's extra restriction being outweighed. Caveat: Annex A(2) is not named, so it supports the result, not the method.

## Contradictions and tensions to note (weaker authority)

- **Flood, seasonal closure in a "Danger to All" zone.** PINS-6010342, The Firs Country Park, Boston, 25 Sep 2026, allowed: in Flood Zone 3 with breach depths of 0.5 to 1.6 m, "The Environment Agency consider that this is an acceptable measure to manage the risk of flooding to the holiday let" (¶23), on the basis of no winter occupation, resilience measures and an evacuation-plan condition. F5 and F7 are not engaged in terms. This sits uneasily with the `f7` pointer "Reliance on flood warnings and good management alone (6010729 ¶18–19)". Do not cite it as F7 authority; note it as a council-style outcome at appeal.
- **Short car trips reduce TR3 harm.** PINS-6010498, Higher Bal (Cornwall), 28 Sep 2026, dismissed on World Heritage Site harm: occupiers "would be largely reliant on private vehicles", but "any additional journeys would be small in number, while their length would often be very short. As such, the amount of ‘on the ground’ harm associated with this issue would be very limited" (¶12). This fits the graph's `tr3Engaged` "no" path (limited weight for a single dwelling), but it is framed as short trips, the shortcut the `tr3` closeCall warns about. It could be added to the lenient readings in the closeCall alongside 6009632 and 6010973.
- **GB7(1)(f)(i) engineering operations.** PINS-6007340, Sky Farm, Knockholt (Bromley), 24 Sep 2026, allowed: "it is not necessary to establish whether there is an identified need or operational requirement for the hard surfacing" (¶18). The letter does not discuss the closing words of GB7(1)(f)(i) on being required in a Green Belt location. The graph has no (f) node, so there is nothing to contradict yet; flag it if one is added.
