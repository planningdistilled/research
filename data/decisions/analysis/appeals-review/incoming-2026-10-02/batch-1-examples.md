# Batch 1: candidate examples for the NPPF Navigator

Appeal decisions dated 2 October 2026. Each entry names the navigator node file and the point, the case and paragraph, a verbatim quote (machine-checked against the letter text), and why it is stronger than what the node cites now, or how it contradicts the node. All are Planning Inspectorate (PINS) appeal decisions. "Hearing" marks the higher-authority ones; the rest are written representations.

## Contradicts a point the graph makes

### 1. One home allowed against "limited" harm to a listed building (heritage.ts, `heBenefits` "housing-small" option and `he64` help)
The node says: "No appeal since August 2026 in the dataset allows 1 to 9 homes against "low" (as opposed to "very low") harm to a designated asset." The `heBenefits` option says "no appeal in the dataset has allowed 1–9 homes against more than very low heritage harm."

- Case: PINS-6007704 ¶36 (Cross Gaits Inn, Blacko, hearing, allowed; listed building consent PINS-6007757 allowed in the same letter)
  Quote: "The degree of harm would be limited because the building, its principal historic form and most surviving features would be retained" (¶36)
  Quote: "while the benefits may appear individually modest, I find that collectively they are sufficient to outweigh the harm to the significance of the listed building" (¶40)

Why it matters: a single dwelling was allowed after a hearing against limited harm to the Grade II listed building itself (not only its setting), with that harm given considerable importance and weight (¶36). The balance was carried by the reuse of a vacant listed building, not by the one home, so the node's statement needs a qualification: it holds where housing is the main benefit, not where the scheme secures the building's long-term reuse.

### 2. A route with a reduced-mobility problem still passed GB7(1)(g)(iii) (tr3.ts, `tr3Footway` help and `tr3` closeCall)
The node says: "A route to services that a wheelchair or pushchair cannot use is not a genuine choice for them under TR3(1)(a)", and the closeCall ends "If the route is unsafe or unattractive for some users, the choice is not genuine for them."

- Case: PINS-6010578 ¶24 (Monks Heath, Cheshire East, permission in principle for 5 homes, Green Belt, allowed)
  Quote: "I am not convinced that the condition of the local footpaths along renders the location unsustainable" (¶24)
  Quote: "the evidence indicates that there remains a genuine and realistic choice of transport modes" (¶25)
- Case: PINS-6008679 ¶11 (Fobbing, Thurrock, 5 homes, Green Belt, limb (iii) passed; dismissed on design)
  Quote: "which would be walkable for some but not all" (¶11)
  Quote: "In the context of the site’s semi-rural location, the proposal would be in a sustainable location" (¶12)

Why it matters: both letters pass limb (iii) while accepting that the route does not work for everyone, and both rely on a "semi-rural" context. At Monks Heath the concern was raised expressly for wheelchair users and others with reduced mobility, on an uneven and mostly unlit footpath (¶14, ¶24). These belong in the closeCall's list of inspectors who "have accepted less in rural areas", and they are Green Belt limb (iii) passes, where the node says no scale threshold applies.

### 3. An explicit design-code limit breached, and allowed with no DP3(3) step (triggers.ts, `dp3Conflicts` "standards" option and `dp3` help)
The node says: ""Explicit design standards" includes a Village Design Statement, design guide, code or masterplan adopted through a development-plan policy", and that the error to avoid is allowing a scheme with a design conflict without asking whether there is clear justification.

- Case: PINS-6010392 ¶6 (Stanton on the Wolds, Rushcliffe, replacement dwelling in the Green Belt, allowed)
  Quote: "The SPD sets out the expectations for new development, but there will be occasions where the application of its policies may not be necessary or beneficial to a given development proposal" (¶6)
  Quote: "the size and height of the proposed dwelling would fail to meet the stipulations of Policy C6.6 of the SPD. However, in this instance, this would not result in harm to the character and appearance of the area" (¶9)

Why it matters: the Rushcliffe Design Code Supplementary Planning Document (SPD) sets a numerical limit (a replacement dwelling must not exceed the original's volume by more than 50%, ¶5). The inspector treated it as an expectation, asked whether the breach caused harm, found none, and allowed the appeal. DP3(3) is not mentioned. The letter does not say whether the code is adopted through a development-plan policy, so it may fall outside the limb as the node defines it. Either way it shows the harm-first reading an applicant will cite, and the node should say how the explicit-standards limb applies where a standard is breached without harm.

## Stronger or gap-filling illustrations

### 4. The first winning example of the HE6(4) reuse benefit, and "optimum viable use" removed (heritage.ts, `heBenefits` "reuse" option; `he64` "outweighed" pointers)
The "reuse" option cites only cases where the benefit was discounted (6006903 ¶22; 6006266 ¶23). The node does not mention that the 2024 "optimum viable use" wording has gone.

- Case: PINS-6007704 ¶38 (Cross Gaits Inn, Blacko, hearing, allowed)
  Quote: "Notably, reference to optimum viable use has been removed" (¶38)
  Quote: "Its use as a dwelling would enable its sensitive repair and renovation and it would secure the long-term conservation of the heritage asset. This would be an important public benefit" (¶39)
  Quote: "on the basis that the existing use of the building as a public house is not viable and it cannot reasonably be made so, the loss of the historic and communal value cannot reasonably be avoided" (¶40)

Why stronger: a hearing decision in which the named HE6(4) benefit wins. It also shows the route: prove that the existing use has no reasonable prospect (here with an unchallenged marketing and viability report, ¶15–17, ¶31), and the harm from losing that use becomes unavoidable. The hearing had explored optimum viable use under 2024 paragraph 215; the inspector records that the 2026 test no longer includes it (¶37–38).

### 5. A replacement dwelling that fails GB7(1)(b) can pass GB7(1)(e) (greenbelt.ts, `gb7e` pointers; `gb7b` fail effect)
The `gb7e` node cites 6010198 ¶14 (size "is not the test") and 6007335 ¶8 (moderate harm). The `gb7b` fail effect reads "Does not meet GB7(1)(b), so it is inappropriate development."

- Case: PINS-6010746 ¶8 (Canons, Matching, Epping Forest, dismissed on heritage and character)
  Quote: "Taken together, the new building would be materially larger than the one it would replace" (¶8)
  Quote: "the site would be previously developed land and the proposal for a replacement detached dwelling would comprise a redevelopment" (¶10)
  Quote: "Given that the proposal would not cause substantial harm to the openness of the Green Belt, the development would accord with criterion e of Policy GB7 of the Framework" (¶15)

Why stronger: one letter runs both categories on the same scheme with figures. Footprint up 38.8% and floorspace up 141.9% (¶7) fails (b), yet the openness harm is "moderate" and so below the (e) threshold (¶15). The site is a house plot outside a built-up area, so the garden exclusion does not apply (¶10). The `gb7b` fail effect should say the scheme is inappropriate unless another category applies. PINS-6010392 ¶3 is a second replacement dwelling taken under (e), there by agreement.

### 6. GB7(1)(f) has no node: two readings of limbs (iii) and (iv) (greenbelt.ts, `gb7cat`)
`gb7cat` offers (b), (c), (d), (e), (g) and (h). GB7(1)(f) appears only in a `gb7e` step as the home of the "significant conflict" purposes test.

- Case: PINS-6012115 ¶9 (St Leonards and St Ives, Dorset, static caravan let as a home, dismissed)
  Quote: "It is not an open-ended category but rather, because of the words “such as”, the uses that will be within it should take their flavour or extent from the examples given" (¶9)
- Case: PINS-6011489 ¶6 (Hemel Hempstead, Dacorum, outdoor wellbeing use with an office unit, dismissed)
  Quote: "I cannot see any reason why the provision of the proposed building is necessary to support those uses" (¶6)
  Quote: "I consider the proposed building would not represent an appropriate facility for the use of the land for outdoor recreation" (¶8)

Why it matters: residential schemes do argue (f)(iii) (a change of use of land), as at St Leonards. The first letter reads (f)(iii) by its examples, so a caravan as a dwelling is outside it. The second reads (f)(iv) "appropriate facilities" as a need test: the outdoor use passed (f)(iii), but the building failed because nothing showed it was necessary for that use. A short help note under `gb7cat` would stop users choosing (g) by default for these cases.

### 7. DP3(3) beats substantial housing weight after a full GB7(1)(g) pass (balance.ts, `balanceTriggered`; triggers.ts, `dp3` help)
The nodes cite 6008167 ¶21 for DP3(3) beating substantial housing weight.

- Case: PINS-6008679 ¶45 (Fobbing, Thurrock, 5 homes, grey belt, 1.02 years' supply, dismissed)
  Quote: "Framework Policy DP3(3) states that development proposals should be refused if, without clear justification, they conflict with DP3(1)" (¶45)
  Quote: "I conclude that the benefits of the appeal proposal would be substantially outweighed by this harm" (¶46)

Why stronger: the letter passes every limb of GB7(1)(g) (¶6–15), gives the five homes substantial weight at a 1.02-year supply (¶9, ¶36), and still dismisses through S5(5), S5(2) and DP3(3) on a layout that breaks a linear settlement pattern, although the houses themselves were "well designed" (¶22). It sets out the S5(5) to S5(2) to DP3(3) chain in three consecutive paragraphs (¶44–46).

### 8. "Clear justification" answered by asking whether the benefits need the harm (triggers.ts, `dp3` contested readings, "Necessity")
The necessity reading cites 6008314 ¶43 (trees) and 6007133 ¶59 (countryside character).

- Case: PINS-6007927 ¶16 (47 Burma Road, Hackney, rear extension beside a locally listed building, dismissed)
  Quote: "they would represent a very limited increase on such benefits which could be achieved without causing harm either to the setting of 42 Clissold Crescent, or to the character and appearance of the area more generally" (¶16)

Why stronger: an urban, householder-scale example under S4, where the node's examples are both rural. DP3(3) is stated with its "without clear justification" words (¶15) and answered by the avoidability of the harm (¶16).

### 9. S4(2)(a)(ii) applied: total loss of a small open space is a "substantial adverse impact" (balance.ts, `balanceTriggered`; no node covers S4(2)(a))
No node mentions S4(2)(a)(ii) or HC7.

- Case: PINS-6009769 ¶37 (Callis Way, Gillingham, Medway, one bungalow on amenity grassland, 3.1 years' supply, dismissed)
  Quote: "As the whole of the existing open space would be lost the adverse impact would, in that respect, be substantial" (¶37)
  Quote: "None of this suggests, much less clearly shows, that the site is surplus to requirements as an open space" (¶11)

Why it matters: this is an express use of the S4(2)(a)(ii) circumstance, with HC7 as the policy applied. "Substantial" is measured against the space lost (all of it), not the size of the site. HC7(1)(a) puts the burden on the applicant to show the land is surplus; other open spaces nearby do not do that (¶10).

### 10. A Connectivity Tool score of 33 passes on local comparators (tr3.ts, `tr3Tool` help)
The node cites 52 failing (6011736) and passing (6010471), and 6006497 ¶42 for reading the score comparatively.

- Case: PINS-6010578 ¶18 (Monks Heath, Cheshire East, allowed)
  Quote: "This represents an above average result for a site in this type of location" (¶18)

Why stronger: 33 sits at the bottom of the node's "middle" band, and it passed because nearby approved schemes "reportedly ranged between 29 and 34" (¶18). It is a clear example of the comparative reading being used to pass a low absolute score, with the comparators named.

### 11. The new Framework reversed an earlier dismissal on the same site (countryside.ts, `s5c`; tr3.ts, `tr3Engaged`)
`tr3Engaged` cites 6010973 ¶20 for one home not being a significant amount of movement.

- Case: PINS-6009255 ¶17 (The Orchard, Charley, North West Leicestershire, garage conversion to a self-build home, appeal planning officer recommendation, allowed)
  Quote: "I am mindful of the previous appeal on the site for a similar proposal. However, the different factor now is the provisions in the new Framework" (¶17)
  Quote: "In the circumstances, the conflict with the principles under Policy TR3 would be limited" (¶19)

Why it matters: a direct before-and-after. The location was found poor again (no public transport, unlit roads with no footways, ¶10), but S5(1)(c) now makes reuse of an existing building a form of development to be approved unless the benefits are substantially outweighed. The TR3 conflict is held limited for one dwelling by reasoning from low traffic and the lack of realistic rural improvements (¶19); the letter does not apply the genuine-choice wording, so it supports the node's "limited weight" effect but not its method. It is an appeal planning officer letter, so it carries less weight.

### 12. Plan policies set aside because they pre-date the Framework (triggers.ts, `devPlan` step 4)
The node says: "Do not reduce the weight of a consistent policy because of its age."

- Case: PINS-6008679 ¶5 (Fobbing, Thurrock, dismissed)
  Quote: "these policies pre-date the current version of the Framework; I have necessarily assessed the appeal scheme against the criteria set out in the Framework" (¶5)
- Case: PINS-6011365 ¶29 (Fowlmere, South Cambridgeshire, permission in principle, dismissed), the contrasting approach
  Quote: "in the context of this appeal, SCLP Policies S/2, S/7, HQ/1 and NH/2 are not materially inconsistent with the Framework and so should continue to be afforded significant weight" (¶29)

Why it matters: the Fobbing letter is a plain example of the age-based shortcut the node warns against: Green Belt and spatial policies are put aside for their date, with no finding of which part is materially inconsistent. The Fowlmere letter does the Annex A paragraph 2 exercise, and keeps countryside and location policies where the scheme meets no S5(1) category.

### 13. A missing self-build obligation cured by a negatively worded condition (benefits.ts, `otherBenefits` "selfBuild" option)
The option reads "Self-build or custom housing, secured by obligation".

- Case: PINS-6010392 ¶21 (Stanton on the Wolds, Rushcliffe, allowed)
  Quote: "This is to my mind an exceptional circumstance, because the securing of self-build means that the proposed development is exempted from statutory Biodiversity Net Gain (BNG)" (¶21)
  Quote: "The imposition of a negatively-worded condition requiring the submission of a planning obligation (14) secures this benefit, in the exceptional circumstances that I have set out" (¶22)

Why it matters: other appeals treat an unsecured self-build exemption as fatal. Here the inspector used a condition requiring a planning obligation before commencement, reasoning that the self-build exemption was removed for applications made after 6 August 2026 and a refusal would leave no route to secure self-build (¶21). The inspector also doubted that an ordinary self-build condition is enforceable (¶19). The node could note this as an exceptional route, tied to applications made before 6 August 2026.
