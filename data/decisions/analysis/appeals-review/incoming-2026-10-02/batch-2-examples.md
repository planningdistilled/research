# Batch 2: candidate examples for the NPPF Navigator

Appeal decisions dated 30 September to 2 October 2026. Each entry names the navigator node file and the point, the case and paragraph, a verbatim quote (machine-checked against the letter text), and why it is stronger than what the node cites now, or how it contradicts the node. All are Planning Inspectorate (PINS) appeal decisions on written representations unless marked "hearing". In quotes, "…" marks a page break in the letter text.

## Contradicts a point the graph makes

### 1. The TR3(1)(a) "significant movement" threshold applied inside GB7(1)(g)(iii) (tr3.ts, `tr3Engaged`)
The node says: "This step is not asked in the Green Belt: GB7(1)(g)(iii) requires a sustainable-location finding whatever the scale of the scheme. One home failed there on the route alone". `patterns.md` proposition 11 records this as 6 to 0 in the Green Belt.

- Case: PINS-6005162 ¶23 (Burnham, Buckinghamshire, one self-build home, allowed)
  Quote: "Having examined the proposal, as a single dwelling, I do not consider that it would result in a significant amount of movement, given its location at the edge of the village of Burnham. I find it would therefore accord with principle (a)."
  Quote (¶29): "Having found the appeal site and proposal to accord with principles (a) to (e) of Policy TR3(1), and to have acceptable levels of connectivity when assessed against TR3(2), I therefore find that the appeal site and proposal would meet the criterion contained within Policy GB7(1)(g) of the Framework."
- Case: PINS-6009720 ¶20 (Hitchin, North Hertfordshire, permission in principle for one home, allowed), where a missing frontage footway was answered by scale
  Quote: "Even if it was considered as part of this first stage, due to the scale of the development, it would not result in a significant adverse impact on the transport network. Consequently, the proposal accords with Policy TR3 of the Framework and meets criterion iii."

Why it matters: both letters pass the Green Belt location limb for one home by reference to its small scale, with no assessment of the walking route. Burnham does it by applying the TR3(1)(a) threshold limb by limb and treating "not significant" as compliance. The node states the opposite as settled. It should record this as a competing reading, and proposition 11's count is no longer unanimous. Neither location was shown to be a failing one (Burnham is about 1 km from a high street with a station; Hitchin is at a town edge), so these are not cases of small scale rescuing a bad route.

### 2. Residential garden land treated as excluded from previously developed land outright (greenbelt.ts, `gb7e`; countryside.ts, the S5(1)(d) steps)
The `gb7e` node says: "A garden outside a built-up area can be PDL (6012162 ¶8–9); one in a built-up area is not (6011803 ¶19)." countryside.ts says: "The garden exclusion applies only in built-up areas."

- Case: PINS-6005162 ¶13 (Burnham, allowed on the grey belt route instead)
  Quote: "As such, it would not meet the definition of PDL in Annex B of the Framework, which specifically excludes residential garden land from the definition of PDL."

Why it matters: the inspector rejects GB7(1)(e) on the ground that garden land is excluded, without asking whether the land is in a built-up area. The same letter describes the site as outside the settlement boundary, "within a land use designation as open countryside" (¶7). The finding did not affect the outcome, but an objector could cite it against the node's reading. The node should note the letter and that it does not engage with the built-up areas qualification in Annex B.

## Stronger or gap-filling illustrations

### 3. HO11 applies to an isolated home in the Green Belt, through S5(2) and S5(5) (countryside.ts, `isolated` and `ho11`; balance.ts, the S5(5) balance)
The `isolated` and `ho11` nodes are shown only outside the Green Belt, on the S5(3) route. The graph has no step for an isolated home that passes GB7.

- Case: PINS-6010112 ¶19 (Balne, North Yorkshire, manager's dwelling at a fishery, dismissed)
  Quote: "As a national decision-making policy, and since the site is within a countryside location, Framework policy HO11 remains applicable to the proposal and S5(2) and … S5(5) engages it in this case even though S5(3) does not."
  Quote (¶34): "That the proposal would run counter to this weighs very heavily against permitting the development - so much so that it substantially outweighs the development’s benefits when assessed against the Framework’s national decision-making policies."

Why it matters: the scheme was not inappropriate under GB7(1)(e), so S5(5) applied and S5(3) did not. The inspector still tested isolation and HO11(1)(a) and (c), and the HO11 conflict decided the S5(5) balance against one home given substantial weight at 2.6 years' supply. This is the correct counterpart to Banks 6011231, which ran S5(3) itself on a Green Belt site. The Green Belt route should ask the isolated-home question after a GB7 pass.

### 4. Benefits that win the HE6(4) balance can still fail DP3(3) "clear justification" (triggers.ts, `dp3`; heritage.ts, `he64`)
The `dp3` node says the question "is stricter than the S4/S5" test. The `he64` closeCall says all 44 appeals that failed HE6(4) were dismissed; it has no case of a pass followed by a dismissal.

- Case: PINS-6011062 ¶44 (Remenham, Wokingham, 13 net extra-care units, dismissed)
  Quote (¶37): "Having attached considerable importance and weight to the identified heritage harm, I nevertheless find that these substantial public benefits outweigh that harm."
  Quote: "While the benefits I outline above are significant, they do not in my view, provide clear justification to allow development that would harm the living conditions of the existing residents in the manner I have described."
  Quote (¶45): "As I have found that the proposal conflicts with Policy DP3, I consider this to be such a case."

Why stronger: one set of benefits is tested twice in the same letter. It outweighs limited harm to two conservation areas under HE6(4), then fails to give clear justification for a DP3(1) conflict, which engages S5(2). It shows that DP3(3) is a separate and stricter question, and that a heritage pass does not carry the design trigger with it.

### 5. DP3(1) used for harm to neighbours' outlook and privacy; P3 held not to cover it (triggers.ts, `dp3Conflicts`)
The node lists design conflicts; it does not say where neighbour amenity sits in the 2026 Framework.

- Case: PINS-6011062 ¶17 (Remenham)
  Quote: "Furthermore, it would conflict with Policy DP3 of the Framework, which requires development to respond positively to its context so that it integrates with and enhances its surroundings, which the proposal would fail to achieve."
  Quote (¶18): "However, that policy is principally concerned with living conditions in relation to matters such as daylight and sunlight, together with air, noise, light, water and other forms of pollution."
- Case: PINS-6009135 ¶32 (Wednesfield, Wolverhampton, two flats, dismissed), which uses L2(1)(d)(ii) instead
  Quote: "The scheme would also create poor living standards for existing and future residents, in terms of provision of external space and effect on privacy, as sought by the Framework’s policy L2(1)(d)(ii), resulting in the creation of unacceptable living standards."

Why it matters: inspectors are placing outlook and privacy harm under different Framework policies. Remenham treats it as a DP3(1) context conflict, which brings in the DP3(3) refusal trigger. Wednesfield and Ealing 6005995 (¶16) use L2(1)(d)(ii), which is a condition of L2 support and not a "should be refused" policy. The node could say that an overbearing or overlooking relationship has been treated as a DP3(1) conflict, and that P3 has been read as limited to daylight, sunlight and pollution.

### 6. A site adjoining a town can still be grey belt (greenbelt.ts, `greyBelt`, purpose (a) pointers and closeCall)
The purpose (a) pointers cite a town-edge fail (6009281 ¶15–16) and village cases. There is no town-edge pass.

- Case: PINS-6009720 ¶12 (Hitchin, permission in principle, allowed)
  Quote: "The site is free from existing development and is adjacent to the town of Hitchin which represents a large built-up area."
  Quote: "These features would also serve to restrict and contain the proposed development to an extent. Accordingly, the site makes a moderate contribution to purpose (a) of the Green Belt as set out in the Framework."
  Quote (¶13): "The site itself however forms a very small part of the gap between the two towns."

Why stronger: this is the Annex E "moderate" category applied at the edge of a genuine large built-up area: housing, an elevated railway and vegetation contain the site. The council's Green Belt review rated the wider parcel strong for purposes (a) and (b), and the inspector judged the site instead (¶10, ¶13). It supports the closeCall with a town-edge example.

### 7. "Fundamentally undermine" tested with plan-area figures and all five purposes (greenbelt.ts, `greyBeltUndermine`)
The steps cite a solar farm (6005916 ¶18) for comparing the site with the remaining Green Belt.

- Case: PINS-6005162 ¶20 (Burnham)
  Quote: "However, the assessment for Policy GB7(1)(g)(i) is not the effect of development on the specific site, but rather it is whether development of the appeal site would fundamentally undermine the purposes (taken together) of the remaining GB across the area of the plan."
  Quote: "The appeal site would, at 0.16 hectares, represent a very small percentage of the remaining GB land."

Why stronger: a housing example that states the test in the node's own terms, gives the plan-area figures (14,100 ha, 87.4% Green Belt) and deals with purposes (c) and (e) at this stage and not at the grey belt stage (¶18–19).

### 8. A replacement dwelling can use GB7(1)(e) as well as (b); the local size limit loses weight (greenbelt.ts, `gb7e` and `gb7b`)
The `gb7e` pointers cite 6010198 ¶14 for size "is not the test".

- Case: PINS-6008122 ¶15 (Canewdon, Rochford, dismissed on biodiversity net gain only)
  Quote: "Whilst the proposal would replace an existing dwelling, it would also redevelop PDL."
  Quote (¶17): "Policy DM21 of the DMP does not contemplate that exception. It is therefore materially inconsistent with Framework policy GB7 as it applies to this proposal."

Why stronger: the letter says in terms that a proposal within one GB7 category can also be tested against another, where the council had argued the scheme failed (b). It then applies Annex A paragraph 2 cleanly to the local replacement-dwelling policy, which Heronsgate 6010198 did not.

### 9. Not-inappropriate development refused in the S5(5) balance on an unsecured self-build exemption (balance.ts, `balancePlain`; patterns.md proposition 7)
- Case: PINS-6008122 ¶9 (Canewdon)
  Quote (¶8): "If for any reason that person ceased to occupy the dwelling before the end of the specified period, the self-build exemption would no longer apply."
  Quote: "In the absence of such a justification the suggested condition would be in conflict with the PPG and would therefore fail the tests of reasonableness and enforceability set out in Framework policy DM6."
  Quote (¶23): "The benefits of the proposal would be substantially outweighed by its adverse effects when assessed against the Framework's national decision-making policies."

Why it matters: the council and appellant agreed that a condition could secure self-build status and so the biodiversity net gain exemption. The inspector held that a personal occupancy condition fails DM6, so the exemption fell away and, with no baseline evidence, the N2(1)(a) conflict decided the balance with no "should be refused" trigger. It adds a new mechanism to proposition 7 (self-build cannot be secured by a personal condition) and is a counter-example to the general rule that non-trigger harms rarely decide the balance.

### 10. The sequential test bites on an access in the flood zone, and its absence is weighed, not automatically fatal (triggers.ts, `f7`)
The node cites 6004952 ¶8–9 for access and escape routes, and says inspectors treat failure of F5 or F7 "as decisive".

- Case: PINS-6010951 ¶13 (Clavering, Uttlesford, one home, dismissed)
  Quote: "Part 2(b)(i) is not met as built development within the site boundary, including access or escape routes, would be located in an area that would be at risk of flooding from any source, now and in the future (having regard to potential changes in flood risk)."
  Quote (¶14): "the Court held that the Inspector erred in law by treating the absence of a sequential test as fatal to the application without carrying out any proper or meaningful balance"
  Quote (¶28): "As no sequential test has been carried out, I have found there would be conflict with policies in the development plan and the Framework which seek to locate new homes in areas of low risk of flooding and substantial weight is attached to this adverse effect."

Why stronger: the dwelling was in Flood Zone 1 and only the access was at risk, and the inspector raised the point herself. Citing Gladman v Secretary of State and Lancaster City Council [2026] EWHC 51 (Admin), she weighed the missing sequential test in the S4 balance (substantial weight) and did not use F5 as an S4(2)(c) trigger. This bears on the open question in `patterns.md` about F5 being used as a "should be refused" policy.

### 11. TR3 read with E4(2) for a rural business reusing farm buildings (tr3.ts, `tr3` closeCall)
The node's examples are all housing.

- Case: PINS-6004899 ¶18 (Underriver, Sevenoaks, farm buildings to storage and light industry in the Green Belt and Kent Downs National Landscape, allowed)
  Quote (¶17): "Users of the appeal site are therefore very likely to be reliant on private transport rather than using more sustainable means."
  Quote: "It goes on to acknowledge that development proposals that meet business needs in rural areas may need to be in locations not served well by public transport."
  Quote (¶19): "As above, the vehicle movements arising from the proposal would be comparable to those which could arise from the existing use and the proposal is not one likely to generate a significant amount of movement."

Why it matters: the inspector found the route facts against the site (no footways or lighting, fast roads) and still found no TR3 conflict, by reading TR3 with E4(2) and judging "significant movement" against what the lawful agricultural use could generate. This is a principled answer for non-residential rural reuse and should not be cited for housing.

### 12. Conversion of existing buildings is not "major" under N4(2); L2 weight is not available for farm buildings (triggers.ts, `n4major`)
The "no" pointer "Replaces or reuses existing built form" has no case.

- Case: PINS-6004899 ¶15 (Underriver)
  Quote: "Given the development is largely derived from the … conversion of the existing buildings and reconfiguration of external spaces, I agree that the development should not be considered ‘major’ for the purposes of policy N4(2) of the Framework."
  Quote (¶32): "Since the site is not previously developed land by definition, and since the acceptability of the proposal relies on the site otherwise being used effectively for agricultural purposes, I have not applied the substantial weight set out in policy L2"

### 13. HE6(4): benefits that do not depend on the harmful element; CO1 weight cut where alternatives were not explored (heritage.ts, `heBenefits` and `he64`)
- Case: PINS-6009979 ¶11 (Barnoldswick, Pendle, uPVC shopfront condition, dismissed)
  Quote: "However, these improvements would not be dependent on the use of uPVC for the ground-floor shopfront and could equally be achieved through the use of a more appropriate alternative material."
  Quote (¶13): "the public benefits of the use of uPVC for the shopfront would not outweigh the considerable importance and weight that should be attached to the identified harm to the CA and the substantial weight that should be given to its conservation, irrespective of the level of harm"
- Case: PINS-6002400 ¶26 (Nottingham, communications kiosk in the setting of a Grade II* station, dismissed)
  Quote: "In the absence of any compelling evidence to demonstrate any consideration of alternative sitings, I cannot be certain that the siting of the proposal is the least harmful one in heritage terms, which significantly limits the overall weight I attach to such benefits."

Why stronger: Barnoldswick ¶13 states HE6(1) and HE6(3) together and correctly in one sentence, and ¶11 is a clear statement of the "achievable with less harm" filter. Nottingham applies the same filter to CO1's substantial weight for telecommunications.

### 14. HO7 weight for one home and for unevidenced need (benefits.ts, `homes`)
The help text says the weight for very small numbers "is not settled".

- Case: PINS-6009720 ¶30 (Hitchin, one home, allowed): substantial
  Quote: "Substantial weight is given to the provision of a new dwelling in accordance with Policy HO7 of the Framework, with the proposal providing a modest contribution to the Council’s housing supply."
- Case: PINS-6008122 ¶22 (Canewdon, one-for-one replacement): limited
  Quote: "However, in the absence of detailed evidence of such need, the replacement of a single dwelling would reasonably have a limited effect in this regard."
- Case: PINS-6010376 ¶68 (Hull, 37 student bedrooms at 3.16 years' supply): modest
  Quote: "Notwithstanding the Council’s unmet housing position, as there is no demonstrated need for the PBSA in this location, I attach only modest weight to its provision, and not the substantial weight as advocated within Policy HO7"
- Case: PINS-6002677 ¶45 (Wisbech, Fenland, 352 homes, hearing, allowed): substantial with a five-year supply
  Quote: "Accordingly, I attach substantial weight to the benefit of providing affordable homes as directed by Framework Policy HO7."

Why it matters: these show what moves the weight. A net new home gets substantial weight on its own at Hitchin (and at Clavering 6010951 ¶30 and Balne 6010112 ¶31). A replacement, or accommodation of a type with no evidenced need, does not. At Wisbech the evidenced need was affordable housing, which sustained substantial weight even though the council had a five-year supply.

## A reading to treat with care (no node)

### 15. Schools said to fall outside HC6
- Case: PINS-6010376 ¶28 (Hull)
  Quote: "There is no mention of schools within Policy HC6 or within the definition of community facilities, consequently the existing use at the appeal site would not be regarded as a community use for the purpose of the Framework."

The letter uses this to give a local community-facilities policy very limited weight under Annex A paragraph 2. HC6 protects key community facilities and public service infrastructure, and Annex B defines public service infrastructure as including education and healthcare facilities of all types. The letter does not address that definition. The Navigator has no HC6 node, so nothing needs changing, but the letter should not be used as an example of HC6 or of Annex A paragraph 2.
