# Site context for the two code-A allowed appeals: Henfield 6007104 and Aston Clinton 6008253

Research note, 1 Oct 2026, by a background research agent at the editor's request; question: does the site itself explain why a bare tier-plus-proximity sentence was enough for the inspector? Distances marked "own measurement" are OpenStreetMap / OSRM routing, not from a document. Anything marked unverified was not obtainable.

## Headline

- **Henfield:** the "accessible location" sentence (¶10) is copied verbatim from the adjoining 191-home decision APP/Z3825/W/25/3376143 ¶11, which the appellant attached to its final comments. The application file (read later on 1 Oct 2026) has no Transport Statement, the highway authority made no site visit and gave advice only on a private road, and neither the officer's report nor the Council's appeal statement mentions accessibility, footways, lighting, buses or walking. The site is about 450–520 m from a lit A281 High Street with footways both sides and a half-hourly bus, which probably carries the finding, but the inspector did not test it, the last stretch of Furners Lane is a private road carrying a public footpath, the adopted lane lacks a continuous footway on the evidence of the neighbouring site's committee report, and the primary school is 1.2–1.4 km away.
- **Aston Clinton:** the highway authority itself said the bus stops (c. 840 m) are "outside of the maximum recommended walking distance" and took a £1,000-per-dwelling contribution "to overcome the issue of the excessive walking distance". The location was common ground; the accessibility is manufactured by mitigation (new footway, bus money, demand-responsive transport stop) rather than inherent in the site.

## Case 1: PINS 6007104, Land adjacent to Cedar Cottage, Furners Lane, Henfield (Horsham DC/24/0535), 5 dwellings, allowed 24 Aug 2026

**Application file read, 1 Oct 2026 (later the same day).** Horsham's planning portal refused every direct fetch, but the council's document host (`iawpa.horsham.gov.uk`, file system DH) served the full list of 78 documents for DC/24/0535. The delegated report, the WSCC Highways response, the Council's and the appellant's appeal statements, the appellant's final comments, the Planning Statement, the Design and Access Statement, the parish response, the decision notice and the PINS questionnaire are saved under `sources/council/site-context/henfield/` with text extracts and the document list. **There is no Transport Statement or Transport Assessment on the file.**

What the file shows about the route:

"The site is located on Furners Lane, a privately maintained road, subsequently the comments within this report are for your advice only." Other sources read: decision letter (`pins-corpus/6007104.txt`); adjoining 191-home decision APP/Z3825/W/25/3376143 (https://acp.planninginspectorate.gov.uk/ViewDocument.aspx?fileid=65612776); Horsham committee report for the 29-home site south of Furners Lane, DC/24/1538, 18 Mar 2025 (https://horsham.moderngov.co.uk/documents/s32496/07%20DC-24-1538%20Appendix.pdf); Henfield Parish Council Plans Advisory Committee minutes 4 Jul 2024 (https://www.henfield.gov.uk/file/1059); bustimes.org; OpenStreetMap via Overpass and the OSRM demo router.

**Walking distance and route**
- Site position: decision ¶5–7, "two fields of undeveloped agricultural land … to the north of Furners Lane and east of The Old Orchard (Cedar Cottage)", with the 191-home site "directly north". OSM places The Old Orchard at 50.9321, -0.2673. Furners Lane leaves the A281 High Street at its north-east end.

That matches the neighbour's statement to the Parish Council that "Access is via a private road" (minutes 4 Jul 2024) and decision ¶16, "Matters relating to the maintenance of a private road are civil matters." The last c. 100 m from the site to the adopted lane is a private road carrying a public footpath (own reading of OSM).
- Distances, own OSRM measurement from a point just east of The Old Orchard via Furners Lane to the A281: Furners Lane bus stops c. 450 m; One Stop convenience store c. 520 m; Post Office c. 500 m; Lloyds Pharmacy c. 600 m; Sainsbury's Local c. 640 m; Henfield Medical Centre (GP) c. 1.0 km; St Peter's CE Primary School c. 1.36 km. From the BN5 9HS postcode centroid 100 m further west: 320 / 390 / 870 / 1,230 m.
- Footway, lighting, speed limit: A281 High Street in OSM is lit, footways both sides, 30 mph. Furners Lane carries no lit/sidewalk/maxspeed tags. The DC/24/1538 committee report ¶6.46: "Officers do however recognise that the concerns raised by representations regarding the lack of a continuous footway along Furners Lane. WSCC Highways have raised similar concerns and recommend that some additional improvements could be made to promote sage [sic] and suitable access for all." ¶6.44: ATC survey "85 percentile speed of 22mph east bound and 20mph west bound"; objectors asked to "reduce Furners Lane and Furners Mead to 20mph" (¶3.23), implying a 30 mph limit. Speed limit and lighting on Furners Lane: **unverified**.

**Bus services.** Stops "Furners Lane" (A281, at the junction) and "Henfield High Street"/"Golden Square". Stagecoach South 17 Brighton–Horsham: roughly half-hourly daytime, hourly evenings (https://bustimes.org/services/17-brighton-horsham-2?date=2026-10-02). Also Compass Travel 100 Horsham–Burgess Hill and 106 Henfield–Worthing; frequency not pinned down.

**Connectivity Tool.** Not mentioned in the decision letter or in 3376143 (22 May 2026, pre-2026 NPPF).

**Council's position.** No officer report obtainable. Decision ¶2: reason for refusal 2 was reptiles; reason 1 the countryside/BUAB location. Nothing in the letter suggests the Council disputed accessibility. ¶16: "the Local Highway Authority do not consider the proposal would have an unacceptable impact on highway safety". Henfield PC objected on HDPF Policies 1–4 and 10, not on accessibility (minutes 4 Jul 2024). The sentence "The site is in an accessible location, close to High Street services, the primary school, and bus stops" (¶10) is word for word the same as 3376143 ¶11 for the 191-home Charlwood Drive site, including the preceding tier-2 sentence.

**Confidence.** High on geometry and distances to the High Street and buses; high on the 17's frequency; medium on "no continuous footway" (from the neighbouring site's report, same lane, a stretch further east); low on Furners Lane lighting and speed limit; nil on the officer report and WSCC's view for this application.

**Was the route tested in the 191-home appeal instead?** Only for traffic. APP/Z3825/W/25/3376143 (Land east of Charlwood Drive, DC/24/1932, allowed 22 May 2026, under the December 2024 Framework) had a Transport Assessment (December 2024), a highway authority response of 1 May 2025, and a s106 package of off-site works, "parking improvements to a layby and pedestrian crossings at identified locations" (¶23), "dropped crossing points and tactile paving" (¶50), a travel plan (¶24) and an emergency access serving "as a route for pedestrians and cyclists" (¶50). The Transport Assessment is used at ¶48 for junction capacity and the share of development traffic on the High Street, not for the quality of any walking route. ¶11 is the same generic sentence. And Charlwood Drive is an estate road inside the built-up area with a secured pedestrian link to Kingsley Square; Cedar Cottage is at the private end of Furners Lane. So the sentence Henfield's inspector copied was written about a different site, under the old Framework, by an inspector who had traffic evidence but did not test the walk.

**Judgement (revised after reading the file).** Henfield is a true code-A case, and a cleaner one than first thought. Nobody tested the route: the highway authority did not visit and treated the matter as advice on a private road; the officer never assessed accessibility; the Council never contested it; the applicant filed no transport evidence and gave two different distances to the same bus stops; and the inspector's location sentence was supplied by the appellant's final comments in the form of the adjoining 191-home decision. The finding probably holds on the ground (c. 450–520 m to a lit High Street with a half-hourly bus), but nothing in the decision or the file demonstrates it.

## Case 2: PINS 6008253, Land East of College Road South, Aston Clinton (Buckinghamshire 25/02250/AOP), up to 66 dwellings, allowed 24 Sep 2026 after a hearing

**Sources read.** Buckinghamshire's portal is now https://publicaccess.buckinghamshire.gov.uk (keyVal SZSS60CLFX900). Downloaded: officer delegated report 21 Oct 2025; Highways comments 18 Sep 2025; Transport Assessment (mode, 30 Jun 2025); Planning Statement; DAS Part 1; Parish Council objection 22 Aug 2025; Rights of Way comment 1 Sep 2025; S106 15 Sep 2026. Plus the decision letter, bustimes.org, OSM. The appeal Statement of Common Ground and the post-August-2026 supplemental statements are **not on the portal and were not found**.

**Walking distance and route**
- TA Table 3.1 (Google Maps distances from the site): The Oak PH 460 m; Baptist Church 610 m; Partridge Arms 760 m; Medivet 800 m; Community Centre 910 m; Shell garage 910 m; Barn Owls Nursery 960 m; "Aston Clinton Primary School 960 [m] 13 [min]"; The Bell 1,110 m; St Michael's 1,210 m; Post Office 1,310 m; Jardines Pharmacy 1,810 m; dental clinic 1,910 m. No convenience store and no GP surgery in the table.
- Shop: DAS Part 1 lists "Post Office, Budgens Supermarket, and Shell Petrol Station" within "a 2km walk". OSM shows a Co-op Food on Aylesbury Road; own OSRM route c. 800 m. Budgens/Co-op naming unresolved.
- GP: not in the TA table. Parish Council: "ACPC note a minimum 24-minute walk to the Aston Clinton Surgery from the entrance of the estate, 26-minute walk to the Aston Clinton Dentist, neither that have spare capacity to take on new patients." Officer report ¶5.103: the ICB "has assessed that the existing GP practices, particularly the Westongrove Partnership (Wendover Health Centre), do not have sufficient capacity"; £62,300 contribution. Surgery location not independently verified.
- Bus stops: TA ¶3.5.1, "The nearest bus stops to the development are located c.840m to the south of the site on London Road." DAS: "The nearest stops are 875m from the site on Aylesbury Road". TA ¶4.5.11: "This distance equates to a c.10-minute walk." Own OSRM measurement to the Weston Road stop: c. 780 m.
- Route quality: TA ¶3.2.1, College Road South "is subject to a 30mph posted speed limit and benefits from footways on either side of the carriageway"; Brook Street 30 mph with a footway on the east side; London Road 30 mph with "continuous footways flanking either side" and a Pelican crossing. Highway Authority: "A new footway is proposed between the site access and the commencement of the existing footway on College Road South, adjacent to no. 50 College Road South … It is recommended that the existing narrow footway on the eastern side of College Road South is widened to a minimum 2 metre width"; "There are also footways and the area is lit" (Green End Street and Brook Street); "a new section of footway is proposed to connect to lit footways along College Road South, Brook Street, and Green End Street." So the site has no footway to the network until the condition 16 works are built.
- Green End Street: the TA does not identify the gap. Decision ¶50: residents raised "the lack of a footway along part of Green End Street which would potentially be used by pedestrians to access the play area and local school"; the appellant "offered to consider this as part of the off-site highway improvements". Condition 16 names only the College Road South footway and "pedestrian dropped kerb crossing points in the wider area". The Green End Street footway is not secured.

**Bus services.** TA Table 3.2: 61 Aylesbury–Dunstable c. 90 min weekdays, no Sunday; 61A 2/day; 62 hourly weekdays, c. 90 min Saturday, no Sunday; 500 Aylesbury–Hemel hourly Mon–Sat, no Sunday; 501 Aylesbury–Watford 4/day weekdays, hourly Sunday; X61 1/day. bustimes stop "opposite Rising Sun": 61/61A about 2/hr, 500 about 1/hr, X61 about 1/hr (https://bustimes.org/stops/040000004318). Combined roughly 2–3 buses an hour to Aylesbury on weekdays, almost nothing on Sundays. Highway Authority: "The site is outside of the maximum recommended walking distance to bus stops along Aylesbury Road and London Road." The £1,000/dwelling bus contribution "is required to overcome the issue of the excessive walking distance between the new development and existing bus stops and to improve bus services." A mobility hub with a demand-responsive transport stop is the proposed answer.

**Connectivity Tool.** Not mentioned in the decision, officer report, TA, highways comments, planning statement or DAS. All council-stage documents pre-date the August 2026 NPPF; the post-Framework statements (decision ¶3) were not obtainable.

**Council's position on location.** Officer report ¶5.25: "Overall, the proposed access arrangements are considered satisfactory". None of the eight reasons for refusal concerns accessibility or transport. Highway Authority: "no objection from a highway perspective"; "The proposed development is within acceptable walking and cycling distance of a range of amenities and facilities", citing the VALP's description of Aston Clinton as a larger village with "at least reasonable access to facilities and services and public transport, making them sustainable locations for development." Counterpoint in the same report, ¶5.34 (design): "The scheme is served by only one vehicular access resulting in poor connectivity between the proposed development and the established settlement. This lack of meaningful connections is likely to result in a car-dependent environment and fails to encourage walking or cycling to local services and amenities." History: the 2019 refusal of 17/02388/AOP on this site included that "the proposal did not make adequate provision for accessibility by non-car modes of travel" (officer report ¶3.1). The TA relies on the 2018 Brandon Close inspector's remark that "it is common ground between the appellant and the Council that the site has acceptable access to surrounding facilities and services" (TA ¶4.5.4). Decision ¶57: "the appeal site is in an agreed accessible location"; ¶42: the off-site works "are necessary to enhance connectivity to local services and facilities".

**Confidence.** High on distances, speed limits, footway and lighting (TA and Highway Authority letter, cross-checked against OSM); high on bus frequencies; medium on the GP walk (Parish Council figure only); nil on the SoCG wording and on any Connectivity Tool score.

**Judgement.** Not self-evidently sound. Village edge on lit 30 mph streets with footways, school about 1 km and supermarket about 800 m supports "reasonable access". But the bus stops are c. 840 m away, which the highway authority described as outside the maximum recommended walking distance and "excessive", the main routes run hourly to 90-minutely with no Sunday service, the GP is around 2 km by the parish's measure with the ICB directing growth to Wendover, no footway exists from the site until condition 16 is built, and the Green End Street gap is only "to consider". The inspector's sentence rests on the Local Plan's tier label and the parties' agreement, not on a route test. The finding was common ground, not demonstrated.

## Outcome

Aston Clinton was moved from code A to code B on 1 Oct 2026: the route was assessed at application stage and the decision records the secured works, so the tier sentence sits beside route evidence. Henfield stays in A on the decision text.

## What this means for the page

Both villages had public transport an inspector could call reasonable (Henfield half-hourly; Aston Clinton 2–3 an hour weekdays, plus a bus contribution). Neither inspector tested the route. The page's line stands: the tier plus proximity was accepted where a bus service existed, and in both cases the walk was on lit streets with footways for most of its length. Claverdon has none of that.
