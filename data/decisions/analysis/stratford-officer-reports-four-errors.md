# Stratford-on-Avon officer reports under the 2026 Framework: four recurring errors, report by report

Note of 13 October 2026, updated 14 October with the six reports for the 21 October Planning Committee. Every Stratford-on-Avon District Council (SDC) officer report we hold for a decision made, a committee resolution passed, or a committee item published, on or after 17 August 2026, classified against four errors. The table is `stratford-officer-reports-four-errors.tsv` beside this file (44 rows, one per report, with page references and verbatim quotations). Not legal advice.

The four errors, as asked:

1. **A village's tier.** The report treats the village's place in the Core Strategy settlement hierarchy (Main Rural Centre, Category 1 to 4 Local Service Village) or a list of its facilities as showing that the location is sustainable, instead of assessing the route and the transport choice.
2. **TR3 is not being assessed.** TR3(1)(a) asks whether a proposal that "could generate a significant amount of movement" offers "a genuine choice of transport modes"; TR3(2) asks for evidence, which is the Connectivity Tool. The report does not run the limbs.
3. **Annex B.** Annex B excludes from "settlement" villages "which lie within and are defined as part of the Green Belt in the development plan" and hamlets outside built-up areas. The report applies S4 or S5(1)(j) in a washed-over village or a hamlet, or uses the Built-Up Area Boundary (BUAB) or tier as the GB7(1)(g)(iii) test.
4. **DP3(3).** A design or character conflict is found and weighed in the balance; the DP3(3) "should be refused … without clear justification" question, which S4(2)(c) and S5(2) turn into a "substantially outweighed" trigger, is not asked.

## Reading the table

Columns: `ref`, `place`, `type` (committee or delegated), `decided` (notice date, or "pending" for a resolution), `outcome`, `homes`, `green_belt`, `washed_over`, `framework`, `template_weight`, then for each error a code and a note. Codes: `error`; `partial` (tier) or `cited-only` and `dodged` (TR3) for the half-way cases; `ok`; `na` where the question does not arise. For tier, TR3 and Annex B that covers householder works, listed building consent, replacement dwellings and employment. For DP3(3) it is narrower, because the policy applies to any proposal with design content, including householder works (a fence at PINS-6010905) and replacement dwellings (Lucy's Farm is an `error`): only three reports are `na`, where the harm is heritage harm alone under HE6 (Upper Quinton), the consent is listed building consent (Trinity College), or there are no external works (Fenny Compton Old School). Every note carrying an adverse code has a page reference and a verbatim quotation from the report text, and every quotation was machine-checked against the text files in the private sources repo. "Not mentioned" claims were grepped.

Judgement calls, so the codes are read the same way across the table:

- `partial` for tier was used wherever any transport fact (a bus stop distance, a footway remark) sits beside the tier finding, even where thin. Ravenstone and Harbury are `partial` on that reading, not `error`.
- `not-mentioned` for TR3 is a fact about the text, recorded even for single dwellings inside a village (Stretton-on-Fosse, Long Marston, Priors Hardwick), where TR3(1)(a)'s threshold is doubtful. The note says so.
- `ok` for DP3(3) includes reports where no design conflict was found, so there was nothing to trigger; the question is still asked in every report with design content, and `na` is reserved for the three cases above. Ravenstone is `ok` on that strict reading: "modest harm" is found but accordance with CS.5, CS.9 and CS.12 is concluded. Fenny Compton barn 26/01801 is a borderline `error`: a Supplementary Planning Document scale conflict is dismissed as "only guidance".
- Ardens Grafton 26/01619 is `ok` on tier because the route was genuinely assessed (no footway, a bus twice a week, 50 mph lanes). Its Annex B error runs the other way from the Green Belt cases: a hamlet with no boundary is held to be a settlement so that S4 applies.
- Decision dates with no notice in sources come from the case file or the officer's sign-off date; the notes say which.

## What the new reports add

- **Checketts Abattoir 25/02369/FUL.** The clearest unconverted 2024 decision. The report lists the "National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF) 2024 (Updated 2025)", runs paragraph 11(d) and recommends refusal on heritage and amenity grounds; members granted on 22 July; the notice is dated 27 August 2026 with no 2026 note anywhere on the file. Its principle finding is the tier and the BUAB ("Category 3 Local Service Village", AS.10 complied with) with no route assessment, and TR3 does not appear. It is a member overturn, so the stated reasons for granting are the committee's, and the minutes should be read when the register publishes them.
- **Ardens Grafton 26/01619/PIP.** Right answer, wrong route. The officer refuses on the route ("neither be safe nor suitable", "almost entirely reliant on the use of the private vehicle", p.8) and cites the 2025 appeal at 6000835 in the same hamlet, but then holds that the site "would fall within the settlement of Ardens Grafton" (p.9) and applies S4, with TR3 appearing only in refusal reason 1. The DP3 conflict in reason 3 is weighed, not put through DP3(3).
- **Lucy's Farm 26/01352/FUL.** A Green Belt replacement dwelling correctly routed through S5(5) to GB7, but the policy list is the 2024 Framework and a CS.20 and AS.10 scale conflict with "moderate harm on the landscape" is weighed away without DP3(3).
- **Fenny Compton 26/01514/FUL.** A five-page 2024 report, with paragraph 49 cited for the emerging plan, decided the day after the Framework commenced. No location or design question arises.
- **Gorcott Hill 26/01776/OUT.** The same template that produced Ravenstone gives the right location answer outside a village: S5(5) to GB7, GB7(1)(g)(iii) tested on the A435 route, TR3 applied. The village question does not arise because Gorcott Hill "does not amount to a village". DP3 is refusal reason 2 but DP3(3) is still not asked.

## The 21 October committee reports

The three housing items repeat the pattern of the 7 October and 23 September reports, and none of the six uses the Connectivity Tool.

- **Stockton 25/00823/OUT (up to 75 homes, recommend grant).** The first large scheme written wholly under the 2026 Framework: S5(1)(j) is the gateway on a 2.43-year supply (p.11), the S5 "substantially outweighed" test is run (p.29), and paragraph 11(d) appears only in an objector's summary. The location finding is the Southam sentence again: "The site adjoins one of the larger and more sustainable settlements in the District (as a Category 2 LSV) with access to a variety of services and facilities. Accordingly, it is considered a sustainable location for new housing" (p.30), after a facilities list (p.11). TR3 appears twice. The p.11 passage says "I have also considered NDMP TR3 (a)" and then answers S5(1)(j)'s scale question with the tier. The p.22 passage is the real one: "on planning balance" a sustainable location, shops in "a short walking distance", a bus stop at about 320 m and an hourly weekday number 63 to Rugby and Leamington. Limb (a) is reasoned, thinly; the genuine-choice wording, TR3(2) and limbs (b) to (e) are absent, and the route to the school has "No crossing facilities" over Becks Lane (p.22), left to the s.278. DP3(3) is the clear error: "moderate" harm and conflict with CS.5, CS.9 and AS.10 are found (p.16), the Urban Design officer says the submission "has failed to demonstrate how the development will deliver a well-designed place" (p.15), but DP3 is never named, the conflict goes to the balance at "only moderate weight" (p.30) and design is deferred to a Design Code condition. The template paragraph is at p.10 and repeated p.11; CS.26 also gets "very limited weight" (p.21) for lacking TR6's refusal wording. HO7 is not cited. Good practice: conditions secure Laurel Drive and Becks Lane improvements, a bound surface on footpath SM29 and a footpath connection (p.33-34). The report also says "only the point of vehicular entry being within the settlement boundary" (p.30) yet never applies S3(2) or S4.
- **Tredington 25/02906/FUL (4 homes, recommend grant).** Inside the Neighbourhood Development Plan BUAB of a Category 3 village, so S4 is the right route and Annex B does not arise. The principle is the tier ("Tredington is identified as a Category 3 Local Service Village", p.7) and the only sustainability reasoning sits inside the L2(1)(d)(iii) exception: pavements so "occupants can walk around the village", a facilities list, "a local bus service" with no stop, distance or frequency, concluding "befitting of its Category 3 LSV status" (p.11); the balance repeats "a Category 3 Local Service Village and therefore a sustainable location" (p.21). TR3's one appearance is inside the quoted L2 text (p.10); no limb is run and Highways is TR6 access and parking only (p.17). No design conflict is found (p.14), so DP3(3) is `ok` on the strict reading, though the L2 paragraph at p.11 finds "some conflict" and "harm to the overall character of the area" and then no harm, and the L2(1)(d)(iii) 50% retention test is failed with S4(2)(a)(ii), quoted at p.9, never applied. The template paragraph (p.8-9) cuts CS.15, CS.16 and AS.10 although the scheme complies with them; NDP H1 and H3 get the same because "NDMP S6 is not engaged" (p.9), which is not the Annex A test. HO7 not cited; the "larger houses only" objection never answered.
- **Upper Tysoe 26/00290/FUL (2 holiday lets to 2 homes, recommend grant).** The quashed 25/00671/FUL decision re-run under the 2026 Framework: the October 2025 permission was quashed by consent in R (Tysoe Parish Council) v Stratford-on-Avon DC, AC-2025-BHM-000396, on the ownership-certificate ground alone (Certificate A signed where the red line took in a neighbour's land), with the Parish Council's grounds on NDP Housing Policy 1 and the tilted balance left undecided; the new report re-runs the same s.38(5) reading of H1 that was ground 1 (p.8-9). The site is an isolated farm complex "approximately 900m east of Upper Tysoe, outside the BUAB" (p.7), so the tier never enters and S5(1)(c) is applied in terms (p.11), which fits a change of use of a lawful permanent building. But location is not assessed at all: TR3 has 0 hits for two new open-market homes with "no public footpaths within the vicinity of the site" (p.14), and the ward member's "reliant on car travel, and inaccessible by safe walking routes" (p.4) is never answered. HO11 is quoted (p.11) but whether the homes are isolated is never decided; HO11(1)(c) needs a "redundant or disused" building when the report finds them in use. No external works, so DP3(3) is `ok`. The template paragraph sweeps CS.15 in with AS.10 (p.13), and the balance then calls AS.10 "not to be materially inconsistent" and still gives it "very limited weight" (p.21). S6 is treated as a benefit of "substantial weight" (p.13), which is not what S6(1) does.
- **Bearley training centre 26/00216/FUL (Green Belt, retrospective, recommend grant).** The first Green Belt report in the set to take the route cleanly: "NDMP S5(5) states that this policy should not apply to sites within the Green Belt" (p.8), then GB6 and GB7, cabins under GB7(e), the plant-training ground under GB7(f)(i), the GB2 purposes run, "very special circumstances are not required" (p.12), then the S5(5) test (p.19). S4, S5(1)(j), GB7(g) and the BUAB do no work because the site is "located away from any recognised settlement" (p.20), so Annex B is respected without being cited. Two slips: previously developed land is found "On planning balance" (p.6) without the Annex B definition, and a change of use belongs in GB7(f)(iii), not (f)(i). On location the officer is candid and adverse, "heavily reliant on the use of private motor vehicles", "not considered to be in a sustainable location" (p.20), but TR3 is never named for a use with up to 8 trainees at a time and a regional catchment; only CS.26 and TR6 run. The balance finds "some harm to the character of the countryside" (p.20) after a no-conflict design finding (p.13): the Ravenstone pattern, coded `ok`. No hierarchy template paragraph.
- **Edgehill 26/00951/FUL (householder, recommend refuse) and Wellesbourne 26/01866/FUL (loft conversion, recommend grant).** Tier, TR3 and Annex B do not arise. Neither finds a design conflict, so DP3(3) is `ok` in both; Edgehill is not the first SDC application of DP3(3): the gates have "quite an urbanising impact" (p.6) but accord "with both NDMP DP3 and Policy CS.9" (p.7). Edgehill's single refusal reason is highway safety under TR4, CS.26 and SPD Part O (p.8); TR6(4), the 2026 policy that says an unacceptable highway-safety impact should be refused, is not cited, and the S5 balance set up at p.3-4 is never run. Wellesbourne carries the emerging-plan paragraph giving six SWLP policies "very limited weight" under "paragraph 49 of the Framework" (p.7), the 2024 numbering, and its conclusion names "the Wootton Wawen Neighbourhood Development Plan" (p.6) for a Wellesbourne site.

The 23 September minutes, published with this agenda, record Home Farm 25/00347/FUL granted unanimously on the officer recommendation and Pillerton Priors 26/01894/PIP refused by 7 votes with 1 abstention on a members' reason that finds "The harm is considered to substantially outweigh any benefits associated with the scheme" and cites S4, L2, DP3 and N2: the committee reached for DP3 and the S4(2) trigger where the officer report had not.
