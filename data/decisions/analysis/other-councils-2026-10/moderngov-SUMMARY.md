# modern.gov committee reports, 24 September to 17 October 2026: four-error scoring

21 officer reports from 8 councils (Basildon 5, Elmbridge 3, North Hertfordshire 3, Dacorum 3, Chorley 2, Cheshire East 2, Tonbridge and Malling 2, Bath and North East Somerset 1), scored with the Stratford rubric. 9 of the 21 are Green Belt sites. Rows are in `rows.tsv` (21 rows, 22 columns). Every quotation in the notes (128) was machine-checked against the pdftotext files with whitespace collapsed; none is over 25 words. Every `not-mentioned` carries a grep count of 0 for `TR3` in the report text. Page numbers are PDF pages of the individual report PDF named in `source_file` (for Elmbridge the pack page range is also given).

## (b) Council by council

**Basildon** (7 Oct; five reports). No settlement hierarchy exists in the 1998 saved plan, so the tier error cannot arise; location is argued from the Transport Assessment (Little Burstead: bus "approximately 600metres north of site along London Road", p.54) or, in the refusal, from the route ("the nearest bus stop is located 0.6 miles away on Glebe Road", private roads that "do not feature footpaths or street lighting", p.21). TR3 is absent from three reports including the 175-home Little Burstead scheme, and is applied properly only where the officer is refusing (Glebe Road, "the site is not and cannot be made sustainable. This is contrary to NPPF: Policy TR3", p.62) or testing grey belt (Pound Lane, (g)(iii) "having particular regard to policy TR3", p.27). The Green Belt route is confused twice: Glebe Road says "As the site lies outside the defined settlement boundary, the proposal falls to be considered against NPPF Policy S5" (p.20) and never cites S5(5); Pound Lane opens with "The principle of development within settlements is set out in policy S4" (p.22) for a washed-over Plotlands site. Both urban estate reports still run "the Tilted Balance of NPPF paragraph 11" (p.1) under an August 2026 heading. No design conflicts are found, so DP3(3) is never tested; the Little Burstead balance uses "significantly and demonstrably outweigh the identified harm" (p.71).

**Elmbridge** (13 Oct; three reports). The strongest set. All three apply S3's split, quote S5(5) or S4 in full, quote DP3(3) in terms, and two use the Connectivity Tool with scores. Moore Place takes the Green Belt route cleanly ("policy S5(5) of the NPPF is of relevance", p.23, then GB7(1)(g)(i)-(iv) and S5(2) at the balance) and runs TR3 limb (a) with distances and the Tool. Its one slip is the DP3(3) one: having quoted paragraph 3 at p.53 it finds "moderate localised harm to the landscape character contrary to Policies CS14 and CS17 of the Core Strategy 2011" and "policy DP3 of the NPPF 2026" (p.66) and sends it to a benefits-and-harms table at moderate weight (p.145). Hersham 2026/0036 is the only report in either set that does what the rubric asks: it finds the DP3 conflict, quotes DP3(3), and says that as per S4 "it is necessary to consider whether the benefits of approving development are likely to be substantially outweighed by adverse effects" (p.61), though it then answers with the 2024 "significantly and demonstrably" wording (p.63).

**Chorley** (6 Oct; two reports). Town-centre over-55s scheme cites the Core Strategy "Key Service Centre" label (p.3) beside "approximately 200m from Chorley town centre" (p.9): partial. TR3 does not appear (the policy list says "T6"). The Heskin reserved-matters report for one Green Belt dwelling lists S5 beside GB6 and GB7 and runs none, and its conditions cite "the National Planning Policy Framework (2024)" (p.7) under a 2026 policy list.

**Cheshire East** (30 Sep Northern; two reports). Both rest on "Local Service Centre" plus a bare facilities sentence ("Bollington is well served by bus services, linking it with Macclesfield and Stockport", p.2; Alderley Edge "0.4 miles to the east of the Alderley Edge train station", p.2): partial. TR3 is absent from the 77-home Bollington report and, at Alderley Edge, quoted only for limb (b) to excuse a parking shortfall. Both ask the S4(2)(c) question in terms ("There is no conflict with any of the national decision-making policies which state that development proposals should be refused in specific circumstances", 25/4697 p.12) and Bollington says the local design policies "conform with NPPF new Policy DP3(3)" (p.14).

**Tonbridge and Malling** (24 Sep reconvened; two reports). The only template-weight paragraph in the set: CP14 "is not complaint with the NPPF 2026 and as a consequence is given very limited weight" (25/01325, p.9). Paris Farm relies on the emerging plan's "Tier 3: primary village" and "Tiers 1 to 4 are considered to be the most sustainable locations for growth" (p.9) with one footpath-lighting fact, and cites TR3 as a heading over an access-and-parking section. Town Hill is better: "bus stops located immediately adjacent to the site, West Malling High Street approximately 700m away, and West Malling railway station around 1.4km distant" (p.12) and DP3(3) paraphrased ("should be refused unless clear justification exists", p.9); members refused it on conservation-area harm.

**North Hertfordshire** (1 Oct; three reports). Two of the three delegated-style reports still head their policy list "National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF) (December 2024)" (p.2) while applying S4, S5 and GB7, and condition reasons cite 2024 paragraphs 187(f), 196 and 197. Little Wymondley is the set's one tier error: "in a sustainable location due to its location within Little Wymondley, with various amenities, and public transport nodes in relative proximity" (p.5), no route fact at all; members refused it anyway on DP3(1). New Pound Farm is the clearest Annex B error: on a Green Belt plot the officer finds GB7(1)(g)(i) met although the parcel "makes a strong contribution to Purpose A" (p.6), then says "the provision of a dwelling on a windfall site outside of a settlement would be supported by Policy S4 (j. i.) of the NPPF" (p.6), running S5(1)(j) by the wrong name on land S5(5) excludes. Graveley (281 homes) converts a July 2025 report with a paragraph-to-policy table (p.16) and never runs TR3.

**Dacorum** (24 Sep and 15 Oct; three papers). Polehanger (700 homes, 302 pages) is the most thorough Green Belt report: grey belt found but the 50% Golden Rule fails so "The development is considered inappropriate in the Green Belt (and must revert to very special circumstances arguments)" (p.27); the route is assessed with distances and frequencies (bus "approximately 800m from the centre of the site", hourly, p.97) and the genuine-choice wording appears in substance ("offering residents a genuine choice to ensure they are not inherently reliant on travel by private car", p.103); Annex A(2) is applied policy by policy. But the landscape conflict with CS1 and CS10 carrying "significant adverse weight against the scheme" (p.35) is weighed, not put through DP3(3), after DP3(1) is quoted at the head of that section (p.29). The Chesham Road update is a model of the S5(5) point: "Policy S5 does not provide an alternative route for approval. S5(5) provides that S5 does not apply to development proposals in the Green Belt" (p.7). Marchmont Farm (350 homes, allocated) never mentions TR3.

**Bath and North East Somerset** (30 Sep; one report). A refusal of a Green Belt log cabin at Clutton that runs the route correctly in the balance ("that route to approval is not engaged", p.16) but still opens with "The proposal must be assessed against the relevant provisions of Policy S5" (p.8) and tests the S5(1) categories. The location finding is the Highways quotation ("without footpaths or cycle facilities to the village of Clutton and which is unlit", p.13); TR3 is listed, not run. The design conflict becomes refusal reason 3 without DP3 being named.

## (c) Where the Stratford coding looks unfair, or is read differently elsewhere

1. **The TR3 "assessed" bar is met by officers who never label the limbs.** Dacorum Polehanger, TMBC Town Hill and Basildon Pound Lane each reason the substance of TR3(1)(a) (distances, frequencies, a new or adjacent bus service, "not wholly reliant on the private car") under local transport policies or GB7(1)(g)(iii), naming TR3 once. Stratford's Stockton was coded `assessed` on the same footing, so the coding is consistent, but a reader of the counts should know that "assessed" rarely means a limb-by-limb run. Only Elmbridge Moore Place quotes TR3 in full and works through it. The "genuine choice" phrase appears in one report of 21 (Polehanger, p.103) and in none of the 44 Stratford reports, so a code that required it would be a bar no officer clears.

2. **"Not-mentioned" for small schemes inside settlements is a fact about the text, not a defect.** Six not-mentioned reports here include an 8-home and a 19-home estate redevelopment in Basildon's urban area, a 14-unit over-55s block 200m from Chorley town centre, and a 77-home affordable estate regeneration inside Bollington. The threshold in TR3(1)(a) ("could generate a significant amount of movement, in the context of the area") plausibly excludes the first three. The Stratford note already flags this for single dwellings; the point extends to urban infill of this scale. The 175-home Little Burstead and 350-home Marchmont reports are the real misses.

3. **The Annex B "S5 run on a Green Belt site" code catches reports that get the answer right.** BathNES Clutton and, to a lesser extent, Basildon Glebe Road both reach the GB6 very-special-circumstances test and refuse; Clutton quotes S5(5) and applies it correctly at the balance after testing the S5(1) categories first. Coding both `error` is faithful to the rubric (and to Stratford's Snitterfield and Butts Lane), but a reader should distinguish a wrong route that changes the test (Pound Lane's S4, New Pound Farm's "S4 (j. i.)") from a belt-and-braces run of S5(1) that S5(5) then switches off. Suggest a note in the Stratford table, or a `soft-error` sub-code, so the two are not summed as one.

4. **The DP3(3) code is largely untested outside Stratford because other councils find no design conflict.** 15 of 21 reports here find none, often for outline schemes where design is reserved. The two errors (Moore Place, Polehanger) are landscape-character conflicts with Core Strategy countryside policies that the officer attaches to DP3(1) or CS character policies; whether a landscape-character conflict is a "design" conflict within DP3(3) is a reading Elmbridge and Dacorum plainly do not take (both treat it as a harm to weigh under N2/landscape policy). Stratford's Lucy's Farm and Stockton rows take the broad reading. The table should say which reading it uses. Elmbridge Hersham shows the narrow version of the step being done: DP3 conflict found, DP3(3) quoted, S4(2) trigger asked, then a weighing. If that is `ok`, Stratford's Snitterfield (DP3(3) named but used as a source of weight) is the right comparator and the distinction is whether the S4(2)/S5(2) trigger is named.

5. **"Partial" is doing a lot of work for town-centre and allocated sites.** Chorley 26/00040 (200m from the town centre), Cheshire East Alderley Edge (0.4 miles from the station) and TMBC Town Hill (bus stops adjacent, 700m to the High Street) are `partial` only because the officer also names the settlement's tier. Their route facts are at least as good as Stratford's `ok` Welford or Bordon Hill. The rubric's rule ("partial wherever any transport fact sits beside the tier finding") makes the code describe the officer's drafting habit, not the quality of the location assessment. A split into `partial-thin` (label plus a bare bus remark: TMBC Paris Farm, Cheshire East Bollington) and `partial-route` (label plus distances and frequencies) would separate them.

6. **Framework "mixed" is now mostly a labelling habit.** The five mixed reports apply 2026 policies in substance; the 2024 traces are a stale heading (North Herts), a stale condition reason (Chorley), or the "Tilted Balance of paragraph 11" sentence in Basildon's small-site template. None changes the test applied, unlike Stratford's unconverted four. Worth saying in the Stratford note that "mixed" and "2024+note" are not the same defect as "2024-unconverted".
