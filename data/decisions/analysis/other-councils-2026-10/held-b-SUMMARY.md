# held-b: southern and northern council reports scored against the four Stratford errors

23 rows in `rows.tsv` (one per application), from 13 councils and 40 held documents, all read from `pdftotext -layout` text in `held-b/txt/`. Every quotation in the notes was machine-checked against the text files (whitespace collapsed, curly quotes normalised); every "not mentioned" is a grep with 0 hits. Page numbers are PDF pages; for the two committee packs they are pack pages with the item number in the `ref` cell.

Set: Basildon 4 (24/01047, 25/00575, 25/01188, 25/01190); BathNES 5 items from the 2 Sep pack (02 Keynsham 200 homes, 03 Bath Press, 04 Old Gas Works co-living, 05 Bathampton padel, 08 Corston manege; items 01, 06 and 07 are neither housing nor Green Belt and were skipped); Chorley 1; Cotswold 2; Dacorum 1; East Devon 1; Elmbridge 1; Maidstone 1; Maldon 2; North Herts 1; Sefton 1; Three Rivers 2; TMBC 1 (the 19 Aug pack holds only the Coblands item; pp.89-112 are conditions and appendices).

Decisions: 23 committee, 0 delegated. 14 Green Belt rows, no washed-over village (Bathampton and Corston are not stated). 9 reports were written on the 2024 Framework and converted by an addendum, update or supplementary report (Basildon x4, Chorley, Maidstone, North Herts, Three Rivers 25/2168, TMBC); 13 are 2026-native; East Devon is mixed (S5(1)(j) applied, 11(d) wording in the conclusion). Eight were decided against the officer recommendation (Basildon 25/00575, 25/01188, 25/01190; BathNES item 04; Chorley; Maidstone; Three Rivers 25/2168; Dacorum's April refusal maintained).

## (b) Council by council

**Basildon (4 Green Belt outlines, 11 to 71 homes, 19 Aug and 9 Sep 2026).** All four are 2024 reports; the 19 Aug addendum says references "should, where appropriate, be read as references to the corresponding or successor provisions" and names no policy, so TR3 has 0 hits in every report and the 2024 para 155 grey belt route is run in full with very special circumstances as fallback. The route is respected: no S4/S5, no boundary as the location test. Location quality varies with the author: 25/00575 and 25/01190 give measured routes ("The nearest bus stops are located on Stock Road, approximately 900 metres from the site, an 11-minute walk and served by Route 300, which provides 2-3 buses per hour", 25/00575 p.42), whereas 24/01047 and 25/01188 rest the 155(c) limb on adjacency ("adjacent to the existing built-up area of Wickford and benefits from access to the transport network along London Road", 25/01188 p.27). None finds a design conflict, so DP3(3) is `ok` throughout. Members refused three of the four against the officer, on Green Belt grounds; the 9 Sep refusal wording for 25/00575 is the only Basildon text that names "policies GB6 and GB7 of the NPPF".

**BathNES (5 items, 2 Sep 2026).** The strongest set. Keynsham (200 homes, Green Belt) is a model of the route: S5 quoted with S5(5), GB7(1)(g)(i)-(iv) run, criterion (iii) sent to the Highways section, TR3(1)(a) answered in its own words ("development should limit the need to travel, particularly by private car, and offer a genuine choice of transport modes", p.57), then "Policy S5(5) of the NPPF" and S5(2) applied in the balance (p.68-69) with the refusal-policy question asked in terms. Bath Press is the only report in either set that uses the Connectivity Tool's output ("a Department for Transport connectivity score of 80-82, categorised as 'very high' nationally", p.116) and reasons TR3 limbs (a) and (b) with the L3 density test. Bathampton runs GB6 to GB7(1)(g)(iii) "with particular reference to policy TR3" on modes rather than a boundary, but never runs the S5(5) balance after finding the padel centre not inappropriate (S5 has 0 hits in pp.232-267) and slips into "paragraph 155". The co-living item is the gap: 272 studios in Bath with TR3 at 0 hits, and the committee refused it for "the impact of the height, scale and massing" after the officer found no design harm, so the design question was decided by vote, not through DP3(3).

**Chorley (58 affordable homes, Green Belt, 8 Sep 2026).** A 2024 report with a four-page 2026 addendum that lists GB6, GB7, GB8, DP3, TR3 and TR4 and says "The proposal would still be assessed as grey belt under the tests set out in Policy GB7" (p.2). The location work is the best route assessment in the set, done under the 2024 paragraphs: "Croston train station is 0.5km and a 7-minute walk", buses 337 and 347 "operate Monday to Saturday", "consistent footways with streetlighting and the speed limit in this location is reduced to 20mph" (p.13), ending "sustainable transport options such as walking, the bus and the train offer genuine alternatives to the car" (p.13). TR3 is `cited-only` on the name test only. Members resolved unanimously that they were minded to refuse for conflict with Core Strategy policy 1 and the emerging plan.

**Cotswold (Aldi store and a hotel pavilion, 9 Sep 2026).** The Aldi report quotes TR3(1)(a)-(e) in full (p.56-57) and then assesses access, visibility and parking under INF4 and INF5 without answering a limb; its location finding sits in the retail sequential test and uses the development boundary as the test with footways and bus stops beside it: "located within the village's development boundary and has therefore been deemed through the Local Plan process to represent a relatively sustainable location" (p.33), hence `partial`. The pavilion raises none of the four questions and finds no harm.

**Dacorum (1,400 homes at Tring, appeal-stance review, 10 Sep 2026).** Not a determination: it asks whether to keep contesting reason 1 after the 2026 Framework; the committee voted 6-5 to keep contesting. It states the route exactly ("Policy S5(5) expressly provides that the policy does not apply", p.8), tests GB7(1)(h) on the station timetable and the 800 m radius, and treats L3(4) as a refusal policy that "could outweigh the S5 presumption" (p.24). It is the set's one DP3(3) `error`: the landscape and character conflict with CS1, CS24 and CS25 is carried at "great weight in the planning balance" (p.15) with DP3 named only in the policy list, in a report that applies the parallel L3(4) "should be refused" wording a few pages later. The outcome is a refusal, so the omission changed nothing.

**East Devon (105 homes at Cranbrook, 1 Sep 2026).** S5(1)(j) is applied and the route is measured ("between 10m and 600m from existing bus stops on London Road (served by 44/44A)", "around 2.9km from Cranbrook Train Station", p.24), but TR3 has 0 hits for 105 homes, the conclusion still asks whether harms "would not significantly and demonstrably outweigh the benefits" (p.57), and the neighbourhood plan is cut because it "is more than five years old and therefore out of date" (p.19), an age-based cut Annex A(2) forbids.

**Elmbridge (replacement clubhouse, Green Belt, 15 Sep 2026).** GB6 and GB7(1)(f)(iv), inappropriate because openness is not minimised, very special circumstances found. The only report in the set to quote DP3(3) in terms ("Development proposals should be refused if, without clear justification, they conflict with paragraph 1 of this policy (DP3)", p.11) before finding no character harm.

**Maidstone (replacement of a mobile home, 20 Aug 2026).** A 2024 report with an update that rewrites the principle under S5(1)(c) and (d) and lists TR3 and DP3 without applying them. The officer concedes the location ("Agreed that the site is in an unsustainable location, however the application does not increase the intensity of residential accommodation", p.4) and finds the design "visually acceptable" (p.7); the committee refused 12-1 for "scale, massing, height and disproportionate enlargement" citing S5, LPRSP15 and LPRQD4 (minutes p.6).

**Maldon (360 homes at Burnham-on-Crouch and 149 at Tollesbury, 2 Sep 2026).** Two candid route assessments ("around a 20 minute walk or five minute cycle" to the station, bus stops "not presently served by a regular route", 26/00018 p.9-10; buses "generally run every 30 minutes", "no services on Sundays", 26/00066 p.11). Tollesbury applies TR3(1)(a) in terms ("limiting the need for private car trips so far as practical as required by NPPF Policy TR3(a)", p.11) and is the only report in either set that lists the Framework's refusal policies for S5(2) and answers DP3(3) in a table ("The design would comply with the relevant NPPF requirements, subject to the detailed design elements", p.36), although it has just found landscape harm "contrary to Local Plan Policies D1 and H4 and NPPF Policies N2, N5 and DP3" (p.16-17) and does not reconcile the two. Burnham names TR3 only in the highways policy sentence. Both cut the S8 conflict to limited weight because the housing policies are "out of date" on supply grounds, the 2024 footnote 8 logic rather than Annex A(2).

**North Herts (50 homes at Pirton, 3 Sep 2026).** The set's one tier `error` and the clearest Stratford pattern outside Stratford: a 2024 report whose principle is adjacency to a "Category A village" ("on land that physically adjoins Pirton", p.9; "a logical extension of the village", p.28) with no bus stop distance, frequency, footway or walking distance anywhere, objectors' "poor walking, cycling, and public transport links" (p.4) unanswered, and a 3 Sep update whose conversion table maps "Paragraphs 110, 115, 116" to "Policy TR6" alone, dropping TR3. The update quotes Annex A(2) in full and leaves the report's wholesale cut ("the weight given to the relevant policies contained within the NHLP are significantly reduced", p.8) in place.

**Sefton (3 homes at Maghull, Green Belt, 16 Sep 2026).** GB7(1)(g) is run and the Golden Rules correctly disapplied, but the sustainable-location limb and TR3 are both carried by one unmeasured sentence, "adjacent to the existing built-up area of Maghull and is within walking distance of bus stops and a primary school" (p.7), in a report that elsewhere records "there is no footway at this location" (p.11); after GB7 the report never runs the S5(5) balance (S5 has 0 hits). Coded `partial` for tier, `assessed` (thin) for TR3, `ok` for Annex B because no boundary or tier is used as the test.

**Three Rivers (70 homes at Carpenders Park, 20 Aug; six industrial units at Rickmansworth, 10 Sep 2026).** The Carpenders Park addendum is the best conversion document in the set: it reruns GB7(1)(g) and the new GB7(1)(h) on the Annex B definitions of reasonable walking distance and well-connected station, then S5(1)(h)/(j) and "Policy S5.5" (addendum p.9); TR3 is named only inside the quoted GB7(1)(g)(iii). Members refused 5-4 for inappropriate development, heritage harm and the missing S106 while counting "sustainable location" among the benefits. Woodcock Hill takes GB7(1)(e) previously developed land to S5(5) and answers TR3(1)(a) and (e) with candour ("not located within a particularly sustainable location", p.34) and secured footway links and bus stop upgrades.

**Tonbridge and Malling (350 homes at Coblands Nursery, Green Belt, 19 Aug 2026).** A 2024 report with a 22-page side-by-side supplementary that re-reads every paragraph as a 2026 policy, converts para 155 to GB7(1)(g) ("In line with g, iii, the site is located" / "sustainably (covered under TR3 below)", supplementary p.6) and answers TR3 by cross-reference to the report's measured route (218 and 219 buses "typically runs twice an hour and operates every day of the week", pack p.58). The supplementary's own TR3 sentence leans on "the most sustainable settlement in the borough", a tier-style claim, and the balance remains the 2024 para 11(d)(ii) one; Annex A(2) is quoted "For information" with no weight cut.

## (c) Does the Stratford coding travel fairly?

1. **The "tier" error is not a national habit; it is a Stratford habit.** 13 of 18 location cases here assess the route with figures (stop distances, frequencies, walk times, footways, lighting, speed limits). Only North Herts does what the Stratford committee reports do, and it does it under the 2024 Framework. The bar is not set where no officer writes: Chorley, Maldon, East Devon, Basildon 25/00575 and 25/01190, Three Rivers and TMBC all clear it. So the Stratford `error`/`partial` codes are fair as applied.

2. **"TR3 not mentioned" needs the conversion context read with it.** Seven `not-mentioned` rows are 2024 reports converted by a one-paragraph addendum (Basildon x4) or a report that applied S5 but kept 2024 habits (East Devon, North Herts). In four of those seven the 2024 equivalents (paras 110 and 115 under para 155(c)) are reasoned on the facts. The Stratford table already handles this by noting "2024 equivalents applied to the route in substance" (Pillerton Hersey); the same note is in these rows. The fair comparison is "TR3 or its 2024 equivalent reasoned on the route": on that reading Basildon 25/00575, 25/01190, Chorley, East Devon and Three Rivers 25/2168 pass and the Stratford housing reports mostly still fail. The code itself should stay, because it records a fact about the text and because North Herts shows a conversion can drop TR3 altogether (paras 110, 115 and 116 mapped to TR6 only).

3. **The name test for `cited-only` produces odd results in both directions.** Chorley is `cited-only` though it contains the fullest limb (a) reasoning in the set, because the reasoning sits under 2024 numbers and TR3 appears only in a list. Cotswold's Aldi report is also `cited-only` though it quotes TR3(1) in full, because nothing follows. These are the same code for opposite failings. If the parent note uses `cited-only` as a count, it should say that the code measures whether the limbs are run against TR3 by name, not whether the location was assessed.

4. **The Connectivity Tool count is slightly understated by a literal grep.** Bath Press uses the Tool's output under its DfT name ("Department for Transport connectivity score") and Keynsham relies on the plan-stage DfT rating. Stratford's "used in none of the 44" is a grep result; the summary should say "named or used in none", and the comparison here should credit BathNES with one use and one indirect use.

5. **Annex B is respected everywhere in the Green Belt without being read.** No council in this set puts S4 or S5(1)(j) on a Green Belt site or uses a boundary as the GB7(1)(g)(iii) test, so the Stratford Annex B error (6 of 17) is a genuine outlier. But none of the 14 Green Belt reports cites the "settlement" entry either; they reach the right route through S5(5) or straight through GB6/GB7. Two reports (Bathampton, Sefton) find development not inappropriate and then never run the S5(5) balance, which the rubric does not score; the parent may want a sub-code for "GB7 passed, S5(5) not applied".

6. **DP3(3) `ok` is mostly "no conflict found", not "question asked".** 22 `ok` rows; in 19 the officer found no design conflict, in 2 the question is asked in terms (Elmbridge, Maldon 26/00066) and in 1 S5(2) is asked generically (Keynsham). The Stratford `error` rate (18 of 41) therefore reflects how often Stratford officers find a design or character conflict and still recommend approval, which these councils rarely do: when design was the problem here, the committee refused (BathNES co-living, Maidstone) or the officer found none. The code is fair, but the honest comparator is "reports that found a conflict": Stratford 18 of 18 weighed it without DP3(3); here 1 of 2 (Dacorum, a refusal) did, and Maldon 26/00066 asked the question and answered it in a way that does not match its own finding.

7. **Landscape-character conflicts are a soft edge of the DP3(3) code.** Dacorum's `error` is a CS1/CS24/CS25 landscape conflict, scored by analogy with Stratford's CS.5 rows. Other officers (BathNES Keynsham, Maldon 26/00018, TMBC) find landscape harm but conclude compliance with the landscape policy, which the rubric treats as no conflict. The distinction between "harm found, policy complied with" and "conflict found" decides the code, and it is a drafting choice officers plainly make differently; the parent note should say so when quoting the Stratford 18.

8. **template_weight is better measured as "weight cut on supply grounds" than as the Stratford "very limited weight" phrase.** Nobody outside Stratford uses the Annex A(2) phrase to cut a hierarchy policy wholesale; three councils (East Devon, Maldon, North Herts) reach the same result by the 2024 footnote 8 route ("out of date" because of the five-year supply) or by age, and TMBC and North Herts quote Annex A(2) without applying it. The Stratford pattern is unusual in form; the substance (hierarchy policy discounted without saying which part conflicts with which national policy) is shared by 4 of 23 here.

## (d) What could not be done

- No report in this set is a delegated decision, so the delegated/committee split in the Stratford table cannot be compared.
- Dacorum is an appeal-stance review, not a determination; it is scored because the brief lists it, with the April refusal as the outcome and the limits noted in every cell.
- Washed-over status for Bathampton and Corston (BathNES) is not stated in the reports and was not checked against the BathNES Local Plan (not held).
- The Basildon 25/00575 decision notice and the Three Rivers 25/2168 final refusal wording ("to be circulated following the meeting") are not in sources; outcomes come from the minutes and the 9 Sep wording report.
- The TMBC pack's pp.89-112 contain no second item (checked for application headers and recommendations), so only Coblands is scored from that pack; the minutes' other items (Avebury Avenue, St Andrews Close, Lodge Farmhouse) are not held.
- Unit counts for BathNES items 03 and 04 are from the report (220 dwellings; 272 co-living studios), not the case files, which record null.
