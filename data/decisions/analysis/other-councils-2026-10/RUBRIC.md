# Shared rubric: scoring council officer reports against the four "Stratford errors"

Context. The research repo holds an analysis of 44 Stratford-on-Avon District Council (SDC) officer reports written under the National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF) of 17 August 2026: data/decisions/analysis/stratford-officer-reports-four-errors.md and .tsv. Read both first (the .md fully; the .tsv header plus 5 rows). We want to know whether OTHER councils make the same errors at the same rate, scored the SAME way, so the codes below must be applied exactly as that table applies them. Do not soften or harden them.

The Framework text is the oracle: data/open-sources/nppf/chunks/policies/{S4,S5,TR3,DP3,GB7,GB6,L2,HO11}.md and data/open-sources/nppf/chunks/annexes/annexB-glossary.md (the "Settlement" entry) and annexA-implementation.md. Quote the policy by code, e.g. TR3(1)(a), S5(1)(j)(i), GB7(1)(g)(iii), DP3(3), S5(5), Annex A(2).

## The four errors

1. tier: the report treats the village's place in the local settlement hierarchy (any tier label: service village, category N, tier N, larger village, local service centre, "sustainable settlement" etc.) or a bare facilities list as showing the location is sustainable, instead of assessing the route and transport choice (footway, lighting, distance, bus frequency, cycling). Codes: error (tier/list is the whole location finding), partial (tier relied on but at least one concrete transport fact given), ok (route genuinely assessed, or tier not used as the location test), na (location not at issue: householder, listed building consent, replacement dwelling, employment, non-housing where no location question arises).
2. tr3: TR3(1)(a) asks whether a proposal that "could generate a significant amount of movement" is in a location that limits the need to travel AND offers "a genuine choice of transport modes"; TR3(2) says the Connectivity Tool "should be used". Codes: not-mentioned (TR3 does not appear at all; grep the text), cited-only (TR3 named or quoted but no limb reasoned), dodged (report says TR3(1)(a) not engaged because the scheme is small), assessed (at least limb (a) reasoned with facts; say which limbs), na (no new trips: householder works, LBC etc.). Also record connectivity_tool: yes/no (grep "Connectivity Tool" and "connectivity tool").
3. annexb: Annex B says "settlement" excludes hamlets and scattered houses outside built-up areas, and "villages which lie within and are defined as part of the Green Belt in the development plan" (washed-over villages). S5(5): S5 does not apply in the Green Belt. Codes: error (S4 or S5(1)(j) or L2(1)(d) "within settlements" applied in a washed-over village or a hamlet; or a settlement boundary / tier used as the GB7(1)(g)(iii) sustainable-location test; or S5 run on a Green Belt site), ok (route respected: Green Belt sites go GB6/GB7 then S5(5); non-GB sites inside a defined settlement go S4; outside go S5), na (no Green Belt or hamlet question arises). Also annexb_cited yes/no, and note whether it is cited only for the grey belt / PDL definitions.
4. dp33: DP3(3): "Development proposals should be refused if, without clear justification, they conflict with paragraph 1 ... or relevant aspects of the principles in paragraph 2, or with any explicit design standards set out in the development plan". S4(2)(c)/S5(2): failing a "should be refused" policy makes benefits "likely to be substantially outweighed". Codes: error (a design/scale/layout/character conflict with DP3 or with a local design policy is found, and is then weighed in the balance without asking the clear-justification question), ok (no design conflict found so nothing to trigger; OR the clear-justification question is asked in terms; OR DP3(3) applied as a refusal reason), na (heritage-only harm under HE6, LBC, no external works). Record whether DP3(3) is named at all, and whether the report was a REFUSAL (so the skipped step could not have changed the outcome) — put that in the note.

Also record: framework (2026 / 2024+note / mixed / 2024-unconverted), and template_weight: does the report cut the weight of settlement-hierarchy / countryside-protection policies wholesale (e.g. "very limited weight" to the whole hierarchy policy) without identifying which part is materially inconsistent with which national policy (Annex A(2))? yes/no with quote.

## Method rules (non-negotiable)

- Work only from the text: run `pdftotext -layout FILE.pdf OUT.txt` for each report (if a PDF is scanned and gives no text, run `uv run tools/ocr_pdf.py IN.pdf OUT.txt` from the research repo root). Put all texts in YOUR OWN scratch subfolder (given in your brief). Never write into the research, sources or main-site checkouts.
- Every adverse code (error / partial / not-mentioned / cited-only / dodged) MUST carry a PDF page reference and a verbatim quotation of up to 25 words, and you MUST confirm the quotation with grep against the text file (collapse whitespace; `tr -s ' \n' ' '` on both sides). Every negative ("TR3 not mentioned") MUST be a grep with 0 hits; say so in the note ("TR3 grep 0 hits").
- Page numbers = PDF page numbers (pdftotext inserts form feeds \f between pages; count them, or use `pdftotext -f N -l N`). For a combined committee pack, give the page within the pack AND the item.
- Note whether the decision was delegated or committee and the outcome, homes, Green Belt yes/no, washed-over village yes/no.
- Do not invent. If a report is missing, unreadable or the site blocks you, say so and move on. Do not retry a blocked host more than twice.
- Give the SAME coding as the Stratford table would. In particular: "partial" for tier wherever any transport fact (a bus stop distance, a footway remark) sits beside the tier finding; "not-mentioned" for TR3 even for single dwellings (record the unit count so the threshold point can be made later); dp33 "ok" includes reports finding no design conflict.
