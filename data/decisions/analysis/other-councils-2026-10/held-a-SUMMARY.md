# Held set A: Midlands and nearby councils, scored against the four Stratford errors

28 rows in `rows.tsv`, one per application, from 37 documents already in `sources/council/` (reports, five Bromsgrove decision notices that carry the delegated reasoning, two update sheets, four sets of minutes). 27 rows are scored; one (Wychavon W/25/01931/OUT, 59 homes) is held as a notice only and is marked `not-scored`. Texts were made with `pdftotext -layout`; every quotation in the notes was machine-checked against the whitespace-collapsed text (159 quotations, 0 misses) and every "grep 0 hits" claim was re-run. Page numbers are PDF pages.

Decisions: 18 delegated, 10 committee. 13 of the 28 sites are Green Belt. Outcomes: 19 approved or resolved to approve, 8 refused (one of them, Nuneaton 041303, by members against an officer recommendation to grant), 1 notice-only approval.

## (b) Council by council

**Wychavon (8 applications, 7 scored).** The South Worcestershire Development Plan Review (adopted March 2026) template is consistent and, on these four points, the best in the set. Every housing report runs S3 then S5 by category, and two apply the Annex B settlement definition in terms: Pinvin quotes it in full and concludes "it is not considered within a settlement as defined within the NPPF. Therefore, in this case the proposal needs to be assessed against Policy S5" (p.6). Tiers are stated but the location finding is always made on the route: Pennylands Bank has a bus stop under 100 m yet the officer finds "the frequency and range of services available are relatively limited" and refuses (p.5). New End is the only report in the set to use the Connectivity Tool in the body ("The application site has a Connectivity Score of 23", p.6) and the only one to cite S5(5) by number before going to GB6 and GB7. Weaknesses: TR3 is absent from three single-plot PIP or FUL reports where the location is nevertheless assessed under SWDPR 06, and DP3(3) is named in the two Matthew Webb reports (Pinvin, Naunton Beauchamp) but nowhere else. No template weight paragraph: with a 5.48-year supply the reports say "full weight can be afforded to the Council's policies for the supply of housing".

**Malvern Hills (5).** Same SWDPR template, less evenly applied. Callow End is the one `partial` tier in Worcestershire: "Callow End is listed in Annex B (Rural Hierarchy of Settlements) (SWDPR) as a category 1 rural settlement" and "future residents would arguably have a 'choice' and as such SWDPR 06 would be addressed" (p.4), with no stop, frequency or footway; the same report applies the NPPF Annex B settlement definition correctly. Hallow runs the fullest route assessment in the set over two pages but gives the character conflict "substantial weight" and balances it (p.19), the Gorcott Hill pattern. Leigh Sinton is the clearest approved DP3(3) error: "some adverse impact on the visual amenities and landscape character of the area contrary to Policies SWDPR05, SWDPR28 and SWDPR34" (p.30), then "any limited harm identified is outweighed by the public benefit of the scheme" (p.36), in a report that lists the August 2026 Framework but applies paragraphs 103, 104, 115 and 116. The two 26 August committee items are 2024 reports converted by an update sheet that maps paragraph numbers to policy codes and says the assessment "remains unchanged"; both passed 6 to 5.

**Worcester (1).** A section 73 access variation in the city centre; none of the four questions arises. The report does what Annex A(2) asks: "No such material inconsistency has been identified in respect of the policies relied upon in this assessment" (p.8).

**Bromsgrove (5 decision notices).** All five are Green Belt and all take GB6 and GB7 first; none applies S4 or S5 as the gateway, and three run GB7(1)(g) limb by limb. Dodford is the clean example: "Criteria iii requires that the development would be in a sustainable location having regard to policy TR3 of the 2026 Framework" (p.4), bus "on a Tuesday and Friday only", so not a sustainable location, so inappropriate, so very special circumstances. The slips are at the edges. Wythall opens with "regard should be had to policy S5(1)(j)" on a Green Belt site (the one `annexb` error here), then runs GB7(1)(g) on bus stops at 60 m, footways and lighting. Hopwood finds GB7(g) satisfied and still runs a planning balance giving openness harm "moderate weight". Fairfield is the one unconverted 2024 decision in the set, dated 17 August 2026, applying paragraph 11(d) to a Green Belt site and borrowing its sustainable-location finding from a 2022 appeal. No notice cites S5(5), and none finds a design conflict (Dodford does not assess design at all).

**Warwick (3).** Two rural worker's dwellings and a village hall, all Green Belt, Burton Green expressly "washed over by Green Belt" (p.1). The route is GB6 then GB7 then very special circumstances in each; S5 is listed at Burton Green but not applied, and S4 and the settlement boundary do no work. The 2026 preamble restates Annex A(2) but cuts no policy. The three TR3 grep hits in the worksheets are the Local Plan's own Policy TR3 (Parking). Nothing to code against on any of the four points.

**Lichfield (2).** Stonnall is the model Green Belt route for a small housing site: GB6, GB7(1)(g) on the 2026 Green Belt Review parcel, unmet need on a 3.5-year supply, then S5(5) in substance, "where development within the Green Belt is not inappropriate through the application of Policy GB7, proposals should be approved unless the benefits" would be substantially outweighed (p.6). Its location finding is thin, a facilities list and public transport "within approximately 500 metres" (`partial`), and TR3 is named only through GB7(1)(g)(iii) with TR3(1)(e) paraphrased. Derry Farm is a rural worker's dwelling with nothing to code. Both reports disclose Copilot drafting assistance.

**West Northamptonshire (1).** Kislingbury (58 homes, refused) is the only report in the set with a template weight paragraph: because of a 4.3-year supply, "the policies most important for determining the application are out-of-date" and "These policies are afforded very limited weight" (p.14), cutting five spatial policies on the supply position rather than on material inconsistency. The tier opens the location finding ("Third Category Secondary Village (A) ... in principle, a sustainable location", p.15) with a facilities list and a bus stop 500 m away (`partial`); TR3 heads the section and "genuine choice" is paraphrased, so `assessed` on the Stockton standard. The DP3 conflict is refusal reason 2 but "carries substantial weight in the planning balance" (p.22), and the final test is the 2024 "significantly and demonstrably outweigh" (p.40), not S5(2) or S5(4).

**Nuneaton and Bedworth (1).** A section 73 trigger-point variation on a 621-home allocated site; the officer applied TR6(4) and recommended grant, and members refused for a "severe adverse impact on the highway network" (minutes p.2). None of the four points arises; the reasons-for-approval paragraph cites the 2024 Framework in a report listing the 2026 one.

**Cheshire East (2).** Knowle House (68-bed care home, Green Belt) is a 2024 report converted by an update sheet published the day after the Framework; the update names TR3 and applies TR3(2), "requires assessment using the Department for Transport's Connectivity Tool" with a 59 to 62% score (update p.2). The report's own route assessment (health centre 600 m, bus 600 m, station 750 m, pavements and lighting) is genuine. The update's policy list says S5's categories include PDL redevelopment, on a Green Belt site, without S5(5); S5 is not run as the gateway so this is noted, not coded. Shavington (9 homes, not Green Belt) is the cleanest small housing report in the set: S5(1)(j) applied, TR3(2) and the Connectivity Tool used ("Local Authority Band B", p.11), bus frequency and footways given, the S5 "substantially outweighed" test run, no design conflict.

## (c) Where the Stratford coding looks unfair or where other officers plainly read the policy differently

1. **TR3 `not-mentioned` for single plots is a fact about the text, not about the assessment.** Four of the six `not-mentioned` rows (Pinvin, Pennylands Bank, Callow End and Hopwood) contain a fuller route assessment than several Stratford `assessed` rows: Pennylands Bank measures the bus stop, the footpath, the missing crossing, the speed limit and the lighting and refuses. The Wychavon template puts the location under SWDPR 06 and does not name TR3 unless GB7(1)(g)(iii) forces it. The code is applied as the rubric requires, but the tally should be read with the `tier` column beside it: in this set the councils that do not name TR3 still assess the route, which is the opposite of the Stratford pattern. Duffledown Farm goes further: it quotes TR3(1)(a) verbatim, including "offer a genuine choice of transport modes", without naming the policy, and has been coded `assessed` on substance; a pure grep rule would make it `not-mentioned`, and the Stratford table should say which rule it used.

2. **`assessed` under GB7(1)(g)(iii) is a low bar and the Stratford table set it.** Dodford, Wythall and Stonnall are `assessed` because TR3 is named through the grey belt limb and the officer then gives one or two route facts. That is the Gorcott Hill standard. If the Stratford table's "limb (a) reasoned with facts" meant the genuine-choice wording had to be engaged, Stonnall and Wythall would drop to `cited-only` and Kislingbury with them. The genuine choice phrase appears in the operative reasoning of only three reports in the set (Drakes Broughton 50 homes, Duffledown, Knowle House via 2024 paragraph 110).

3. **The Connectivity Tool bar is one no council in this set reaches by habit.** Three uses in 27 reports, one of them in an update sheet; the Stratford finding of none in 44 is not an outlier. TR3(2) says the Tool "should be used", so the code stands, but the comparison shows an absence common to every authority rather than a Stratford fault.

4. **DP3(3) `error` turns on whether a weight is assigned.** Three refusals in the set find a design or character conflict and refuse on it. Callow End and New End are `ok` because the conflict is treated as a determinative breach (New End also cites paragraph 139's refusal wording); Hallow and Kislingbury are `error` because the same kind of conflict is given "substantial weight" and balanced before the same refusal. That is the Gorcott Hill rule from the Stratford table, applied here consistently, but it penalises the more carefully written refusals and rewards the terser ones, and in no refusal could it have changed the outcome. The genuinely significant cases are approvals where a found conflict is weighed away: one here (Leigh Sinton) against roughly half of the Stratford housing approvals. The table should separate approvals from refusals when it reports the DP3(3) count.

5. **DP3(3) is named in terms by two Wychavon officers and quoted by a third.** Pinvin and Naunton Beauchamp quote the refusal limb; Drakes Broughton quotes it and makes the DP3 conflict a refusal reason; Pennylands Bank paraphrases it as "NPPF page 67". So the Stratford observation that only one of 44 reports names DP3(3) does not hold for its neighbour, though the officers who name it are the ones who found no conflict or were refusing anyway. Nobody in either set has yet asked the clear-justification question of a conflict and then approved.

6. **S5 on Green Belt land appears as a listing habit, not a route.** The Wythall `error` is a single sentence; the determination is GB7(1)(g). The Knowle House update and the Burton Green policy list do the same thing without applying S5. If the Stratford table coded Snitterfield as `error` because S5(1)(j) was actually run as the gateway in a washed-over village, Wythall is a lesser fault and the two should not be counted as the same thing. I have coded Wythall `error` because the sentence tells the reader which policy governs, and flagged the difference here.

7. **The template weight paragraph is not a Stratford peculiarity but its basis differs.** Kislingbury cuts five spatial policies to "very limited weight" on the housing-supply position, reasoning from the out-of-date language of the 2024 Framework rather than Annex A(2) material inconsistency; Stratford's template cuts CS.15, CS.16 and AS.10 by asserting inconsistency without saying which part is inconsistent with what. Both skip the Annex A(2) question; the Worcester s73 report is the only one in the set that answers it in terms.

8. **Washed-over villages barely arise.** Only Burton Green is stated to be washed over, and it is a village hall under GB7(1)(f)(iv). New End and Dodford are unclear on the text. The set therefore cannot test the Stratford Annex B finding (6 errors of 17 Green Belt or hamlet cases) directly; what it shows is that Bromsgrove, Warwick and Lichfield officers do not reach for S4 or S5(1)(j) in the Green Belt at all, which is the route Annex B requires.

9. **2024 reports converted by a note.** Three committee reports written before 17 August were carried over by update sheets that map paragraph numbers to policy codes and declare no change. Stratford's `2024+note` rows did the same. For Cales Farm and Pendock nothing turned on it; for Knowle House the update did add the Connectivity Tool, which is more than any Stratford note did.
