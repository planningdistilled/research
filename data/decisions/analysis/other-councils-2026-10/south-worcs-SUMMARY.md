# South Worcestershire (Wychavon, Malvern Hills, Worcester City): 15 officer reports on housing decisions issued 20 September to 17 October 2026, scored against the four Stratford errors

Working files: `sources:council/other-councils-2026-10/south-worcs/` (rows.tsv, the PDFs and the text extracts in `txt/`). Scored 18 October 2026 from pdftotext text; every quotation and page number in rows.tsv was machine-checked against the text files (`bin/verify.py`, 0 failures) and every "grep 0 hits" was re-run. None of the 15 is in the held list. All 15 decisions fall inside the window (decision dates from the portal application pages).

Set: 9 Wychavon (8 delegated, 1 committee), 5 Malvern Hills (all delegated), 1 Worcester City (delegated). 10 approvals, 5 refusals. 8 are village-edge or countryside sites of 1 to 7 homes; 1 is a 12-home affordable scheme inside a Category 1 village; 1 is a section 73 house-type change on a 14-home village scheme; 5 are inside settlement boundaries (two in towns, one in Worcester). No Green Belt site and no washed-over village: none of the window's housing decisions fell in Wychavon's north-east Green Belt fringe, so the Annex B Green Belt limb could not be tested.

## (b) How each council handles the four points

**Wychavon (9).** The 2026 delegated template is better on location than Stratford's: the Offenham PIP reports quote TR3(1)(a) in full, give a Connectivity Tool score, and decide on footway, speed limit, bus distance and frequency, with the Category 1 label as context rather than conclusion: "connectivity could be better, on this occasion the proposal is considered to be acceptable" (W/26/01940 p.8). Outside that template TR3 vanishes: the barn conversion at Yardings Farm, "approx. a mile north of the heart of the village of Ombersley" up "a long unmade private drive" (W/26/01031 p.1), has no location assessment at all and never names S5, S5(1)(c) or HO11. Annex B is respected without being cited: two ribbons of houses outside the Offenham boundary are held not to "amount to a settlement for the purposes of the NPPF" (W/26/01940 p.6) and S5(1)(e) is run; the in-boundary reports apply "the definition set out within the glossary of the NPPF (2026)" (W/25/02384 p.4) before S4. On DP3(3) Wychavon is the only council in either set that quotes the refusal rule: "NPPF page 67 is clear that development proposals should be refused, if, without clear justification, they conflict with Paragraph 1 of Policy DP3" (W/25/02384 p.5; also W/26/01031 p.6, W/26/01900 p.5), and both design-conflict refusals carry the conflict as a reason rather than a weight. Slips: "Paragraph 78" and "paragraph 139" (2024 numbering) survive in the 2026 template; W/26/01515 p.8 ends the location paragraph with "significant conflict" where the sister report says acceptable; the Hartlebury 12-home committee report (Aug 2025, SWDP 2016, para 11(d) tilted balance) was issued on 24 Sep 2026 with no 2026 note. W/26/01900 (authorised 15 Oct) records that appeal 6006301 of 9 Oct 2026 found the councils "can only demonstrate a 4.49 year housing land supply at best" (p.4), which will move later reports from S5(4) to S5(1)(j).

**Malvern Hills (5).** Location is argued at length and candidly, with distances to school, shop and bus and the absence of footways and lighting, and the tier is never the finding. But TR3 is absent from all five (grep 0), and the reasoning does not control the outcome. Brookfield, Hallow (M/26/01143) finds "The site is approximately 0.7 kilometres from the relevant Category One settlement, 1.3 kilometres from Hallow Primary School" (p.7), no footpath or lighting, a Highway Authority objection that "the site occupies an inherently unsustainable location" (p.2), conflict with SWDPR02, 03 and 06 and "the tilted balance is not engaged" (p.8), then approves 3 to 4 homes because of "a reasonable case that it constitutes previously developed land, bringing Policy S5 of the NPPF into consideration" and "On balance, the principle of one dwelling is considered acceptable" (p.12), without running S5(1)(d)'s substantially-outweighed test. Colletts Green (M/26/01422) approves a dwelling 4.3 km from the nearest shop with no footway to a bus stop 10 m away, on the settlement boundary and scale. Sinton Green (M/26/01336) is the set's one DP3(3) error: a "valued gap in support of local rural character" (p.4) conflict with SWDPR28 B.vi goes only into the S5(4) balance, DP3 unnamed; it is a refusal, and its header says "Recommendation: APPROVE" (p.1). Copy-paste is visible: 112 Cowleigh Road, Malvern "lies outside the defined settlement boundary of Hallow" (M/26/00475 p.6); 2024 chapter numbers ("Section 11") and consultee citations of paragraphs 110 and 112 persist.

**Worcester City (1).** An urban infill dwelling: S4 named, design found to "accord with SWDPR 5 and SWDPR 28 and with Policies DP1 and DP3 of the NPPF" (p.9), TR3 not named, TR6(4) applied. Nothing in it tests the four errors.

## (c) Does the Stratford coding hold up here?

1. The tier error is a Stratford habit, not a universal one. 0 errors in 15 against 6 in 44 at Stratford. Every South Worcestershire officer who reaches a location conclusion gives route facts, usually footway, lighting, speed limit and distance to a bus stop, and the Wychavon PIP template gives bus frequency and a Connectivity Tool score. The bar is set where officers do write. The one `partial` is a 2025 report. Two edge cases the Stratford rule handles oddly: a report that assesses the route honestly, finds it poor, and approves anyway (M/26/01143, M/26/01422) scores `ok` on tier, because the code tests the method not the result; and a report with no location reasoning at all for an isolated barn (W/26/01031) scores `na`, following the Stratford Fenny Compton barn and Upper Tysoe rows. "Approved against the officer's own route finding" needs a separate column if the research wants it.

2. TR3 "not-mentioned" would fail most councils, but the two Offenham reports show the bar is reachable in an ordinary delegated report. 10 of 15 do not name TR3 (67%) against Stratford's 13 of 44 not mentioned (30%; 20 of 44, 45%, counting cited-only). Eight of the ten are 1 to 5 homes, where TR3(1)(a)'s "significant amount of movement" threshold is arguable, as the Stratford note already flags. The three `assessed` rows are limb (a) only, with the "genuine choice" words never applied, exactly as Stockton and Snitterfield were coded; one (W/25/02229) also mis-states TR3 in 2024 paragraph 109 wording. The Connectivity Tool appears in 2 of 15 against 0 of 44: both are the same Wychavon officer's template, so this is one team's practice, not a regional norm.

3. Annex B cannot be tested here (no Green Belt), but the non-Green-Belt limb reads the other way from Stratford. Stratford's Ardens Grafton error was holding a hamlet with no boundary to be a settlement so S4 applied. Every South Worcestershire officer facing a cluster with no boundary (Sinton Green, the Shoulton Lane cluster, two Offenham ribbons) held it not to be a settlement and ran S5. None cites Annex B; three cite "the glossary" by name. For consistency: "a small hamlet" with a defined boundary (Lower Moor) is `na` because the plan defines it; and the Annex B hit in W/26/00476 is only the quoted L3(2)(b) text, so `annexb_cited yes` there is not the settlement definition, and the note says so.

4. DP3(3) is the Stratford error that transfers least. 1 error in 15 (a refusal) against 18 in 41 at Stratford. First, design conflicts were rarely found: 11 of 15 found none, three because PIP defers design to technical details, and the Stratford rule codes those `ok`. Second, where a conflict was found Wychavon quoted the refusal rule and used the conflict as a refusal reason without assigning it a weight (W/26/01940, W/26/01515, W/26/01900), coded `ok` as "DP3(3) applied as a refusal reason". Stratford coded Gorcott Hill and Ardens Grafton `error` although DP3 was a refusal reason there, because the report also gave the conflict "moderate weight" in a balance. The line drawn here: weighed at a stated weight = error; carried as a reason, or the "should be refused ... without clear justification" rule quoted = ok. W/26/01900 is on the line (rule quoted at p.5, then "These adverse impacts carry significant weight in the planning balance" at p.9) and is `ok` on the strength of the quoted rule; the lead may wish to harden it. The 2024-numbering citation "paragraph 139 ... should be refused" in W/26/01940 is treated as the same rule; if a 2024 paragraph number does not count as applying DP3(3), that row becomes `error`.

5. Template weight. The Stratford paragraph has no South Worcestershire counterpart because the SWDPR was adopted in March 2026 and the councils (until 9 October) claimed a 5-year supply, so every report gives the hierarchy policies "full weight". The comparison is between a council with a 2016 plan and a short supply and councils with a fresh plan and (then) a surplus; the Stratford template paragraph may be a supply-position artefact as much as an error. The 4.49-year finding of 9 October 2026 (W/26/01900 p.4) is the natural re-check point: reports after mid-October will show whether Wychavon reaches for Annex A(2) weight-cutting once S5(1)(j) opens.

6. Framework column. 2024 paragraph numbers inside otherwise 2026 reports ("Paragraph 78", "paragraph 139", "Section 11", "Paragraphs 110 and 112" from the highway authority) are common. These are coded 2026 with the slips in the notes, as the Stratford table used "mixed" only where the 2024 Framework itself was listed. Two report versions on the W/26/01031 file show the conversion happening mid-case: the first lists 2024 chapter headings and the December 2025 consultation draft, the second (scored) converts.

## (d) What could not be got, and what was left out

- No Green Belt or washed-over case exists in the window, so error 3's main limb is untested for these councils.
