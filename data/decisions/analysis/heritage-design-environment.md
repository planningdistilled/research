# Heritage, design/land use and environment under the August 2026 NPPF

Analyst: analyst-heritage-design, 23 Sep 2026. Inputs: 792 case files (`index/cases.json`), 1,088 PINS letters in `data/open-sources/pins-corpus/` (17 Aug–23 Sep 2026), NPPF text (`data/open-sources/nppf/NPPF-August-2026.pdf`). Paragraph refs are to decision letters (DL). Counts are from `cases.json` unless marked "corpus". Tier-2 codes are thin, so treat single-code counts as indicative.

**The new heritage text in one line.** HE5(2)(c) asks for the *degree* of harm, and only "substantial harm" is defined ("would seriously affect a key element of the asset's significance"). HE6(1) gives **substantial weight to the asset's conservation**, "irrespective of whether any potential effect amounts to a positive effect, harm, substantial harm, or total loss". HE6(3) says **any harm is "a matter of considerable importance and weight"**. HE6(4) runs the harm–public-benefits balance and names two "important public benefits": long-term reuse of a vacant or underused listed building, and enabling energy efficiency and low-carbon heating. HE6(5)/(6) keep the old substantial-harm tests. HE4(2) keeps "clear and convincing justification". "Less than substantial" and "great weight" are gone.

## 1. Headline propositions

### A. Heritage

**H1. The harm label changed, but the harm scale did not.** Inspectors now name a degree of harm inside a single "harm" category: "very low", "low", "minor", "slight", "moderate", "lower end". They still use the old spectrum language; they just drop the label. *Strong.*
- Best: **PINS-6007355** ¶26: "there would be harm (insofar as it relates to the categories of "harm" listed in the Framework) … I have judged the extent of harm to be minor … Nevertheless, still of considerable importance and weight." **PINS-6012043** ¶14: "Whilst there is no longer the concept of less than substantial harm…". **PINS-6007221** ¶33 ("a low level of harm … a very low level of harm to the significance of the HCA"). **PINS-6012915** ¶34 ("I do not find the harm … to be substantial").
- **PINS-6004673** ¶31 ("to a lower level within this category") keeps the old scale without naming it.
- Corpus, 176 heritage letters citing HE codes and no 2024 paragraph:
  - "lower/low end/level/degree": 62 letters;
  - "moderate harm": 23;
  - "spectrum" or "scale of harm": 16;
  - "less than substantial": 14 (8%).

**H2. The old wording is fading fast but has not gone. Most remaining uses are transitional or template slips.** *Strong (corpus count).* Corpus, 418 letters touching heritage assets, split into three date bands:

- All 16 heritage letters citing 2024 paragraphs 202–219 use "less than substantial". Examples: PINS-6005881, 6006868, 6006948, 6010301, 6003168 (a 9 Sep letter still citing ¶215).
- 14 letters use "less than substantial" *while applying HE6*: 6005067, 6005108, 6005433, 6007287, 6007478/6007479, 6007762, 6009838, 6010036, 6010391, 6012452, among others.
- "great weight" appears in 15 HE-citing letters, about 12 of them for heritage. Examples: 6008659, 6006054, 6008484, 6009776, 6010197, and 6005108 ("lower end of less than substantial, but nevertheless of great weight", ¶91, inquiry).

**H3. The weight words are being mixed up, and the mix-ups can be challenged.** *Emerging, with about 8 examples.*
- **Correct form.** HE6(1) gives substantial weight *to conservation*. HE6(3) gives considerable importance and weight *to the harm*. PINS-6007221 ¶34, 6005980 ¶32–33 and 6006422 ¶43 state both correctly.
- **Errors seen:**
  - substantial weight given to the *harm* (PINS-6005225 ¶17, 6006259);
  - "great weight" to harm (6005108, 6007287, 6012452);
  - **PINS-6007478** ¶23 presents 2024 ¶212 wording as the 2026 Framework: "great weight … moderate to high level of 'less than substantial' harm as set out in the Framework";
  - PINS-6010396 brings the HE6(5) "substantial public benefits" test into an HE6(4) balance;
  - PINS-6004909 ¶13 scales harm to "the modest nature of the proposal", contrary to HE5(3) ("effect on significance rather than the scale of the development").
- None of these errors changed an outcome, since the letters were dismissals where harm was found anyway. They are still live grounds for an s288 challenge or for rebuttal.

**H4. Any harm to a designated asset is now close to decisive against small schemes. Graded-down harm still carries "considerable importance and weight".** *Strong.*
- Inspector appeals where heritage harm was found: 124 dismissed, 10 permitted (7%). Where no harm was found: 52 of 81 permitted (64%).
- One-to-nine-home schemes lost on low or moderate harm despite supply shortfalls. The strongest are those with the harm graded low and the supply stated:
  - **PINS-6009545** (1 home, 3.68 yrs, "low level", ¶17);
  - **PINS-6007054** (1 home, 2.98 yrs, moderate, ¶18);
  - PINS-6007136 (1 home, 3.73 yrs, Grade I setting);
  - PINS-6007466 (9, 3.9 yrs, minor harm to WHS);
  - PINS-6003055 (1–9, 3.8 yrs);
  - PINS-6004909, PINS-6006054, PINS-6007403;
  - Stratford Ilmington (stratford-26-01399-PIP, 2.21 yrs).
- Householder and listed-building-consent cases lost on "minor" or "slight" harm: 6007355 (a 67% carbon cut still lost), 6012775, 6007221, 6011314.
- **Counter-examples:**
  - PINS-6011786: 1 home, "very low" harm to a conservation area's setting seen only from a few gardens, "clearly outweigh[ed]" by an urgently needed home (¶16–18);
  - PINS-6010036: an NDHA, a school fence;
  - stratford-26-01906-PIP (delegated): harm folded into S4 with no separate HE6(4) balance, which is a method error.

**H5. Housing wins the HE6(4) balance at scale, not at the margin.** For major schemes with a shortfall, low or lowermost harm to Grade II settings is outweighed. Moderate harm, harm to high-grade assets, or harm to functional settings is not. *Emerging (5 allowed, 3 refused/dismissed).*
- Allowed:
  - PINS-6005664 (110 homes, 4.04 yrs, "lowermost end", inquiry);
  - PINS-6005108 (31 homes, 4.9 yrs, low-end harm from highway works, inquiry);
  - APP-E3335-W-25-3375062 (20 homes, no 5YHLS, "minor", hearing, ¶12–13, 40);
  - PINS-6004144 (132 homes: heritage "very lowest level" outweighed, but the appeal was lost on odour);
  - PINS-6007184 (58 homes, NDHA limited harm).
- Refused or dismissed:
  - **PINS-6006475** (35 homes, 40% affordable, 3.16 yrs, hearing): a Grade II* cloth hall, a Grade I church and a CA. "three of the listed buildings are of the highest significance, this amplifies the level of weight" (¶38). Benefits of substantial weight were "substantially outweigh[ed]" (¶68).
  - westnorthants-WNS-2022-0673-MAF (58 homes, 4.3 yrs, moderate CA-setting harm, committee);
  - threerivers-25-2168-OUT (70 homes, 1.2 yrs, members added low-level harm as a refusal reason).

**H6. The HE6(4) public-benefit examples are acknowledged but rarely delivered. The recurring filter is "could a less harmful scheme achieve the same benefit?"** *Strong.*
- **Energy efficiency** wins only when it is real, quantified and on the *existing* building, and no less harmful route exists. Wins:
  - **PINS-6006240** (roof solar on a 1980s roof slope: CC2(2) gave "substantial weight", and the limited harm was outweighed, ¶17–18);
  - **PINS-6006422** (a biomass flue on an outbuilding: "important public benefit" under HE6(4), plus CC2(2) and W3(1)(a), outweighed "very low" harm, ¶59–61);
  - malvern-M-25-01044-FUL (solar, committee).
- Energy-efficiency losses:
  - discounted to limited because unquantified or achievable less harmfully: PINS-6012775 ¶17–19, 6006059 ¶18 (CC2(2) "does not disapply other parts of the Framework"), 6008669, 6007478 ¶25–26, 6005225;
  - CC2(2) not engaged, because a new extension does not improve the existing building: PINS-6011314 ¶36, 6007221 ¶38;
  - efficiency must go "beyond compliance": PINS-6006027 ¶35.
- **Optimum viable use / reuse.** Limited weight where the asset is already in optimum viable use (PINS-6006027 ¶36–37). Only "a small amount of weight" where reuse is unsecured by obligation or phasing (PINS-6004673 ¶35).
- **Private benefits** such as living space or convenience are excluded: PINS-6011314 ¶39, 6004673 ¶37, 6007355 ¶28.
- **Heritage gains** inside a more harmful whole carry no weight: PINS-6005980 ¶36.
- **Location-neutral benefits**: benefits "not specific to this site, so could be achieved elsewhere" (PINS-6007466 ¶23; also PINS-6012915 ¶39 for telecoms).

**H7. Heritage harm is not an S4(2)/S5(2) "should be refused" trigger unless it is substantial. It decides the S4/S5 balance anyway, usually after a stand-alone HE6(4) balance.** *Strong.*
- HE6(5) is the only refuse-limb, and it applies only to substantial harm. Its one use was PINS-6006506: substantial harm from a rear extension across a 17th-century elevation, ¶16, 20.
- The usual sequence is HE6(4) fail, then S4(1)/S5(1) "substantially outweighed": PINS-6007054 ¶22 ("S4(2) is not exhaustive"), 6007221 ¶47–51, 6011314 ¶49, 6006475 ¶59/68.
- Where DP3(3) is also breached, it supplies the formal trigger: PINS-6007221, 6011314, 6010197, 6009228.
- Method decides outcome. SDC refused Ilmington after running HE6(4) first, and approved Long Marston by folding harm into S4, on similar one-dwelling facts a month apart (stratford-26-01399-PIP vs stratford-26-01906-PIP).

**H8. Setting is read functionally and historically, not just visually.** *Emerging, and the most useful point for Claverdon.*
- **PINS-6006475** ¶14: "buildings that are in close proximity but are not visible from each other may have a historic or aesthetic connection". The Grade II* farmhouse "would become an historic farmhouse without adjoining farmland" (¶32).
- Rural approaches and gateways to conservation areas are protected: PINS-6008539 (Bagnall CA gateway), 6003055 (village-to-countryside transition), 6007403 (development "spilling over" an edge), 6010301 (historic field pattern), 6007136 (loss of glimpsed views out to countryside).
- A site that is only a "minor component" of setting can still cause moderate harm if the development dominates a key element (PINS-6007054 ¶13–14).
- Limited public visibility does not remove harm: PINS-6005980 ¶28–31 (CA); 6006027 ¶31 ("irrespective of whether or not public views"). HE4(2) "clear and convincing justification" is applied as a separate requirement (6006475 ¶59; 6006054; 6008562).

**H9. NDHAs (HE7) get an ordinary balance, and HE7(3) total loss still bites.** *Emerging (13 HE7(2) findings).*
- Limited NDHA harm is routinely outweighed: PINS-6007184, 6010036, eastdevon-26-0534-MOUT.
- Total loss of a low-significance NDHA was not outweighed by one replacement home: **PINS-6007188** (HE7(3)).
- HE10 is cited in no letter in the corpus.

**H10. The statutory duties are unchanged and routinely recited. HE6(1) is treated as the policy expression of s66/s72.** *Strong.* "Special regard/attention" appears in 57% of heritage letters. PINS-6009762 ¶8–9 pairs s72 directly with HE6(1). No letter treats the 2026 changes as diluting the duties. Pre-2026 case law (Barnwell, Bramshill) is not cited; the Framework text now carries the "considerable importance and weight" formula itself.

### B. Design and land use

**D1. DP3(3) "should be refused … without clear justification" works as an S4(2)(c)/S5(2) refusal trigger in about 20 appeals. Most design dismissals still run plan-led, with DP3 cited generically.** *Strong that the route exists. Not yet universal.*
- Trigger cases: **PINS-6008167** ¶21 (substantial housing weight at 3.55 yrs still "substantially outweighed"), 6008785 ¶39–42 (S5(1)(j) *met* but lost via DP3(3)/S5(2)), 6010905 (householder fence), 6005325 ¶23–25 (TPO oak loss under DP3(2)(c) "maintaining and enhancing tree cover"), 6003086 (CO1 substantial weight defeated), 6009866, 6010197, 6009228, 6010826, 6011648, 6011373, 6006644, 6010619, 6011498.
- Corpus: 174 letters cite DP3, but only 31 cite DP3(3) by limb (28 dismissed). About 59 have "should be refused" near DP3.
- **Counter / limits:**
  - allowed with DP3(3) cited:
    - **PINS-6009340** ¶22: a verge micro-BESS had a minor DP3(1) conflict, but W3 substantial weight supplied the "clear justification". So a substantial-weight benefit can satisfy DP3(3) itself.
    - 6009632: DP3 principles "applied proportionately" to a rural hamlet.
    - 6012221: DP3(3) compliance weight used *for* the scheme.
    - Contrast PINS-6010987: an identical kind of unit was dismissed on DP3, with W3 not cited.
  - two harvest slices (corpus-rest-1 and -2) found DP3(3) "almost never" used as a trump;
  - **the reach of DP3(1) is contested.** At inquiry, PINS-6008238 ¶124 held that DP3(1) "relates to matters such as on-site scale, layout, landscaping and appearance rather than to the effect of a proposal on the landscape character and visual attributes of the countryside", so landscape harm went to N2, not DP3(3). Contrast 6008785 ¶39 (edge-of-village encroachment as a DP3(1) conflict);
  - **"clear justification" is read two ways, each now used after a hearing.** Necessity, to refuse: PINS-6007133 ¶59. Level balance, to allow: PINS-6008253 ¶86. Level balance, to refuse: PINS-6005590 ¶46 (substantial housing weight did not justify departing from explicit accessibility standards);
  - SDC cited DP3(3) at Snitterfield but did not use it as an S5(2) trigger.
- DP3(3)'s second sentence ("substantial weight to compliance with relevant development plan policies") is read as substantial weight *against* non-compliance with a local space standard (PINS-6011088, 6009127).

**D2. The S4(2)/S5(2) trigger list in practice.** All from `cases.json`; every one is a dismissal or refusal.
- DP3(3): see D1.
- TR6(4): 6006761, 6006846, 6008848, 6011101, 6011209, 6011256, 6009997, 6006644.
- F5, F6, F7, F9: 6006629, 6008500, 6009598, 6010682, 6008881, stratford-26-01376-FUL.
- N2(2) and N6: 6008404, 6009588, 6010848.
- TC3: 6009871.
- L3(4): 6010196.
- S4(2)(a)(i) loss of a safeguarded or community use: 6010035, 6011055, 6009917.
- S4(2)(a)(ii) L2(1)(d) curtilage impact: 6006644, 6010619.
- S4(2)(a)(ii) N4: 6003055.
- Contrary: none. Where no trigger applies, schemes usually pass S4/S5 (PINS-6005664 ¶45; northherts-25-02064-OP).

**D3. L2(1)(d) (garden, backland and upward development) is a two-way test. Its criteria are gates to the substantial weight and grounds for refusal.** *Strong (53 L2(1) findings: 26 fail, 20 benefit).*
- **Pass:** PINS-6011148 (a cul-de-sac-head plot got substantial L2 weight on top of HO7, and was allowed as a PiP); PINS-6012221 (householder, L2(1)(d)(i)–(iii) met). Corpus-only householder allowals, with no case file yet: 6011690, 6012259, 6013026.
- **Weight withheld:** PINS-6008667 ¶13 (more than twice the footprint → substantial weight "not available").
- **Refusal grounds:**
  - L2(1)(d)(i) street scene and (ii) living standards: PINS-6007161, 6008337, 6008944 (a mansard in a CA), 6011521;
  - via S4(2)(a)(ii): 6010619 ¶58–59, 6006644.
- Meeting L2(1)(d) does not override HE6 (PINS-6007054 ¶24–26).

**D4. L3(4) "should be refused" bites on under-use even without character harm, but only one appeal so far.** *Single case, plus one LPA.*
- **PINS-6010196** ¶23–24: four houses at about 4 dwellings per acre on a PiP site. L3(2)(b) applies to edge land with permission. There was no character harm (¶20), and the appeal still failed.
- dacorum-25-01880-MOA: officers flagged the station-area 45 dph minimum.
- bathnes-25-03592-FUL: L3(2)(c) passed within 400 m of a 2-tph station.
- Counter: PINS-6006475 ¶67: "this policy is not just about maximising development capacity"; L3 does not trump heritage. PINS-6006581 ¶88: the 35 dph station minimum was inappropriate in a National Landscape.

**D5. DP3(5) substantial weight for innovative or sustainable design depends on fitting the "overall form and layout".** *Emerging.* PINS-6007355 ¶15; 6012221 (not engaged); PINS-6009032 (Passivhaus substantial weight, outweighed).

### C. Environment and other (proportionate)

**E1. Missing evidence and missing obligations are fatal and cannot be conditioned away.** This is the single commonest non-principle reason for dismissal. *Strong.* See the table in §2.

**E2. Flood: the F-policies are "should be refused" policies and decide cases outright.** Sequential-test failures turn on search area and discounting (§2). *Strong (F5 16, F7 12 findings, almost all fail).*

**E3. Highway safety: "unacceptable impact on highway safety" in TR6(4) is a real, stand-alone refusal ground, separate from "severe".** Visibility splays that need third-party or hedge land fail. Parking inconvenience does not. *Strong (TR6(4): 29 fail, 12 pass).*

**E4. CO1 substantial weight rarely decides. In heritage settings it loses to HE6 almost every time; outside them, siting and clutter decide.** *Strong (27 CO1 findings, 26 of them in dismissals).*

## 3. Test sequences as actually applied

### Designated heritage (clean template: PINS-6007221, 6005980, 6006422, 6004673)

```
1 Significance + setting (HE5(1); Annex B setting)       ← evidence gap here is fatal (HE5(1) fail: 3 cases)
2 Degree of effect (HE5(2)): positive / none / harm [graded] / substantial / total loss
     └ none → HE6 neutral → heritage drops out (64% permitted)
3 HE6(1) substantial weight to conservation ("irrespective…"; more for Grade I/II*)
4 HE6(3) harm = considerable importance and weight
5a substantial harm? → HE6(5) refuse unless substantial public benefits / (a)–(d)   [6006506 only]
5b otherwise → HE6(4) harm vs public benefits
     filters: private? unsecured? achievable less harmfully / elsewhere? existing building?
6 HE4(2) clear and convincing justification (often stated as a separate conclusion)
7 Carry the result into S4(1) / S5(1) "substantially outweighed" (S4(2) not exhaustive — 6007054 ¶22),
  or into GB6 VSC; DP3(3) often added as the formal S4(2)(c)/S5(2) trigger
8 s38(6) plan-led conclusion; local heritage policies keep full weight (Transitional(2) argument fails: 6007541, 6007314, 6006475 ¶66)
```

Where decision-makers diverge or go wrong:
- **Step 2.** The legacy label is used (H2). Harm is scaled to the size of the scheme (6004909).
- **Steps 3–4.** Weights are swapped or inflated (H3).
- **Step 5b skipped.** Harm is weighed only inside S4 (stratford-26-01906-PIP). This is the key SDC inconsistency.
- **Step 5b double-counted.** HE6(4) is run, then the same harm is re-weighed inside S4. That is conventional and not treated as an error.
- **Advertisements.** Inspectors split on whether HE6's balancing limbs apply: inapplicable in 6009587 and 6011428, applied in 6009003.

### Design and land use

```
S4 (within) / S5 (outside) → is a "should be refused" policy breached?
   DP3(3) "without clear justification" conflict with DP3(1)/(2) or explicit plan standard
   L3(4) under-use; L2(1)(d) criteria (via S4(2)(a)(ii))
   ├ yes → S4(2)(c)/S5(2): benefits "likely" substantially outweighed → usually dismiss even vs substantial HO7/L2/CO1/E2 weight
   └ no  → ordinary S4/S5 "substantially outweighed" balance (applicant usually wins absent heritage/landscape harm)
```

- Divergence: some inspectors keep design plan-led under s38(6) and cite DP3 as "consistent", without S4(2)(c).
- Some write "significantly and demonstrably" (the pre-2026 tilted-balance words) and then apply S4(2): 6010619 ¶56.

### Highways (TR6(4))

```
safety (unacceptable) ─┐
                       ├─ either limb → "should be refused" → S4(2)(c)/S5(2)/S5(5) trigger
capacity (severe) ─────┘
```

- Splay deliverability comes first: in the red line or highway, with no third-party land.
- The arboricultural consequences of the splay are a separate harm under DP3/N2/N4 (6007807).

## 5. Distillation notes (for a future agent reading a new decision)

**Heritage: capture all of these:**
1. The asset(s), their grade, and whether the effect is on the asset or its setting.
2. The **exact degree words** (quote them), and whether "less than substantial" or "great weight" appears. Tag `old-heritage-wording` if so.
3. Whether HE6(1) and HE6(3) are stated separately and correctly. Flag misattributions with `heritage-weight-misstated`.
4. Whether HE6(4) was run *before* and separately from S4/S5.
5. Each public benefit, with its weight and the reason it was discounted (private / unsecured / less-harmful-alternative / elsewhere / not existing building).
6. HE4(2) "clear and convincing".
7. The s66/s72 duty, and whether local heritage policies were held consistent (Transitional(2)).
8. Supply years and units, to support the H4/H5 thresholds.

**Coding traps:**
- **`weight` on HE6(1) findings is ambiguous.** Harvesters record "substantial" (the weight to conservation) or "considerable" (the weight to harm) inconsistently. 27 HE6(1) findings, mostly "substantial", mean conservation weight, not harm severity. See the schema proposal below.
- **Inspector mis-citations.** HE7(4) does not exist (corrected in PINS-6011786). "LP2/LP3" means L2/L3 (6009545). "HE6:4" and "HE6.1" are house styles. Map them to canonical codes and note the slip.
- **17 Aug letters** often apply 2024 ¶¶212–215 silently. Code them `nppf_applied: "2024-12 (transitional)"` and map 212→HE6(1), 215→HE6(4), 214→HE6(5), 216→HE7(2).
- **N3** is trees in *new* development (tree-lined streets, maintenance). Retention and loss of existing trees and hedges is **N2(1)(d)** and **DP3(2)(c)**. Several files code tree loss as N3. Record it as N2(1)(d) and add N3 only where the letter cites it.
- **N2 is used both for landscape/natural beauty (N2(1)(a)) and for biodiversity/BNG.** Always add the limb.
- **Statutory BNG** (TCPA Sch 7A) is not an NPPF code. Keep `BNG (Sch 7A TCPA)`, but also tag `bng-exemption-*`. The date rule is: small-site exemption at 0.2 ha, and the self-build exemption removed, for applications made from **6 Aug 2026** (6009460 ¶13, 6008441 ¶22).
- **"Should be refused" triggers.** Always record the S4(2)/S5(2) limb as its own finding and name the triggering policy in the note.

**Schema and tag proposals (not applied):**
- Add a **`heritage:` block** per asset: `{asset, grade, effect: positive|none|harm|substantial|total-loss, degree_words: "low", he6_4: pass|fail|skipped, public_benefits: [{benefit, weight, discount_reason}]}`. This removes the overloaded `weight` field and makes the H4/H5 thresholds queryable.
- Add **`s4s5_trigger:`** `[DP3(3)|TR6(4)|F5|F6|F7|N2(2)|N6|L3(4)|TC3|HC5|HE6(5)|S4(2)(a)(i)|S4(2)(a)(ii)]`.
- Normalise the heritage-wording tags. The variants `heritage-less-than-substantial`, `less-than-substantial-legacy-wording`, `old-heritage-wording` and `old-wording-slip` should become `old-heritage-wording` (label) plus `old-wording-slip` (any other pre-2026 phrase).
- New tags: `he6-4-skipped` (harm folded into S4 only), `splay-third-party-land`, `splay-tree-hedge-loss`, `energy-benefit-discounted`, `setting-functional`.
- Add `highway_evidence:` (speed survey, collision data, LHA stance) for TR6 cases. Outcomes turn on it (6001260 vs 6006819).

## 6. Gaps and open questions

- **No appeal yet allows 1–9 homes against "low" (not "very low") designated-asset harm with a shortfall.** The dataset cannot say whether any supply level rescues small schemes. Target: October letters from authorities under 2.5 years.
- **Major housing versus moderate heritage harm at appeal.** Only PINS-6006475 (high-grade assets). No inquiry decision yet weighs moderate harm to a Grade II setting against 50+ homes.
- **HE6(4) "long-term reuse of a vacant or underused listed building"** has no winning example.
- **HE9(1)(a)/(2) demolition of positive CA buildings**, and HE10: almost no cases.
- **s66/s72 challenge risk from H3 misstatements.** Watch for s288 claims (6007478 is the best target).
- **L3(4)** has one appeal. Station-area 35/45 dph minima are untested at appeal.
- **DP3(3) as a trigger.** It is unresolved whether PINS will standardise it. Track the ratio of trigger to plan-led design refusals month by month.
- **TR6(4) "unacceptable" safety.** No letter yet engages with pedestrian safety on unlit, footway-less rural lanes as a TR6(4) *refusal* ground, as opposed to a TR3/GB7(1)(g)(iii) locational fail. 6006819 is the closest.
- **CC2(2)/W3 consistency** across identical micro-BESS units (6009340 vs 6010987).
- Committee-level heritage practice outside Stratford and the Midlands is thin (5 approvals, 3 refusals).

## Transferable to Claverdon-type objections (Hall setting / CA; hedgerow and tree loss for splays; highway safety)

**Heritage: Hall setting and conservation area**
- **PINS-6006475** ¶14, 32, 38, 59, 68. Setting includes historic *functional* links such as a farmhouse and its farmland, "not visible from each other" notwithstanding. Heritage harm "substantially outweighs" substantial housing and affordable weight at 3.16 yrs. HE4(2) clear and convincing justification is a separate requirement.
- **PINS-6007221** ¶33–34 and **PINS-6009545** ¶17–23. Even "low" or "very low" harm carries considerable importance and weight (HE6(3)) on top of HE6(1)'s substantial weight to conservation.
- **Rural CA edge and approach:**
  - PINS-6008539 (CA rural gateway, Green Belt, 1 dwelling);
  - PINS-6003055 (village-to-countryside transition);
  - PINS-6010301 (historic field pattern);
  - PINS-6007136 (views out lost).
- **Method point for SDC.** Insist on a stand-alone HE6(4) balance before S4/S5. Compare stratford-26-01399-PIP (refused) with 26-01906-PIP (approved without HE6(4)).
- **Anticipate the rebuttal.** Minor harm has been outweighed for 20–110 homes with a shortfall (3375062, 6005664, 6005108). Distinguish them on asset grade and functional relationship, and on the absence of any harm-free alternative.

**Hedgerow and tree loss for visibility splays**
- **PINS-6007807** ¶9, 11, 22. The splays "would inevitably result in the loss of further trees within the splay itself or require works within the root protection area". The arboricultural assessment ignored the splays. Replanting "would take years to mature". The appeal was dismissed despite 3.9 yrs supply and an S5(1)(d) pass.
- **PINS-6006819** ¶20–23. A substandard access in a CA-setting lane, with hedgerow loss within the highway unrebutted: TR6 fail.
- **PINS-6012481** (a hedge above driver eye height; the splay "could not be addressed by condition" given hedgerow impacts and third-party land) and **PINS-6010090** (visibility achievable only by hedge removal).
- **Policy hooks:** N2(1)(d) "conserve and enhance existing natural features … established trees and hedgerows … where possible"; DP3(2)(c) "maintaining and enhancing tree cover", which through DP3(3) is a "should be refused" policy (PINS-6005325 ¶23–25).
- **Counter.** PINS-6007231 accepted a *longer* replacement hawthorn hedge. Show why replacement cannot compensate here: maturity, CA or Hall setting, splay geometry.

**Highway safety**
- **TR6(4) safety limb is independent of "severe"**: PINS-6006819 ¶20 ("residual impacts … would not be severe, the impacts on highway safety of a substandard access would be unacceptable, particularly for pedestrians or cyclists"); PINS-6006846 (30 mph road near junction and crossing).
- **Splay deliverability**: PINS-6009997 ¶14–15 (third-party hedge land: TR6(4) beats 4 homes at 1.98 yrs); PINS-6006761, 6008848, 6009618, 6006496 (2.4 × 160 m on 50 mph not shown).
- **Evidence standards**: PINS-6009573 ¶23 (an absence of recorded collisions is "not a reliable indicator", since only injury crashes are recorded); ¶8–9 (DMRB CD 143 has little weight off trunk roads, so use Manual for Streets / Inclusive Mobility on village roads).
- **Beware:**
  - PINS-6001260 allowed a 43 m carriageway walk on low speed/flow with accident data and an LHA objection withdrawn;
  - PINS-6011097 gave highway officer support substantial weight over anecdotal objections.
  - Objections need measured speeds, geometry and a reasoned LHA-independent case, not assertion.
