# Principle of development outside the Green Belt, and the "substantially outweighed" balance (NPPF Aug 2026)

Analyst: analyst-principle. Date: 2026-09-23. Scope: S3–S6, the Annex B "settlement" definition, the S5(1) categories, S5(2)–(4), HO10, HO11, TR3 outside the Green Belt, the S4/S5 balance, and the weighting of housing, self-build, BNG and missing-obligation points. Green Belt (GB6–GB8, S5(5)) is left to `green-belt.md`.

**Base.** 184 cases carry an S5, S6, HO10 or HO11 finding. About 140 of them are outside the Green Belt. I read every case cited below, and checked the quotes against `data/open-sources/pins-corpus/<ref>.txt`. There is **no SoS or court authority yet**. Everything below is inspector (mostly written representations) or council practice.

Count conventions: "n x/y" means x supporting cases out of y cases where the issue arose. These are inspector cases outside the Green Belt unless marked.

---

## 1. Headline propositions

**P1. Outside a settlement, the S5(1) category is the whole case. Inside a category it is "approve unless substantially outweighed"; outside every category it is S5(4), "refuse unless the benefits substantially outweigh", and S5(4) almost never passes.** *Strong.*
- S5(4): **n 37 fail / 3 pass** at appeal (recounted 2 Oct 2026 from case frontmatter).
  - The passes: PINS-6011253 (Cople), with no TR3 harm, a PIP range of 1–9, a self-build deficit and a 3.35-year supply; PINS-6011694 (Honeysuckle Bottom), with an active HGV sawmill as fallback, PDL and 20% BNG; and PINS-6008253 (Aston Clinton, hearing, 66 homes), where a "substantial" supply shortfall, 25% affordable housing and the council's emerging strategic allocation outweighed moderate character harm (DL ¶82, ¶85). The Honeysuckle Bottom letter is flawed: the site is in the Green Belt but S5(5) was ignored, and S5 is miscited as "S3". Aston Clinton is the only pass carried mainly by supply, so supply rarely, not never, decides.
  - Councils: 1 pass (wychavon-W-26-00329-FUL, on a Class Q fallback) and 2 fails.
- Best quote. PINS-6012985 ¶18: "Part 4 of S5 says that proposals that do not fall into such categories should only be approved in exceptional circumstances. The benefits of a proposal must substantially outweigh the adverse effects including promoting sustainable patterns of movement. I attach substantial weight to the harm identified in respect of patterns of movement." ¶22: "the benefits of the proposal do not substantially outweigh the adverse effects". That was with no five-year supply and HO7 at substantial weight.
- Other S5(4) failures, all at a 1.5–4.8-year supply or with no 5YHLS:
  - PINS-6009838 (¶44-49)
  - PINS-6010442 (¶20-21)
  - PINS-6006900
  - PINS-6008115 (1.53 years, HDT 48%)
  - PINS-6008634 (2.63 years, HDT 35%)
  - PINS-6011150
  - PINS-6004691 (3.1 years, HDT 60%)
- Counter-examples: the two passes above. Both turned on the *absence* of transport harm (Cople) or a fallback that cancelled it (Honeysuckle Bottom).

- Scope: 26 appeal cases outside the Green Belt found (j) met. **11 were allowed:**

  - PINS-6005970

  - PINS-6008548
- **The 15 dismissals, by reason:**
  - **Protected landscape, heritage or World Heritage Site (6):** PINS-6003055, -6005903, -6006475, -6006893, -6007416, -6008569.
  - **Evidence or obligations (4):**
    - PINS-6004496: the UU was drafted on the wrong land;
    - PINS-6010616: species surveys missing;
    - PINS-6010848: SAC s106 not executed;
    - PINS-6009588: a £304 GIRAMS payment not made (also DP3(3)).
  - **DP3(3) via S5(2) (2):** PINS-6008785, -6010826.
  - **S6 (2):** PINS-6007431, -6009220.
  - **TR6(4) (1):** PINS-6009618.
- Countryside encroachment wins only when it can be framed as DP3(3) "without clear justification". In PINS-6008785 ¶39 the (j) pass came with an encroachment harm: "National decision-making policy DP3(3) advises that development proposals should be refused if, without clear justification, they conflict with DP3(1)".
- Plain spatial-strategy conflict is cut to limited or very limited weight under Annex A(2): PINS-6005809 ¶74-76, PINS-6001260 ¶28, PINS-6004385.
- Councils: 11 of 12 (j) passes were approved. The exception is westnorthants-WNS-2022-0673-MAF, refused on five harms including missing HRA evidence.

**P3. Supply opens (j); "physically well-related" closes it. That limb is a physical-form test, but inspectors keep importing access, character and settlement status into it.** *Strong on outcome, contested on method.*
- Scope: (j) failed in about 26 appeals. **All but 3 failed on "well-related"** (PINS-6007348 and PINS-6009303 failed on need; PINS-6010393 was decided in the alternative on DP3).
- Four readings are in use. The divergence is likely to be litigated.

| Reading | Cases | Key line |
|---|---|---|
| Physical relationship to built development generally; no adjoining or footway requirement | PINS-6001260 ¶24-25; PINS-6005328 (Corbett: a road between is not a break); PINS-6005970 | "I do not read Policy S5 … as requiring a site to adjoin the principal built form of a particular settlement or to be served by a continuous segregated footway" (6001260 ¶24) |
| Physical connectivity, **not** landscape or visual | PINS-6006893 ¶69 | "In physical connectivity, rather than landscape and visual terms, it would be sufficiently well related" |
| Settlement form and contiguity: gaps, separate access, being "set away from" a visually contained edge | PINS-6009838 ¶15; PINS-6004691 ¶15; PINS-6007403 (spill over a hedged ridge); PINS-6012985 (verdant gaps plus hill) | "regardless of whether it would be of a scale which could be accommodated … it would not be physically well-related" (6009838 ¶15) |
| Access or accessibility read in: unlit lane, bypass crossing, or services folded into the "infrastructure" sub-limb | PINS-6006900 ¶15-17; PINS-6010442; PINS-6010834; PINS-6011872 ¶26; PINS-6009303; PINS-6006819; PINS-6007158 ¶44; PINS-6007352 ¶27 | "its need for access to services and facilities could not be accommodated by available infrastructure" (6011872 ¶26) |
| Character and setting read in (two hearings, late September) | PINS-6007133 ¶40; PINS-6008253 ¶80; PINS-6010422 ¶42 (via the "nature of the development" proviso) | compliance is "not just the site's own spatial relationship to the settlement … but also how the proposal itself would relate to the surrounding character" (6007133 ¶40) |

- Counter-point: the same inspector (PINS-6005809 ¶56) used "spatially, functionally and visually" three days after writing "physical connectivity only" at Whitchurch.
- The physical reading has inquiry support: a trunk road in cutting is "a soft connection bridging the settlement … rather than being perceived as being a hard boundary" (PINS-6008238 ¶22). The character reading now has two hearings (above), so the heading's "physical-form test" is one reading among several, not settled.

**P4. "Evidenced unmet need" means any 5YHLS shortfall, however marginal. The list is also open-ended, so thin compliant supply plus affordable need, or a self-build deficit, can qualify.** *Strong for a shortfall; emerging beyond it.*
- Marginal shortfalls counted:
  - 4.9 years (PINS-6005108);
  - 4.77 (PINS-6008739);
  - 4.8 (PINS-6006900);
  - 4.61 (PINS-6008785, -6009618, -6010166);
  - 4.1 (maldon-26-00066-OUTM).
- PINS-6005809 ¶57 (5.34 claimed, about 88 homes of headroom, HDT 177–193%): "'including, but not limited to', indicating that the absence of a five year deliverable supply and HDT outcomes are not the only means by which an unmet need can be proven to exist"; ¶60 adds a "very marginal" supply plus 2,904 on the housing register.
- Self-build deficit as the unmet need where 5YHLS is shown: PINS-6011872 ¶19-22 (456 plots). It still failed on location.
- Counter-examples. With a supply shown and no other evidenced need, (j) is closed:
  - PINS-6007348 (5.31 years);
  - wychavon-W-26-01322-OUT (council: "none of the types of development set out within policy S5 are applicable").
  - PINS-6009303: an unenforceable self-build UU means no self-build need counts.

**P5. "Should be refused" policies decide the "substantially outweighed" balance. A trigger beats any stack of substantial-weight benefits; with no trigger, local-plan-only harms rarely do.** *Strong.*
- Coded S4(2)(c)/S5(2) failures: **n 36, all refused or dismissed**. Trigger families:

| Trigger | Count | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| DP3(3) | 17 | 6008785, 6011648, 6010826, 6009211, 6011373, 6008167 |
| TR6(4) | 8 | 6008848, 6011256, 6009228, 6006846 |
| F5, F6, F7 or F9 | 7 | 6009598, 6006629, 6010682, 6008500, 6008881; stratford-26-01376-FUL |
| N6 / Habitats Regulations | not coded as S5(2), but it operates the same way | 6010848 ¶34-35, 6009588, 6007128 |

- PINS-6010836 was dismissed although HO7, E2 *and* TC2 each carried substantial weight, because DP3 failed with "no clear overriding justification".
- The other way round (no trigger, so allowed despite plan conflict):
  - PINS-6010195 ¶26: an undersized bedroom "does not represent specific conflict against the national decision-making policies in the Framework";
  - PINS-6011148 ¶24: "I have not concluded that there would be any substantial adverse effects when assessed against the national decision-making policies";
  - PINS-6010909;
  - PINS-6010397;
  - PINS-6008643, where parking was amenity only and below TR6(4).
- Contrary line: PINS-6009545 had low-level heritage harm "substantially" outweigh substantial housing weight with no trigger and no explanation. PINS-6007161 and PINS-6007128 reached "substantially outweighed" on L2(1)(d) and DP3(1) inside S4(1), without naming S4(2).
- Soft spots in the trigger list:
  - F5 says "should not be located", not "should be refused", yet it is used as a trigger (PINS-6010682, -6008500).
  - F9 says "should not take place" (PINS-6008881).
  - PINS-6010682 used S5(2) to skip S5(4) altogether.

**P6. DP3(3) is the countryside refusal machine. Under its "explicit design standards" limb, local standards (space, communal space, design codes) come back as Framework-level harms.** *Strong for encroachment; the local-standards point is contested.*
- Encroachment and edge cases:
  - PINS-6008785 (a lane marking the village edge);
  - PINS-6009588 ("reads with the fields");
  - PINS-6004691;
  - PINS-6008739 ¶16-21;
  - PINS-6011150;
  - council: wychavon-W-26-01322-OUT ("unwarranted and conspicuous encroachment").
- Local standards through DP3(3):
  - PINS-6009127 ¶19: "DP3(3) states that development proposals should be refused if, without clear justification, they conflict with any explicit design standards set out in the development plan … I therefore apply substantial weight";
  - PINS-6011088 ¶22 (HMO communal space).
- The literal text of DP3(3) supports this reading ("explicit design standards set out in the development plan"). PINS-6010195 did not engage DP3(3) at all.
- "Well-related" for (j) and "integrates with surroundings" for DP3 are treated as different tests, so a site can pass one and fail the other (PINS-6009588, -6008785).

**P7. Settlement status (S4 or S5) is decided on the ground under Annex B, and the answer is inconsistent at the margins.** *Emerging.*
- Pulled inside S4:
  - PINS-6008314 ¶102-103 (Polegate Aldi): "it is clear from the use of 'includes' that being within an area defined as settlement in the development plan is not a pre-requisite"; built-up on three sides plus a consented estate on the fourth.
  - PINS-6010393 ¶19: a rear garden "likely" inside.
- Held outside:
  - PINS-6009618 ¶32: development on three sides but still outside. This is in direct tension with Polegate.
  - PINS-6009588 ¶23: the village is a settlement but the plot beyond the last house is not.
  - PINS-6010228: an undeveloped plot is not "predominantly built-up".
  - PINS-6010826: outside plan-defined village confines.
- Not a settlement at all:
  - PINS-6011150 ¶10-11 (Chavel: a ribbon plus petrol station, "prevailing sense of openness");
  - PINS-6010834 (Coryates);
  - PINS-6009632 (a hamlet);
  - PINS-6009593 ¶13;
  - wychavon-W-26-00329-FUL.
- The plan-defined limb helps: PINS-6010973 ¶12 (a Category 2 village is a settlement because the plan says so).
- Split sites (S3(2)), with S4 applied to the access and S5 to the rest: maldon-26-00066-OUTM, stratford-25-00346-OUT.
- SDC treats Green Belt washed-over villages as settlements, contrary to Annex B (stratford-lpa pattern 6; see `green-belt.md`).

**P8. S5(1)(e) "limited infilling within groups of houses" is the one small-site route that needs no shortfall and no settlement. It is read strictly on physical form.** *Strong.*
- **n 4 pass / 12 fail.**
- Passes:
  - PINS-6009593 ¶15-16: a plot "sandwiched between existing dwellings"; "Policy S5 does not require that proposals for redevelopment of PDL and/or limited infilling … are physically well-related to a settlement, since that requirement only appears within parts 1h and 1j".
  - PINS-6010973: passed with a 5YHLS and a new plan.
  - PINS-6009632: a non-linear group, counting houses under construction.
  - PINS-6007348: passed (e), then lost on TR3 (Connectivity Tool 13/100, supply 5.31 years).
- Fails:
  - one neighbour only or end of row (PINS-6008739 ¶8-10, -6006900 ¶26, -6012985 ¶10);
  - extending rather than being "within" the group (PINS-6010442 ¶20);
  - the group includes caravans or light industry, or the neighbour is a mobile home (PINS-6010354 ¶20-21);
  - a field running on into farmland (PINS-6009618);
  - consented but unbuilt houses disregarded (PINS-6008264 ¶9-10; contrast 6009632, where houses under construction counted).
- Council: wychavon-W-26-01828-PIP passed (e) despite failing TR3. (e) carries no location test.

**P9. (c) re-use, extension or replacement and (d) PDL let schemes into S5(1) easily, but S5(3) isolation, "same use" and proportionality close the housing loopholes, and TR6(4) visibility kills the rest.** *Strong.*
- (c) passes: **n 11 at appeal, 3 allowed** (PINS-6009486, -6009367, -6011694). The other 8 lost on:
  - TR6(4) splays or access (6008848, 6011256, 6010542);
  - F-policies (6009598, 6008881);
  - DP3(3) (6011648);
  - heritage (6011314);
  - S5(3) (6008115).
- Rules applied:
  - A replacement must be for the **same use**, so no barn or stable to house under (c) (PINS-6010348 ¶27, -6009409 ¶18, -6010166 ¶19).
  - Proportionality is judged "irrespective of the size of the host plot" (PINS-6011045 ¶25).
  - An unbuilt permission is not an "existing building" (PINS-6005119).
  - Under fn25 the baseline is the building as at 17 Aug 2026 (PINS-6011648: 22.4%, not 70.6%).
  - (c) covers any lawful building "irrespective of intended use" (PINS-6008848 ¶14-16), including a house becoming a children's home (PINS-6009486 ¶24).
- (d) rules:
  - Agricultural buildings are not PDL (PINS-6007348, -6009181, -6009303).
  - Partial PDL does not qualify (PINS-6006950 ¶30, -6009409).
  - Temporary DCO compounds are not PDL (PINS-6006832).
  - A **rural garden is PDL** because the Annex B garden exclusion applies only "in built-up areas" (PINS-6011217 ¶17; councils stratford-26-01558-FUL, 3.4× replacement approved; maidstone-26-501191-FULL, where officers accepted it and members rejected it).
  - (d) "contains no restrictions relating to the scale" (stratford-26-01558-FUL). This is a proportionality bypass to watch.
- S5(3) sits above (c)/(d): see P10.

**P10. S5(3) isolation is spatial ("not within a group"), overrides (c)/(d), and routes to HO11. HO11(1)(c) needs the building to be redundant or disused *and* the scheme to enhance its setting.** *Strong.*
- Isolation:
  - PINS-6009718 ¶60-61: "close to, rather than within, the group of houses … irrespective of whether the proposal would involve the redevelopment of previously developed land".
  - PINS-6008115 ¶26-29: (c) met, but the barn was in storage use, so not disused, and the domestic conversion did not enhance. Dismissed at 1.53 years.
  - PINS-6011694 ¶26-27: the barn conversion met HO11(c); the two new-builds did not.
  - PINS-6009042 ¶20: a group of one other house is still isolated.
  - PINS-6008634 ¶10: a holiday let with no marketing is not "redundant".
- Not isolated:
  - PINS-6011431 ¶7-8: next to a health hub, pub and houses; a plan text treating everything outside the boundary as isolated was not followed;
  - PINS-6006289;
  - PINS-6008500;
  - PINS-6011045.
- HO11(1)(a) rural workers turn on evidence of essential need:
  - Fails: PINS-6010187, where CCTV, netting and seasonal cover had not been tried; PINS-6007601; PINS-6008895, where a flat on the holding was available.
  - Pass: PINS-6006151 (32,000 hens, response times quantified).
  - Councils: all passed with independent appraisal (warwick-W-26-0134, warwick-W-25-0302, lichfield-26-00849-FUL).
- A shortfall does not rescue an isolated home. PINS-6010187 ¶30 (1-year supply): being agreed isolated means not "well-related".

**P11. S5(1)(b) "shown to be necessary" requires a positive, site-specific case. A general tourism or economic benefit is not enough; an established adjacent business is.** *Strong.*
- **n 3 pass / 6 fail.**
- PINS-6000903 ¶9: "does not identify why this location, rather than a site within or better related to a settlement, is necessary".
- Other fails: PINS-6008804, -6009443, -6009817 (cross-subsidy is not diversification), -6010228, -6006832.
- Pass: PINS-6014952 ¶8, 11 (expansion of an established storage business "a logical consequence of that expansion"). E4's acceptance of poor public transport rescued none of the fails (PINS-6008804, -6010729).

**P12. TR3 outside the Green Belt turns on route quality, not distance. The mitigations below are routinely rejected: bus stops without timetables, "short car trips", EVs, homeworking, field PROWs. Small schemes can escape TR3(1)(a) but still carry car-reliance harm.** *Strong.* Tables in §2.
- PINS-6011431 ¶13: "I have no substantive evidence relating to the timetables, or frequency … I cannot be certain that public transport would be a realistic alternative".
- ¶16: "relatively short car journeys … does not negate the fact that future occupiers would remain heavily dependent on the private car".
- ¶17: "Electric Vehicles and the growth in home working are not persuasive factors".
- Scale threshold. TR3(1)(a) applies to proposals that "could generate a significant amount of movement". For one or two dwellings it is "not relevant" (PINS-6008739 ¶37; PINS-6008848 ¶19), yet car reliance still "weighs heavily" under the plan-led spatial strategy (6008739 ¶37).
- Small scale has also downgraded car dependence to moderate (PINS-6005970 ¶30, -6009593). Counter: PINS-6012985 ¶18 gave *substantial* weight to movement harm for one dwelling; PINS-6010642 ¶27 *significant* weight for one; PINS-6010422 ¶49 found TR3(1)(a) not engaged for one home yet gave "moderate negative weight". Car dependence has also been routed through DP3(2)(d) and DP3(3) as a trigger for one home (PINS-6007677 ¶27).
- Counter-example on "short trips": PINS-6009632 ¶7 accepted "the length of car journeys … would be relatively short", together with weekday buses within a reasonable walk on a 30 mph lane. SDC reports also use it (stratford-lpa pattern 4).

**P13. Using benefit-side or plan-making Framework policies as harms is contested, and the majority view is "not permissible".** *Emerging split.*
- Against, with Framework intro ¶8 ("the plan-making policies should not be used when making decisions on development proposals"):
  - PINS-6011521 ¶28 (HO1 "relates to plan-making and not decision making policies");
  - PINS-6009486 ¶18;
  - PINS-6008410 ¶27;
  - PINS-6010909 ¶15 (absence of need as a "substantial negative" under HO7 "is not explicitly stated in the Framework");
  - PINS-6008643 ¶14 (HO1 family-housing harm needs evidence of a local shortage).
- For:
  - PINS-6011088 ¶21 (HO7 "I afford the loss of family housing substantial weight");
  - PINS-6010946 ¶30 (HO1 as the harm);
  - PINS-6006078 ¶23 ("absence of a demonstrated overriding locational and business need" counted as an adverse effect for an S5(1)(a) agricultural barn).
- Council misuse: the westnorthants-WNS-2022-0673-MAF notice cites L1, a plan-making policy, as a refusal reason.
- Direction of travel: HO7 and HO1 as harms need specific local evidence, and a plan-making hook is vulnerable.

**P14. Housing weight scales with units, tenure fit and delivery certainty, not with the supply figure. HO7's "substantial" is routinely cut for single, uncertain or mismatched homes.** *Strong.* Table in §2.
- PINS-6009618 ¶28: at 4.61 years, "little weight … to the contribution that 3 houses would make".
- PINS-6005664 ¶52: substantial weight regardless of improving supply, "because it is a national requirement for Local Planning Authorities to have a 5YHLS".
- PINS-6011786 ¶29: "even a single dwelling carries considerable positive weight".
- Cut for:
  - a PIP "up to 3" (PINS-6011431 ¶23, limited);
  - homes not matching local need (PINS-6010196; -6009409);
  - a more sensitive scheme that could deliver the same home (PINS-6008115).

**P15. S6 protects a neighbourhood plan only if it is ≤5 years old at the decision date *and* has allocations. Past five years, it gives no shield and its policies are ordinary, often very-limited-weight conflicts.** *Strong.*
- Applied:
  - PINS-6007431 ¶21-23 (NP made April 2022): "no reason … to conclude that the Neighbourhood Plan does not continue to make provision … I therefore give substantial weight to the conflict". Slow allocations did not matter without evidence that they cannot be delivered.
  - PINS-6009220 ¶14. The letter calls it the "Sheepy Parish Neighbourhood Plan 2019" (¶7) yet finds it within five years; unresolved.
- Lost at five years:
  - PINS-6007104 ¶29-34 (made 23 June 2021; decided 24 Aug 2026; S6(a) fails although S6(b) is met, and an NP requirement set before the standard method is not "a credible indicator");
  - PINS-6010848 (2016 plan);
  - councils: eastdevon-26-0534-MOUT (2018 plan), westnorthants-WNS-2022-0673-MAF (2017 plan, "very limited weight"), stratford-26-00772-PIP (2017).
- In-date S6 is used as a positive: stratford-26-01906-PIP.

**P16. Missing obligations and evidence are free-standing, fatal and not curable by condition.** *Strong.* Cases lost that would otherwise have won:
- SAC/SPA mitigation s106 not executed (PINS-6010848 ¶34-35: "The requirements of the Habitats Regulations are determinative");
- £304 GIRAMS unpaid, with DM6 barring a condition requiring payment (PINS-6009588);
- SAMM on a form only (PINS-6007128);
- no metric under DMPO Art 7(1A) (PINS-6011375);
- self-build UU on the wrong land, so no BNG exemption (PINS-6004496);
- species surveys out of season (PINS-6010616);
- no third-party visibility agreement (PINS-6008848).
- A PIP workaround is accepted: SAC tariff paid under a s111 LGA 1972 undertaking (PINS-6005328, -6007431).
- Complete paperwork wins: PINS-6011786 (SPA UU plus deed of variation for self-build).

**P17. Self-build and BNG after the 6 Aug 2026 regulations.** *Strong on the rule, split on PIPs.*
- The 0.2 ha small-site BNG exemption applies only to applications made on or after 6 Aug 2026 (PINS-6009042 ¶34; PINS-6010834; corpus-rest pattern PINS-6009460, -6008441).
- Self-build status must be secured by s106 or UU to count or to claim the exemption: PINS-6006289, -6010834, -6009303 (UU unsigned), -6004496 (wrong land).
- Caravans are not "buildings", so no self-build exemption (PINS-6007474).
- Self-build weight:
  - moderate where secured but uncertain (PINS-6011872 ¶21, marketing for one year only);
  - reduced where the council over-supplies plots (PINS-6006289);
  - an open-market fallback in the UU cut weight to moderate (PINS-6008569).
- Can a PIP secure self-build? No: PINS-6006900, -6012985 ¶20, -6011217 ¶5. Cople PINS-6011253 was more generous (per the appeals-nongb log).

**P18. Fallbacks swing S5(4) and neutralise car dependence, which moves the fight to character.** *Strong.*
- Won on a fallback:
  - wychavon-W-26-00329-FUL (Class Q);
  - PINS-6008264 (extant PIP for 3; allowed despite failing (e) and TR3);
  - PINS-6008548 (extant self-build permission);
  - PINS-6011694 (sawmill).
- Lost because the fallback was less harmful than the proposal: PINS-6010354, -6010401, -6010166, -6009181, -6010348.

**P19. HO10 exception sites must be 100% affordable under the Annex B definition. Self-build plots are not affordable without valuation, build-cost and eligibility evidence.** *Single case.*
- PINS-6006581 ¶73 (14 homes, 57% social rent plus 6 self-build plots) failed HO10. It passed S5(1)(h) (Haslemere station, "well-connected" agreed) and still lost on N4 at 1.28 years.
- No HO10 approval is in the dataset.

## 2. Fact thresholds

### 2a. S5(1)(j)(i) "physically well-related"

| Pass | Fail |
|---|---|
| Adjoins established housing, even not the named village (PINS-6001260: 43 m of carriageway walking at 30 mph, low flows, no injury accidents) | ~400–500 m across open fields (PINS-6009181); ~700 m across fields or a golf course (PINS-6009985) |

## 3. Test sequences as actually applied

```
1. Green Belt or LGS?  → yes: GB6/GB7/HC8, then the S5(5) default (green-belt.md)
2. Settlement? (Annex B, on the ground + plan-defined limb; S3(2) splits mixed sites)
      inside → S4(1). Look for S4(2) triggers: (a)(i) allocation loss; (a)(ii) HC7/HC8/N6/N4/L2(1)(d);
               (b) cemetery/flood storage; (c) any "should be refused" policy
      outside → 3
3. S5(3): isolated home (not within a settlement or group of houses)? → HO11(1)(a)–(e) or fail
      (applied before or over (c)/(d): 6009718, 6008115)
4. S5(1) category?  (a) land uses  (b) necessity shown  (c) same use, lawful, not disproportionate (fn25)
      (d) whole-site PDL (rural gardens count)  (e) within a group, houses both sides
      (f) HO10 / CRtBO / NDO  (g) evidenced traveller need  (h) well-connected station ≤800 m
      (i) allocation  (j) evidenced need + well-related + scale/infrastructure
   none → S5(4): benefits must substantially outweigh harm, incl. character and movement → usually fail
5. In a category → S5(2) trigger check (DP3(3), TR6(4), F5–F7/F9, N6, L3(4), HE6(5)…) → any fail = refuse
6. Else, "substantially outweighed" balance: HO7/HO8/E2/HC4/CC2(2) benefits v adverse effects
   "assessed against the national decision-making policies"; plan conflict weighted via Annex A(2)
7. S6 overlay (housing): NP ≤5 yrs at decision date + allocations → conflict "likely" decisive
8. Paperwork gate at any point: HRA/SAC obligation, BNG metric/exemption, self-build UU, third-party land
```

**Where decision-makers diverge or go wrong:**
- Running S5(1)(j) with the old "significantly and demonstrably" wording: PINS-6007104 ¶48, -6007466 ¶30, -6009042 ¶41, -6010619 ¶56, -6011694 ¶37.
- Running the S5(1) "substantially outweighed" framing after finding the scheme **outside** every category, instead of S5(4): PINS-6008742. Inverting S4/S5 (appeals-nongb pattern 12).
- Skipping S5(4) by using S5(2): PINS-6010682.
- Running heritage as a free-standing HE6(4) test first or folding it into S4, with opposite results on similar facts: stratford-26-01399-PIP (refused) against stratford-26-01906-PIP (approved).
- Not considering an obvious category:
  - (e) not argued for a garden between two dwellings (PINS-6010834);
  - (c)/(d) not considered for a lawful light-industrial shed (PINS-6006123);
  - (c) and HO11(1)(d) not considered (PINS-6006388);
  - (j) not tested at 2.4 years (PINS-6011431).
- Councils:
  - a plain "outweigh" balance where (d) was met (malvern-M-26-01131-FUL);
  - S5(1)(j) cited on a Green Belt site (bromsgrove-26-00845-PIP);
  - a plan-making policy (L1) cited as a refusal reason (westnorthants-WNS-2022-0673-MAF).
- Mislabelling S5(1)(j) as (h), or (j)(ii) as a sub-limb of (i): PINS-6005664 ¶45, -6006289 ¶37, -6010826 ¶23.

## 5. Distillation notes (for a future agent reading a new decision)

**Capture every time:**
- Settlement finding, and **how** it was reached (plan boundary, on the ground, allocation or permission limb, hamlet exclusion), with the ¶. Note split sites under S3(2).
- Which S5(1) category was argued, and which the decision-maker found, limb by limb. For (j), record separately:
  - (i) need source: 5YHLS figure, HDT %, self-build deficit or other evidence;
  - the "well-related" reading used (physical, visual, accessibility or form);
  - scale and infrastructure.
- Whether S5(3) was engaged, and the HO11 limb.
- Which balance was run: S5(1) "substantially outweighed" or S5(4) reversed. Quote the conclusion ¶ verbatim, because old "significantly and demonstrably" wording is common.
- Any "should be refused" trigger, **named by its code** (DP3(3), TR6(4), F6, F7, N6…), and whether it was routed through S4(2)(c) or S5(2).
- The weight word for each benefit (HO7, HO8, E2, HC4, L2, CC2(2)), and whether HO7 was named or merely paraphrased.
- TR3 facts in numbers: distances, footway and lighting, speed limit, bus frequency, Connectivity Tool score, whether timetable evidence was produced.
- Paperwork: HRA/SAC mitigation status, BNG metric or exemption and application date relative to 6 Aug 2026, self-build securing mechanism, third-party land for splays.
- Fallbacks (Class Q, extant PIP, lawful use) and the weight given.
- S6: NP made date, the decision date, and whether it has allocations.

**Tags to reuse:**
- `s5-1-j`, `s5-4-exceptional`, `isolated-home`, `infill-rejected`;
- `dp3-refuse-trigger`, `substantially-outweighed`;
- `sustainable-location-fail`, `rural-lane-no-footway`;
- `s6-neighbourhood-plan`, `fallback`, `old-wording-slip`, `drafting-slip`, `housing-shortfall`.

**Traps:**
- Letters mislabel the limbs: "S3(3)" and "Part 4 of S3" for S5 (6011694); (j) called "(h)" (6006289, 6005664); "SD5"/"H07" (6008569); "T3" (6006123, 6006388); "S5(c)" (6009367); "(j)(ii)" for (j)(i) (6010826). Code what the words mean and note the slip.
- Plan-making policies (HO1, HO5, L1, TR1) cited as decision tests. Code them, but flag them: Framework intro ¶8 bars their use in decisions.
- Tier-2 files sometimes code TR3 where the letter applied only a local policy (6006819, 6010401). Check the `note` field.
- S5 codes on Green Belt sites (6011694, 6007334, 6011301): check `green_belt` and S5(5).
- Transitional letters (17–24 Aug) that apply 2024 ¶11(d) (6007179). Do not count them as S5 authority.

**Schema and tag proposals (not applied):**
- Add `settlement_finding: inside | outside | split | not-a-settlement` and `settlement_basis: plan-boundary | on-ground | allocation-limb | hamlet-excluded`.
- Add `s5_category_found` (a–j or none) and `balance_run: s4-1 | s5-1 | s5-4 | s5-5 | gb6-vsc | plan-led`.
- Add `refusal_trigger: [DP3(3), TR6(4) …]` as a structured list rather than prose.
- Add `j_wellrelated_reading: physical | visual | accessibility | form`.
- Add `tr3_facts` sub-fields: `walk_m`, `footway`, `lit`, `speed_mph`, `bus_per_hour`, `connectivity_tool`.
- Add `housing_weight` (the word actually used) alongside `units`, so weight-by-scale can be computed.
- Add `obligation_failure: hra | bng | self-build | third-party-land | s106-other`.
- Tags: `tr3-1a-small-scale`, `pdl-rural-garden`, `plan-making-policy-as-harm`, `s5-2-used-to-skip-s5-4`.

## 6. Gaps and open questions

- **No SoS, High Court or inquiry-level S5(1)(j) authority beyond a few inspectors.** The four readings of "physically well-related" (P3) are unresolved. Watch for s288 challenges from late September 2026, and harvest them first.
- **S5(1)(h) well-connected station:**
  - only PINS-6006581 passed (h) (agreed);
  - fails were decided on frequency (wychavon-W-26-01874-PIP) or distance (PINS-6011431, -6009474);
  - no reasoned appeal applies the Annex B timetable test. Target a harvest.
- **S5(1)(i) allocations outside settlements**: only one council case (stratford-25-01765-FUL). **S5(1)(f)/HO10**: one failed appeal, and no rural exception site or community-led approval yet. **HO11(1)(e)** exceptional design: none decided; there is a WNC lead (2025/2549/FULL Rathvilly Farm).
- **Majors outside settlements are thin**: only 8 appeals outside the Green Belt with 20+ units carry an S5 finding (5 allowed; 3 dismissed, all on protected landscape, heritage or design harm). Most data is 1–9-unit written representations.
- **Local standards through DP3(3) against the national-policies-only reading of S4(1)**: PINS-6009127 against PINS-6010195 needs more cases.
- **HO7 or HO1 as harms**: 3 for, 5 against. Needs more HMO and children's-home letters.
- **Not yet coded consistently:**
  - the TR3(1)(a) "significant amount of movement" threshold;
  - F5 used as a "should be refused" trigger when its wording is "should not be located".
- **Council committee data outside Stratford and the Midlands is sparse.** No council case refuses at S5(2) on DP3(3) alone yet (SDC cited DP3(3) at Snitterfield but did not use it).
- **Cross-check pending:** `analysis/green-belt.md` did not exist when this file was written. The TR3 thresholds in §2b should be reconciled with its GB7(1)(g)(iii) table. Point of likely difference: council GB7(1)(g)(iii) passes are more generous than the non-Green Belt TR3 appeal fails (e.g. SDC Tanworth, where unfooted village-core sections were accepted because "Villagers appear to cope"; not Snitterfield, where the Update Report corrected the route to made footpath throughout).
