# Adversarial review: "Service Village, Not Sustainable" (published page)

Reviewed 1 Oct 2026 against the live page at `planningdistilled.org/research/england/service-village/` (then at its earlier address), the register `appeals-review/settlement-tier-usage.tsv`, the letters in `open:pins-corpus/` and the August 2026 NPPF text. Brief: challenge the finding; find any evidence that, under the 2026 NPPF, a settlement tier has been accepted as a valid argument for sustainable location. Every quotation below was machine-checked against the letter (27 of 27 matched).

## Verdict

The page's narrow claim survives: no inspector in the dataset allowed a scheme where the tier was the *only* reasoning on location. Its broad claims do not. The tier has been accepted as part of a sustainable-location finding in at least eight allowed appeals under the 2026 Framework, in one of them to overcome admitted poor services and poor public transport. The page is right that the rural allowance has gone from the text (TR3(1)(e) is an improvement duty), but one Green Belt appeal was still allowed on the deleted sentence, so the allowance has to be met as an argument even though it is no longer policy.

## 1. Claims on the page that the evidence contradicts

| Page wording | Problem |
|---|---|
| "the tier plays no part in it" (the route test) | Inspectors have used the tier inside TR3 / GB7(1)(g)(iii) reasoning in allowed appeals (§2). |
| "A village's tier is not evidence about that journey" | Chaldon 6006497 ¶44 used Category 2 settlements' Connectivity Tool scores as the benchmark for a sustainable location. |
| "On the question that matters most, whether this site is sustainable, the hierarchy counts for nothing" | Broadwas 6010973 ¶17 used the tier to answer the council's unsustainable-location case. |
| "That rural allowance sentence is gone" | True of the text. But one Green Belt appeal was allowed on the deleted sentence quoted as current, and a few inspectors gloss TR3(1)(e) as recognising rural constraints (§3). |
| "No inspector in the dataset allowed a scheme on the settlement tier" (banner body) | True only with "alone". Henfield 6007104 and Aston Clinton 6008253 were allowed with no route-quality analysis beyond the tier and proximity, which is the standard the page uses to code Kineton as A2. |
| 6010848 Pitstone listed under A2 ("no route analysis") | ¶20 is a route analysis (footways, lighting, topography, buses) and ¶21 finds accordance with TR3. It belongs in A1. |

## 2. Allowed appeals where the tier was part of the sustainable-location reasoning

All under the 2026 Framework. Letters in `pins-corpus/<ref>.txt`.

- **Broadwas 6010973** (Malvern Hills, 11 Sep 2026, one dwelling, S5(1)(e) infill, allowed). The closest analogue to Claverdon's profile. ¶16: "I acknowledge that there are limited services and facilities in the settlement" and "I accept that there is limited public transport and that this might not offer convenient access to employment." ¶17: "Nevertheless, Broadwas is defined in the SWDPR 2026 as a category 2 settlement. It is a settlement where Policy SWDPR 03 would support new housing". ¶18: "The reliance on the private car for access to other facilities and employment would be no different for the residents of the appeal site and the residents of existing housing in the village". ¶20: "I find that the appeal site is in a suitable and sustainable location for the scale of the development". The tier was the pivot from admitted weakness to a pass. Distinguishing point: Category 2 in that plan requires two key services and a daily bus. Claverdon scored 0 for public transport.
- **Aston Clinton 6008253** (Buckinghamshire, 24 Sep 2026, up to 66 dwellings, hearing, allowed). ¶32: "Aston Clinton is defined within Policy S3 as a larger village which has reasonable access to facilities and services and public transport making it a sustainable location for development." Location was common ground (¶57 "the appeal site is in an agreed accessible location"). The tier is the inspector's only articulated reason on location.
- **Henfield 6007104** (Horsham, 24 Aug 2026, 5 dwellings, allowed). ¶10 is the whole location finding: "Henfield is a 'tier 2 settlement' … These settlements offer a good range of services and facilities, strong community networks, local employment provision, and reasonable public transport services. The site is in an accessible location, close to High Street services, the primary school, and bus stops." No footway, lighting or frequency facts. By the page's own Kineton standard this is the shorthand, and it was allowed by an inspector.
- **Biddulph Moor 6011103** (Staffordshire Moorlands, 7 Sep 2026, up to 9 dwellings, Green Belt, GB7(1)(g)(iii), allowed). ¶13 recites that "'Rural Area Larger Villages' are the most sustainable settlements in the rural areas", then ¶19 walks the route. Tier framed the test; the route confirmed it.
- **Thundersley 6007184** (Castle Point, 14 Sep 2026, major, GB7, allowed). ¶95: "Thundersley is identified to be one of the most sustainable settlements in the borough to accommodate growth." Location was agreed (¶38).
- **Chaldon 6006497** (Tandridge, 25 Sep 2026, 30 dwellings, inquiry, GB7(1)(g)(iii), allowed). Full route analysis, but ¶44 anchors the conclusion to the hierarchy: "the connectivity score is comparable to many areas within the settlement boundary of Caterham as well as several Category 2 settlements as defined in the TDCS including Woldingham (56), Godstone (55), Nutfield (52) and Smallfield (50) where certain types of development could be permitted." The tier set the bar for "sustainable".
- **Beckington APP-E3335-W-25-3375062** (Somerset, 18 Sep 2026, 20 dwellings, allowed with costs). Primary village; route facts given. Tier plus route.
- **Kingston Bagpuize 6005809** (Vale of White Horse, allowed, register code B). Larger Village; the council ran TR3(1)(a) against it; ¶50–53 found no conflict on a 20-minute bus and a 1.6 km school walk "comparable to existing dwellings in KBS". The tier's own description of the village framed the TR3 answer.

Dismissed on other grounds, but with the tier accepted on location: **Battle 6007272** ¶36 "Battle is one of the larger settlements in the district's hierarchy and is considered a sustainable location for housing development as a matter of principle", ¶59 "In the context of Policy TR3 of the Framework, the appeal site would be in a sustainable location"; **Braceborough 6007771** ¶20; **Great Witley 6010196** ¶13; **Pitstone 6010848** ¶17–21.

**Reframed count.** Of the twelve appeals the register itself codes A, the location finding went the appellant's way in all twelve. Against them stand the six C cases where the tier was noted and the route lost. Read as "does the tier argument work on location when an inspector engages with it", the dataset is 12 for, 6 against. The page's "0 allowed on the tier" counts outcomes, not location findings, and the three A2 appeals were lost on density, an unexecuted obligation and other matters, not on location.

## 3. The rural allowance has gone from the text, but not from every inspector

- **TR3(1)(e)**, August 2026 NPPF: "In rural areas, opportunities to improve walking, wheeling, cycling and public transport and enhance the connectivity of an area should be taken where they exist and can be supported by the development proposed." On its text this is a duty on the applicant to improve connectivity, not an allowance for car dependence (editor's point, 1 Oct 2026, and correct). **Trewarmett 6000903** (Cornwall, dismissed) applied it that way: TR3(1)(e) and E4(2) "both expect proposals to take opportunities to improve rural connectivity … and the appeal proposal has not been shown to do so". The allowance that survives in decisions comes from inspectors' habit and the old wording, not from (e):
- **Tarleton 6007484** (West Lancashire, 23 Sep 2026, PIP for 6–9 dwellings, Green Belt, GB7(1)(g)(iii), allowed). Does not cite (e) at all. ¶13 quotes the deleted 2024 wording as current: "the Framework recognises that opportunities to maximise sustainable transport solutions will vary between urban and rural areas and that this should be taken into account in decision-making." ¶14: "Future occupants would be likely to rely upon the private car for a proportion of journeys. However, that is not unusual in a rural context and does not, in itself, render the location unsustainable." ¶15: "recognising the rural context of the site, I am satisfied that it would be in a sustainable location for the purposes of Policy GB7(1)(g), having particular reference to Policy TR3." Same limb as Claverdon, passed on a rural allowance that the 2026 text does not contain. Open to challenge on that ground.
- **Holsworthy Beacon 6009632** (Torridge, allowed) ¶17: "Framework policy TR3 recognises that connectivity opportunities may differ in rural areas." A gloss on (e) that its words do not quite bear.
- **North Newton 6008548** (Somerset, allowed) ¶15: car reliance "is not unusual in rural areas".
- **Kilkhampton 6009443** (dismissed): "Policy TR3(1)(e) recognises the constraints on connectivity opportunities in rural areas." Hamerton 6008987 and Harpley 6010422 (both dismissed) recited (e)'s improvement wording and then used it to reduce the weight of the access harm. Same gloss; none of the three rescued a site.
- Counter-current, for balance: South Nutfield 6009966 ¶27 "Whilst some reliance on the private car is to be expected in a rural location" then found the reliance "especially pronounced"; Great Easton 6008739 ¶13 "Whilst limited sustainable transport options are characteristic of many rural areas, this does not justify further residential development in locations that are already heavily dependent on private vehicle travel".

## 4. Other points an applicant would take

- **TR3(1)(a) threshold.** The test bites on proposals "which could generate a significant amount of movement, in the context of the area". South Nutfield 6009966 ¶27 held five dwellings "would be expected to generate only a modest level of movement" and did not engage (a) "to the same degree"; Newchapel 6010313 ¶13 held five dwellings significant "in this context". For five houses at Claverdon the point is live either way, though GB7(1)(g)(iii) still requires a sustainable location "with particular reference to" TR3.
- **The 2016 Inspector's endorsement cuts both ways.** The page quotes ¶271 (the categorisation "effectively ranks them so that housing is directed to the largest settlements with facilities … that would, taken together, reduce the dependency of prospective residents on the private car"). An applicant will say the examining Inspector therefore found every LSV, Claverdon included, fit to take some housing on car-dependency grounds, and at ¶450 endorsed limited infilling within Green Belt LSVs. The answer is that this is plan accordance for sites inside the BUAB, and the Station Road site is outside it; the page should say so rather than leave the quote standing alone.
- **"Very limited weight" is overstated as a general rule.** Annex A(2) reduces weight only for parts of a policy materially inconsistent with a named national decision-making policy. In 43 register cases the tier counted against a site, often with full weight, and the plan-making role of a hierarchy is one S2(1)(a) and HO6(1)(b) ask for. The page's own §10 of the analysis note makes this point; the page does not.
- **Register regex gaps.** Labels the sweep did not catch include "principal settlement", "main settlement", "market town", "Rural Settlement" (as a defined tier, 6009632), "identified in the development plan as a village" (6007484) and bare "Tier 4" (6008548 was caught only via "larger villages"). None changes the headline, but the "every decision" claim should be qualified to "every decision matching the search".

## 5. What still holds, and the honest version of the headline

- No appeal in the dataset was allowed where the tier was the sole location reasoning *and* the village's transport was as thin as Claverdon's. The favourable cases had a daily or better bus (Broadwas, Chaldon, Kingston Bagpuize), lit footways (Biddulph Moor, Pitstone) or an urban setting (Thundersley, Battle).
- The C cases show the route can defeat the label where the route is bad: Findon, Chilworth, Welland, Bolney.
- The Earlswood and Kineton approvals remain the only pure-shorthand approvals, and both are the Council's.

Defensible wording: "Under the 2026 NPPF inspectors still cite a village's tier when finding a location sustainable, but in every allowed case the finding also rested on a daily bus, lit footways or an urban setting. Where the route was poor, the label did not save the site." That is weaker than "counts for nothing", and it is what the letters support.
