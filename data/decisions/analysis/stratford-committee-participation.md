# Stratford-on-Avon: speaking at Planning Committee, and what a ward member may say

Note of 9 October 2026. The rules that govern who may address Stratford-on-Avon District Council's (SDC) Planning Committee, what they may draw on, and when a councillor's advocacy becomes a conflict. Not legal advice.

Sources (saved 8 October 2026 from democracy.stratford.gov.uk, "The Constitution"):

- Committee Procedure Rules, Constitution Part 4(3): `sources:stratford-dc/SDC-Constitution-Part4-3-Committee-Procedure-Rules.pdf`, text `sources:stratford-dc/public-note/sdc-committee-procedure-rules.txt`.
- Members' Planning Code of Good Practice, Constitution Part 5(3), updated 15 May 2024: `sources:stratford-dc/SDC-Constitution-Part5-3-Members-Planning-Code.pdf`, text `sources:stratford-dc/public-note/sdc-members-planning-code.txt`.

## Who may speak, and the deadlines

| Who | Rule | Time | Register by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Public, applicant, supporters, objectors, parish or town council | Procedure Rules 11.5, 11.6 | 30 minutes in all, split equally between parish/town council, applicant and supporters, and objectors | 2 pm the last working day before the meeting; 2 pm two working days before if submitting documents, to committeemanagers@stratford-dc.gov.uk |
| Local ward member, a substitute they nominate, or the Group Leader | Procedure Rule 11.4 | up to five minutes | 2 pm the working day before; anything to be *shown* by 2 pm two working days before |
| Any councillor not on the committee, on "items of a particular local concern to his/her/their ward" | Procedure Rule 16.6 | may address the committee and "propose amendments to any relevant recommendations, but not vote" | |
| A councillor with a disclosable pecuniary interest, on their own application | Procedure Rules 11.2, 11.3 | may speak, must leave for the debate and vote | |

After a ward member speaks, committee members "may question the member or substitute member, through the Chair, on points of clarification and fact" (11.4). The Chair may invite anyone to speak again to clarify facts (11.7).

## What a ward member may draw on

Anything they have read or been told, including private correspondence from residents. The Planning Code's rules on "fettering discretion" protect the vote, not the speech:

- Section 6.7: a member will not have fettered their discretion through "Listening or receiving viewpoints from residents or other interested parties", through comments that do not amount to prejudging, or through "Seeking information through appropriate channels".
- Section 4.8: a member who has "represented your views or those of local electors and fettered your discretion" should "take the opportunity to exercise your separate speaking rights".
- Section 6.4: members should "Copy or pass on any lobbying correspondence you receive to the Head of Development at the earliest opportunity". A resident's email to a ward member can therefore reach the case file as a representation in its own right, which the report or Update Sheet then has to summarise.

The Code's bars are narrow: a disclosable pecuniary interest (section 3.3), or being a member, leader or representative of "an organisation whose primary purpose is to lobby to promote or oppose planning proposals" (7.1). The second removes a member from the committee's decision on that application; it does not remove the speaking right.

## When advocacy does become a conflict

Only for a member who also sits on the Planning Committee. Section 4.6: "Do not speak or vote on a proposal where you have fettered your discretion." Section 4.8: a committee member who speaks as advocate should tell the proper officer or Chair before the item, "Remove yourself from the member seating area for the duration of that item", and "Ensure that your actions are recorded". Section 25 of the Localism Act 2011, cited at 4.1, means that having expressed a view earlier does not by itself close a member's mind; the test is whether they appear to have decided how to vote before hearing the officer and both sides (4.2).

## Limits during the meeting

- Section 9.1: members must not let the public "communicate with you or pass messages to you during the committee's proceedings (orally or in writing) other than through the scheme for public participation".
- Questions to a ward member after their five minutes are confined to "clarification and fact" (11.4), so the policy argument must be made within the slot.
- Section 11.8 (committee members): before deciding against the officer recommendation, discuss the areas of difference with officers, put detailed reasons in the motion, and consider deferral if the reasons' validity is in doubt; "a detailed minute of the reasons" must be made.

## Practical use

The ward member's slot is the one place where the Framework questions can be put to the officer in front of the committee before the vote: which route the report took (S4, S5, or GB6 and GB7), whether the site is a "settlement" under Annex B, whether TR3 was assessed and the Connectivity Tool used, and how CS.15, CS.16 and AS.10 were weighted under Annex A(2). A resident whose email is to be quoted should say in writing whether they may be named, so the member need not hesitate on the night.
