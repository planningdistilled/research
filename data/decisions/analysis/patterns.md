# How the August 2026 NPPF is being applied: cross-topic patterns

Synthesis of `green-belt.md`, `principle-and-balance.md`, `heritage-design-environment.md` and `transition-and-decision-makers.md`, written 23 Sep 2026.
- **Base:** 794 decisions dated 17 Aug–23 Sep 2026: 722 inspector, 37 committee, 33 delegated, 2 SoS (both DCO, and both applied the 2024 text).
- **Authority:** there is **no SoS or court authority on the 2026 Framework yet**. Everything below is inspector practice (mostly written representations) plus council practice.
- **Counts** are the analysts' figures. "n a/b" means a cases for the proposition and b against, or a of b where the issue arose.
- **Confidence:** *strong* means consistent across many letters; *emerging* means a clear direction with limited numbers; *single* means one case.
- **Selection bias:** appeals are refusals under test, and the council sample is skewed to notable approvals. Raw permit rates (inspector 31%, committee 70%, delegated 63%) measure selection, not leniency.
- **Updates:** the propositions were written from the 23 Sep base. Lines marked "2 Oct 2026" add counter-examples and recounts from the 1,064 cases held at that date (984 inspector, 38 committee, 40 delegated, 2 SoS); the other counts are unchanged from the base.

Case links point to `../cases/<id>.md`. For the full list of cases per policy see `../index/policy-index.md`; for the curated picks see `reference-cases.md`.

## A. Principle and the balance

**1. Supply opens the gate; it rarely decides the case on its own.** *Strong.*
- Any five-year shortfall, however marginal (4.61–4.9 years), or an HDT below 75% makes fn41 / S5(1)(j) "evidenced unmet need" automatic for housing.
- Inspector permit rates are flat across supply bands: under 2 years 3/18; 2–2.99 years 4/27; 3–3.99 years 5/33; 4–4.99 years 4/17. Waverley, at 1.28 years, lost all four of its appeals.
- What decides a case is location, a "should be refused" trigger, or heritage.
- The exception: at Aston Clinton (PINS-6008253, hearing, 66 homes) a "substantial" shortfall was the main benefit that carried an S5(4) balance, alongside 25% affordable housing and the council's own emerging allocation (DL ¶38–39, ¶82, ¶85).
- Best cases: [PINS-6005664](../cases/PINS-6005664.md) ¶44 (inquiry: "such a marginal difference being unimportant"); [PINS-6008528](../cases/PINS-6008528.md) ¶10 (HDT below 75% with a five-year supply); [PINS-6005809](../cases/PINS-6005809.md) ¶57 (inquiry: "including, but not limited to").

**2. Outside settlements, the S5(1) category is the whole case. Falling outside every category (S5(4)) is almost always fatal.** *Strong. n 40 fail / 3 pass at appeal (recounted 2 Oct 2026 from case frontmatter, after the 2 Oct additions).*
- The three passes have no single route. Two turned on the *absence* of transport harm or a fallback that cancelled it (Cople; Honeysuckle Bottom). The third, Aston Clinton, turned on the size of the benefits: a substantial supply shortfall, 25% affordable housing, and a site inside the council's emerging strategic allocation, set against only moderate character harm (6008253 ¶85: "taken together the substantial benefits of the proposal would substantially outweigh the moderate weight I have applied to the adverse effects to the character of the countryside").
- Best cases: [PINS-6012985](../cases/PINS-6012985.md) ¶18–22 (movement harm at substantial weight beats substantial HO7 weight with no five-year supply). Counter-examples: [PINS-6011253](../cases/PINS-6011253.md) (Cople); [PINS-6011694](../cases/PINS-6011694.md) (a sawmill fallback, but a flawed letter); [PINS-6008253](../cases/PINS-6008253.md) (Aston Clinton, hearing).

**3. S5(1)(j) opens easily and closes on "physically well-related". There are four competing readings of that phrase, and it is the likeliest issue to be litigated first.** *Strong on outcome; method contested.*
- Appeals: (j) passed 26 times and failed 30. Councils passed it 13 of 13 times.
- About 40% of (j) passes were allowed. The rest lost on designated-asset harm, a trigger, paperwork or S6. Countryside or spatial harm alone never sank a (j) pass.
- The readings range from permissive to restrictive: physical relationship only ([PINS-6001260](../cases/PINS-6001260.md) ¶24); physical, not visual ([PINS-6006893](../cases/PINS-6006893.md) ¶69; at inquiry, [PINS-6008238](../cases/PINS-6008238.md) ¶22); settlement form ([PINS-6009838](../cases/PINS-6009838.md) ¶15); accessibility read in ([PINS-6011872](../cases/PINS-6011872.md) ¶26; [PINS-6007158](../cases/PINS-6007158.md) ¶44). Two hearings (late September) also read in character and setting: [PINS-6007133](../cases/PINS-6007133.md) ¶40 and [PINS-6008253](../cases/PINS-6008253.md) ¶80.

**4. "Should be refused" policies decide the "substantially outweighed" balance. A trigger beats any stack of substantial-weight benefits; without a trigger, local-plan-only harms rarely do.** *Strong.*
- 36 coded S4(2)(c) or S5(2) failures, all refused or dismissed.
- Triggers seen: DP3(3) 17; TR6(4) 8; F5–F9 7; N6 operating the same way.
- Best cases: [PINS-6010836](../cases/PINS-6010836.md) (HO7, E2 and TC2 all substantial, and still lost on DP3); [PINS-6008167](../cases/PINS-6008167.md) ¶21.
- Converse: [PINS-6010195](../cases/PINS-6010195.md) ¶26 (a local-only conflict "does not represent specific conflict against the national decision-making policies").
- Counter-example: [PINS-6009545](../cases/PINS-6009545.md) (low heritage harm prevailed with no trigger named).
- 2 Oct 2026: two more counter-examples, both small urban schemes. At Preston [PINS-6006018](../cases/PINS-6006018.md) ¶19 character harm alone, with no trigger named, substantially outweighed E2 and L2 benefits "given the low number of additional retail units and dwellings provided". At Southend [PINS-6009119](../cases/PINS-6009119.md) ¶23–24 a single P3 noise conflict did the same.
- 2 Oct 2026: triggers other than DP3 are now run in terms. S4(2)(a)(ii) with HC7 at Gillingham [PINS-6009769](../cases/PINS-6009769.md) ¶37 (the whole of a small open space lost); S4(2)(a)(ii) with N6(1)(c) considered and not met at Backworth [PINS-6006322](../cases/PINS-6006322.md) ¶36–37; L3(4) through S5(2) at Brigsley [PINS-6009363](../cases/PINS-6009363.md) ¶9, ¶24 (nine homes at very low density: "the policy is clear that the development should be refused") and Glentham [PINS-6011337](../cases/PINS-6011337.md) ¶29, ¶32.

**5. DP3(3) is the main refusal trigger, and through its "explicit design standards" limb local standards come back as Framework-level harms.** *Strong that the route exists; emerging on how uniformly it is used.*
- About 20 appeals used DP3(3) as a trigger. The corpus has 174 letters citing DP3, but only 31 cite it by limb.
- Best cases: [PINS-6008785](../cases/PINS-6008785.md) ¶39 ((j) met, lost via DP3(3)/S5(2)); [PINS-6011803](../cases/PINS-6011803.md) ¶20 (backland); [PINS-6009127](../cases/PINS-6009127.md) ¶19 (a local space standard given substantial weight).
- Counter-example: [PINS-6009340](../cases/PINS-6009340.md) ¶22. W3 substantial weight supplied the "clear justification", so a substantial benefit can satisfy DP3(3) itself.

**6. HO7 "substantial" weight is not automatic for 1–9 homes, and the weight word predicts the result.** *Strong.*
- Inspectors split 67 substantial against 69 lower words. Councils gave substantial weight 9 of 11 times.
- Where the weight was below substantial, 2 of 53 appeals were allowed; where it was substantial, 24 of 83.
- The weight is cut for scale, mismatch with local need, a PIP range, or car dependence.
- Best cases: [PINS-6009281](../cases/PINS-6009281.md) ¶24 (4 homes at 1.98 years: "moderate"); [PINS-6011972](../cases/PINS-6011972.md) ¶16. Counter-example: [PINS-6009357](../cases/PINS-6009357.md) ¶14 (one home, substantial).

**7. Missing obligations and evidence are free-standing and fatal, and cannot be cured by condition.** *Strong.*
- The recurring gaps: an unexecuted HRA or SAC obligation, an unpaid tariff, no BNG metric, a self-build UU that is unsigned or on the wrong land, species surveys, third-party splay land.
- Self-build counts only when it is secured (20 cases tagged `self-build-unsecured`). The small-site BNG exemption applies only to applications made from 6 Aug 2026.
- Best cases: [PINS-6010848](../cases/PINS-6010848.md) ¶34–35 (won every planning point, lost on the SAC obligation); [PINS-6009588](../cases/PINS-6009588.md) (£304 tariff unpaid); [PINS-6011375](../cases/PINS-6011375.md) (no metric).
- 2 Oct 2026: three more BNG paperwork dismissals (Canewdon [PINS-6008122](../cases/PINS-6008122.md) ¶8–10; Harrow [PINS-6006890](../cases/PINS-6006890.md) ¶12–15; Clavering [PINS-6010951](../cases/PINS-6010951.md) ¶18). Two counter-examples: Stanton on the Wolds [PINS-6010392](../cases/PINS-6010392.md) ¶22 secured self-build by "a negatively-worded condition requiring the submission of a planning obligation", in "exceptional circumstances"; and Market Harborough [PINS-6008177](../cases/PINS-6008177.md) ¶19–21, a permission in principle (PIP), left visibility splays over third-party land to the technical details stage.

**8. S6 protects a neighbourhood plan only if it was made five years or less before the decision date *and* contains allocations.** *Strong.*
- Best cases: [PINS-6007431](../cases/PINS-6007431.md) ¶21–23 (applied); [PINS-6007104](../cases/PINS-6007104.md) ¶29–34 (lost at five years and two months).
- SDC misreads the clock: it measured Ilmington against the IPPS rather than the decision date.

**9. Annex A ¶2 is applied clause by clause, and the cuts fall on spatial restrictions. Heritage, design, amenity and access policies keep their weight; settlement-boundary and countryside restrictions usually lose it.** *Strong. Recounted 2 Oct 2026 over the 96 appeal letters that weigh a plan policy against the Framework ([`appeals-review/issue-3-a2-recode.tsv`](appeals-review/issue-3-a2-recode.tsv), coded from the verified reasons in `issue-3.tsv`).*
- Heritage, design and amenity policies: kept 29, cut 1 (6007466 ¶26 cut consistent design policies, an error). Access and highway policies: kept 9, cut 0.
- Spatial policies (settlement boundaries, countryside restraint, spatial strategy), 45 letters: **cut 30, kept 15.**
  - 16 cut for a named conflict with S4/S5, 9 of them clause only (6001260 ¶28; 6005809 ¶76; 6009632 ¶24; 6010911 ¶31) and 7 as whole policies. 8 of the 16 were allowed.
  - 13 cut because supply or delivery is short, the 2024 "out of date" route in new clothes (Cornwall 6007466 ¶25, 6010442 ¶16; 6008785 ¶38; 6008253 ¶39). 4 were allowed.
  - 1 whole-policy cut by "degree of consistency" with no part named (6007104 ¶27).
  - 11 kept after an Annex A check, usually in the context of a scheme that meets no S5(1) category (6009303 ¶23: "in the context of this appeal"; 6009844 ¶36); none of those appeals was allowed. 4 more applied with no check.
- Green Belt policies: 4 clause cuts, each of a clause barring what GB7 now allows (6006497 ¶17; 6009621 ¶9; 6007335 ¶9; 6008176 ¶17); 8 kept; 1 set aside wholesale (APP/B1550/C/25/3372995 ¶22).
- So the cut follows the S5 result rather than producing it: inspectors cut the restriction where the scheme meets an S5(1) or GB7 category, and keep it where it does not. This matches the published practitioner view that settlement-boundary policies will usually be "materially inconsistent" (Landmark Chambers; see [`../../nppf-2026-guidance/analysis/guidance-vs-our-method.md`](../../nppf-2026-guidance/analysis/guidance-vs-our-method.md) §2.1). Our earlier wording ("spatial-preference policies keep full weight", about 13 cut / 17 kept) understated it.
- Best cases: [PINS-6001260](../cases/PINS-6001260.md) ¶28 (clause cut); [PINS-6005809](../cases/PINS-6005809.md) ¶76 (restrictive parts cut, related policies kept at great weight); [PINS-6011217](../cases/PINS-6011217.md) and [PINS-6007541](../cases/PINS-6007541.md) ¶3–4 (kept); [PINS-6006637](../cases/PINS-6006637.md) ¶40 (Warwick TR1 access policy kept).

## B. Location and transport

**10. On TR3 and GB7(1)(g)(iii), the quality of the walking route decides; distance does not. Walking in the carriageway is the most reliable fail fact, and lighting does not cure it.** *Strong.*
- Green Belt housing, limb (iii): inspectors passed 11 and failed 9.
- Every contested fail had at least one of: no footway, no lighting, carriageway walking, 40–60 mph traffic, or thin or unevidenced buses.
- Every contested pass had a near-continuous footway plus an evidenced bus service.
- Fails have come at 350 m ([PINS-6006637](../cases/PINS-6006637.md), hearing); passes at 1.6 km.
- Best cases: [PINS-6006637](../cases/PINS-6006637.md) ¶21–26 ("necessity rather than choice"); [PINS-6007428](../cases/PINS-6007428.md) ¶14–15; [PINS-6011736](../cases/PINS-6011736.md) ¶4–7.
- Counter-examples: [PINS-6009645](../cases/PINS-6009645.md) (unlit, continuous footway, evidenced DRT bus); [PINS-6011301](../cases/PINS-6011301.md); [PINS-6001260](../cases/PINS-6001260.md) (43 m of carriageway, low speed and flow).

**11. Small scale does not rescue a failing location in the Green Belt. Outside it, the TR3(1)(a) threshold is split.** *Was 6/0 in the Green Belt at 23 Sep; 6/2 at 2 Oct 2026, so now contested there too. Contested outside.*
- Green Belt: 1–9 homes are "significant movement … in this context", and "TR3 must be read as a whole" ([PINS-6010313](../cases/PINS-6010313.md) ¶13; [PINS-6009966](../cases/PINS-6009966.md) ¶27).
- Outside the Green Belt, some letters hold TR3(1)(a) "not relevant" for 1–2 homes but still weigh car reliance ([PINS-6008739](../cases/PINS-6008739.md) ¶37). Others give movement harm substantial weight for one home ([PINS-6012985](../cases/PINS-6012985.md) ¶18).
- 2 Oct 2026: two Green Belt allowals go the other way. Burnham [PINS-6005162](../cases/PINS-6005162.md) ¶23 holds that a single dwelling would not "result in a significant amount of movement", so TR3(1)(a) is met; Hitchin [PINS-6009720](../cases/PINS-6009720.md) ¶20 (PIP) answers a missing frontage footway by "the scale of the development". Neither tests the walking route.

**12. Transport claims must be evidenced and mitigation secured.** *Strong.*
- Evidence and mitigation that failed: bus stops without timetables; "short car trips"; EVs and homeworking; unsecured TROs or s278 works; travel vouchers.
- A highway authority's no-objection does not settle location.
- The Connectivity Tool corroborates but does not decide: a score of 52 has both failed and passed.
- Best cases: [PINS-6011431](../cases/PINS-6011431.md) ¶11–17 (the catalogue of rejected mitigation); [PINS-6011736](../cases/PINS-6011736.md) ¶6–7 (CT 52 fail); [PINS-6010471](../cases/PINS-6010471.md) ¶20 (CT 52 pass).
- 2 Oct 2026, counter-example: Weeton [PINS-6012202](../cases/PINS-6012202.md) ¶11 (allowed under S5(1)(d)) accepted that "driving distances would be short to both Weeton and the larger settlement of Wesham, moderating the extent of harm".

**13. The station routes, GB7(1)(h) and S5(1)(h), are applied literally under Annex B. No appeal has yet passed (h) on a contested station.** *Strong on failure.*
- Under 4 tph daytime, or under 2 tph each way, the station is not "well-connected". About 2× the reasonable walking distance fails.
- The only S5(1)(h) pass was agreed between the parties ([PINS-6006581](../cases/PINS-6006581.md)).
- Best cases: [PINS-6006637](../cases/PINS-6006637.md) ¶18 (Hatton, on the Claverdon line); [PINS-6004144](../cases/PINS-6004144.md) ¶29.

**14. TR6(4) highway safety ("unacceptable") is a stand-alone refusal ground, separate from "severe". Visibility splays over third-party or hedge land fail.** *Strong: TR6(4) 29 fail / 12 pass.*
- Best cases: [PINS-6006819](../cases/PINS-6006819.md) ¶20; [PINS-6009997](../cases/PINS-6009997.md) ¶14–15 (beat 4 homes at 1.98 years after grey belt was conceded); [PINS-6007807](../cases/PINS-6007807.md) (splays destroying a tree tunnel).
- Counter-example: [PINS-6001260](../cases/PINS-6001260.md), which had accident data and low speed and flow.
- 2 Oct 2026, counter-example at PIP stage: Market Harborough [PINS-6008177](../cases/PINS-6008177.md) found "an unacceptable risk to highway safety" without splays over third-party land (¶19), then allowed because the constraint could be overcome "at the technical details consent stage" (¶21).

## C. Green Belt

**15. Grey belt is usually won at a village edge.** *Strong: grey belt 30 pass / 7 fail. Most fails were member refusals, and the one appeal-level rejection was an uncontained town edge.*
- Villages are never "large built-up areas" (Annex E ¶3). The site is assessed, not the study parcel. Purpose (c) is irrelevant.
- "Fundamentally undermine" has never failed.
- Best cases: [PINS-6011103](../cases/PINS-6011103.md) ¶12–16 (members' overturn reversed); [PINS-6010471](../cases/PINS-6010471.md) ¶12–15; the rejection is [PINS-6009281](../cases/PINS-6009281.md) ¶15–17.

**16. Limb (ii) is automatic for housing when footnote 41 bites. Every other use needs evidence specific to its type.** *Strong.*
- A householder's "desire for a larger house" is not unmet need ([PINS-6008745](../cases/PINS-6008745.md) ¶14).
- Solar has passed on national targets ([PINS-6005916](../cases/PINS-6005916.md) ¶22), and a traveller site on the GTAA ([APP-G2245-C-26-3377906](../cases/APP-G2245-C-26-3377906.md) ¶14–15).

**17. Once a scheme is inappropriate, housing supply has never produced VSC at appeal.** *Strong: 0 of about 20.*
- Small schemes get "moderate" weight for their homes, and car dependence "moderates" the benefit further.
- Councils find VSC only for essential rural-worker need, fallbacks or specialist care.
- Best cases: [PINS-6009966](../cases/PINS-6009966.md) ¶45 (substantial weight, still no VSC); [PINS-6012481](../cases/PINS-6012481.md) ¶42; [PINS-6006637](../cases/PINS-6006637.md) ¶60 (28 affordable homes at "very significant" weight).

**18. Passing GB7 is not approval.** *Strong: 17 of 34 non-householder "not inappropriate" appeals were permitted.*
- The losses came through S5(2) triggers: DP3(3), TR6(4), F5, N6, L3, N4, HE6. Openness is not re-weighed.
- Failing GB7 switches the S5(5) balance off altogether.
- Best cases: [PINS-6011803](../cases/PINS-6011803.md) ¶20; [PINS-6007428](../cases/PINS-6007428.md) ¶28 (S5(5) "not engaged"); [PINS-6011301](../cases/PINS-6011301.md) ¶18.

## D. Heritage

**19. Any harm to a designated asset is close to decisive against small schemes.** *Strong.*
- Where heritage harm was found, 10 of 134 appeals were permitted. Where no harm was found, 52 of 81.
- HE6(4) failed in 44 inspector findings, with 0 permitted.
- "Low" harm has beaten 1 home at 3.68 years, and "moderate" harm has beaten 1 home at 2.98 years.
- Best cases: [PINS-6009545](../cases/PINS-6009545.md) ¶17; [PINS-6007054](../cases/PINS-6007054.md) ¶13–22; [PINS-6007221](../cases/PINS-6007221.md) ¶33–34.
- Counter-example: [PINS-6011786](../cases/PINS-6011786.md) ¶16–18 ("very low" harm outweighed).
- 2 Oct 2026: two more allowals against harm above "very low". Blacko [PINS-6007704](../cases/PINS-6007704.md) ¶36–40 (hearing): "limited" harm to a listed pub from loss of its historic use, outweighed mainly by reuse of a building empty for over three years. Hildenborough [PINS-6010459](../cases/PINS-6010459.md) ¶18–19, ¶29 (PIP): conservation-area harm "at the lower end of the scale", outweighed by 5–7 homes at substantial weight with a 2.89-year supply.

**20. Housing wins the HE6(4) balance at scale, not at the margin.** *Emerging: 5 allowed, 3 refused.*
- Major schemes with a shortfall outweigh low or lowest-end harm to Grade II settings.
- They do not outweigh harm to Grade I or II* assets, moderate harm, or harm to functional settings. Setting is read functionally, not only by intervisibility.
- Best cases: [PINS-6006475](../cases/PINS-6006475.md) ¶14, 32, 38, 68 (hearing; 35 homes lost); [PINS-6005664](../cases/PINS-6005664.md) (inquiry; 110 homes won); [APP-E3335-W-25-3375062](../cases/APP-E3335-W-25-3375062.md) ¶12–13, 40.

**21. The HE6(4) public-benefit examples are acknowledged but rarely delivered. The recurring filter is whether a less harmful scheme could achieve the same benefit.** *Strong.*
- Energy benefits win only when quantified, applied to the *existing* building, and not achievable less harmfully. Private benefits are excluded. Reuse benefits must be secured.
- Wins: [PINS-6006240](../cases/PINS-6006240.md) ¶17–18; [PINS-6006422](../cases/PINS-6006422.md) ¶59–61.
- Losses: [PINS-6012775](../cases/PINS-6012775.md) ¶17–19; [PINS-6011314](../cases/PINS-6011314.md) ¶36–39.

**22. The harm label changed but the harm scale did not.** *Strong (corpus counts).*
- Inspectors grade harm inside a single "harm" category ("very low / low / minor / moderate").
- Legacy wording is fading: "less than substantial" appeared in 21% of heritage letters in 17–31 Aug and 7% in 11–23 Sep. Some letters still misattribute the weights (substantial or great weight to *harm*), which is rebuttal or challenge material.
- Best cases: [PINS-6007355](../cases/PINS-6007355.md) ¶26; [PINS-6012043](../cases/PINS-6012043.md) ¶14 ("no longer the concept of less than substantial harm"). The error example is [PINS-6007478](../cases/PINS-6007478.md) ¶23.

## E. Transition and decision-makers

**23. Letters dated 17 Aug are 2024 decisions. Consulting the parties became the norm only in September. Old wording keeps leaking into later letters.** *Strong.*
- 64 of 65 letters dated 17 Aug have no switch paragraph.
- Share of letters that consulted the parties: 8% in 18–31 Aug, 28% in 1–11 Sep, 42% in 12–23 Sep.
- "Significantly and demonstrably" is the operative conclusion in 15 post-switch letters. It is immaterial in dismissals, because "substantially outweighed" is at least as demanding. The exposure is in allowals that skipped the S4/S5 category step (6007104, 6010973).
- Best cases: [PINS-6002759](../cases/PINS-6002759.md) ¶28 (140 homes allowed under 11(d) on 18 Aug); [PINS-6005108](../cases/PINS-6005108.md) ¶145 (a consulted inquiry letter concluding in the 2024 formula); [PINS-6005119](../cases/PINS-6005119.md) ¶18 (11(d) arguments "no longer of relevance").

**24. On the same tests, councils pass what inspectors fail. The gap is widest on route quality, triggers and heritage method.** *Strong.* See section F.

**25. Fallbacks approve schemes at council level; inspectors make the appellant prove them.** *Emerging.*
- Councils accepted 7 of 8 fallbacks. Inspectors allowed about 11 of about 60 appeals that argued one, and rejected at least 10 fallbacks as unproven or less harmful than the scheme.
- A Class Q fallback neutralises car dependence and moves the fight to character.
- Best cases: [PINS-6010313](../cases/PINS-6010313.md) ¶35 (fallback less harmful, so limited weight); [PINS-6008264](../cases/PINS-6008264.md) (an extant PIP carried the appeal); council example [wychavon-W-26-00329-FUL](../cases/wychavon-W-26-00329-FUL.md).

**2 Oct 2026: eight more SDC decisions (notices 23 Sep – 2 Oct).**
- Refusals on the balance have started. Members refused Pillerton Priors against the officer recommendation, on "substantial harm" from backland development ([stratford-26-01894-PIP](../cases/stratford-26-01894-PIP.md)); an officer refused one dwelling at Earlswood on character and L2(1)(d) after passing it as village infill under GB7(1)(c) ([stratford-26-01542-FUL](../cases/stratford-26-01542-FUL.md)).
- Both reports quote S4(2)(a)(ii) with L2(1)(d); neither applies it as a trigger. An inspector did the same exercise at Hesketh Bank ([PINS-6007730](../cases/PINS-6007730.md) ¶17) and found no "substantial adverse impact".
- One report applies the Annex B exclusion: an Earlswood site "is not considered to be within a settlement because it lies within the Green Belt" ([stratford-26-01614-FUL](../cases/stratford-26-01614-FUL.md)). Eight days later another report treats a site in the same village as within a settlement (stratford-26-01542-FUL).
- Fallbacks were tested rather than assumed in three reports ([stratford-26-01660-OUT](../cases/stratford-26-01660-OUT.md), [stratford-26-01801-FUL](../cases/stratford-26-01801-FUL.md), stratford-26-01614-FUL).

**Member overturns** (8 found, 4 tested on appeal).
- **How members overturn:** by regrading a matter of judgement, such as grey-belt purpose (a), "around 800m" or "severe", rather than by disputing facts.
- **What survives appeal:** overturns resting on a reading available on the ground (landscape, heritage). Example: [PINS-6005903](../cases/PINS-6005903.md), where members' National Landscape refusal was upheld.
- **What is reversed:** overturns resting on a label the Framework defines. Example: [PINS-6011103](../cases/PINS-6011103.md), village treated as a large built-up area. Costs have been refused so far, because these are "matters of judgement".
- **What wins:** limb (iii) refusals on walking-route facts. Inspectors upheld every Green Belt limb (iii) refusal where the route lacked a usable footway: Tandridge ×4, Mole Valley, West Lancs, Sevenoaks and Warwick.

- No SoS or court construction of any 2026 test yet.
- The four readings of S5(1)(j)(i) "physically well-related" are unresolved.

- No appeal has yet passed GB7(1)(h). GB7(1)(c) village infill has one appeal analysis so far, on a non-residential scheme ([PINS-6010709](../cases/PINS-6010709.md) ¶8–11: no discernible village on the ground; 1.3 ha is not limited infilling). - Limb (ii) "type" has not been tested for market housing against an affordable-only need.
