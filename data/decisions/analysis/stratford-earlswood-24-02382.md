# 24/02382/FUL, Land adjacent to 141 Earlswood Common, Earlswood: what the Framework requires and what the file shows

Note of 9 October 2026. An analysis of an undecided Stratford-on-Avon District Council (SDC) application, written before the officer report exists, from the documents on the council's file at 9 October 2026 (text extracts in `sources:stratford-dc/earlswood/24-02382-FUL/`, list in `documents-2026-10-09.tsv` there). It sets out the route the National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF) of August 2026 prescribes, the facts on the file, and the points at which the report is likely to repeat the errors catalogued in the Stratford note. Not legal advice. Residents' comments are not read or quoted; officers are referred to by role.

## In short

- A full application by a rural housing association for 11 affordable homes (9 social rent, 2 shared ownership) on 0.4 ha of a paddock on the west side of The Common (B4102), Earlswood, owned by a parish charitable trust. Earlswood is a Category 3 Local Service Village washed over by the West Midlands Green Belt and within the Arden Special Landscape Area. The site is outside the village's built-up area boundary (BUAB) but is allocated for exactly this purpose by policy H1 of the made Tanworth-in-Arden Neighbourhood Development Plan (NDP, December 2022).
- Validated 26 November 2024. The Parish Council, the ward member and SDC's housing enabling officer support it; the highway authority withdrew its objection in September 2025; a section 106 agreement was engrossed in June 2026. Nothing on the file triggers committee, so a delegated approval could issue at any time, on a report whose only published reasoning so far, the policy officer's advice of 17 September 2025, is written entirely in December 2024 Framework terms.
- Under the 2026 Framework the natural route is GB7(1)(d), "limited affordable housing for local community needs under policies set out in this Framework or the development plan". A scheme within that category is not inappropriate and "should not be regarded as harmful to the Green Belt". The Council's advice instead steers the case officer to the grey belt route, GB7(1)(g), which it misdescribes as the only way to avoid weighing openness, and which carries a TR3 location test that this site may not pass.
- The location evidence is thin either way: a footway and a 30 mph limit on The Common, the village's handful of services about 1 km north, The Lakes station (a request stop with one train an hour each way and a steep ramp) about the same distance, Earlswood station over 2 km, and "little more than a daily" bus. No one has run the Connectivity Tool. The Council's advice treats the NDP allocation as settling the question: "it is reasonable to assume that the Inspector found the location to be sustainable at the time of Examination".
- On the merits this is a scheme the Framework is designed to allow: a 100% affordable local needs scheme on an allocated site, with need validated against 2024 waiting-list data. The probable and defensible outcome is approval. The risk is the reasoning: a 2024 "tilted balance" report, the wrong Green Belt route, TR3 assumed rather than assessed, "Category 3" standing in for a location test, and the design conflict with the NDP's own character statement never put through DP3(3).

## The development plan

- **NDP policy H1** allocates "Land adjoining 141 The Common, Earlswood" for "a small-scale community-led Local Needs Housing Scheme" on three criteria: exclusively or predominantly affordable housing meeting the 2021 survey "or any subsequent update"; a section 106 in accordance with Part S of the Development Requirements SPD; and "The density, layout and construction of housing is compatible with the relevant Character Assessment that accompanies this plan". Policy H3 bars housing outside the BUABs "except the schemes allocated under Policies H1 and H2".
- **The NDP's own measure of scale.** Its site search "took into account that residents would prefer small scale developments (10 or less homes on each site)" (¶2.15); H2 at Wood End is capped at "10 or fewer". H1 carries no number. The Character Assessment for Earlswood (Appendix A) says "development units typically consist of one individual house or small groups of between two and four houses, with very few exceptions". The emerging South Warwickshire Local Plan (Regulation 19, July 2026) carries the allocation forward as ERL.1 with an indicative capacity of 6.
- **Core Strategy.** CS.15 part G: "Within and adjacent to settlements, development may include small-scale community-led schemes brought forward to meet a need identified by that community." AS.10(a) supports such schemes "on land within or adjacent to a village". CS.10(a) treats them as not inappropriate in the Green Belt "subject to it not being harmful to the openness of the Green Belt". CS.12 protects the Special Landscape Area by "resisting development proposals that would have a harmful effect on their distinctive character and appearance". CS.5 and CS.9 apply.
- **NDP BE1** requires development to "demonstrate that appropriate account has been taken of the local character as described in the Character Assessments", including "The use of space between buildings or groups of buildings to preserve the rural nature of the environment and to protect public views of open land beyond". NE1 protects the landscape and valued views; the site is not within a listed valued view.

Under Annex A(2) of the Framework these policies keep their weight unless a part of them is materially inconsistent with a national decision-making policy. None of H1, H3, BE1, CS.12 or CS.5 is: the Framework itself provides for local needs affordable housing on allocated sites (GB7(1)(d), HO10) and for landscape and design control (N2, DP3). The NDP was made in December 2022, within five years of any decision this year, so S6 is in play if the plan "contains allocations to meet its identified housing requirement"; either way S6 only strengthens a plan-compliant scheme.

## The route the Framework prescribes

1. **Green Belt first.** The site is in the Green Belt, so S5(5) sends it to GB6 and GB7. S4 cannot apply: Annex B excludes from "settlement" villages "which lie within and are defined as part of the Green Belt in the development plan", and S5(1)(j) is equally unavailable. Annex B also means HO10(2)(a), "adjoin or be physically well-related to a settlement", cannot be applied literally; the development plan supplies the test instead, which is what GB7(1)(d) contemplates.
2. **GB7(1)(d).** "Limited affordable housing for local community needs under policies set out in this Framework or the development plan (for instance, on a rural exception site)". The scheme is 100% affordable, for local need evidenced by survey and waiting list, on a site allocated by the development plan for that purpose and supported by CS.15 G and AS.10(a). The only open question is "limited". If the scheme falls within (d) it "should not be regarded as harmful to the Green Belt or be required to demonstrate very special circumstances" (GB7(1)). There is then no openness harm to weigh, and the decision moves to the S5(5) balance: approve "unless the benefits of doing so would be substantially outweighed by any adverse effects", applying S5(2).
3. **GB7(1)(g) as the alternative.** Grey belt (the site does not strongly contribute to purposes (a), (b) or (d)), unmet need (2.43 years' supply), a sustainable location "with particular reference to policy TR3", and, because 11 homes is major development, compliance with the GB8 Golden Rules (50% affordable by default, infrastructure, accessible green space). The difference from (d) is that TR3 is a gateway: fail limb (iii) and the development is inappropriate, with substantial weight against it under GB6(2).
4. **TR3 applies in either case.** It is a free-standing decision-making policy. Eleven homes in a village of this kind "could generate a significant amount of movement"; an inspector found five did at Bournheath (6011301). Under (d) a poor result is an adverse effect in the S5(5) balance, as it was at Banks (6011231); under (g) it is decisive. TR3(2) says the Connectivity Tool "should be used".
5. **Design and landscape.** N2 (character of the countryside), DP3(1), and the NDP's explicit standards (H1(3), BE1, Appendix A). DP3(3): refuse "if, without clear justification, they conflict with paragraph 1 of this policy … or with any explicit design standards set out in the development plan (including those in locally-specific policies, guides, codes or masterplans)". A conflict without clear justification feeds S5(2).
6. **HO10.** Exception sites should be supported where the need is "evidenced through a local housing needs survey or secondary data which is no more than five years old" (1)(a) and the built development is no more than one hectare or 5% of the settlement (2)(b). The 2021 survey is now more than five years old; the June 2024 waiting-list data is not. The 0.4 ha site passes (2)(b) on the hectare limb alone.

## What the Council has written so far, and the errors it foreshadows

The policy officer's advice of 17 September 2025 is the only reasoning from the planning side on the public file after the pre-application response. It was written eleven months before the 2026 Framework and reads as follows.

- **Supply shortfall as a weight-cutter (error 6).** "At the time of writing this policy advice, the Council can demonstrate 2.74 years housing land supply. Consequently the tilted balance is engaged, and limited weight can be afforded to Core Strategy policies relating to the distribution of housing development." The 2026 Framework has no tilted balance. The shortfall is evidence of need for GB7(1)(g)(ii) and S5(1)(j); it is not a reason to reduce the weight of CS.15 or AS.10, and Annex A(2) forbids reducing weight "simply because they were adopted prior to the publication of this Framework".
- **The wrong Green Belt mechanics.** The advice reasons from 2024 paragraphs 153, 154(f) and footnote 55: via the local needs exception "harm to the openness of the Green Belt should be given substantial weight in the planning balance", whereas via grey belt "harm to the openness of the Green Belt may not be weighed against the proposal". Under GB7(1) of the 2026 Framework every category, (d) included, is "not … harmful to the Green Belt". The distinction the advice draws no longer exists, and it points the case officer at the route with the harder test.
- **Location assumed from the allocation, and from the tier (errors 4 and 5).** On the sustainable-location limb: "As the site has been allocated in the NDP, it is reasonable to assume that the Inspector found the location to be sustainable at the time of Examination", adding that "it is relevant to consider the proximity of facilities within this Category 3 Local Service Village". The NDP examiner tested the allocation against the 2021 Framework, not TR3; the NDP's own text records one train an hour and a daily bus. A village's Core Strategy category is not evidence about a route. The Council's past Earlswood reports show the same move: at Tithe Barn Lane (26/01458/FUL) limb (iii) was passed because the dwelling was "within the BUAB of an identified local service village".
- **Annex B (error 4).** The advice does not use S4 or S5(1)(j), which is correct, but it does not say why. The case officer who wrote the Malthouse Lane report (26/01542/FUL) in the same village applied S4 and L2(1)(d) inside the BUAB; the Ardencroft report (26/01614/FUL) applied Annex B. This site is outside the BUAB, so the S5(1)(j) "well-related to an existing settlement" wording is the one to watch for.
- **DP3(3) (error 1).** Nothing on the file engages it. Yet the Council has, on the applicant's account, objected on landscape; the 2012 study put the frontage's capacity at three houses; and the NDP's character statement describes groups of two to four houses. If the report finds harm to character or to the Special Landscape Area, as the pre-application advice anticipated, the DP3(3) question must be asked.

## The merits, issue by issue

**1. Is 11 homes "limited" affordable housing for local needs?** Four benchmarks exist: the 2012 landscape study's three houses for this frontage; the NDP's expressed preference for ten or fewer and its H2 cap of ten; the emerging plan's indicative six; and the pre-application view that 11 "could be considered as small-scale" having regard to the 450-home Category 3 total. The need evidence supports the number: eight households from the survey plus three from the waiting list, against 28 registered. A report that finds the scheme within GB7(1)(d) should say why 11 is limited in Earlswood's context, not in the parish's; the applicant's percentage argument uses the parish of 1,350 dwellings. Inspectors have refused an eight-home rural exception site at Towan Cross (6009030) partly because it "likely" exceeded the HO10 size limit; here the hectare limb is met, so scale goes to character rather than to HO10.

**2. Location.** The facts favour the scheme more than most Green Belt village sites in the district: a continuous footway on the site's side of a 30 mph road, the village's services within about a kilometre, and a station within reasonable walking distance on the Framework's own measure of around 800 m to 10 minutes. They fall short on what is at the end of those routes: five modest services and no school or surgery; a request-stop station with one train an hour each way, which is well below the Annex B "well-connected station" threshold of four an hour overall or two in one direction; a bus that is no alternative; and a walk of over 2 km to the better station. For social-rent and downsizing households car ownership cannot be assumed, which cuts both ways. On comparable facts inspectors have gone both ways. Bournheath (6011301, allowed): walkable pubs and hall, a lit 30 mph route to a school and shop, a county on-demand bus and a Connectivity Tool score in the 90th percentile. Copthorne (6011736, dismissed): an hourly bus, a short stretch without footway, Connectivity score 52, "not a genuine choice of transport modes". Hatton Station (6006637, dismissed): 28 affordable homes 350 m from a station with services to Birmingham and London, failed because the station was not well-connected and the route unsafe. Newchapel (6011972, dismissed): eight homes, a bus three or four times a day, car-dependent. The honest answer for Earlswood Common is that TR3 is arguable and has to be assessed on the route, the services and a Connectivity Tool score, and that a report which passes it on the allocation and the tier will have skipped the test. Under GB7(1)(d) a failure is survivable in the S5(5) balance; under (g) it is not.

**3. Landscape, character and DP3(3).** The site is in the Special Landscape Area, in a parcel the Council's own study rated high/medium sensitivity with capacity for three houses on this frontage, in a village whose made plan describes development units as "one individual house or small groups of between two and four houses". The scheme is an 11-unit cul-de-sac behind a frontage, with the roadside hedge removed for four accesses. The pre-application advice asked for the frontage to be built up and the hedge replaced; the applicant's second addendum describes a Council landscape objection to a "suburban street" appearance and the changes made in answer. H1(3) and BE1 are explicit development-plan design standards, so a finding that the layout is not "compatible with the relevant Character Assessment" is a DP3(3) conflict unless there is clear justification. The affordable local need is a strong candidate for clear justification, as substantial benefits were at Aston Clinton (6008253); but the report has to ask the question and give the answer. Mitigation by condition, a tree-lined hedge to the open side and replacement roadside hedging, goes to the degree of harm, not to whether DP3(3) is engaged.

**4. Need and the section 106.** The 2021 survey identified 25 households in need, eight of them for affordable rent or shared ownership. The housing enabling officer validated this against June 2024 data (28 households) and property prices in December 2024. By the time of decision the survey is more than five years old, so HO10(1)(a) rests on the "secondary data" limb; a short update of the waiting-list figure would close that point. The engrossed section 106 secures all 11 units as affordable in perpetuity, 9 social rent and 2 shared ownership, with a local connection cascade from the parish outward; its boilerplate references to "Open Market Units" do not reflect any market housing in the scheme. This is the mechanism GB7(1)(d), HO10 and NDP H1(2) all require.

**5. Golden Rules, if GB7(1)(g) is used.** Affordable housing at 100% exceeds the 50% default; county contributions for road safety and travel packs are agreed; open space of 375 sqm is on site with Malthouse Lane park nearby, though the play area was removed in 2025 and the report should say how GB8(1)(c) is met. Under (d) the Golden Rules do not formally apply to the category, but GB8(1) is drafted to apply to any major housing "on sites in the Green Belt subject to a planning application", so the report should address them regardless.

**6. The balance and the likely outcome.** Benefits: 11 affordable homes for local people against a 2.43-year district supply and a parish with no affordable completions since 2020, on a site the community chose and the development plan allocates, delivered by a registered provider, with biodiversity gain. Harms: some loss of a paddock's openness and rural character at the village edge in a Special Landscape Area, a layout denser than the village's pattern, a location with weak public transport and few services, and loss of roadside hedgerow. Under S5(5) the question is whether the benefits are "substantially outweighed". On the evidence they are not, and refusal would be hard to sustain at appeal given the allocation. The scheme should be approved; the point of this note is that it should be approved on the Framework's reasoning.

The Connectivity Tool scores are relative to the best-connected square in England and Wales, and the Department says they should be read against the national distribution for the square's rural-urban class rather than in isolation; those thresholds are not published in a form this note could read. For context within the district, the same tool gives these overall scores: Harbury, Mill Street 38; land south of Henley-in-Arden 42; Field Farm, Wootton Wawen 33; Ravenstone, Tanworth 28; Station Road, Claverdon 26; Hampton Lucy 26; School Lane, Bearley 23; Greenfingers, Claverdon 21; Kineton Road, Pillerton Priors 14. Inspectors have allowed a Green Belt village site with a score of 43 and a county on-demand bus (Bournheath) and dismissed one with a score of 52 and an hourly bus where part of the walk was in the carriageway (Copthorne), so the score is a starting point and the route evidence decides.

What this adds up to: a site with a proper footway onto a 30 mph village road, a kilometre from a handful of services and from an hourly train, with no usable bus, no school or surgery in the village, and a walking score in the bottom part of the national range. TR3 is arguable either way on these facts. Under GB7(1)(d) the weakness goes into the S5(5) balance, where the affordable local need is likely to carry it; under GB7(1)(g) it would be the whole case.

## What to look for in the report

1. Which Green Belt category it uses, and whether it says that development within GB7(1)(d) is not harmful to the Green Belt, rather than giving harm to openness "substantial weight in the planning balance" as the 2025 advice, in 2024 terms, said it should.
2. Whether it mentions Annex B and avoids S4, S5(1)(j) and "within the settlement" language for a washed-over village.
3. Whether TR3 is assessed on the route, the services, the station frequency and a Connectivity Tool score, or passed on the NDP allocation and "Category 3 Local Service Village".
4. Whether it finds harm to character or to the Special Landscape Area and, if so, whether it asks the DP3(3) question against H1(3) and BE1 and gives a reasoned "clear justification".
5. Whether CS.15, AS.10, CS.12 and the NDP are weighted under Annex A(2) part by part, or cut wholesale because of the supply figure, and whether the figure used is 2.43 years at 31 March 2026.
6. Whether the need evidence is brought up to date beyond July 2021 and the "limited" question is answered for Earlswood rather than the parish.
7. Whether the Golden Rules are addressed, including accessible green space after removal of the play area.
