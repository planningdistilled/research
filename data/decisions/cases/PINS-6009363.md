## Summary
Outline permission for nine houses on a 1.3 ha field next to Brigsley's development boundary was dismissed. With no five-year supply, the site met S5(1)(j) as physically well-related to the settlement, and nine homes had substantial weight. But at 6.8 dwellings per hectare the scheme did not make effective use of land, and L3(4) says such proposals should be refused. That engaged S5(2). With a poorly connected location and landscape harm each given considerable weight, the benefits were substantially outweighed.

## Issues and findings
- **Transition.** "The main parties have had the opportunity to comment on the content of the National Planning Policy Framework 2026 (Framework) including Policy L3; and given its expectations, the effective use of land has been included within the main issues." (DL ¶3).
- **Plan weight (Annex A ¶2): part by part.** Of Local Plan Policy 5 Part 1: "This is generally consistent with the Framework and can be afforded full weight. Part 3 of the policy is inconsistent with the Framework and can be afforded very limited weight." (DL ¶4).
- **S5(1)(j)(i): PASS.** "As it is accepted that the local planning authority cannot demonstrate a five year supply of deliverable housing sites, it … satisfies Policy S5(1ji) in that it would be physically well-related to an existing settlement." (DL ¶5; the break is a page break).
- **Density, L3: FAIL.** "I am not persuaded that the provision of nine dwellings on this site represents an effective use of land or satisfies Framework Policy L3. In these circumstances, the policy is clear that the development should be refused." (DL ¶9).
- **DP3(3): NOT TRIGGERED.** "the details of design are reserved matters and it would not result in clear conflict with Policy DP3 to the extent that it would trigger the requirement that it should be refused" (DL ¶12). The landscape harm is instead recorded against N2(1)(a) (DL ¶11).
- **Location, TR3(1)(a): FAIL.** "The Framework defines a reasonable walking distance as being around 800m. Distances from the site are prohibitive for walking to schools, shops and healthcare facilities." and "as no evidence is provided with regard to bus frequency, it cannot be assumed that a convenient service would be available" (DL ¶14). "The site performs poorly with regard to the promotion of sustainable travel and it does not offer a genuine choice of transport modes for residents." (DL ¶17).
- **Housing, HO7: substantial.** "The provision of houses must be given substantial weight." (DL ¶21).

## Planning balance
S5(1) with S5(2): "These conflicts, particularly but not limited to Framework Policy L3, represent the circumstances, set out in Framework Policy S5(2), in which the benefits of approving development proposals are likely to be substantially outweighed by the adverse effects." (DL ¶24). Substantial weight to nine homes and considerable weight to social and economic benefits, with biodiversity and tree planting in support, against the L3 refusal policy and considerable weight each to the TR3 conflict and the landscape harm.

## What made the difference
Density. The site passed the S5(1)(j) gateway and the housing benefit had full weight, but nine houses on 1.3 ha could not be called the best use of the site's potential, and the appellant gave no net density figure. The inspector raised L3 after the Framework changed. A denser scheme would have removed the refusal policy, though it would have sharpened the landscape and location objections, which also carried considerable weight.

## Transferable points
- L3(2)(b) and L3(4) apply to housing on unallocated land outside a settlement that relies on S5(1)(j); very low density is a "should be refused" failure that engages S5(2) (DL ¶7-9, ¶24).
- An inspector may add effective use of land as a main issue after consulting the parties on the new Framework (DL ¶3).
- Local support for low density and the character of neighbouring housing did not justify a very low density (DL ¶9).
- At outline stage with design reserved, harm to the character of the area was held not to be a clear DP3 conflict triggering DP3(3); it was weighed under N2(1)(a) instead (DL ¶11-12).
- A site adjoining the development boundary was treated as physically well-related to the settlement without further analysis (DL ¶5, ¶22).
- Bus stops without evidence of frequency do not show a convenient service (DL ¶14).
- The Annex B "around 800m" reasonable walking distance was used as the measure for walking to services, beyond the station policies it is written for (DL ¶14).
- A local plan policy can be split under Annex A ¶2, one part at full weight and another at very limited weight (DL ¶4).
