## Summary
A takeaway and delivery-only food use in a former barber shop in Hackney was dismissed. The premises are about 315 m from a school and outside any designated centre, contrary to the local 400 m exclusion policy and to Framework policy HC5. Activity from delivery riders and walk-in customers on a narrow pavement beside a Red Route would also have an unacceptable impact on highway safety under TR6(4).

## Issues and findings
- **HC5: CONFLICT.** "if permission were to be granted for a sui generis use it would not be possible to control by condition or any other means the proportion of walk-in orders, including from children, compared with deliveries" (DL ¶6). The scheme would be "in harmful conflict with Local Plan Policy LP39; London Plan Policy E9 and with Government policy in Policy HC5 of the recently revised National Planning Policy Framework August 2026" (DL ¶7).
- **TR6(4): FAIL.** "the comings and goings from cycles, motorbikes and cars associated with premises on a site with a narrow pavement, very limited parking and fronting onto a Red Route highway would be such as to result in an increase in hazards and overall have an unacceptable impact on highway safety" (DL ¶11).

## Planning balance
None is run. Each issue is a conflict with the plan and with a Framework policy that says development should be refused, and "the appeal fails" (DL ¶12). S4 is not mentioned.

## What made the difference
The use class. Permission would be for a sui generis takeaway, and no condition could hold the operator to deliveries only or to a particular menu. The delivery-led business model therefore did not answer either the school-proximity policy or the Red Route parking problem.

## Transferable points
- HC5 is applied alongside a local 400 m school exclusion zone; a site about 315 m from a school and outside a designated centre conflicts with both (DL ¶3–4, ¶7).
- A delivery-only or healthier-food business model carries little weight where the permission could not control walk-in sales or the food sold (DL ¶6).
- TR6(4) "unacceptable impact on highway safety" can arise from delivery and customer activity at premises with no lawful stopping place (DL ¶8–11).
