## Summary
A two-storey side extension and conversion to create two more flats at the end of a small parade in Wednesfield was dismissed. The extension would dominate an open corner and breach the building line, and the shared amenity space and the relationship with one ground-floor flat were poor. The inspector tied both harms to the L2(1)(d) criteria and found the benefits substantially outweighed under S4.

## Issues and findings
- **Transition.** "the provisions in respect of the matters relied on by the main parties are not materially changed. Therefore, I did not need to consult with the main parties regarding the revised Framework" (DL ¶2).
- **Character: harm.** "the proposal would demonstrably harm the appearance of the streetscene" (DL ¶9).
- **Living conditions: harm.** The scheme would "create a poor relationship for the occupiers of flat 3 with users of the external space, resulting in poor living conditions through inadequate privacy, and unacceptable levels of noise and general disturbance" (DL ¶18).
- **Parking and highway safety: acceptable.** "the proposal would not create a significant increase in on-street parking pressure or result in an adverse effect on highway safety" (DL ¶24).
- **L2(1)(d)(i) and (ii): CONFLICT.** "the proposal would not be consistent with the overall streetscene, in conflict with policy L2(1)(d)(i) of the Framework. The scheme would also create poor living standards for existing and future residents, in terms of provision of external space and effect on privacy, as sought by the Framework’s policy L2(1)(d)(ii)" (DL ¶32).

## Planning balance
S4(1), after a plan-led finding of conflict given significant weight (DL ¶29). HO7 and the accessible location weigh in favour (DL ¶30–31). "the benefits of the proposed development, would be substantially outweighed by the identified adverse effects of the proposal, when assessed against the national decision-making policies of the Framework as a whole" (DL ¶33). DP3 is not cited; the design harm is routed through L2(1)(d)(i).

## What made the difference
The corner. The existing single-storey extension left space above it, and a full-height addition close to the footway would end that openness. A scheme confined to the change of use, or with a set-back upper floor and a usable private amenity area, would have avoided both harms.

## Transferable points
- The L2(1)(d) criteria work as tests of harm as well as conditions for L2 weight: street-scene inconsistency and poor external space or privacy were recorded as conflicts with L2(1)(d)(i) and (ii) (DL ¶32).
- Nearby public open space does not make up for poor on-site amenity space for flats (DL ¶15).
- Meeting a maximum parking standard and showing fewer trips than the lawful commercial use answered the parking objection (DL ¶19–20, ¶24).
