## Summary
A two-storey building with three shops and six flats on a cleared corner plot on Blackpool Road, Preston was dismissed. Its height and gabled roof would make it a bulky, dominant feature at the back of the pavement, out of keeping with the street. The inspector held that the support in L2(1)(d) for higher buildings at street corners did not apply, and that under S4 the harm to character substantially outweighed the benefits of two more shops and two more homes.

## Issues and findings
- **Transition.** "The main parties were provided with an opportunity to comment. I have had regard to the 2026 version of the Framework in my decision" (DL ¶4).
- **Character: HARM.** "the proposal would appear as a dominant addition, narrowing the street scene, exacerbated by its location at the back of the pavement on a prominent corner plot, creating an abrupt transition to the residential character beyond" (DL ¶9).
- **L2(1)(d)(i): FAIL.** "Given the above identified harm to the character and appearance of the area, the proposal is not consistent with the overall street scene and it is not appropriate to have higher buildings at this corner plot. Therefore, the proposal does not benefit from this part of Policy L2" (DL ¶10).
- **Living conditions: no unacceptable harm.** Overlooking "would be limited and not meaningfully exacerbate the aforementioned existing mutual overlooking from rear windows of surrounding properties" (DL ¶14).

## Planning balance
S4(1). "The Framework gives substantial weight to the economic benefits of proposals for commercial development and supporting the overall vitality and viability of town centres, and making better use of vacant and underutilised land and buildings. Nevertheless, given the low number of additional retail units and dwellings provided, these benefits would be substantially outweighed by the harm to the character and appearance of the area" (DL ¶19). DP3 is not cited, and no policy directing refusal is named.

## What made the difference
The roof. The eaves matched the former building, but a higher ridge and a full gable in place of a hipped roof, at the back of the pavement, removed the stepped transition into the side street that both the old building and the earlier extension permission had kept. Net benefits of two shops and two flats were too small to carry the day. A hipped or lower roof, as previously approved, would have removed the objection.

## Transferable points
- L2(1)(d) support for "higher buildings at street corners" is not automatic: the inspector asked whether this corner was one where a higher building was appropriate, and found it was not (DL ¶10).
- Framework benefits that attract substantial weight in principle were discounted for the small net numbers (2 shops, 2 homes) (DL ¶19).
- Character harm alone, with no DP3(3) or other trigger named, was held to substantially outweigh the benefits under S4(1) (DL ¶19).
