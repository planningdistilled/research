## Summary
A hybrid scheme for 352 homes on an allocated site at the southern edge of Wisbech was allowed after a hearing. The dispute was about culverting an Internal Drainage Board (IDB) watercourse to form the access. The inspector applied DM7 to assume the separate land drainage consent regime would operate effectively, found compelling reasons under F8(3) for enclosing the watercourse, and approved under S4 with substantial weight to homes that would help meet an affordable housing shortfall.

## Issues and findings
- **Transition.** "I have consulted the main parties and taken any comments into account in reaching my decision" (DL ¶3).
- **Separate regimes, DM7: PASS.** "Notwithstanding the comments of the IDB, as an application for Land Drainage Consent has not been made or determined, there is no certainty that the proposed culvert would fail to accord with the Strategy" (DL ¶13).
- **Grampian condition.** "Given the options available for the maintenance and management of the culvert, there is at least some prospect of the action being performed" (DL ¶17).
- **Culverting, F8(3): PASS.** "Any access to the site via Halfpenny Lane would require culverting at least part of the watercourse" (DL ¶22). "These matters form compelling reasons for enclosing the Halfpenny Lane watercourse such that the scheme would not conflict with the Framework in this respect" (DL ¶22).
- **Flood risk elsewhere: no harm.** "the culverting of this section of open watercourse in order to provide site access would not increase the upstream flood risk" (DL ¶20).
- **Affordable housing and viability.** 49 affordable homes; "a policy compliant scheme would be unviable" (DL ¶37).
- **S4(2): no trigger.** "While the list is not exhaustive, none of the circumstances apply in this case" (DL ¶42).

## Planning balance
S4(1), with the scheme also found to accord with the development plan (DL ¶46). Against: reduced infrastructure contributions, moderate weight, "Although there would be no associated development plan harm" (DL ¶43). For: 352 homes; the Council has a five-year supply but "a significant shortfall in affordable provision" (DL ¶44), so "I attach substantial weight to the benefit of providing affordable homes as directed by Framework Policy HO7" (DL ¶45).

## What made the difference
The allocation. It supplied both the principle and the "compelling reasons" F8(3) requires for enclosing a watercourse, since no other access was practical. The drainage board's opposition was answered by DM7 and by a negatively worded condition with some prospect of being met. Without the allocation, or with a determined refusal of land drainage consent, the F8(3) and condition findings would have been much harder.

## Transferable points
- DM7: a consenting body's statement that consent is "unlikely" is not clear evidence that a separate regime will fail, where no application has been made (DL ¶12–13).
- F8(3) "compelling reasons" for enclosing a watercourse can be an allocation combined with the absence of another suitable access (DL ¶22).
- Land allocated in a broad location for growth at a town edge was decided under S4 (DL ¶22, ¶41).
- HO7 substantial weight is available where there is a five-year supply but an evidenced affordable housing shortfall (DL ¶44–45).
- Infrastructure contributions reduced for viability can meet the Regulation 122 tests and still count as moderate harm in the S4 balance (DL ¶38, ¶43).
