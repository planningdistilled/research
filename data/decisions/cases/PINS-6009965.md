## Summary
Change of use of a family house in Barking to a small house in multiple occupation (HMO) for up to four people. The inspector found the loss of a family home unjustified, the location poorly served by public transport for London (Public Transport Accessibility Level 0), and two bedrooms below the space standards. Parking was adequate. Under S4 the adverse effects substantially outweighed any benefits. Dismissed.

## Issues and findings
- **Transition.** A revised Framework "replaces previous iterations and the contents of which have been considered in the determination of this appeal" (DL ¶2).
- **Loss of a family home: CONFLICT.** "the removal of a single-family home from the existing housing stock in a location where there is a need for such accommodation would be harmful" (DL ¶5). The local policies "are broadly consistent with Framework policies relating to the delivery of a sufficient supply of homes" (DL ¶4).
- **Accessibility, TR3: CONFLICT (local policies).** "although the appeal site is located only approximately 0.4 miles from the nearest high frequency service bus stops on Longbridge Road, this is a significant distance in this London context" (DL ¶7). "it is likely that future residents would rely on use of a private car to undertake at least some of their day-to-day needs" (DL ¶7).
- **Living conditions: CONFLICT.** "there are two very small bedrooms that fall below the requisite … floorspace requirements" (DL ¶11; the … marks a page break).
- **Parking: PASS.** The proposal "would provide 3 car parking spaces and would therefore be unlikely to create a significant demand for on-street car parking" (DL ¶15).

## Planning balance
S4(1). "In this case, given the conflict with Framework policies relating to the sufficient supply of homes and living conditions, the adverse effects would substantially outweigh any benefits of approving the scheme" (DL ¶17). The Framework policies are not named. The letter treats the loss of a family dwelling as a conflict with the Framework's housing-supply policies, which is a use of those policies as a harm.

## What made the difference
The local plan's priority for family housing, with no evidence of need for an HMO, and two bedrooms measurably below the standard. The accessibility finding added to it: in London terms, a 0.4 mile walk to a frequent bus and a rating of 0 was a poor location.

## Transferable points
- Accessibility is judged in context: 0.4 miles to a high-frequency bus stop is "a significant distance in this London context" (DL ¶7).
- A Public Transport Accessibility Level score is used as evidence of poor connectivity in London, in the way the Connectivity Tool is used elsewhere (DL ¶7).
- An HMO licence does not settle planning acceptability (DL ¶5).
- Loss of a family dwelling was treated as conflicting with the Framework's housing-supply policies as well as the local plan (DL ¶4, ¶17).
