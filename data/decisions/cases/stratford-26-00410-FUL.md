## Summary
Three houses and a garage in place of one house at the edge of Tanworth-in-Arden, a Category 4 village washed over by the West Midlands Green Belt, on the house's curtilage and an adjoining conifer woodland. The officer ran S4 for the two plots inside the Built-Up Area Boundary and S5(1)(j) for the third, found the whole site grey belt and all four limbs of GB7(1)(g) met, and ran the S5(5) balance: modest landscape harm against substantial weight to a net gain of two homes at a 2.21-year supply. The Update Sheet replaced the report's bus evidence with two stations about a mile away. Committee granted on 7 October 2026; the decision notice is dated 8 October. The report, the Update Sheet and the results sheet are the sources; the minutes had not been published when this was written.

## Issues and findings
- **Weight to the development plan: Transitional(2), very limited weight.** CS.15, CS.16 and AS.10 are "materially inconsistent with the relevant NDMPs" because chapter 4 of the Framework "allow[s] for forms of development that would not be supported by" them "including where there is an evidenced unmet need, as highlighted in NDMP S5(1)(j)" (p.7). Under "Housing Land Supply": "This shortfall is particularly relevant to the weighting that I can give to the Development Plan" (p.10). CS.10 gets very limited weight "in light of the requirements set out in NDMPs GB7 and GB8" (p.11), CS.26 because it "does not include the same express requirement to refuse development" as TR6 (p.20), and CS.8 and BE2 because HE5 "provides a new set of criteria" (pp.17-18). NDP H1 gets very limited weight "accordingly" after the S6 finding (p.7).
- **S4 and S5(1)(j) in a washed-over village.** "Both Plots 1 and 2 of the proposed scheme, fall within the BUAB of Tanworth-in-Arden" so S4 applies, with S3(2) splitting the site (pp.6, 8); S5(1)(j) "does provide support for development which is well related to an existing settlement and where there is an evidenced unmet need, such as this" (p.9). The report then quotes S5(5) and defers the Green Belt question. Annex B's exclusion of "villages which lie within and are defined as part of the Green Belt" from "settlement" is not mentioned.
- **S6: not engaged.** The NDP "was made within the last 5 years (2022) but it does not contain sufficient housing allocations to meet its identified housing need" (p.7).
- **GB7(1)(e) and (c): FAIL for the scheme as a whole.** The curtilage is previously developed land and one replacement plot would pass (e), but two "could amount to substantial harm to the openness"; plot 3 is "not within the village" and fills no "clear gap", so (c) fails (p.11).
- **Grey belt, GB7(1)(g)(i): PASS.** Tanworth-in-Arden "is a village, a reasonably significant distance from any large built-up areas and towns", so no strong contribution to (a) or (b); no role in (d) (p.12). For "fundamentally undermine", purposes (c) and (e) were also tested and the encroachment found "limited" (p.13).
- **Unmet need, GB7(1)(g)(ii): PASS** on the 2.21-year figure (p.13).
- **Sustainable location, GB7(1)(g)(iii) / TR3: PASS.** "The proposed development would be well clustered with the existing settlement and whilst it would involve, walking a short distance up Well Lane, the village centre of Tanworth-in-Arden is readily accessible … Whilst the lack of made footpath on Well Lane is not ideal, given the more recent development, further to the east of the village, which also requires pedestrians to walk on the Well Lane highway to access the village, there is a clearly established pattern of development for pedestrians walking on Well Lane" (p.13). The bus stop "is in infrequent use" (p.13). "In respect of NDMP TR3(a), the development would not generate a significant amount of movement and there are opportunities available so not to be solely reliant on the use the private vehicle" (p.13). TR3(1)(b) is answered with "the existing bus service" (p.14); the Update Sheet corrects this to "the existing train service, with both Wood End and Danzey Green railway stations situated approximately 1 mile from the application site … Both stations operate an hourly train service". TR3(1)(e) improvements are "not proportionate" given the scale (p.14). The Connectivity Tool is not mentioned. No distance, speed limit, lighting or carriageway width is given.
- **GB8: not engaged.** "constitutes minor development for the purposes of the NPPF. Accordingly, the 'Golden Rules' set out in NDMP GB.8 do not apply" (p.14). The sentence giving the site area is lost at a page break in the OCR text.
- **Landscape and design.** Removal of the conifer group "would result in modest harm to the character of the streetscene and Special Landscape Area", with a landscaping condition offering long-term benefit (p.17); the garage forward of plot 3 is "generally discouraged" by the SPD but accepted on topography (p.16). Assessed against CS.5, CS.9, CS.12 and NDP policies; DP3 is not cited and no "clear justification" or S5(2) question is asked.
- **Heritage: no harm** to the conservation area or the setting of Grade II Aspley House (p.18).
- **Fallback.** Outline permission 24/01858/OUT for two houses on the curtilage, no reserved matters submitted: "moderate weight"; "I do not see the construction of proposed plots 1 and 2 or the improvement to the access to the site to be a contentious issue in principle" (p.25).

## Planning balance
"In accordance with the requirements of NDMPs S3, S4 and S5, the proposed development submitted in application 26/00410/FUL should be granted unless the adverse impacts substantially outweigh the benefits" (p.26). Against: "some harm the character of the local area, including the Arden Special Landscape Area, by virtue of the loss of mature trees". For: substantial weight to "a net increase of 2 dwellings"; the part outside the BUAB "would be physically well-related to Tanworth-in-Arden, which is a Category 4 Local Service Village and therefore a sustainable location capable of accommodating small-scale residential development"; limited weight to economic benefits, climate measures, biodiversity net gain and CIL. Conclusion: benefits "would not be substantially outweighed by any adverse effects" (p.26).

## What made the difference
GB7(1)(g)(iii) carried the scheme, and it was found without the facts the limb turns on. The report does not state the distance to the village centre, the speed limit or width of Well Lane, or a Connectivity Tool result; it concedes there is no footway and that the bus is infrequent, and treats other residents walking in the road as the answer. TR3(1)(a) is set aside because three houses are not "significant" movement, and TR3(1)(b), which is about optimising density where infrastructure exists, is used as the accessibility finding, first with the bus and then, by the Update Sheet on the day, with two stations about a mile away whose route and frequency were not tested against the "well-connected station" standard in GB7(1)(h). The Annex B question was not asked, so S4 and S5(1)(j) were run for a washed-over village; because S5(5) was also applied and gives the same tilt, that error did not change the result. Had the officer applied TR3 as inspectors have done since August (South Nutfield 6009966 ¶21, Copthorne 6011736 ¶4 to 7, Halsall 6007428 ¶13 to 16, Langley 6010253 ¶17 to 19), a carriageway walk with no footway would have been expected to fail limb (iii), making the scheme inappropriate and sending it to GB6(2).

## Transferable points
- SDC's third Tanworth report in a month uses the same template: CS.15, CS.16, AS.10, CS.10 and CS.26 "materially inconsistent", S4 or S5(1)(j) run first, GB7(1)(g)(iii) found on proximity to the village without a route assessment (compare stratford-26-00918-PIP).
- The Update Sheet changed the TR3 evidence from a bus to two stations a mile away on the day of committee, "in respect of TR3(b)", and summarised a five-email objection on GB7(1)(g)(iii) and TR3 in one bullet (Update Sheet pp.1-2).
- "Category 4 Local Service Village and therefore a sustainable location" (p.26) is the tier doing the work of TR3, in a report that gave the tier policy very limited weight nineteen pages earlier.
- Plan weight is cut for the supply shortfall in terms ("This shortfall is particularly relevant to the weighting that I can give to the Development Plan", p.10), the 2024 paragraph 11(d) logic carried into the 2026 Framework.
- An extant outline permission with no reserved matters was given moderate weight as a fallback and used to treat two of the three plots as uncontentious "in principle" (p.25).
