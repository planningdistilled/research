## Summary
Retention for three years of a modern pergola behind a Grade II listed villa used as a private hospital in Mill Hill. The inspector found harm to the building's significance through its setting, gave that harm considerable importance and weight, and held that comfort for patients and visitors is a private benefit, not a public one. Dismissed.

## Issues and findings
- **Transition.** "Whilst the format has altered, any considerations that are material to this decision have not fundamentally changed" (DL ¶5).
- **Harm: HE6(3).** "I therefore find that the proposal would result in harm to the significance of the designated heritage asset, to which I attach considerable importance and weight" (DL ¶10). The degree of harm is not graded.
- **Public benefits: HE6(4) FAIL.** "such advantages are inherently private in nature and do not amount to public benefits in the heritage context" (DL ¶11).
- **Temporary permission.** "the temporary nature of the development attracts only limited weight and does not affect my overall conclusion on the proposal" (DL ¶12).
- **Conflict.** "The development therefore conflicts with Framework Policy HE6" (DL ¶13).

## Planning balance
HE6(4): harm against public benefits, run on its own terms before the development plan conclusion. The letter attributes the weighing to "Policy HE5" (DL ¶10); the weighing is in HE6(4), and the conclusion at DL ¶13 cites HE6. The slip is incidental.

## What made the difference
The benefits were all to users of a private facility, and part of the structure only sheltered plant and lockers. A three-year limit did not help because the harm would last the whole period.

## Transferable points
- Comfort and convenience for users of a private healthcare facility are private benefits and do not count in the HE6(4) balance (DL ¶11).
- A temporary permission does not reduce heritage harm while the structure stands; it attracted only limited weight (DL ¶12).
- A structure that does not touch listed fabric can still harm significance through the loss of open space in the setting (DL ¶10).
