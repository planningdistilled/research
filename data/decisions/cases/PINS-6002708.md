## Summary
Permission in principle (PIP) for up to three homes at the northern edge of Lea was dismissed. The site is the last open buffer between modern housing and the Grade II* listed Castle End, and building on it would cause moderate harm to the asset's significance through its setting. Given considerable importance and weight, and greater weight for a Grade II* asset, that harm was not outweighed by three homes in an authority without a five-year supply.

## Issues and findings
- **Transition.** "The Council and the appellant have had opportunity to comment on this and I have taken into account any representations received" (DL ¶6).
- **Setting.** "in being mostly open from development, save for dilapidated outbuildings at the rear of the site, the appeal site does contribute positively to the setting of Castle End by being a remaining buffer" (DL ¶14).
- **HE6(1)/(3): harm, moderate.** "I find the degree of harm to the heritage asset to be at a moderate level but nevertheless is of considerable importance and weight" (DL ¶22).
- **HE6(4): FAIL.** "in giving considerable importance and weight to the harm to the significance of this designated heritage asset, I find that this would not be outweighed by the public benefits that the proposal would generate" (DL ¶25).
- **HE4(2): FAIL.** "the harm identified to the significance of the designated heritage asset has not been supported by clear and convincing justification" (DL ¶26).
- **Non-designated heritage assets (NDHA): not found.** "I conclude therefore on this matter that these structures do not merit NDHA status" (DL ¶20).
- **Character and appearance: no harm.** "the proposal would not harm the character and appearance of the surroundings" (DL ¶34).

## Planning balance
The heritage balance is run first (DL ¶23–26), then S5: "Framework Policy HE6 (3) sets out that any harm to a designated heritage asset will be a matter of ‘considerable importance and weight’" (DL ¶40). "I find therefore that, in accordance with Framework Policy S5, the benefits of the proposal would be substantially outweighed by the identified adverse effects" (DL ¶41). The letter does not say which S5(1) category applies. The weight given to the homes is stated two ways: moderate in ¶24 and ¶38, substantial in ¶39.

## What made the difference
The grade of the asset and the fact that this was the last undeveloped land between it and the modern estate. Limited intervisibility did not help: the open space mattered for how the listed building is experienced, and earlier development next door made the remainder more important. Nothing at PIP stage could show a layout that kept the buffer.

## Transferable points
- Setting harm does not depend on intervisibility: an open buffer can contribute to significance where views are limited by walls and planting (DL ¶10–11, ¶14).
- Development of neighbouring land increases the importance of what open setting remains (DL ¶16).
- Moderate harm to a Grade II* setting outweighs up to three homes without a five-year supply; HE6(1) gives greater weight for a more important asset (DL ¶22–25, ¶40).
- A council raising NDHA status late, on an assumption from historic maps, needs sound evidence of heritage merit (DL ¶17–20).
- At PIP stage, harm to setting from the amount and location of development is a matter of principle (DL ¶15).
