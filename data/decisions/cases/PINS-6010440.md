## Summary
An extension of a holiday park at Swarland onto seven holes of its golf course, with up to 180 more static caravans or lodges and a leisure hub, was allowed. The inspector found no harm to landscape or character because the site is enclosed by woodland, and decided the appeal on the development plan. The Framework appears only in the switch paragraph, the conditions tests and the conclusion.

## Issues and findings
- **Transition.** "In light of this I have sought the views of the main parties in writing and comments received have been taken into consideration when determining this appeal" (DL ¶2).
- **Landscape: no harm.** "the proposal would not be an overly obtrusive feature in the landscape and would be read as an extension to the existing rural tourism development" (DL ¶6).
- **Location.** "it is not unusual for developments of this type to be in rural locations which are not heavily supported by local services" (DL ¶12).
- **Conclusion.** "The proposed development would not conflict with the development plan as a whole, including the NP, and there are no other considerations, including the provisions of the Framework, which outweighs this finding" (DL ¶22).

## What made the difference
Enclosure. Mature woodland on most sides meant 180 lodges would not be seen from wider viewpoints, and the scheme read as part of the existing park. With no plan conflict found, the inspector did not go on to test the proposal against S5 or TR3, so the letter gives no guidance on how S5(1)(b) applies to rural tourism.
