 Appeal Decision
 Site visit made on 15 July 2026
 by JJ Packman MPlan MRTPI
 an Inspector appointed by the Secretary of State
 Decision date: 7 September 2026


 Appeal Ref: 6011109
 16 Cricket Green, Hartley Wintney, Hampshire, Hook, RG27 8PP
 •    The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
      against a refusal to grant planning permission.
 •    The appeal is made by Mark Ellis against the decision of Hart District Council.
 •    The application 26/00303/HOU.
 •    The development proposed is the installation of a new external soil and vent pipe.

Decision
1. The appeal is allowed and planning permission is granted for the installation of a
   new external soil and vent pipe at 16 Cricket Green, Hartley Wintney, Hampshire,
   Hook, RG27 8PP in accordance with the terms of the application 26/00303/HOU
   and the plans submitted. with it subject to the condition set out below:
     1) The development hereby permitted shall be retained in accordance with the
        following approved drawings: Location Plan, Block Plan, Front Elevation
        Existing, Front Elevation Proposed, Side Elevation Existing, Side Elevation
        Proposed.
Preliminary Matters
2. The description of development in the banner heading above has been adapted
   from the planning application form but shortened to more clearly describe the
   development the subject of this appeal. During my site visit, I observed that the
   external soil vent pipe had already been installed. I have determined the appeal on
   that basis.
3. Since the Council made its decision, the National Planning Policy Framework was
   revised in August 2026. The revised Framework is a material consideration in this
   appeal, and I have taken relevant national decision-making policies into account.
Main Issue
4. The main issue is the effect of the proposed external soil and vent pipe on the
   character and appearance of the host dwelling and the Hartley Wintney
   Conservation Area.
Reasons
5. The appeal property (No.16) forms part of the terrace of two-storey dwellings
   known as Nos. 10–19 Cricket Green situated within the Hartley Wintney
   Conservation Area. The Conservation Area derives much of its significance from

     the relationship between its historic buildings, mature landscaping and the open
     expanse of Cricket Green, which together create a distinctive village character.
6. The front elevation of Nos. 10–19 Cricket Green faces a narrow access path and,
   owing to the presence of the intervening terrace at Nos. 5–9 Cricket Green, is
   largely screened from wider public views. I observed that it has been subject to a
   range of modern alterations, including replacement materials, extensions, external
   fittings and the installation of a number of black plastic soil and vent pipes.
7. The rear elevation of the terrace is readily visible in views from Mount Pleasant and
   across Cricket Green. It retains a cohesive appearance, characterised by its
   brickwork, chimney stacks, roofscape and traditional architectural detailing,
   notwithstanding some domestic alterations. These qualities make an important
   contribution to the character and appearance of the Hartley Wintney Conservation
   Area.
8. The proposal is for the installation of an external black plastic soil vent pipe to serve
   a new first-floor WC. The pipe has been fixed to the front elevation of the appeal
   property, extending vertically from ground level to above the eaves before
   terminating above the roofline.
9. The soil and vent pipe introduces a modern plastic feature to the front elevation of
   the appeal property. Although this has resulted in a degree of harm to the character
   and appearance of the host dwelling and the Hartley Wintney Conservation Area, I
   find only a very limited degree of harm, not amounting to substantial harm.
10. The degree of harm is limited because the proposed soil vent pipe is located on the
    front elevation of the terrace, which is principally experienced from the narrow
    access path. Wider views are filtered by the intervening terrace at Nos. 5–9 Cricket
    Green. This elevation has been subject to a range of modern alterations, including
    replacement materials, extensions, external fittings and black plastic soil vent
    pipes. Whilst these alterations do not justify further change, they mean the proposal
    makes only a very limited additional contribution to the cumulative alteration of this
    elevation. Furthermore, the more cohesive rear elevation, which makes an
    important contribution to the character and appearance of the Conservation Area,
    remains unaffected by the proposals.
11. I have had regard to the Council's view that the soil vent pipe could be routed
    internally, thereby avoiding any visual impact. However, the appeal before me
    relates to the proposal as submitted. Whilst an internal solution may be possible, I
    have no detailed evidence before me regarding its feasibility or implications. In any
    event, the existence of an alternative solution does not alter my assessment of the
    effect of the proposal before me.
12. Policy HE6(4) of the National Planning Policy Framework (the Framework) requires
    that where a proposal would harm the significance of a designated heritage asset
    the effect on the asset and its significance should be weighed against any public
    benefits resulting from the proposal.
13. In this case, the public benefits are modest. They arise from facilitating the
    provision of a first-floor WC, enabling the dwelling to be adapted to meet the needs
    of its occupants and supporting its continued residential use. Given that I have
    found the heritage harm to be very limited, I find that this public benefit is sufficient
    to outweigh that harm.

14. Taking all of the above into account, and having paid special attention to the
    desirability of preserving the character and appearance of the Hartley Wintney
    Conservation Area, I conclude that the limited degree of harm identified would be
    outweighed by the public benefit of the proposal. The proposal therefore accords
    with Policy NBE8 of the Hart Local Plan (Strategy and Sites) 2016–2032, which
    seeks to conserve or enhance heritage assets having regard to their significance
    and the tests set out in the Framework, and Policy NBE9, insofar as it seeks to
    protect or enhance surrounding heritage assets, and the heritage objectives of the
    Framework.
Conditions
15. Whilst the development appears to have already been carried out, I have imposed
    a condition specifying the approved drawings in the interests of certainty.
Conclusion
16. For the reasons outlined above, and taking into account all other matters raised, I
    conclude that the appeal should be allowed.
