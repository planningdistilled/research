Site visit made on 6 August 2026

Decision date: 28 August 2026


Appeal Ref: 6009938
Bulls Head Public House, 96 Cambridge Street, St.Neots PE19 1PJ
•   The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
    against a refusal to grant planning permission.
•   The appeal is made by Mr Jeyaseelan Thambirajah Noble Trading (UK) Ltd against the decision of
    Huntingdonshire District Council.
•   The application Ref 25/01539/FUL, dated 5 August 2025, was refused on 30 December 2025.
•   The development proposed is the demolition of single-storey structure, change of use from former
    public house (Sui Generis) to convenience store (Use Class E) with ancillary first-floor
    accommodation and associated access and car parking area.

Decision
1. The appeal is allowed and permission is granted for the demolition of single-storey
   structure, change of use from former public house (Sui Generis) to convenience
   store (Use Class E) with ancillary first-floor accommodation and associated access
   and car parking area at Bulls Head Public House, 96 Cambridge Street, St.Neots
   PE19 1PJ, in accordance with the terms of the application, Ref 25/01539/FUL,
   dated 5 August 2025, subject to the attached schedule of conditions.
Main Issue
2. The main issue is whether the loss of the public house is justified.
Reasons
3. The former Bulls Head Public House is situated on the southern side of the B1428
   Cambridge Street about 500m east of the town centre. The road forms a main
   route out of the town with the section concerned primarily residential in nature
   although with some services nearby including an evangelical church, two petrol
   stations, a barber’s shop and a vet.
4. The premises lie within the eastern fringes of the St Neots Conservation Area (CA).
   There is no dispute that the demolition of the single storey structure and minor
   external alterations would not adversely affect the character or appearance of the
   CA but the addition of some signage and internal graphics to the ground floor
   windows would result in some less than substantial harm. However, since the
   building would remain in community use as a convenience store the public benefit
   of the proposal would outweigh this modest amount of harm.
5. The public house, owned by the Greene King brewery chain, operated with a long-
   term tenant until August 2023 when a further tenant could not be found. The public
   house closed at that time and a decision was made to sell the premises. Everard
   Cole, specialist surveyors in the licensed leisure/hospitality sector, were instructed

      to sell the property on 31 July 2023 and it was marketed from 15 August 2023 for
      £350,000 with a series of mailings, inclusion on websites including rightmove and
      an open viewing day on 6 September 2023. Over a fifteen-month marketing period
      there were a total of 38 enquiries, 16 viewings and one offer which was withdrawn
      after a serious water damage incident occurred in December 2023. After this the
      sale price was reduced to £350,000 and a sale was achieved (to the current
      appellant) on 1 November 2024.
6. Feedback from the viewings was poor including kitchen too small, no parking, poor
   condition, disrepair and too much work needed.
7. According to Everard Cole total sales in the last year of operation would have been
   about £140,000, gross profit £85,550, operating costs about £86,850 and after an
   allowance for necessary investment the ‘fair maintainable operating profit’ would
   have been negative, an annual loss of about £7,7001. The lack of offers on the
   basis of continued use as a public house is not therefore surprising.
8. Whilst there are no other public houses in the immediate vicinity there are nine
   within a 1 km radius as this includes St Neots town centre to the west with its wide
   variety of premises. These include Ye Old Sun, the Ale Taster, The Hyde Park and
   The Coach House. This competition was also a likely contributing factor to the lack
   of interest in a public house use.
9. Policy LM22(e) of the Huntingdonshire Local Plan 2019 (the HLP) allows the loss of
   a community facility such as a public house if there is no reasonable prospect of it
   being retained or restored because either (i) there is insufficient community support
   for its continuation or (ii) reasonable steps have been taken to effectively market
   the property for its current use without success2. The evidence on (i) is ambiguous
   but not necessary in any event as a judgement can be made in respect of (ii).
10. The Council argue that the marketing exercise was insufficiently robust as there is
    no proof that certain websites listed in the marketing report were actually used (an
    unwarranted allegation), that the premises were only actively marketed for eleven
    months (a gap whilst an offer proceeds is standard practice) and that the asking
    price was unrealistic. However, the numerous viewings and clear feedback
    indicate an effective marketing exercise. The asking price reflected achieved sales
    figures in the £275,000 - £375,000 range for a series of local comparable public
    houses. The Council draw attention to a similar appeal in Brampton3 which was
    dismissed but in that case marketing was brief, intermittent, and the asking price
    was not disclosed. Such concerns do not arise here. It can be concluded that
    reasonable steps were taken to market the public house without success.
11. For these reasons the loss of the public house has been sufficiently justified and
    complies with HLP Policy LM22 and Policy HC6 of the recently published National
    Planning Policy Framework4.
Other Matters
12. Whilst there are other convenience stores along Cambridge Street, in particular a
    SPAR to the east, LIDL to the west and two small stores associated with the petrol

        stations, there is no planning objection to a further store. Policy LM22 supports the
        provision of new community facilities within the built-up area and the floorspace of
        about 158 m² is of a scale which would serve local needs5.
13. The existing single storey shelter in the pub garden would be demolished and the
    garden used for car parking with six spaces (one disabled) and turning facilities
    accessed from Cambridge Street. Deliveries would be made to the front, like those
    previously to the public house, with refuse bins stored in and collected from a small
    compound off Shortsands Yard. The local highway authority are satisfied with
    these arrangements subject to conditions. Given the modest size of the store and
    its local role traffic generation and parking demand is likely to be relatively limited
    and not significantly greater than the previous public house use.
14. The car parking and turning area would be immediately adjacent to two detached
    houses in Shortsands Yard. Whilst this may generate some noise and disturbance
    the existing lawful pub garden use must be taken into account and the Council’s
    Environmental Health Officer considers the resulting noise levels, albeit of a
    different nature, are likely to be broadly comparable6. A noise impact assessment
    of the proposed air conditioning units has been prepared which demonstrates that
    predicted noise levels would be below measured background levels and thus would
    have minimal impact on nearby properties, again subject to conditions.
Conclusion
15. The loss of the public house has been sufficiently justified in this instance and the
    use as a convenience store would represent another, albeit different, community
    facility. The detailed proposals for the convenience store and its operation are
    satisfactory subject to a series of conditions.
16. The Council suggested several conditions should the appeal be allowed and these
    have been assessed against the relevant tests. In addition to the standard
    implementation time limit it is necessary to define the approved plans in the
    interests of certainty. Further conditions are necessary to ensure the car parking/
    turning area is provided and to control details of the car park access, visibility
    splays and drainage in the interests of highway safety. It is also necessary to limit
    the opening hours of the store and the operation of air conditioning units to protect
    the amenity of nearby residents. Following consultation with the main parties, a
    further condition is necessary to limit occupation of the first-floor accommodation to
    a person working for the business on the ground floor as the living conditions
    offered would not be suitable for an independent unit of accommodation.
17. The Council suggested a condition requiring further details of the materials to be
    used in the external surfaces of the development including a 1m² brickwork sample
    to be erected on site but this is unnecessary as the proposal is primarily a change
    of use of the existing building.
18. Having regard to the above the appeal should be allowed.

       turning area shall be laid out, demarcated, levelled, surfaced and drained in
       accordance with the approved plan and thereafter retained for that specific use.

    5. Prior to the commencement of the use hereby permitted visibility splays of 1.5m x
       1.5m shall be provided each side of the vehicular access measured from and
       along the back of the footway. Such splays shall thereafter be maintained free
       from obstruction exceeding 0.6m above the level of the footway.

    6. The access shall be constructed with adequate drainage measures to prevent
       surface water run-off onto the adjacent public highway in accordance with a
       scheme submitted to and approved in writing by the Local Planning Authority.

    7. The premises shall be open to customers only between 06:30 and 23:00 Monday
       to Saturday and 08:00 to 21:00 on Sundays, Bank Holidays and Public Holidays.

         Outside the hours specified, the operation of air conditioning units shall be limited
         to one unit (JEHS-0200-B2-M-1) and shall be operated in accordance with
         Chapter 4 of the approved Noise Impact Assessment by Nova Acoustics dated 23
         May 2025 and received by the LPA on 14/08/2025.

    8. The residential accommodation on the first floor shall only be occupied by the
       owner, co-owner or an employee of the commercial business on the ground floor
       and the dependents of such a person.
