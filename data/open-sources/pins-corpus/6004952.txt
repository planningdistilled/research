Appeal Ref: 6004952
Valera Ltd, Plox Brow, Tarleton, Lancashire PR4 6HB
•   The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
    against a refusal to grant planning permission.
•   The appeal is made by Bella Homes NW Ltd against the decision of West Lancashire Borough
    Council.
•   The application reference is 2025/0330/FUL.
•   The development proposed is residential development, including five dwellings and an apartment
    block (4 flats) with associated access, parking and landscaping following demolition of existing
    buildings.

Decision
1. The appeal is dismissed.
Preliminary Matter
2. Since the appeal was submitted, a revised National Planning Policy Framework
   (the Framework) has been published (August 2026). The parties were given the
   opportunity to comment on the implications of the revised Framework, and their
   responses have been taken into account. I have considered the appeal against the
   policy context now in place, including the revised Framework.
Main Issues
3. The main issues are:
           •    whether the proposed development would be appropriately located having
                regard to flood risk; and
           •    the effect of the proposed development on the existing and potential
                employment use of the site.
Reasons
4. The appeal site comprises a previously developed parcel of land within the
   settlement boundary of Tarleton. It is occupied by a former office building and
   warehouse premises associated with the former Valera food equipment
   manufacturing and distribution operation and, more recently, used for storage
   purpose. Access is taken from Plox Brow via an existing track. The surrounding
   area is characterised by a mixture of residential development, including nearby
   park home accommodation, together with areas of open land and watercourses,
   and commercial premises.

Flood Risk
5. The site lies within an area affected by flood risk. Policy GN3 of the West
   Lancashire Local Plan 2012-2027 Development Plan Document (October 2013)
   (the DPD) seeks to reduce flood risk through the location and design of
   development. In this respect, Policies GN3 and GN5 of the DPD are broadly
   consistent with the sequential approach in the Framework, which seeks to steer
   development towards areas of lowest flood risk. Policy F4(3) of the Framework
   indicates that a site-specific flood risk assessment should be proportionate to the
   scale, nature and location of the development.
6. The appellant contends that the submitted Flood Risk Assessment and Drainage
   Strategy (FRADS) demonstrates that the proposal should be exempt from the
   sequential test. The FRADS seeks to demonstrate that the proposed dwellings
   would be located outside the identified flood zone and that they could be made safe
   through measures including raised finished-floor levels, flood resilience measures
   and emergency planning. However, Policy F4(3) concerns the scope and content of
   a site-specific flood risk assessment and provides that such an assessment should,
   where relevant, support consideration of the sequential and exception tests.
7. Policy GN5 of the DPD and Policy F5 of the Framework require the sequential test
   to be applied in areas known to be at risk from flooding. Policy F5 identifies
   specified exceptions to this requirement. One such exception arises where a site-
   specific flood risk assessment clearly demonstrates that no built development,
   including access or escape routes, would be located within an area at risk from
   flooding, now or in the future.
8. The FRADS identifies part of the access serving the site within Flood Zone 2 and
   the more recent flood modelling identifies a greater extent of flood risk than was
   previously understood. Consequently, I do not consider that the proposal falls
   within the exception set out in Policy F5. The proposed dwellings being located
   outside the identified flood zones would not address the position of the access and
   escape route, which Policy F5 expressly requires to be considered.
9. A proportionate approach to an assessment does not displace the requirement in
   Policy F5 for a sequential test where the development is in an area known to be at
   risk from flooding and does not fall within one of the identified exceptions. Nor does
   the absence of an objection from the Environment Agency establish that such an
   exception applies.
10. While the FRADS provides detailed site-specific analysis and mitigation for the
    development, this is relevant to whether the development could be made safe in
    accordance with Policy F7 of the Framework, if permitted. The sequential test is
    required, and the FRADS does not undertake a wider comparison of reasonably
    available alternative sites, at lower risk of flooding, and capable of accommodating
    the development. Accordingly, I cannot conclude that the evidence before me
    demonstrates that the proposal satisfies the sequential test.
11. Drawing these matters together, I find that the proposed residential development
    would not be appropriately located having regard to flood risk. It would therefore
    conflict with Policies GN3 and GN5 of the DPD and with Policies F5 and CC3 of the
    Framework. Together, these policies seek to direct development towards areas at
    lower risk of flooding and minimise vulnerability to the effects of climate change.

Employment Use
12. The appeal site forms an established employment site and is occupied by buildings
    capable of employment use. Policy EC1 of the DPD permits the redevelopment of
    employment sites only in specific circumstances. Policy EC2 of the DPD requires it
    to be robustly demonstrated that a site is unsuitable for ongoing viable employment
    use. In a similar vein, Policy GN4 of the DPD seeks to retain land and premises in
    employment use unless there is evidence of non-viability, unsuitability, or a lack of
    demand. Against that background, the principal question is whether the evidence
    before me demonstrates that the continued employment use of the site is no longer
    appropriate.
13. The appellant states the site is no longer suitable for industrial or storage purposes.
    I acknowledge that the site is subject to operational constraints regarding its
    relationship with the surrounding residential area, the route to the strategic road
    network and the constraints associated with HGV movements through the
    settlement. However, the existence of such constraints does not demonstrate that
    the site is unsuitable for employment use. Rather, it is necessary to consider
    whether the limitations identified are such that the land and premises can no longer
    reasonably fulfil an employment function.
14. The relevant development plan policies are concerned with the suitability of the
    land and premises for employment use rather than the circumstances of a
    particular occupier or business model. The evidence does not demonstrate that the
    site is unsuitable for the range of protected employment uses that could reasonably
    operate from it. There is also limited evidence that options such as adapting the
    premises, redeveloping them for employment or finding a less intensive
    employment use have been fully explored.
15. The outline permission granted in 2020 for residential redevelopment of the site is a
    material consideration. At that time, the buildings were reported to have been
    unused since 2012 and the Council accepted, on the evidence then available, that
    the site’s location and the marketing undertaken justified its redevelopment.
    However, the premises were subsequently brought back into employment use for
    storage until November 2023. That intervening use demonstrates that,
    notwithstanding the identified operational constraints, the premises have recently
    been capable of fulfilling an employment function. In the absence of up-to-date
    evidence demonstrating that they are now unsuitable or unviable for such use,
    including through marketing, adaptation or occupation by a less intensive
    employment use, the circumstances materially differ from those underlying the
    earlier permission.
16. A comprehensive and up-to-date marketing exercise would not be necessary if the
    site’s unsuitability were otherwise established. However, this is a case where it
    might have assisted in demonstrating whether the identified constraints materially
    limit demand for the land and premises. In the absence of such information, and
    having regard to the other evidence before me, I am unable to conclude that the
    operational limitations are such that the employment function of the site should be
    relinquished.
17. Accordingly, while I recognise that the site is subject to certain operational
    constraints, the evidence before me does not demonstrate that the land and
    premises are no longer appropriate for employment use. Consequently, on the

Planning Balance
18. For the reasons given above, I have found that the proposal has not demonstrated
    that housing should be directed to this site having regard to flood risk and that the
    evidence before me is insufficient to justify the loss of its existing and potential
    employment use. These findings give rise to conflict with the DPD's policies relating
    to flood risk and the protection of employment land and premises. Taken together, I
    conclude that the proposal would conflict with the development plan as a whole.
19. Policy S3 of the Framework requires decisions on development proposals to apply
    a presumption in favour of sustainable development. As the appeal site lies within
    the settlement boundary, Policy S4 is applicable in this case. Policy S4 provides
    that development proposals within settlements should be approved unless the
    benefits of doing so would be substantially outweighed by any adverse effects
    when assessed against the national decision-making policies in the Framework.
20. Policy HO7 of the Framework states that substantial weight should be given to the
    benefits of providing homes which contribute towards meeting the evidenced
    accommodation needs of a community. Although the proposal is modest in scale, it
    would deliver 9 additional dwellings at a time when the Council is unable to
    demonstrate an adequate supply of deliverable housing sites. I therefore attach
    substantial weight to this benefit.
21. The proposal would redevelop previously developed land within the settlement
    boundary. Policy L2(1)(b) of the Framework directs substantial weight to the
    benefits of proposals that would make better use of vacant and underutilised land
    and buildings. Although the premises are presently vacant, they were used for
    storage until November 2023 and, for the reasons given above, it has not been
    demonstrated that they are no longer capable of continued employment use or that
    there is no demand for them. I am therefore not satisfied that the site is
    underutilised in the sense contemplated by Policy L2(1)(b). Consequently, the
    proposal does not attract the substantial weight directed by that policy.
    Nevertheless, the reuse of previously developed land is a benefit to which I attach
    limited weight.
22. The proposed landscaping, tree planting and biodiversity enhancements are further
    benefits, to which I also attach limited weight.
23. Set against those benefits, the proposal does not satisfy the sequential approach
    required by Policy F5 of the Framework and would therefore fail to accord with
    Policy CC3, which seeks to minimise vulnerability to flooding through the
    application of Policies F4 to F8. The absence of a sequential assessment means
    that I cannot conclude that housing should be located on this site when reasonably
    available sites at a lower risk of flooding may exist. This adverse effect attracts
    significant weight.
24. The proposal would also result in the loss of land and premises safeguarded for
    employment use without evidence sufficient to demonstrate that the safeguarding is

     no longer appropriate. That loss would reduce the stock of land and premises
     available for employment use and is an adverse effect to which I also attach

25. Drawing these matters together, I find that the benefits arising from the provision of
    additional housing and the redevelopment of previously developed land would be
    substantially outweighed by the adverse effects identified. Consequently, when
    assessed against the national decision-making policies of the Framework, the
    proposal would fail to satisfy Policy S4. Accordingly, the presumption in favour of
    sustainable development set out in Policy S3 does not indicate that planning
    permission should be granted.
Conclusion
26. The proposal would conflict with the development plan as a whole, and the material
    considerations do not indicate that the appeal should be determined otherwise than
    in accordance with it. Accordingly, for the reasons given, the appeal should be
    dismissed.
