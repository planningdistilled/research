 Appeal Decision
 Site visit made on 21 August 2026
 by N Perrins MRTPI
 an Inspector appointed by the Secretary of State
 Decision date: 24 September 2026


 Appeal Ref: 6010597
 43 Oriel Grove, Southdown, Bath BA2 1JE
 •    The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
      against a refusal to grant planning permission.
 •    The appeal is made by Mrs Brown against the decision of Bath and North East Somerset Council.
 •    The application Ref is 25/03601/FUL.
 •    The development proposed is the erection of a two storey side and rear extension.

Decision
1. The appeal is allowed and planning permission is granted for the erection of a two
   storey side and rear extension at 43 Oriel Grove, Southdown, Bath BA2 1JE in
   accordance with the terms of the application Ref 25/03601/FUL and subject to the
   following conditions:
     1) The development hereby permitted shall be begun before the expiration of three
        years from the date of this decision;
     2) The development hereby permitted shall be carried out in accordance with the
        following approved plans: (i) Proposed Plans and Elevations Rev B;
     3) The materials to be used in the construction of the external surfaces of the
        development hereby permitted shall match those used in the existing building.
Preliminary Matters
2. The National Planning Policy Framework (the Framework) was updated on 17
   August 2026. I am satisfied, however, that the relevant policy position for this
   appeal is not materially different and that its publication has not prejudiced any
   party. Accordingly, I have taken the current version of the Framework into account
   in reaching my decision.
Main Issue
3. The main issue is whether the proposed development would result in an
   overbearing impact on, and unacceptable overshadowing to, the occupiers of No.
   42 Oriel Grove.
Reasons
4. The appeal property forms one of a pair of semi‑detached dwellings situated on the
   outer side of Oriel Grove. The land slopes such that No. 42 Oriel Grove (No. 42),
   the unattached neighbour, sits at a slightly higher ground level to the appeal site.

5. The proposal comprises a two-storey rear extension projecting 2m beyond the
   existing rear wall and a two-storey side extension. The side extension would begin
   approximately halfway along the depth of the dwelling and extend around 8.2m to
   the rear, rising to an eaves height of around 5m to match the host dwelling, with a
   hipped roof above. It would be constructed on what is currently open
   hardstanding/patio space. The scheme would increase the number of bedrooms at
   the appeal property from three to five. There is no dispute between the parties
   regarding the proposed rear extension element of the proposal. I agree that this is
   acceptable and the assessment that follows focuses solely on the merits of the side
   extension component.
6. The proposed two-storey side extension would run along the shared boundary with
   No. 42, first beside its original two-storey side wall and then its single-storey rear
   projection. It would extend no further than that projection. It would not, therefore,
   run alongside or stand immediately adjacent to No. 42's main usable garden or
   amenity space, which from the information before me begins beyond the rear
   projection. Any increased sense of enclosure or dominance experienced from
   within No. 42's outdoor space would accordingly be confined to the side passage
   which, while a functionally usable part of the plot, did not appear, from what was
   visible from the appeal site, to be a primary space used for sitting out or otherwise
   enjoying outdoor amenity.
7. This outcome reflects the particular position of No. 42's own single-storey rear
   projection, which sets back the point at which its main garden and amenity space
   begins. Absent such a feature, a two-storey side extension built hard against a
   shared boundary would be more likely to result in an unacceptable degree of
   enclosure or overshadowing to a neighbour's principal garden area, which for the
   reasons set out does not arise here.
8. The higher ground level at No. 42 would further moderate the perceived height and
   dominance of the extension when viewed from the neighbour's land. Taking this
   together with the limited extent of No. 42's outdoor space affected, I do not find that
   the scale and proximity of the extension would, of itself, result in an unduly
   overbearing presence, or unacceptably affect the occupiers' enjoyment of their
   outdoor amenity space.
9. The flank elevation of No. 42 facing the boundary contains a small first-floor
   window, together with small ground-floor openings and a side door providing
   access into No. 42's rear projection. The window and ground-floor openings, given
   their modest dimensions, positions, and in some cases use of obscure glazing,
   likely serve non-habitable or secondary spaces such as circulation, utility, or
   sanitary facilities. The side door provides access to that projection and is unlikely to
   be a primary source of outlook. Even if any of these openings served a habitable
   room, their limited scale means that any reduction in outlook resulting from the
   proposed development would be modest and unlikely to be materially harmful to
   living conditions.
10. On overshadowing, neither party has submitted any technical shadow or sun-path
    assessment. Based on the spatial relationship observed during my site visit, I
    accept that a two-storey wall of the height and length proposed could cast shadow
    along the side passage at times during the day. However, this shadow would fall on
    an area that, as set out above, does not form part of No. 42's primary usable
    garden or amenity space. Given that the extension would extend no further than the

     single-storey rear projection, I am satisfied that any overshadowing would not
     extend materially into the garden beyond it. The higher ground level of No. 42
     would, in any event, further reduce its reach. In the absence of any evidence to
     demonstrate a significant effect on the usable garden itself, I am satisfied that the
     proposal would not unacceptably harm the usability or enjoyment of No. 42's
     principal outdoor amenity area.
11. In conclusion, while the proposed extension would represent a visible change along
    the boundary, its impact having regard to the site-specific circumstances in this
    case would be confined to No. 42's side passage area rather than its main usable
    garden or amenity space. Any overbearing impact or overshadowing affecting the
    garden beyond the rear projection, where that amenity is principally enjoyed, would
    be minor. Having regard to the mitigating effect of the higher ground level, No. 42's
    existing rear build line, and the absence of any materially affected habitable room
    windows, I am satisfied that the development would preserve an acceptable
    standard of living conditions for the occupiers of No. 42.
Other Matters
12. No objections were received from neighbouring occupiers. While the absence of
    representations is not determinative, it is consistent with my findings that the
    proposal would not result in unacceptable harm.
13. The proposal is acceptable in terms of design, character and appearance, and
    highway and parking considerations.
14. The appellant has referred to benefits relating to additional accommodation,
    accessibility and fostering. While I acknowledge that the additional bedroom and
    bathroom space would improve the functionality of the dwelling and provide modest
    benefits to the occupiers, I do not need to consider these matters further in this
    decision as I have found the proposal to be acceptable in any event.
Conditions
15. I have considered the Council’s suggested conditions having regard to the tests in
    the Framework and national planning practice guidance. In this regard, I agree that
    the standard time‑limit condition and a plans condition are necessary for certainty
    as to when development must commence, and to confirm what is approved. A
    condition to require materials match those used on the host dwelling is also
    necessary to safeguard the character and appearance of the area.
Conclusion
16. For the reasons given above, I conclude that the proposal would not result in
    unacceptable harm to the living conditions of the occupiers of No. 42. The
    development accords with Policy D6 of the CSPMP. There are no material
    considerations that indicate a decision contrary to the development plan.
17. The appeal is therefore allowed.
