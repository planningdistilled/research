Site visit made on 4 August 2026
by Gary Deane BSc (Hons) DipTP MRTPI
an Inspector appointed by the Secretary of State
Decision date: 17 August 2026


Appeal Ref: 6005849
17 Allesley Old Road, Coventry CV5 6QR
•   The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
    against a refusal to grant planning permission.
•   The appeal is made by Nafisa Akhtar against the decision of Coventry City Council.
•   The application reference is PL/2025/0001016/FUL.
•   The development proposed is the change of use from E class to C3 to consist 3 no one bedroom
    studio flat and 1 no 2 bedroom flat at 17 Allesley Old Road, Coventry.

Decision
1. The appeal is dismissed.
Procedural matters
2. While the description of development given in the above heading refers to the
   proposed change of use, the submitted plans additionally show various internal and
   external alterations to the appeal building and associated works within the site. I
   have assessed the proposed development on that basis.
3. The appellant is critical of the Council for determining the application before the
   expiry of an agreed time-period to allow for the submission and consideration of
   further acoustic information. My assessment is based on all the submitted
   evidence at both the application and appeal stages, which includes the appellant’s
   Environmental Noise Survey, dated 30 June 2025 (ENS), and the Internal Noise
   Survey, dated 2 January 2026 (INS). While I have had regard to the circumstances
   relating to the timing of the Council's decision, I have assessed the proposal on its
   own planning merits.
Main issues
4. The main issues are whether the proposed development would provide satisfactory
   living conditions for future occupiers, with particular regard to noise; and the effect
   of the proposal on the continued operation of The Four Provinces public house.
Reasons
Living conditions
5. The proposal is primarily to convert and alter the appeal property, which is a
   mid-terrace building, to form four self-contained flats. It adjoins The Four Provinces
   public house, which has an entertainment licence for live music, and faces a busy
   road near a junction. Given the potential for noise to disturb future occupiers, the

     Council is concerned that the submitted acoustic information does not adequately
     demonstrate that satisfactory living conditions would be achieved.
6. The INS addresses sound transmission from the adjoining public house using
   measurements undertaken when a DJ was operating. It considers a range of
   frequencies, including the lower frequencies associated with amplified music, and
   identifies additional treatment to the separating wall. With that treatment, the INS
   predicts an internal sound pressure level of about 21 dB(A) from the assessed
   source. It concludes that the resulting internal noise levels would be significantly
   below the relevant night-time guideline. I am therefore satisfied that noise
   transmission through the separating wall has been adequately addressed and
   could be covered by conditions.
7. The ENS also specifies mitigation measures including acoustic glazing and
   acoustic ventilators designed to achieve the required acoustic performance. Taken
   together with the treatment to the separating wall with the public house, I am
   satisfied that satisfactory internal living conditions can be achieved without reliance
   on a ventilation arrangement that could result in an oppressive internal environment
   for future occupiers.
8. However, the position in relation to the external amenity space at the side and rear
   of the appeal building is different. The INS identifies the monitoring position on the
   north-east façade of the building that overlooks that area and records a daytime
   noise level of 68.4 dB LAeq,16hr. This is substantially above the desirable value of
   50 dB and the upper guideline value of 55 dB for external amenity space that is
   referred to in the submitted acoustic evidence.
9. These values are guidelines rather than absolute limits. BS 8233:2014 "Guidance
   on sound insulation and noise reduction for buildings" acknowledges that the
   guidelines will not be achievable in all circumstances, and that development may
   nevertheless be desirable. In such cases, the objective is to achieve the lowest
   practicable noise levels within external amenity areas rather than necessarily
   preventing development.
10. In that regard, the appellant places some reliance on the ENS, which recommends
    a 1.8 metre close-boarded acoustic barrier and predicts that this would reduce
    garden noise to between 47.8 and 52.8 dB LAeq,T. That prediction is based on a
    daytime level of 57.8 dB and assumed attenuation from the proposed barrier of
    between 5 and 10 dB. However, I am not satisfied that the 57.8 dB baseline
    provides a sufficiently reliable basis for assessing the proposed garden.
11. Specifically, the ENS's tabulated results attribute 57.8 dB to Position 1, while
    Position 2 records 67.3 dB during the daytime. Nevertheless, its external amenity
    assessment attributes the 57.8 dB figure to Position 2 when deriving the predicted
    post-mitigation garden levels. This inconsistency reduces the weight I can attach to
    the resulting prediction.
12. The INS provides a clearer basis for considering this matter. It records a daytime
    level of 68.4 dB LAeq,16hr at the garden-facing location. Even applying the
    maximum claimed attenuation of 10 dB to this measurement, the resultant level of
    58.4 dB would remain above the upper guideline value of 55 dB. I am therefore not
    satisfied that the appellant’s earlier prediction of 47.8–52.8 dB represents the
    conditions likely to be experienced within the proposed garden.

13. I have carefully considered whether the flexibility recognised by BS8233 would
    nevertheless justify the proposed development. However, the residual exceedance
    would not be marginal. Furthermore, there is no evidence before me of further
    practicable mitigation or alternative amenity provision that would satisfactorily
    address it. I am therefore not satisfied that the proposal demonstrates that the
    lowest practicable noise levels would be achieved within the proposed external
    amenity area or that it would provide satisfactory conditions for future occupiers.
14. A condition requiring the proposed fence as a barrier would secure the mitigation
    already assessed, but it would not overcome this shortfall. Similarly, post-
    completion verification would establish the performance of the completed scheme
    rather than demonstrate that an acceptable external environment is capable of
    being achieved. Consequently, this is not a matter that could be left to conditions.
15. On the first main issue, I conclude that, notwithstanding that satisfactory internal
    conditions could be achieved, the proposed development would not provide
    satisfactory living conditions for future occupiers with regard to external noise. It
    therefore conflicts with Policies DS3 and H3 of the Coventry Local Plan insofar as
    they seek to secure a satisfactory residential environment. It is also contrary to the
    relevant provisions of the National Planning Policy Framework (the Framework)
    that concern the effects of noise on health and quality of life.
The adjoining public house
16. The proposal would introduce a noise-sensitive use adjoining the public house,
    which is an established use. The responsibility for mitigating the potential effects
    on the operation of the existing business therefore rests with the new development.
17. The proposed internal acoustic measures do not depend upon restrictions to the
    operation of the public house. Although the possibility of future complaints from
    occupiers of the appeal building cannot be ruled out, the evidence does not
    demonstrate that unreasonable restrictions upon the business would be likely.
18. On the second main issue, I conclude that the proposal would be compatible with
    the continued operation of the adjoining public house. It is therefore consistent with
    Policy EM15 of the emerging Coventry Local Plan Review (Regulation 19 Proposed
    Submission) and the Framework concerning the relationship between noise-
    sensitive development and existing businesses. This favourable finding does not
    overcome the harm that I have identified in relation to the first main issue.
Other matters
19. The appeal property lies within the Chapel Fields Conservation Area (CA) and
    forms part of a row of former watchmakers' dwellings, which contribute positively to
    its established character. I have paid special attention to the desirability of
    preserving or enhancing the character or appearance of the CA, as required by
    section 72(1) of the Planning (Listed Buildings and Conservation Areas) Act 1990.
20. Few changes are sought to the front façade, with alterations to the fenestration at
    the side and rear and a small new dormer and roof light within the main rear roof
    slope. Given the modest scale and position of these alterations, which are largely
    away from the road, the character and appearance of the CA would be preserved.
    The related site works, including bicycle and bin storage, boundary treatment and
    pedestrian access, would also be limited in nature and scale. Nothing before me

     indicates that they would result in an unacceptable effect upon neighbouring

21. The Council found the principle of residential development acceptable and
    identified no unacceptable effects in relation to highway safety, internal space
    standards or the living conditions of neighbouring occupiers. From the submitted
    evidence, I have no reason to reach a different conclusion on these matters.
Planning balance
22. I have found that satisfactory internal living conditions can be achieved, and that
    the proposal would be compatible with the continued operation of the adjoining
    public house. The character and appearance of the CA would also be preserved;
    this is a neutral matter in the planning balance. The proposal would bring a vacant
    building in disrepair back into use and would also provide four additional dwellings.
    I attach moderate weight to these benefits.
23. However, the evidence does not demonstrate that satisfactory conditions for future
    occupiers would be achieved within the proposed external amenity space. I attach
    substantial weight to this harm and the resulting conflict with the development plan.
    The benefits of the proposal do not outweigh it.
Conclusion
24. I therefore conclude that the proposal would conflict with the development plan
    when read as a whole. There are no material considerations which indicate that the
    appeal should be determined otherwise. The appeal is therefore dismissed.
