 by N Perrins MRTPI
 an Inspector appointed by the Secretary of State

 Appeal Ref: 6011602
 28A Ridley Road, London NW10 5UA
 •    The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
      against a refusal to grant planning permission.
 •    The appeal is made by Mr Paul Kaffel against the decision of the London Borough of Brent.
 •    The application Ref is 26/0191.
 •    The development proposed is a new rear staircase leading to a new side door to first floor flat.

Decision
1. The appeal is allowed and planning permission is granted for a new rear staircase
   leading to a new side door to the first floor flat at 28A Ridley Road, London NW10
   5UA in accordance with the terms of the application Ref 26/0191 and subject to the
   following conditions:
     1) The development hereby permitted shall be begun before the expiration of three
        years from the date of this decision.
     2) The development hereby permitted shall be carried out in accordance with the
        following approved plans: (i) Location Map (1:1250); (ii) Proposed Plans Ref:
        002 REV 02; and (iii) Existing and Proposed Elevations Ref: 003 REV 02.
     3) The external staircase and landing hereby permitted shall be finished in black
        powder-coated steel, and the first floor side door shall be finished in uPVC to
        match the existing rear windows of the building. The staircase and landing shall
        be retained in that finish thereafter.
Preliminary Matters
2. The National Planning Policy Framework (the Framework) was updated on 17
   August 2026. I am satisfied, however, that the relevant policy position for this
   appeal is not materially different and that its publication has not prejudiced any
   party. Accordingly, I have taken the current version of the Framework into account
   in reaching my decision.
Main Issue
3. The main issue is the effect of the development on the character and appearance
   of the host property and wider area.

Reasons
4. The appeal site comprises the first-floor flat of a two-storey mid-terraced Victorian
   property located on the north-western side of Ridley Road. The surrounding area is
   predominantly residential, characterised by terraced housing with traditional rear
   outriggers and enclosed rear gardens.
5. The proposal comprises the installation of an external metal staircase running
   along the flank of the rear outrigger, providing access from a newly created side
   door at first-floor level down to the rear garden. The staircase would measure
   around 0.85m in width, with the main flight extending approximately 4.5m along the
   side passage as it descends from first floor to ground level. It would be constructed
   in black powder-coated steel, and the new door would be finished in uPVC to
   match the existing rear windows.
6. Policy DMP1 of the Brent Local Plan (2019–2041) requires development to be of a
   siting, scale, materials, detailing and design that complements the locality. The
   Framework seeks development that is sympathetic to local character, while not
   preventing or discouraging appropriate innovation or change.
7. The staircase would be seen from the rear gardens and upper-floor rear windows of
   some neighbouring properties. However, it would be narrow, of open, lightweight
   steel construction, and tucked into the side passage between the outrigger and the
   boundary. It would sit below the eaves of the outrigger and would not alter the roof
   form or the proportions of the host building. In views from the rear, it would read as
   a modest, secondary structure against the flank wall of the outrigger, rather than a
   prominent addition. Given its width of 0.85m and its confinement to the side
   passage, I find nothing to indicate that it would be out of scale with the host
   building. Accordingly, I do not agree with the Council that it would be highly
   prominent or visually jarring.
8. The rear of the terraces is also not uniform. At my site visit I saw variation in the
   rear elevations of the surrounding properties and in their detailing. The staircase
   would be seen in that varied context rather than against an unaltered historic rear
   elevation. As such, it would sit inconspicuously within the rear townscape of the
   terraces and would not harm the character or appearance of the host property or its
   surroundings.
9. The Council contends that external staircases are not a feature of the immediate
   area, and that the proposal would therefore appear out of place. While I saw no
   external staircases on the rear of the nearest surrounding properties, the absence
   of similar features in the vicinity does not in itself mean that a proposal would cause
   harm. For the reasons given above, this staircase would be discreetly sited and
   would not harm the character or appearance of the host property or the area. I have
   therefore not needed to rely on the permission at No 6A (Ref 16/1199), although I
   note that the Council does not dispute its earlier finding that a comparable staircase
   in the same row was acceptable, albeit in a different development plan context. The
   other examples the appellant cites are on the opposite side of Ridley Road and are
   not seen in the same context as the appeal site, and they weigh neither for nor
   against the proposal.
10. In conclusion, the proposal would not harm the character and appearance of the
    host property or the surrounding area. It would accord with Policy DMP1 of the
    Brent Local Plan, and with the design policies of the Framework.

Other Matters
11. At my site visit I saw that the first floor flat has no internal access to the garden.
    The proposal would therefore provide its occupiers with direct access to outdoor
    space, which is a modest benefit. As I have found no harm, this benefit is not
    decisive, but it adds some support to my conclusion.
12. Although the Council did not cite living conditions in its reason for refusal, its appeal
    statement argues that, as the first floor flat has no existing garden access, the
    staircase would give its occupiers access to the garden, from which they could
    overlook the ground floor flat and erode its defensible space. This is inconsistent
    with the officer's report that found no harm to neighbouring amenity. Given its
    narrow width and small landing, the staircase itself would be used for access rather
    than sitting out. Any views to and from the staircase would be of the kind that is
    normal where a garden serves more than one flat in a converted house. The rights
    of each flat to use the garden are also a private matter between the owners and
    occupiers. I am therefore satisfied that the proposal would not cause unacceptable
    harm to the living conditions of the occupiers of the ground floor flat and accords
    with Policy DMP1 in this respect.
13. The Council also argues that allowing the appeal would encourage external
    staircases across the borough. However, each proposal is determined on its own
    merits. The appeal proposal is acceptable because of its discreet rear position, lack
    of street visibility and site-specific context. Allowing it would not prevent the Council
    from resisting harmful proposals elsewhere.
Conditions
14. I have considered what conditions are required having regard to the tests in the
    Framework and national planning practice guidance. In this regard, the standard
    time‑limit condition and plans condition are necessary for certainty as to when
    development must commence, and to confirm what is approved. A condition
    specifying the finish of the staircase, landing and door is necessary to protect the
    character and appearance of the area.
Conclusion
15. For the reasons given above, the proposal accords with the development plan
    taken as a whole, and there are no material considerations that indicate a decision
    other than in accordance with it. The appeal is therefore allowed.
