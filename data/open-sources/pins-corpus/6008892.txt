Appeal Decision
Site visit made on 1 September 2026
by K Reeves BA (Hons) MA MRTPI
an Inspector appointed by the Secretary of State
Decision date: 02 October 2026


Appeal Ref: 6008892
Thurlestone Golf Club, Links Court Service Roads, Thurlestone, Devon TQ7 3NZ
•    The appeal is made under Section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
     against a refusal to grant planning permission.
•    The appeal is made by Thurlestone Golf Club against the decision of South Hams District Council.
•    The application Ref is 2345/25/FUL.
•    The development is retention of a temporary hospitality trailer at land to the south of the golf course,
     adjacent to the South West Public Footpath.

Decision
1.    The appeal is dismissed.
Preliminary Matter
2.    At the time of my site visit, the appeal development, a hospitality trailer, was
      already in situ. I have therefore determined the appeal on the basis that the
      development is retrospective.
Main Issue
3.    The main issue is the effect of the development on the character and appearance
      of the area, with particular regard to the South Devon National Landscape (SDNL)
      and the Undeveloped Coast.
Reasons
4.    The appeal site occupies a position on the South Devon coast adjacent to the
      South West Coast Path between Thurlestone golf course and Leasfoot Beach. It is
      within the SDNL and the Undeveloped Coast. The South Devon AONB
      Management Plan 2019 – 2024 describes the coastline as largely undeveloped,
      wild and rugged. The shore here is defined by resistant rock formations, intimate
      coves and a long-established naturalness that has altered little over time. The
      Management Plan identifies this wider coastal character as one of the SDNL's
      core landscape qualities, highlighting the interplay of hard-rock cliffs, sandy coves,
      expansive sea views and pronounced tranquillity that together create a strong and
      distinctive scenic identity.
5.    The landscape's relatively undeveloped character is readily apparent along this
      section of the coast path. Built form in the wider area is either concealed behind
      the dune system or set sufficiently far back from the shoreline that it does not
      detract from the experience of a largely natural and open coast. Consequently, this
      part of the route maintains a strong visual relationship with the coastline, with

      development generally remaining a subsidiary element when viewed from the
      coast path.
6.    The trailer occupies a prominent position immediately adjacent to the coast path. It
      is readily visible to users of the path and appears against a backdrop which is
      otherwise largely open and undeveloped. Notwithstanding its modest dimensions,
      its form, colouring and commercial function draw attention to it within the coastal
      scene. Unlike the more established development associated with the golf club,
      which is set well back from the coast, the trailer is experienced directly within the
      immediate path corridor and therefore has a much greater influence on the
      character of the route.
7.    The appellant emphasises that the trailer is small in scale, seasonal, mobile and
      fully reversible. It is present only between April and October, is not fixed to the
      ground and can be removed from the site without leaving permanent infrastructure.
      The appellant also contends that it is experienced within an active recreational
      environment associated with the golf course, beach and South West Coast Path
      rather than within an isolated or untouched coastal landscape. Furthermore,
      mitigation measures are proposed, including muted external colours, the removal
      of signage, the absence of external seating, operational controls and noise
      mitigation, together with a willingness to accept conditions, a temporary consent
      and a review period.
8.    I have taken these matters into account. The trailer's modest dimensions,
      seasonal presence and reversible nature distinguish it from permanent built
      development, and the proposed controls relating to its appearance and operation
      would lessen its effects. Likewise, I have had regard to the appellant's willingness
      to accept a temporary permission, review period and other planning controls.
      Nevertheless, these measures would not overcome the fundamental issue arising
      from the siting of the trailer in this location. Whilst the site forms part of a wider
      recreational area, development along this stretch of coastline remains limited and
      generally recessive when experienced from the coast path. The trailer therefore
      remains an incongruous commercial feature that fails to reflect the undeveloped
      and largely natural character of its surroundings. I am not persuaded that the
      mitigation proposed, whether individually or in combination, would overcome the
      harm identified above.
9.    For these reasons, the development causes unacceptable harm to the character
      and appearance of the area, with particular regard to the SDNL and the
      Undeveloped Coast. Consequently, it conflicts with Policies SPT1, DEV20,
      DEV23, DEV24, DEV25 and DEV36 of the Plymouth and South West Devon Joint
      Local Plan 2019 – 2034 and Policies TP1, TP17, TP22 and TP23 of the
      Thurlestone Parish Neighbourhood Plan 2015 – 2034, which collectively require
      development to conserve and enhance the natural beauty, landscape character
      and scenic qualities of the protected landscape, preserve the coast's undeveloped,
      tranquil and visually sensitive character, avoid isolated or incongruous built form,
      and prevent activity that would diminish the quiet enjoyment of this protected
      stretch of shoreline.
10. Furthermore, having had regard to the duty under Section 85 of the Countryside
    and Rights of Way Act 2000 (as amended), I find that the development fails to
    conserve and enhance the natural beauty of the SDNL and does not further the
    purpose of its designation.

Planning Balance
11. Policy S3 of the National Planning Policy Framework (the Framework) states that
    decisions on development proposals should apply a presumption in favour of
    sustainable development and, in relation to development outside settlements, that
    Policy S5 of the Framework should be applied.
12. Policy S5 states that only certain forms of development should be approved
    outside settlements, including development for rural businesses and services,
    such as tourism, where a location outside a settlement is shown to be necessary.
    The policy further states that such development should be approved unless the
    benefits of doing so would be substantially outweighed by any adverse effects
    when assessed against the national decision-making policies in the Framework.
13. The appellant contends that the trailer must be positioned close to the coast path,
    beach and lower part of the golf course in order to serve its intended users. Given
    the nature of the facility and the users it is intended to serve, I accept that a
    location outside a settlement is reasonably necessary. To that extent, the
    development attracts support under Policy S5.
14. In addition, Policies E2 and E4 of the Framework recognise the benefits of
    supporting economic growth, business investment and the expansion of rural
    businesses, including tourism-related development. The trailer provides
    refreshments for golfers, beach users and users of the South West Coast Path and
    contributes to the operation of an established rural business. It therefore provides
    tourism, recreational and economic benefits and attracts support under Policies
    S5, E2 and E4 of the Framework. However, those benefits are limited by the
    development's small scale and seasonal nature. I therefore afford them moderate
    weight.
15. Weighing against the development is my finding that it causes unacceptable harm
    to the character and appearance of the area, with particular regard to the SDNL
    and the Undeveloped Coast. The development introduces an isolated commercial
    feature into a part of the coastline whose openness, naturalness and absence of
    built form are defining characteristics. The Framework requires substantial weight
    to be given to conserving and enhancing the natural beauty of protected
    landscapes.
16. Whilst I have taken into account the benefits associated with supporting a rural
    business and tourism activity, together with the support provided by Policies S5,
    E2 and E4 of the Framework, I find that those benefits are modest. Taking these
    matters together, I conclude that the benefits of the development are substantially
    outweighed by its adverse effects when assessed against the national decision-
    making policies in the Framework.
Conclusion
17. For the reasons given above, the development conflicts with the development plan
    and the material considerations do not indicate that the appeal should be decided
    other than in accordance with it. Accordingly, the appeal should be dismissed.
