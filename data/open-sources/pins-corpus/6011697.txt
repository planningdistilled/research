Appeal Ref: 6011697
27-31 Westgate, Peterborough PE1 1YH
•    The appeal is made under Regulation 17 of the Town and Country Planning (Control of
     Advertisements) (England) Regulations 2007 (as amended) against a refusal to grant express
     consent.
•    The appeal is made by Lloyds Banking Group against the decision of Peterborough City Council.
•    The application Ref is 26/00056/ADV.
•    The advertisement proposed is described as new external banking signage.

Decision
1.     The appeal is allowed and express consent is granted for the display of the1no
       internally illuminated fascia sign, 1 no internally illuminated projecting sign, 2no
       internally illuminated ATM surrounds and 1no address panel at 27-31 Westgate,
       Peterborough PE1 1YH in accordance with the terms of the application, Ref
       26/00056/adv, dated 1 January 2026. The consent is for five years from the date
       of this decision and is subject to the five standard conditions set out in Schedule 2
       of the 2007 Regulations and the following additional condition:
       1. The luminance of the internally illuminated advertisements hereby granted
          consent shall not exceed the candela per square metre and the level of
          illumination within the limits recommended by the Institution of Lighting
          Professionals in the publication "PLG05 The Brightness of Illuminated
          Advertisements" (or any such publication that revokes or replaces that
          publication with or without amendment).
Preliminary Matters
2.     In allowing the appeal I have used the description of development in accordance
       with the Council’s decision notice as this more accurately reflects the description of
       the proposal.
3.     The Town and Country Planning (Control of Advertisements) (England)
       Regulations 2007 (the Regulations), the National Planning Policy Framework (the
       Framework) and the national Planning Practice Guidance (PPG) confirm that
       advertisements should be subject to control only in the interests of amenity and
       public safety. Regard does not need to be had to the development plan. I have
       taken relevant policies into account as a material consideration; however, they
       have not, by themselves, been determinative for this appeal.
4.     The Council raises no concerns in relation to public safety. Based on the evidence
       before me and my site visit, I see no reason to take a contrary view to the Council
       on these matters.

5.     A new National Planning Policy Framework (the Framework) was published on 17
       August 2026. This has introduced significant changes to its structure and
       presentation, including the incorporation of development management policies.
       The Council and the appellant were invited to make further comments. My decision

Main Issue
6.     The main issue is the effect of the proposal on visual amenity.
Reasons
7.     The host property is a modern 20th-century brick building with a modernist
       shopfront located to the south side of Westgate. The appeal site lies within the
       Peterborough Conservation Area (CA) and is proximate to a number of heritage
       assets.
8.     Although advertisement consent is concerned solely with the interests of amenity
       and public safety, the effect of the proposal on designated heritage assets is a
       material component of the amenity assessment. Accordingly, substantial
       importance and weight must be given to the statutory duties under Section 66(1)
       and 72(1) of the Planning (Listed Buildings and Conservation Areas) Act 1990 to
       preserve the setting of listed buildings and preserve or enhance the character or
       appearance of the conservation area.
9.     The CA encompasses the historic heart of the city, including the medieval
       Cathedral and Precincts, together with other prominent landmarks such as the
       Church of St John the Baptist and the Guildhall. The city centre has largely
       retained its medieval street pattern. However, its townscape is predominantly
       characterised by 19th and 20th century development, with only a limited number of
       buildings surviving from earlier periods. Commercial uses are prevalent throughout
       the CA, reflecting its role as the principal commercial and civic focus of the city.
10. The Bull Hotel is a Grade II listed building located on the opposite side of the road
    to the north-east of the appeal site. It is a late eighteenth century colour-washed
    stone and brick building beneath a tiled roof and comprises two storeys with attic
    accommodation and hipped dormers. Its significance is principally derived from its
    evidential and historic value as a surviving eighteenth century coaching inn,
    together with its architectural interest, reflected in its traditional form, materials and
    detailing. The building makes a positive contribution to the historic character and
    appearance of the town centre and is appreciated as a prominent heritage asset
    within the street scene.
11. Located some distance away to the west of the appeal site is no 53 Westgate,
    formerly known as the Royal Hotel, a Grade II listed building. It is an eighteenth-
    century two-storey building constructed of ashlar under a slate roof, with stone
    steps and iron railings to the principal entrance. Insofar as is relevant to this
    appeal, its significance is derived from its architectural and historic interest as a
    surviving example of late eighteenth-century development within the town. The
    quality of its external finish, traditional form and detailing, together with its historic
    association as a former hotel, contribute to its special interest. The building also
    makes a positive contribution to the character and appearance of the historic
    streetscape.

12. My attention has been drawn to the entrance to the Westgate Arcade, the Beales
    department store and No 33 Westgate as buildings of local interest. Whilst I have
    limited information before me regarding the history of these buildings, I observed
    them at my site visit and concur that these buildings provide visual interest and
    denotes the historic evolution of the city over the centuries.
13. In contrast, the appeal building is a three-storey building that has a contemporary
    brick frontage with limited architectural detailing at all its levels The appeal building
    has a neutral effect on the significance of the significance of the CA. Furthermore,
    whilst the appeal site and the Bull Inn and No 53 Westgate are visible within the
    same street scene, intervisibility alone is insufficient to establish that the appeal
    site forms part of the asset's setting. I have therefore considered whether the
    appeal site's surroundings contribute to the significance of the heritage assets. In
    this regard, I find that do not make a meaningful contribution to that significance.
14. ‘Amenity’ as defined in the PPG, is usually understood to mean the effect on visual
    and aural amenity in the immediate neighbourhood of an advertisement. The PPG
    advises that the local characteristics of an area are relevant when assessing
    amenity, including the presence of any feature of historic, architectural, cultural or
    similar interest.
15. It is the 2no internally illuminated ATM surrounds that the Council objects to which
    are located on the left side of the commercial premises. The Appellant advances
    that the LED tubing surround forms part of the bank’s corporate branding.
16. The appeal shopfront has a modern appearance, a characteristic shared by a
    number of nearby commercial premises. There is no dispute between the parties
    that the original ATM and its advertisement surround had consent, including
    individually lit fascia lettering (halo illumination) and internally illuminated projecting
    signs.
17. I find that the internally illuminated surround of the ATM would sit comfortably
    within the contemporary façade. The evidence indicates that they are of a similar
    size, design and position to branding around the original ATM. Consequently, they
    do not appear visually intrusive or incongruous within the wider street scene and
    would not materially harm the visual amenity of the area.
18. The Council contends that the green LED luminance level of the appeal
    advertisement is unacceptable. I have considered whether any perceived harm
    could be addressed through the imposition of a condition restricting or
    extinguishing the illumination during certain hours, as is agreeable by the
    Appellant. However, given my finding that the proposed ATM surrounds would not
    materially harm visual amenity, I see no justification for such a restriction. In these
    circumstances, a condition controlling the illumination in this manner would neither
    be necessary nor reasonable and would therefore fail to satisfy the relevant tests
    for planning conditions.
19. The display would be subject to the standard advertisement consent conditions,
    which require the advertisement to be maintained in a manner that preserves
    visual amenity and public safety. In the interests of highway safety, the illuminance
    of the advertisement is to be restricted.
20. I have had regard to Policies LP16 and LP19 of the Peterborough Local Plan
    2016-2036 (2019). Policy LP16 states that all development proposals are expected

Other Matters
21. The Council has not raised any concerns regarding the internally illuminated fascia

Conclusion
22. For the reasons given I conclude that the appeal is allowed.
