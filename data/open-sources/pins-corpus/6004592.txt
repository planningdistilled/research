Site visit made on 28 July 2026

Appeal Ref: 6004592
14 St Catherines Road, Hayling Island, Hampshire PO11 0HE
•    The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended) against
     a refusal to grant outline planning permission.
•    The appeal is made by Mr Alan Capaldi-Tallon against the decision of Havant Borough Council.
•    The application Ref is APP/25/00185.
•    The development proposed is outline application for construction of a single storey two bed dwelling to
     the west garden area with a new access from St Catherines Road to become 14A St Catherines Road.

Decision
1.    The appeal is dismissed.
Preliminary Matters
2.    The application is in outline form with approval sought for access, layout and scale,
      whilst appearance and landscaping are reserved matters. As far as any details
      relating to those reserved matters have been provided on the submitted plans, I have
      treated these as indicative.
3.    The Council's second reason for refusal states that no supporting arboricultural
      information had been submitted. However, the Council's appeal statement clarifies
      that arboricultural information accompanied the application, but raises concerns
      regarding its adequacy for assessing the effects of the proposal on existing trees. I
      have considered the appeal on that basis.
Main Issues
4.    The main issues are:
      i)        the effect of the proposal on the character and appearance of the area; and
      ii)       whether the evidence before me demonstrates that the proposed access and
                layout could be implemented without unacceptable harm to existing trees.
Character and Appearance
5.    The appeal site forms part of the rear garden of 14 St Catherines Road (No. 14), a
      detached dwelling located within an established residential area. The surrounding
      area is characterised principally by detached dwellings set within varied and relatively
      spacious plots, with substantial rear gardens, mature landscaping and a strong
      presence of established trees. These features contribute positively to the verdant
      character of the area and its sense of spaciousness. Most dwellings have a direct
      relationship with, and frontage onto, St Catherines Road.

6.    The Havant Borough Design Guide Supplementary Planning Document identifies
      potential issues associated with tandem and backland development, including the risk
      of cramping and harm to local character.
7.    The proposal would subdivide the existing residential plot and introduce a single
      dwelling to the rear of No. 14 in a backland arrangement that differs from the
      predominant frontage pattern along St Catherines Road. However, the fact that a
      proposal departs from the prevailing pattern does not in itself establish harm. Rather,
      the effect of that departure must be assessed having regard to the scale of the
      proposal and the characteristics of the site and surrounding area.
8.    Examples of additional residential development within large domestic plots are
      evident on St Catherines Road and elsewhere in the wider area. However, the
      circumstances of those developments differ in various respects from the appeal
      proposal, including plot dimensions, site layout, the form of development and the
      policy context against which they were assessed. In particular, those examples
      appear generally to comprise infill development alongside existing dwellings, rather
      than the creation of backland plots in a tandem arrangement. Consequently, they
      carry limited weight in my assessment. Nevertheless, they demonstrate that the
      subdivision of residential plots and the introduction of additional dwellings are not
      entirely alien to the wider character of the locality.
9.    I have also had regard to the development relating to land at the rear of 21 St
      Catherines Road. Whilst that case was determined under a different policy
      framework, it demonstrates that tandem or backland development in this locality has
      not been found unacceptable in principle where the specific characteristics of a site
      supported such a conclusion.
10. In this case, the proposal would not fully reflect the prevailing pattern of development,
    which is characterised predominantly by detached dwellings sited adjacent to one
    another, fronting the road and occupying spacious plots with generous rear gardens.
11. Nonetheless, the proposed dwelling would be single-storey and modest in scale.
    Furthermore, the evidence before me indicates that both the existing and proposed
    dwellings would retain plots of a size commensurate with the varied character of the
    area, together with adequate private amenity space.
12. Whilst No. 14 is positioned further back within its plot than some neighbouring
    properties, the submitted layout indicates that adequate separation would remain
    between the existing and proposed dwellings. Consequently, I am not persuaded that
    the resulting relationship would appear unduly cramped or materially erode the
    spacious character that is evident in the area.
13. Having regard to the size of the resulting plots, the scale and form of the proposed
    dwelling, and subject to the retention of mature landscaping, I am not persuaded that
    the development would appear unduly cramped, overdeveloped or incongruous.
14. Although the proposal would introduce a degree of change to the existing pattern of
    development, it would not unacceptably undermine the spacious qualities that
    characterise the area. Accordingly, the effect on the character and appearance of the
    area would be limited.
15. For the above reasons, the proposal would respond satisfactorily to its surroundings
    and would not harm local character, appearance or distinctiveness. Therefore, the

      proposal accords with Policy CS16 of the Havant Borough Core Strategy (CS), which,
      amongst other things, seeks to ensure that development is sympathetic to local
      character and distinctiveness, respects the scale, form and pattern of surrounding
      development, and contributes positively to a sense of place. It would also accord with

Trees
16. The site contains several mature trees, including a protected Oak tree towards the
    front of the appeal site, which contributes positively to the character and visual
    amenity of the area and is located close to the proposed access route. As already
    stated, at this stage, the proposal seeks approval of the access and layout and
    therefore fixes the position of the access road in relation to those trees.
17. Arboricultural information accompanied the application. However, that material has
    not been provided as part of the appeal submissions. It is for the appellant to
    substantiate its case and I must determine the appeal on the basis of the information
    before me.
18. Furthermore, the arboricultural information submitted at the application stage is
    accepted by the main parties as not having been prepared in accordance with
    BS5837:2012. However, my findings do not turn solely on that matter. Even if I were
    to set aside the question of compliance with that standard, the evidence before me
    remains insufficient to demonstrate that the approved access and layout could be
    implemented without unacceptable harm to the protected Oak tree and other retained
    trees on the site.
19. In particular, the evidence before me does not adequately assess the relationship
    between the proposed access road and the root protection areas of the protected Oak
    and other retained trees. Nor does it explain the construction methodology that would
    be employed within those areas, demonstrate how harm could be avoided or
    mitigated, or establish whether a no-dig or other specialist construction solution could
    be employed whilst maintaining a layout and access arrangement capable of serving
    the development.
20. In the absence of such information, I cannot reasonably assess whether the approved
    access arrangement is feasible without unacceptable encroachment into root
    protection areas or other unacceptable effects on retained trees. Consequently, I
    cannot be satisfied that the approved access and layout could be implemented whilst
    safeguarding those trees.
21. The intention to retain existing trees carries some weight. However, the retention of
    trees in principle does not demonstrate that the approved access and layout could be
    implemented without damage to roots, loss of amenity value or other adverse effects.
22. Whilst I note the Council's suggested condition stating that Development shall be
    carried out in accordance with an Arboricultural Method Statement and Tree
    Protection Plan prepared in accordance with BS5837:2012, conditions should not be
    used to defer consideration of a matter fundamental to establishing whether the
    development is acceptable in principle. Because access and layout are matters for
    approval in this appeal, sufficient arboricultural evidence is required at this stage to
    demonstrate that the approved access alignment and layout could be implemented
    without unacceptable effects on retained trees. The suggested conditions do not
    overcome the absence of such evidence.

23. Accordingly, in the absence of sufficient evidence to demonstrate that the approved
    access and layout could be implemented whilst safeguarding the site's mature trees
    (including the protected Oak) and their root protection areas, I cannot be satisfied that
    the proposal would adequately protect important natural features of the site. I
    therefore find conflict with Policies CS11 and DM8 of the CS, which seek, amongst
    other things, to protect natural features of landscape, nature conservation and
    amenity importance, including trees, and to ensure their continued survival as part of
    new development. The proposal would also conflict with paragraphs 136 and 187 of
    the Framework, which recognise the important contribution existing trees make to the
    character and quality of places and seek to protect and enhance the natural
    environment.
Other Matters
24. I have had regard to the appellant's submissions regarding the proposed energy-
    efficient design, enhanced insulation standards and sustainable construction
    methods. These considerations weigh modestly in favour of the proposal.
25. The absence of objections from Hampshire Highways, Southern Water, Natural
    England and the Council's Ecology Officer does not alter my findings in respect of the
    second main issue.
Planning Balance and Conclusion
26. The Council's evidence indicates that it can demonstrate only around 1.3 years of
    housing land supply. Consequently, paragraph 11(d) of the Framework is engaged
    and the presumption in favour of sustainable development applies.
27. I attach significant weight to the delivery of an additional dwelling given the extent of
    the housing land supply shortfall. I also recognise the associated economic and social
    benefits arising from the proposal.
28. However, the proposal fails to demonstrate that the approved access and layout could
    be implemented whilst safeguarding mature trees and their root protection areas.
    Given the contribution those trees make to local character, visual amenity and the
    environmental quality of the site, the uncertainty regarding whether the approved
    access and layout can be delivered without unacceptable harm attracts substantial
    weight against the proposal.
29. Taking all matters into account, including the significant weight I attach to additional
    housing provision, I conclude that the uncertainty surrounding the effects of the
    approved access and layout on important retained trees and their root protection
    areas, together with the resulting conflict with Policies CS11 and DM8 of the CS and
    the relevant provisions of the Framework, would significantly and demonstrably
    outweigh the benefits when assessed against the policies in the Framework taken as
    a whole.
30. I therefore conclude that the appeal should be dismissed.
