•    The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
     against a refusal to grant planning permission.

•    The application Ref is DM/25/02546/FPA.
•    The development proposed is the change of use from grazing paddock to all weather horse arena.

Decision
1.     The appeal is allowed and planning permission is granted for the change of use
       from grazing paddock to all weather horse arena at High Kays Lea, Road Leading
       to High Kays Lea Farm, Hamsterley, Durham DL13 3QW in accordance with the
       terms of the application, Ref DM/25/02546/FPA, subject to the conditions in the
       attached schedule.
Preliminary Matters
2. A new National Planning Policy Framework (the Framework) was published on 17
   August 2026. This has introduced significant changes to its structure and
   presentation, including the incorporation of development management policies.
   However, having carefully considered the new Framework, I am satisfied that,
   insofar as the issues determinative to this case are concerned, the relevant policy
   approach has not fundamentally changed, and my consideration of it would not
   cause procedural unfairness or prejudice to parties. Therefore, the parties have not
   been invited to make further comments.
Main Issues
3.     The main issue is the effect of the proposed development on the character and
       appearance of the area, with particular regard to the rural landscape.
Reasons
4.     The appeal site relates to an area of existing paddock associated with High Kays
       Lea Farm, which occupies a rural location approximately 2.5km from Hamsterley
       Forest. The farmstead comprises a farmhouse and a number of agricultural
       outbuildings, accessed via a gravel track extending from the local highway
       network. The nearest neighbouring dwelling, Pool Tree Farm, lies approximately
       220m to the west. The existing paddock is situated to the north of the farmhouse
       and sits approximately 5.5 metres below the level of the farmhouse. It is separated
       from the farmstead by a mature belt of trees.

5.       The surrounding area is characterised by an undulating landform, mature
         vegetation and dispersed farmsteads, which provide a varied rural setting. The
         appeal site and surrounding landscape are designated as an Area of Higher
         Landscape Value (AHLV). There are also two Public Rights of Way1 (PROWs)
         which are close to the appeal site.
6.       Planning permission is being sought to convert part of the existing paddock into a
         dedicated horse arena to improve animal exercise and welfare, as well as to
         prevent ground erosion. The proposed all-weather horse arena would be for
         private use only, would measure approximately 50 metres by 30 metres, enclosed
         by a 1.5-metre-high timber post and rail fencing, with associated landscaping.
         There is no lighting proposed.
7.       Policy 10 of the County Durham Plan, adopted 2020 (the CDP), states that
         development in the countryside will not be permitted unless exceptional
         circumstances apply, or if the proposal would be allowed for by another specific
         policy in the Plan.
8.       In this case, Policy 13 of the CDP relates to equestrian development. Policy 13(c)
         states that equestrian development will be permitted where proposals would not,
         by virtue of their siting, design, scale, materials, layout or lighting, or through the
         inappropriate intensification of existing bridleways, routes or land, unacceptably
         affect the character, heritage or nature conservation value of the locality, either
         individually or cumulatively with other development. Policy 13(d) requires
         proposals to provide appropriate measures for screening building, hard standings,
         arenas and storage areas with trees and hedges.
9.       Policy 13(a) and (b) of the CDP are concerned with the provision, extension or
         conversion of equestrian buildings. Whilst the Council refers to criterion (b), the
         proposal comprises an engineering operation to create an outdoor riding arena
         and does not involve any new buildings. Consequently, I attach limited weight to
         the Council's concerns in respect of this criterion.
10. The appeal site is physically detached from the farmhouse and associated
    buildings. Nevertheless, it forms part of an existing paddock associated with the
    equestrian use of the holding and would continue to function as part of that use.
    Whilst physically separated from the main building group, it would not represent a
    new or unrelated land use within the countryside.
11. The proposed arena would be located within the part of the paddock closest to the
    existing farmhouse and associated buildings, and adjacent to established field
    boundaries. Its siting would take advantage of existing boundary features and the
    site's established landscape structure, helping to minimise its visual impact and
    prominence within the wider countryside. By utilising boundaries that already
    define and contain this part of the paddock, the proposal would reduce the
    perception of the arena as a standalone or intrusive feature within the field.
    Consequently, its position would assist in assimilating the development into its
    surroundings and limiting its effect on the character and appearance of the area.
12. The appeal site is visible only in limited views from the surrounding road network
    and nearby properties, primarily at longer distances, where the scale of the
    development would be diminished by distance and its visual effects would be

       limited. I acknowledge that users of PROW 13 would experience relatively close-
       range views of the proposal. However, those views would be experienced over a
       limited section of the route and within the context of an existing rural holding which
       is already used for equestrian activities. Consequently, whilst the development
       would be noticeable, it would not appear unduly prominent.
13. I acknowledge the Council's concerns regarding the introduction of an engineered
    feature into an open paddock. However, the proposal is limited in its physical form,
    consisting principally of a surfaced riding arena enclosed by post-and-rail fencing.
    Such fencing is a common feature within rural and equestrian landscapes and
    would not appear incongruous in this setting. Whilst some ground works would be
    required to create a level riding surface, the lower-lying position of the site would
    assist in containing views of the development and reducing its visual prominence
    within the wider landscape. Although the arena would introduce a more formal
    appearance than the existing grazing land, this change would be localised in
    extent and would not fundamentally alter the character of the wider landscape.
14. Whilst the Council considers the arena to be larger than would typically be
    associated with personal use, no substantive evidence has been provided to
    demonstrate that the proposed dimensions are inappropriate for the appellant's
    stated needs. Moreover, the visual effects of the proposal arise principally from its
    siting and appearance rather than its precise dimensions. The Council further
    contends that the proposal would have a transformative effect on rural character.
    However, I do not share that view. The development would occupy only part of a
    larger field and would not result in the loss of significant landscape features. Nor
    would it introduce substantial built form, lighting or other urbanising elements that
    might erode the intrinsic rural qualities of the area.
15. In respect of surfacing materials, whilst bark may be less visually apparent than
    sand, the appellant has explained that it would be less suitable for the intended
    use and would require greater maintenance and replacement. Given the practical
    requirements of an equestrian arena, I find no compelling reason to conclude that
    the proposed surface would give rise to unacceptable visual harm.
16. The submitted plans include additional planting to reinforce the existing mature
    tree belt between the paddock and the farmhouse. Although this landscaping
    would not entirely screen the development, complete screening is neither
    necessary nor generally achievable in a rural environment. The planting would
    further soften views of the development and, together with the existing tree belt,
    ensure that no significant harm to the character or appearance of the area would
    arise.
17. During consideration of the application, the Council has identified the farmhouse
    as a non-designated heritage asset (NDHA), with its significance deriving from its
    historic interest, traditional agricultural practices, aesthetic merits and group value.
    The Council did not identify any harm to the significance of the NDHA, by virtue of
    the separation distance and difference in land levels. Based on the evidence
    before me and my own observations, I see no reason to disagree with this
    conclusion. Accordingly, the proposed development would preserve the
    significance of the NDHHA.
18. For the above reasons, whilst the proposal would result in some change to the
    appearance of part of the paddock, that change would be modest and localised in

       nature. Therefore, I conclude that the proposal would not cause harm to the
       character and appearance of the area, with particular regard to the rural landscape

       countryside by ensuring that equestrian development is sensitively sited, designed

       effects.
Conditions
19. I have had regard to the Council’s suggested conditions and considered them
    against the statutory tests outlined in the Framework and the advice in the
    Planning Practice Guidance (PPG). I have made minor amendments, where
    necessary, to ensure the conditions comply with these documents.
20. I have imposed standard conditions concerning commencement (1) and
    compliance with the submitted plans (2). It is necessary that the development be
    carried out in accordance with the approved plans for the avoidance of doubt and
    in the interests of certainty.
21. Section 100ZA(5) of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 sets out that if an
    Inspector is minded to grant planning permission subject to pre-commencement
    conditions, they may only do so with the written agreement of the appellant.
22. Biodiversity Net Gain (BNG) is required under a statutory framework introduced by
    Schedule 7A of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990. Under this framework,
    every grant of planning permission (with a few exceptions) is deemed to have
    been granted subject to the condition that the biodiversity objective is met.
    Accordingly, it is not necessary to set out this condition in the schedule of
    conditions.
23. However, a pre-commencement condition (3) is necessary to agree a Habitat
    Management and Monitoring Plan in accordance with the Biodiversity Gain Plan to
    ensure the development delivers BNG on the site. Therefore, the appellant's
    written agreement to this condition has been obtained.
24. A further condition (4) is necessary to limit the use of the riding arena to the
    exercising of the appellants’ own horses and not for commercial purposes, in the
    interests of highway safety and amenity.
25. The Council also suggested that a condition restricting external lighting should be
    imposed. However, no lighting is proposed as part of the development and there is
    nothing before me to indicate that such lighting would be necessary for its
    operation. As planning conditions should only be imposed where they are
    necessary, I am not persuaded that a lighting condition would be justified in this
    instance.
Conclusion
26. For the above reasons and taking account of all other matters raised, the appeal is
    allowed.

Schedule of Conditions

    1. The development hereby permitted shall be begun before the expiration of three
       years from the date of this permission.

    2. The development hereby approved shall be carried out in accordance with the
       following plans: Proposed Exercise Arena 417/06 C; Proposed Arena Detail
       417/11 and Proposed Arena Section.

    3. No development shall commence until a Habitat Management and Monitoring
       Plan (HMMP) has been prepared in accordance with the approved Biodiversity
       Gain Plan and has been submitted to and approved in writing by the Local
       Planning Authority. The HMMP shall include:
          • a non-technical summary;
          • the roles and responsibilities of the people or organisation(s) delivering
              the HMMP;
          • the planned habitat creation and enhancement works to create or improve
              habitat to achieve the biodiversity net gain in accordance with the
              approved Biodiversity Gain Plan;
          • the management measures to maintain habitat in accordance with the
              approved Biodiversity Gain Plan for a period of 30 years from the practical
              completion of the development or the first occupation of the development,
              whichever is the sooner; and
          • the monitoring methodology and frequency in respect of the created or
              enhanced habitat to be submitted to the Local Planning Authority.
         Notice in writing shall be given to the Local Planning Authority when:
            • the approved HMMP has been implemented; and
            • the habitat creation and enhancement works as set out in the HMMP
                have been completed.

         The development shall not be occupied or brought into use until such time that:
            • the habitat creation and enhancement works set out in the approved
               HMMP have been completed; and
            • a completion report, evidencing the completed habitat enhancements, has
               been submitted to, and approved in writing by the Local Planning
               Authority.

         The created and/or enhanced habitat(s) specified in the approved HMMP shall
         be managed and maintained in accordance with the approved HMMP.

    4. The hereby approved all weather horse arena shall be used for purposes
       ancillary to the residential property High Kays Lea. No commercial or business
       uses shall be conducted at the property in respect to the arena and equestrian
       purposes.
