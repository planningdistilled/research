•   The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
    against a failure to give notice within the prescribed period of a decision on an application for
    planning permission.
•   The appeal is made by Mr and Mrs James Goolnik against the Council of the London Borough of
    Camden.
•   The application reference is 2026/0326/P.
•   The development proposed is roof extension with rear dormer, first-floor rear extension and
    replacement single storey rear extension.

Decision
1. The appeal is dismissed and planning permission is refused.
Application for costs
2. An application for costs was made by Mr and Mrs James Goolnik against the
   Council. This application is the subject of a separate decision.
Preliminary Matters
3. The appeal relates to a planning application that was not determined by the Council
   within the prescribed period. I note that, had the Council issued a decision, it would
   have refused planning permission. In establishing the main issue, I have had
   regard to the submissions of both parties.
4. Subsequent to the Council’s decision on the application to which this appeal
   relates, the Government published a new National Planning Policy Framework (the
   Framework) on 17 August 2026, which replaces the previous Framework published
   in December 2024, and all other iterations. Both the appellant and the Council were
   given the opportunity to comment on any changes relevant to the main issue in this
   appeal, and I have taken those comments into account in reaching this decision.
5. The appeal site lies within the Fitzjohns/ Netherhall Conservation Area (CA). I am
   therefore vigilant of my duty imposed under Section 72 of the Planning (Listed
   Buildings and Conservation Areas) Act 1990 to pay special attention to the
   desirability of preserving or enhancing the character or appearance of the CA.
Main Issue
6. The Council has raised no concerns regarding the effect of the proposed
   development on the living conditions of neighbouring occupiers. Based on my
   observations at the site visit and the evidence provided, I have no reason to take a
   different view.

7. The main issue is the effect of the proposed development on the character and
   appearance of the host property and the surrounding area, with particular regard to
   whether it would preserve or enhance the character or appearance of the CA.
Reasons
8. The appeal site is situated towards the northwest of the CA. The significance of the
   CA derives from the historic interest and architectural quality of its buildings. The
   Fitzjohns/ Netherhall Conservation Area Character Appraisal & Management Plan
   (FNCA) (2022) states that Ellerdale Road contains buildings of varying heights and
   architectural styles, with a mixture of Queen Anne, Italianate, Gothic and modern
   influences. Adjacent to the appeal site, Nos. 20 and 22 Ellerdale Road (Nos. 20
   and 22) are identified in the FNCA as buildings which make a positive contribution
   to the character and appearance of the CA.
9. The property comprises a brick-built building with a pitched natural slate roof and
   painted timber sliding sash windows. Although the two-storey building is smaller in
   scale than the neighbouring properties and is set back substantially from Ellerdale
   Road, it remains clearly visible within the street scene and makes a neutral
   contribution to the character and appearance of the CA.
10. To the front of the appeal property, the combined extensions and alterations would
    have a significant effect on the simple form of the existing building and its roof
    profile. The increase in roof height and introduction of a vertical timber clad addition
    at second floor level, together with a curved zinc roof form, would appear
    discordant and at odds with the building’s existing proportions and materiality,
    resulting in prominent and incongruous additions. Furthermore, the addition of a
    substantial first floor front extension, incorporating a projecting balcony and flat
    roof, would appear visually intrusive in this setting, detracting from the composition
    and architectural balance of the front elevation. By virtue of their size, scale, siting
    and design, the combined alterations would fail to appear subordinate to the host
    building.
11. I noted at my site visit that the appeal property is readily visible in views along
    Ellerdale Road and from the junction with Prince Arthur Road. Given the size,
    design and scale of the proposed alterations to the property, together with the
    proposed materials, the eye would be drawn unfavourably to the resultant
    alterations. As a result, it would detract from the character and appearance of the
    host property and undermine the contribution made by the neighbouring properties
    at Nos 20 and 22 to the significance of the CA, to the detriment of the surrounding
    area and the CA as a whole.
12. To the rear of the property, the proposed alterations at ground floor level would
    largely retain the existing depth of projection. However, the increased width of the
    rear extension beyond the profile of the existing building towards Nos. 22 and 22A
    Ellerdale Road would create an unbalanced form that fails to respect the
    proportions of the original building. In addition, the combined alterations to the
    upper levels of the property would appear excessive and bulky, resulting in the loss
    of the building’s characterful rear gable feature and the introduction of an
    incongruous, raised roof addition incorporating varied roof levels, alongside multiple
    terrace and balcony areas. Although situated to the rear, the alterations would
    remain visible in glimpsed views from neighbouring properties and, when
    considered cumulatively, would not appear subordinate to the building. By virtue of

     their size, scale, siting and design, the combined alterations would appear
     excessive and overbearing, causing harm to the character and appearance of the
     host property, the surrounding area and the CA.
13. Policy HE9 of the Framework recognises that not all elements of a conservation
    area will necessarily contribute to its significance. However, I have found that the
    combined alterations would appear excessive and overbearing, detracting from the
    character and appearance of the area and would undermine the contribution made
    by the neighbouring properties identified in the FNCA as making a positive
    contribution to the CA. Consequently, it would cause harm to the significance of the
    CA.
14. Given the relatively modest scale of the proposal, the proposed development
    would, under the terms of the Framework, result in a moderate level of harm to the
    significance of the CA. It is noteworthy that even a moderate level of harm to the
    significance of a designated heritage asset carries considerable importance and
    weight. Policy HE6 of the Framework establishes that when considering the
    potential effect of a development proposal on the significance of a designated
    heritage asset, substantial weight should be given to the asset’s conservation.
    Where a development proposal would harm the significance of a designated
    heritage asset, the effect on the asset and its significance should be weighed
    against any public benefits resulting from the proposal.
15. I have had regard to the benefits identified by the appellant, including that the
    proposed development would create a larger, functional three-bedroom family
    home through increased living space. However, any such benefits would be
    countered, and in my judgment, comfortably outweighed by the harm that I have
    identified to the significance of the CA.
16. In addition, I have been provided with insufficient information to demonstrate that
    the claimed benefits of the development could not be achieved in a less harmful
    manner. Whilst I have given careful consideration to the public benefits advanced
    by the appellant, it is nevertheless my judgement that, when considered
    cumulatively, they would not outweigh the moderate harm that I have identified to
    the significance of the CA. Conflict therefore arises with the historic environment
    conservation and enhancement policies contained within the Framework.
17. For the above reasons, I therefore conclude that the development would have a
    harmful effect on the character and appearance of the host property and the
    surrounding area, which would fail to preserve or enhance the character or
    appearance of the CA. The development is contrary to Camden Local Plan (2017)
    (CLP) Policies D1 and D2 which together seek to secure high-quality design which
    respects local context and character, and which preserves or enhances the historic
    environment including conservation areas. I further find conflict with the Hampstead
    Neighbourhood Plan 2025 – 2040 (2025) (HNP) Policy DH1 which requires
    extensions to be subservient to the original footprint and mass of the house and to
    contribute positively to the character of the area. The proposal is also contrary to
    HNP Policy DH2 in so far as it requires development to make a positive contribution
    to the appearance and character of Conservation Areas.

Other Matters
18. I note the extensive planning history of the site, including previous planning
    applications and recent dismissed appeal decisions. The appellant and an
    interested party draw my attention to an earlier planning application1 that was
    approved by the Council at the appeal site in 2005 which is similar in nature to the
    proposal subject to this appeal. I have had regard to the evidence submitted,
    including the approved plans and officer report relating to that proposal. The
    previous application predates the current CLP and the adoption of the HNP, where
    the proposal subject to this appeal does not comply with the adopted development
    plan policies. Furthermore, the existence of an earlier lapsed consent does not
    outweigh the harm I have identified for the reasons set out above. In any event,
    each proposal must be assessed on its own merits in accordance with the current
    policy position. Accordingly, it does not alter my overall conclusion.
19. An interested party representation has drawn my attention to a development at 79
    Fitzjohn’s Avenue. However, I have not been provided with sufficient details of that
    scheme to enable a meaningful comparison with the proposal before me.
    Consequently, it does not lead me to a different overall finding.
20. I note the appellant’s concerns over the Council’s handling of the application and
    pre application advice. However, my determination of this appeal is based on the
    planning merits of the case.
Planning Balance and Conclusion
21. The proposed development would create a larger, functional three-bedroom family
    home. However, the proposed development would have a harmful effect on the
    character and appearance of the host property and the surrounding area, which
    would fail to preserve or enhance the character or appearance of the CA. The
    proposal therefore conflicts with the development plan, taken as a whole.
22. Policy S4 of the Framework states that development proposals within settlements
    should be approved unless the benefits of doing so would be substantially
    outweighed by any adverse effects, when assessed against the national decision-
    making policies in the Framework.
23. Policy L2 of the Framework requires substantial weight to be given to the creation
    of additional floorspace within settlements through the use of the airspace above
    existing residential premises, in order to make effective use of land. Whilst I
    acknowledge that the proposal would provide enlarged residential accommodation,
    the Framework Policy L2 1.d)i. requires such development to be consistent with the
    overall street scene, or that building upwards would not cause substantial harm to
    the character of a building and its surroundings. I have found in my reasoning
    above that the proposal would result in unacceptable harm to the character and
    appearance of the host property, and the surrounding area, and would fail to
    preserve or enhance the character or appearance of the CA. Furthermore, the
    public benefits advanced by the appellant would not outweigh the moderate harm
    identified to the significance of the CA.
24. Furthermore, Policy DP3 1. of the Framework requires development proposals to
    respond to their context, so that they integrate with and enhance their

     surroundings. This should not preclude innovation or change where appropriate.
     For the reasons set out in my reasoning above, I have found harm to the character
     and appearance of the host property, the surrounding area and the CA and conflict
     with the historic environment conservation and enhancement policies contained
     within the Framework.
25. Taken together, I find the benefits of the proposal would be substantially
    outweighed by the adverse effects when assessed against the national decision-
    making policies of the Framework. As such, Policy S4 of the Framework does not
    point to permission being granted in this instance.
26. In the final balance, material considerations in the proposal’s favour are not of
    sufficient weight to suggest the decision should be made other than in accordance
    with the development plan. Therefore, for the reasons given, and having regard to
    all other matters raised, the appeal should be dismissed.
