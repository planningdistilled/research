 •    The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
      against a refusal to grant planning permission.
 •    The appeal is made by Mr Cenap against the decision of the Council of the London Borough of
      Lewisham.
 •    The application Ref is DC/25/142452.
 •    The development proposed is construction of a front porch and single-storey front extension.

Decision
1. The appeal is allowed and planning permission is granted for construction of a front
   porch and single-storey front extension at 30 Horncastle Road, Lewisham, London
   SE12 9LA in accordance with the terms of the application, Reference
   DC/25/142452, and the plans submitted with it, subject to the following conditions:
     1)       The development hereby permitted shall begin not later than 3 years from the
              date of this decision.
     2)       The development hereby permitted shall be carried out in accordance with
              the following approved plans: 2528_01-00, 2528_02-01, 2528_02-02.
     3)       The materials to be used in the construction of the external surfaces of the
              development hereby permitted shall match those used in the existing
              building.
Preliminary Matters
2. A new National Planning Policy Framework (the Framework) was published on 17
   August 2026. However, I am satisfied that the policy approach relevant to this
   appeal has not fundamentally changed and that no party has been prejudiced by its
   publication. Accordingly, I have had regard to the current version of the Framework
   in reaching my decision.
3. I have used in the banner heading above the description of development given on
   the Council’s decision notice, removing words that are not development, as this
   most accurately identifies the proposal. This is an administrative clarification that
   does not alter the substance of the appeal, and no party would be prejudiced.
Main Issue
4. The main issue is the effect of the proposed development on the character and
   appearance of the host dwelling and the surrounding area.

Reasons
5. The appeal property comprises a two-storey end-of-terrace dwelling within a short
   row of dwellings in a predominantly residential area. The row is positioned on a
   bend in the road and, as a result, the appeal property does not align with the
   adjoining terrace to the west. The dwelling appears to have been extended to the
   side, while alterations to the front elevations of adjacent dwellings, including
   porches, have disrupted some of the original symmetry of the group. Nevertheless,
   a degree of coherence remains through the consistent roof forms and profile and
   the rhythm of the projecting bays.
6. The wider street comprises terraced and semi-detached dwellings of broadly
   comparable scale, a number of which have been altered or extended, including
   through additions to their front elevations, canopies and porches. Consequently,
   while elements of the original architectural form and rhythm remain evident, the
   street scene is not wholly uniform.
7. The proposal comprises a front porch addition together with a single-storey
   extension forward of the existing garage. The porch would project approximately 1
   metre from the principal elevation, with the front extension brought forward broadly
   in line with it. The existing garage door would be replaced with a window and the
   roof canopy across the front of the property would be raised. The proposed
   development would be finished in materials to match the existing dwelling.
8. The Council raises no objection to the proposed porch. The Officer's Report finds
   that its limited scale, matching materials and subordinate relationship to the
   dwelling would ensure that it would not appear visually intrusive or disrupt the
   character of the dwelling or area. I see no evidence to disagree with that
   assessment.
9. The Council considers that the front extension would disrupt the symmetry of the
   terrace and that, together with the raised canopy, would appear incongruous and
   visually intrusive. The Council's Alterations and Extensions Supplementary
   Planning Document (2019) (the SPD) advises that front extensions are rarely
   desirable as they can unbalance a building, create undue prominence or disrupt the
   continuity of a terrace or group. However, the SPD is guidance, and the proposal
   must be assessed having regard to the particular characteristics of the site and its
   specific effects on the host dwelling and surrounding area.
10. In this case, the existing garage already projects forward of the principal elevation,
    broadly reflecting the projection of the ground and first-floor bay features. The
    proposed extension would bring the building line further forward to align with the
    proposed porch, which the Council considers acceptable. This would create a
    coherent single-storey element across the frontage. Its limited scale and alignment
    with the porch would also reduce its prominence, particularly in views from the east
    and alongside the use of matching materials ensures it remains subordinate to the
    main dwelling.
11. I acknowledge that the proposal would alter the symmetry of the terrace to some
    degree. However, the appeal property is at its western end and the adjoining
    terrace beyond is on a different alignment due to the bend in the road.
    Consequently, the extension would not interrupt a continuous building line or
    materially disrupt the wider rhythm of development within the street. Furthermore,
    the existing first-floor projecting bay would remain prominent above the extension

     and the relatively limited increase in the height of the canopy would not materially
     alter the established form or appearance of the terrace. I therefore do not share the
     Council's view that the proposal would be visually intrusive or incongruous.
12. Although front extensions are not characteristic of the immediate vicinity, the
    proposal would be read in the context of a wider street scene containing a variety of
    alterations and extensions to dwellings, including additions to their front elevations.
    In this context, the proposal would represent a modest domestic alteration that
    would not appear unduly prominent or incongruous.
13. Overall, I conclude that the proposal would not harm the character or appearance
    of the host dwelling or the surrounding area. As such, the proposal would accord
    with Policy QD9 of the Lewisham Local Plan - An Open Lewisham as part of an
    Open London (2025), which amongst other things, seek to deliver design that
    would not adversely affect the architectural integrity of buildings. For the same
    reason, the proposal accords with overall design objectives of the SPD.
Other Matters
14. The appellant has raised concerns regarding the Council's handling of the
    application. However, these matters do not alter my assessment of the planning
    merits of the proposal, and I have therefore not considered them further.
Conditions
15. I have considered the tests of Policy DM6 of the Framework and advice in the
    Planning Practice Guidance. In addition to the statutory time limit condition, a
    condition specifying the relevant plans is necessary to provide certainty. A condition
    relating to materials is also reasonable and necessary in the interests of
    safeguarding the character and appearance of the area.
Conclusion
16. For the reasons given above, having taken account of the development plan as a
    whole, along with all other relevant material considerations, the appeal should be
    allowed.
