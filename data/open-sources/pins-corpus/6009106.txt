Appeal Decision
Site visit made on 13 August 2026
by Laura Bartle BA(Hons) MSc MRTPI
an Inspector appointed by the Secretary of State
Decision date: 30 September 2026


Appeal Ref: 6009106
Land at Tuttle Farm, Lock Road, North Cotes, Grimsby, Lincolnshire DN36 5UP
•      The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
       against a refusal to grant permission in principle.
•      The appeal is made by Mr E Milburn against the decision of East Lindsey District Council.
•      The application Ref is 00434/26/PIP.
•      The development proposed is develop land at Tuttle Farm for nine houses.

Decision
1.       The appeal is dismissed.
Preliminary Matters
2.       The proposal is for permission in principle. Planning Practice Guidance (PPG)
         advises that this is an alternative way of obtaining planning permission for
         housing-led development. The permission in principle consent route has two
         stages: the first stage (or permission in principle stage) establishes whether a site
         is suitable in-principle and the second (‘technical details consent’) stage is when
         the detailed development proposals are assessed. This appeal relates to the first
         of these two stages.
3.       The scope of the considerations for permission in principle is limited to location,
         land use and the amount of development permitted1. All other matters are
         considered as part of a subsequent technical details consent application if
         permission in principle is granted. I have determined the appeal accordingly.
4.       A plan showing how the development could be laid out is before me. However, as
         this appeal does not relate to technical details, I have taken the submitted plan to
         be illustrative only.
5.       During the course of the appeal, a revised National Planning Policy Framework
         (the Framework) was published on 17 August 2026. The main parties were given
         an opportunity to comment on the implications of the revised Framework for the
         appeal, and I have taken those comments received into account in reaching my
         decision. References to the Framework in my decision are to the August 2026
         version.
Main Issue
6.       This main issue is whether the site is suitable for residential development, having
         regard to its location, including its position within the Coastal Area and Flood Zone
         3.

Reasons
7.     The appeal site comprises agricultural land on Lock Road. Although there are
       sporadic residential and agricultural buildings in the surrounding area, the site lies
       outside the built-up part of North Cotes, separated from the settlement by open
       agricultural fields. There is no footway connecting the site to the village along Lock
       Road and for the most part it is unlit. Accordingly, the site is physically detached
       from the settlement and occupies a countryside location for planning purposes.
8.     Policy SP1 of the East Lindsey District Council Local Plan Core Strategy (LPCS)
       seeks to direct development to sustainable locations in accordance with the
       settlement hierarchy. As the site lies outside the settlement of North Cotes, the
       proposal would conflict with that strategy and therefore fail to accord with Policy
       SP1. For the same reason, it would also conflict with Policy SP2 of the LPCS.
9.     The site lies within the Coastal Area identified by Policies SP17 and SP18 of the
       LPCS and within Flood Zone 3, where there is significant risk of coastal flooding.
       The proposal would introduce nine market dwellings and their occupants into an
       area where the LPCS seeks to restrict residential growth because of flood risk.
       Policy SP18 supports only limited forms of housing development within the coastal
       area, including certain brownfield sites, sites benefiting from existing planning
       permission, and specified affordable or specialist accommodation where an
       evidenced need exists. The proposal would not fall within any of those categories.
       It would therefore conflict with the approach set out in Policy SP18 of the LPCS.
10. I have been directed to the absence of objection from the Environment Agency.
    However, this does not in itself demonstrate that the proposal would accord with
    either the LPCS or the Framework's flood risk policies F4 and F5.
11. In this case, flood risk is a fundamental constraint to the suitability of the site and it
    is necessary to determine whether the principle of locating residential development
    in this location would be acceptable. Whilst detailed mitigation measures would
    ordinarily fall to be considered at the Technical Details Consent stage, the
    absence of any site-specific flood risk evidence limits my ability to conclude that
    the site would be suitable for residential development in principle, particularly given
    its location within Flood Zone 3 and the coastal flood risk area.
12. I have carefully considered the Sequential Test statement submitted during the
    appeal. However, it contains only broad assertions regarding the absence of
    alternative sites and provides little substantive evidence demonstrating why the
    search area was appropriately restricted to North Cotes and nearby settlements. It
    therefore does not adequately demonstrate that there are no reasonably available
    sites in areas of lower flood risk capable of accommodating the proposed
    development. Nor does it overcome the underlying conflict with the local strategy
    for coastal housing development.
13. Consequently, the evidence before me neither demonstrates compliance with the
    sequential approach nor satisfactorily establishes that the site would be suitable
    for residential development having regard to the flood risk constraints affecting it.
    Moreover, even if the Sequential Test were satisfied, the proposal would still
    conflict with the coastal strategy embodied in Policies SP17 and SP18 of the
    LPCS.

14. In addition, the suggested mitigation measures do not alter the fact that the
    proposal would increase the number of people living within an area identified as
    being at significant risk from coastal flooding. The purpose of the sequential
    approach in the Framework is to steer development towards areas at the lowest
    probability of flooding before reliance is placed on site-specific mitigation
    measures. The proposal would fail to demonstrate compliance with that approach.
    Furthermore, even if suitable mitigation could potentially be secured at a later
    stage, that would not overcome the conflict with Policies SP17 and SP18, whose
    purpose is to restrict additional residential development within the coastal area.
15. For these reasons, I find that the site would not be suitable for residential
    development having regard to its location, including its position within the Coastal
    Area and Flood Zone 3. Consequently, the proposal would conflict with LPCS
    Policies SP17 and SP18 insofar as they seek to limit new housing in areas at high
    risk of flooding and would conflict with Policy SP1 and the approach to sustainable
    development at Policy SP2. In reaching this conclusion I have had regard to
    Policies F4 and F5 of the Framework.
Planning Balance
16. The proposal would provide up to nine dwellings and would therefore make a
    modest contribution towards housing supply at a time when the Council cannot
    demonstrate a five-year housing land supply. It would also generate economic
    benefits during both the construction and occupation of the development. I afford
    these benefits moderate weight.
17. Against those benefits, I have found that the proposal would provide market
    housing within the Coastal Area in circumstances that conflict with the approach
    set out in Policies SP17 and SP18 of the LPCS. I have also found that the
    evidence before me does not demonstrate that the site is suitable for residential
    development in principle having regard to the significant flood risk constraints
    affecting it. These conflicts with the development plan and national flood risk policy
    attract substantial weight.
18. Whilst the benefits of the proposal weigh in its favour, they are insufficient to
    outweigh the identified policy conflicts and the objectives of directing development
    away from areas at greatest risk of flooding. Taking all matters into account, I find
    that the adverse consequences of the proposal would outweigh its benefits.
Conclusion
19. The proposal would conflict with the development plan when read as a whole.
    There are no material considerations, including the Framework, that indicate the
    decision should be made other than in accordance with the development plan.
    Accordingly, the appeal should be dismissed
