Appeal Ref: 6009837
Land at 260A Hawkes Mill Lane, Allesley, Coventry CV5 9FJ
•   The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
    against a refusal to grant outline planning permission.
•   The appeal is made by Lee Clarke and Sandy Parrott against the decision of Coventry City Council.
•   The application Ref is PL/2025/0001987/OUT.
•   The development proposed is erection of two dwellings (all matters reserved except for access).

Decision
1. The appeal is allowed and planning permission is granted for erection of two
   dwellings (all matters reserved except for access) at Land at 260A Hawkes Mill
   Lane, Coventry CV5 9FJ in accordance with the terms of the application, Ref
   PL/2025/0001987/OUT, subject to the conditions in the attached schedule.
Preliminary Matters
2. Following the submission of the appeal, a new National Planning Policy Framework
   (the Framework) was issued on 17 August 2026. The main parties were given the
   opportunity to comment on the implications of the new Framework for this appeal.
   Where comments have been received, I have taken them into account in reaching
   my decision.
3. The application was made in outline with all matters reserved except for access. I
   have determined the appeal on that basis. I have taken the details shown on the
   submitted plans into account only insofar as they relate to access or otherwise
   assist in demonstrating how the site could be developed, recognising that matters
   of appearance, landscaping, layout and scale would be considered at reserved
   matters stage.
Main Issue
4. The main issue relevant to this appeal is whether the proposed development would
   be inappropriate development in the Green Belt having regard to the Framework
   and relevant development plan policies and, linked to this, whether the site is in a
   sustainable location.
Reasons
Whether Inappropriate Development and Location
5. Policy GB6 of the Framework states that development in the Green Belt is
   inappropriate unless it falls within one of the categories in Policy GB7. Policy
   GB7(1)(g) provides that development is not inappropriate where it would utilise grey
   belt land and would not fundamentally undermine the purposes, taken together, of

     the remaining Green Belt across the area of the plan; where there is an evidenced
     unmet need for the type of development proposed; and where the development
     would be in a sustainable location, with particular reference to Policy TR3.
6. Grey belt is defined in the Framework glossary as land in the Green Belt
   comprising previously developed land and/or any other land that, in either case,
   does not strongly contribute to purposes (a), (b), or (d) in Policy GB2. The relevant
   purposes are to check the unrestricted sprawl of large built-up areas, to prevent
   neighbouring towns merging into one another, and to preserve the setting and
   special character of historic towns.
7. The site lies within a small group of houses to the rear of dwellings fronting Hawkes
   Mill Lane. The surrounding pattern of development is not one of a wholly open or
   undeveloped rural landscape, but one where the appeal site is visually and
   functionally associated with existing built form and domestic curtilages. The
   proposal would introduce two dwellings within that context and would not project
   development beyond the existing extent of built form or create a new outward
   extension into open countryside. It would therefore have a contained relationship
   with the settlement pattern and would not give rise to unrestricted sprawl of a large
   built-up area. I therefore find that the site does not strongly contribute to purpose
   (a). The appeal site is also physically and visually separated from nearby villages
   and its development would not reduce the gap between towns or contribute to their
   coalescence. It does not strongly contribute to purpose (b). Nor is the site located
   so as to form part of the setting of a historic town, and the evidence does not show
   that its openness makes an important contribution to the special character or
   appreciation of any such town. It therefore does not strongly contribute to purpose
   (d).
8. Having regard to those matters, I consider that the appeal site is grey belt land. The
   proposal would involve two dwellings on a modestly sized and contained parcel of
   land which is seen in close association with existing residential development rather
   than as part of a broad, open sweep of countryside. As a result, it would not
   undermine the purpose of safeguarding the countryside from encroachment. Its
   development would not undermine the purposes of the surrounding Green Belt in
   checking sprawl, maintaining separation between settlements or preserving the
   setting and special character of historic towns. Nor would it result in the loss of land
   which performs a strategic Green Belt function across the wider plan area. Given
   the limited scale of the appeal site, its relationship with existing built form and
   domestic land, and the significant extent of the remaining Green Belt across the
   plan area, the proposal would not fundamentally undermine the purposes, taken
   together, of the remaining Green Belt across the area of the plan.
9. The Housing Delivery Test (HDT) is the Government’s published measurement for
   assessing housing delivery and forms part of the basis for national policy. I
   acknowledge the Council’s submissions that evidence supporting the emerging
   Local Plan Review has questioned the ONS figures that informed the HDT, that the
   emerging plan is progressing through examination, and that adoption may lead to a
   different position in due course. However, the Local Plan Review has not yet been
   adopted and the HDT remains the published measure for the purposes of applying
   national policy at the time of this decision. I have not been provided with
   substantive evidence that would justify disregarding the published HDT result or
   giving it limited weight in this appeal. The appeal must therefore be determined
   having regard to the currently adopted development plan and the material

     considerations that apply now. In that context, the published HDT result remains a
     relevant and important consideration.
10. For applications involving the provision of housing, footnote 41 to Policy GB7
    explains that an evidenced unmet need exists where the HDT result was below
    75% of the housing requirement over the previous three years. On the evidence
    before me, and having regard to the published HDT result, I find that there is an
    evidenced unmet need for the proposed dwellings.
11. Policy TR3 of the Framework addresses the relationship between new
    development, movement and transport infrastructure. It promotes locations where
    day-to-day travel can be undertaken by a choice of modes, where access is
    suitable for different users and where existing or planned transport provision can be
    used effectively. It also seeks to reduce reliance on the private car, while
    recognising that opportunities to improve walking, wheeling, cycling and public
    transport in rural areas will depend on what is practicable and capable of being
    supported by the scale and nature of the development.
12. Hawkes End contains only limited services and facilities. However, the site is near
    to Allesley, where a range of day-to-day needs could be met. A footpath connection
    is available between the site and Allesley. Although the part of the route nearest to
    the site is not fully street lit, the route is lit for much of its length and would provide
    a walking route for future occupiers. Dedicated cycle infrastructure is limited, but
    the distance involved is such that cycling would remain a practicable option. In
    addition, nearby bus services would provide an alternative to reliance on the private
    car for journeys to Coventry, where a wider range of services, facilities and
    employment opportunities is available.
13. Taking these factors together, future occupiers would have access to services,
    facilities and transport choices, including walking, cycling and public transport
    options, such that day-to-day travel would not be wholly dependent on the private
    car. The proposal would therefore accord with Policy TR3 of the Framework. I
    therefore conclude that the site is in a sustainable location. As the proposal would
    utilise grey belt land, there is an evidenced unmet need for the proposed dwellings,
    and the site is sustainably located, the development would satisfy the relevant
    requirements of Policy GB7(1)(g).
14. For the reasons given above, the proposal would not be inappropriate development
    in the Green Belt and would be in a sustainable location. The development would
    consequently accord with Policies GB1, H3, AC1, AC4 and AC5 of the Coventry
    Local Plan. These seek, amongst other matters, to protect the Green Belt from
    inappropriate development and ensure that new development is accessible by a
    choice of transport modes, including walking, cycling and public transport. For the
    reasons set out above, the proposal would accord with those policies and the
    Framework.
Legal Agreement
15. A Unilateral Undertaking, made pursuant to section 106 of the Town and Country
    Planning Act 1990, has been submitted which seeks to secure the proposed
    dwellings as self-build dwellings. The Framework sets out the policy tests for
    planning obligations, which reflect the statutory tests in Regulation 122 of the
    Community Infrastructure Levy Regulations 2010. These require an obligation to be
    necessary to make the development acceptable in planning terms, directly related

https://www.gov.uk/planning-inspectorate   3
Appeal Decision 6009837


     to the development, and fairly and reasonably related in scale and kind. The
     Planning Practice Guidance provides further guidance on the use and drafting of
     planning obligations, including that they should only be sought where they meet
     those tests and cannot appropriately be addressed by condition.
16. The undertaking identifies the land and parties and states that the dwellings, or any
    dwelling forming part of the development, shall be occupied only as self-build
    dwellings. However, it also refers in places to “the Dwelling”, notwithstanding that
    the proposal is for two dwellings, and the occupation control is framed by reference
    to “the first occupier” rather than clearly applying to each dwelling. The deed also
    does not define “dwelling” or “self-build dwelling” with sufficient precision, and its
    drafting creates uncertainty as to how the restriction would apply and be enforced
    in practice. Although the obligation refers to the Council’s application reference, I
    am satisfied that this would not, of itself, be fatal given the wider references to the
    land and development. Nonetheless, read as a whole, the undertaking lacks the
    clarity and precision required for an enforceable planning obligation. In any event,
    given my conclusions above that the proposal would not be inappropriate
    development in the Green Belt, would be in a sustainable location and would not
    depend on its self-build status to be acceptable, the obligation is not necessary to
    make the development acceptable in planning terms. I have therefore not relied
    upon it as a reason for granting planning permission, and it does not satisfy the
    relevant tests in the Framework, the Planning Practice Guidance and Regulation
    122 of the Community Infrastructure Levy Regulations 2010.
Other Matters
17. As the self-build nature of the proposal would not be secured, the development
    would not benefit from any self-build exemption from the statutory biodiversity net
    gain requirement. If planning permission were otherwise to be granted, the
    development would therefore be subject to the biodiversity net gain condition.
18. The Planning Practice Guidance explains that the statutory biodiversity net gain
    requirement has principally been designed as a post-permission matter.
    Compliance would involve the discharge of the Biodiversity Gain Condition
    following the grant of planning permission. The Biodiversity Gain Plan would be the
    mechanism for confirming whether the development would meet the biodiversity
    gain objective, and the development could not commence until that plan had been
    approved.
19. The appeal site is modestly sized for two dwellings. Although the proposal may
    result in some loss of existing habitat, there is no substantive evidence before me
    to demonstrate that the mandatory 10% biodiversity net gain could not be
    delivered, either on site or, where necessary, through the statutory biodiversity gain
    process. I am therefore satisfied that, if permission were otherwise granted, the
    biodiversity gain requirement could be secured through the statutory condition.
20. Comments from interested parties raised concerns about the site being within the
    Green Belt, which have been considered in my assessment of the main issue. I
    have also taken into account the comments in support of the proposal, including
    that the land is unused and that its redevelopment would improve the appearance
    of the area.
21. Several comments from interested parties refer to the effect of the proposal on
    wildlife, habitat and biodiversity. The Ecological Appraisal identifies that the habitats

https://www.gov.uk/planning-inspectorate   4

     on the site are of low local ecological value, being common, widespread and in
     unexceptional condition. It also identifies that the site has low to moderate value for
     faunal species, including foraging and commuting bats, nesting birds, reptiles,
     amphibians and hedgehogs. However, it does not identify any ecological constraint
     that would prevent the development. The recommended measures include a
     sensitive lighting strategy, and biodiversity enhancements including bat and bird
     boxes and habitat management. I am satisfied that these matters can be
     adequately secured by conditions as well as the management of the ponds shown
     on the submitted plans and that subject to these matters the development would
     not harm protected and other species.
22. Concerns have also been raised about access, parking, traffic, emergency access,
    bin storage, noise, privacy and overdevelopment. The appeal is made in outline,
    with only access for consideration. The submitted access plan shows that the
    dwellings would be served from the existing track between 244 and 248 Hawkes
    Mill Lane, rather than from the private driveway serving 260A Hawkes Mill Lane.
    The proposal would add only two dwellings and there is no substantive highway
    evidence before me to show that this would generate traffic movements of such
    scale or character as to significantly harm highway safety, lead to severe
    congestion, or obstruct neighbouring properties or the public highway. Matters of
    internal layout and parking would be considered at reserved matters stage. On the
    evidence before me, I have no basis to conclude that a satisfactory reserved
    matters scheme, alongside suitably worded conditions, could not provide suitable
    parking, turning and bin storage arrangements for two dwellings. The limited scale
    of the development is also such that it would not be likely to prevent emergency or
    refuse vehicles from operating safely in the locality. Taking into account the
    evidence before me, I am satisfied that the site is capable of accommodating two
    dwellings without unacceptable effects on highway safety, subject to the suggested
    conditions and the detailed consideration of reserved matters.
23. Construction activity may cause some temporary disturbance. However, this can be
    controlled through the pre-commencement construction method statement
    condition, which requires measures to manage working hours, noise, dust,
    construction traffic, parking and liaison with local residents. Concerns about
    possible damage to private property, ownership boundaries, subsidence, private
    rights and loss of private views are matters outside the scope of this planning
    appeal and would need to be addressed between the relevant parties. Matters such
    as overlooking and privacy would need to be considered at reserved matters stage.
    However, based on the distances between the appeal site and nearby dwellings, it
    is not evident that a satisfactory detailed scheme could not be achieved without
    causing a significantly harmful loss of privacy for nearby occupiers.
24. I have also considered comments about the publicity given to the application. The
    Council responded to those concerns during the application process, and interested
    parties have had the opportunity to make representations on the proposal. I have
    taken those representations into account.
Conditions
25. I have considered the suggested conditions against the tests in the Framework and
    Planning Practice Guidance. Where necessary, I have amended the wording in the
    interests of precision and enforceability. Conditions 1 and 2 are the standard outline
    time limit conditions, and condition 3 is necessary in the interests of certainty.

26. Condition 4 requires a method statement to be submitted and approved before the
    development commences. This is necessary because construction works could
    affect the living conditions of nearby occupiers. The condition is therefore
    necessary to secure appropriate measures to manage working hours, noise, dust
    and other emissions, temporary lighting, construction-related activity and liaison
    with local residents before works begin.
27. Condition 5 relates to the implementation of landscaping details approved at
    reserved matters stage. This is necessary to ensure that hard and soft landscaping
    is implemented in the interests of the character and appearance of the area.
28. The Ecological Appraisal recommends ecological mitigation and enhancement
    measures, and the scheme includes the provision of ponds on site. Conditions 6, 7
    and 10 are therefore necessary to protect and enhance biodiversity, including bats
    and birds, and to secure ecological mitigation and enhancement measures. The
    Appraisal states that there should be 1 bat box per dwelling and, as such, I have
    revised condition 6 to reflect this. Condition 10 is also necessary to ensure that any
    external lighting is designed to minimise impacts on emerging and foraging bats
    and foraging corridors.
29. I have amended condition 8 to link the parking provision to the details approved at
    reserved matters stage, as layout is reserved. I have amended condition 9 so that
    bin storage details must be submitted and approved before occupation, as those
    details are not fixed by the outline plans. These changes are necessary to make
    the conditions precise and enforceable. The conditions are necessary in the
    interests of highway safety, the efficient operation of the site and the living
    conditions of future and nearby occupiers.
30. Condition 11 removes permitted development rights for buildings and enclosures.
    The Framework identifies that permitted development rights may be restricted
    where there is clear justification. Layout, scale, appearance and landscaping are
    reserved matters. Given the relationship of the site with nearby dwellings, the
    detailed siting and scale of the approved dwellings will need to be considered
    through the reserved matters process. Further buildings or enclosures within the
    curtilages of the approved dwellings could affect the living conditions of nearby
    occupiers. Condition 11 is therefore necessary to ensure that any such
    development does not cause harm to the living conditions of nearby occupants.
Conclusion
31. The proposed development complies with the development plan as a whole, and
    the material considerations do not indicate that a decision should be taken
    otherwise than in accordance with it. Therefore, for the reasons given above, I
    conclude that the appeal should be allowed.

       statement detailing the control of emissions into the air during the construction
       phase shall be submitted to and approved in writing by the local planning
       authority. The method statement shall include: a) proposed hours of work; b) map
       with nearest receptors and distances for dust and noise; c) noise impact on
       nearest neighbours and control measures as required; d) monitoring methods and
       measurement locations for dust and noise recording details; e) dust mitigation
