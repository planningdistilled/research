•   The appeal is made by Ian Perrett against the decision of Bournemouth Christchurch and Poole

•   The application Ref is P/25/04438/HOU.
•   The development proposed is first floor front extension.

Decision
1. The appeal is dismissed.
Appeal Procedure
2. The site visit was undertaken by a representative of the Inspector whose
   recommendation is set out below and to which the Inspector has had regard before
   deciding the appeal.
Preliminary Matters
3. A revised National Planning Policy Framework (the Framework) was published in
   August 2026. In this instance, the issues most relevant to the appeal are unaffected
   by the revisions to the Framework. I am therefore satisfied that there is no
   requirement to seek further submissions on the revised Framework, and that no
   party would be disadvantaged by this.
Main Issue
4. The main issue is the effect of the proposed development on the character and
   appearance of the host dwelling and the surrounding area.
Reasons for the Recommendation
5. Wharncliffe Gardens and the adjoining Waterford Gardens form part of an
   established suburban residential area characterised predominantly by detached
   chalet bungalows set within generous plots. The prevailing townscape is defined by
   modestly scaled dwellings with pitched or hipped roofs, some incorporating small
   dormers that remain clearly subservient to the host building. Consistent building
   forms, open boundary treatments and spacious landscaped frontages combine to
   create a coherent and attractive suburban character. While there is some variation
   in architectural detailing, the area's defining characteristics are its consistent
   roofscape and the proportionate domestic scale of its built form.

6. The proposed development would introduce a two-storey front extension with a flat
   roof extending across a substantial part of the principal elevation. Whilst it would be
   positioned above an existing projection, the proposal would materially alter the form
   and appearance of the host dwelling by replacing a modest, pitched-roof projection
   with a broader, box-like mass. As a result, the proposal would disrupt the
   established architectural composition of the dwelling, eroding its characteristic
   chalet-bungalow form and introducing a discordant feature that neither reflects the
   prevailing roofscape nor the proportions of surrounding development.
7. The sites prominent position on a corner plot with an open frontage prominence of
   the site, occupying a corner plot with open frontage, would further exacerbate the
   visual impact of the proposal. The extended form would be readily visible from
   multiple vantage points within the public realm, increasing its influence on the street
   scene. It would appear as a dominant two-storey feature that would not read as a
   subordinate addition, but rather as a competing element that overwhelms the host
   dwelling.
8. The extensive flat roof, combined with the width and depth of the extension, would
   result in a bulky and visually intrusive addition that contrasts sharply with the
   consistent pattern of pitched and hipped roofs that characterises the surrounding
   area. This departure from the established roof forms would undermine the visual
   cohesion of the street, introducing an incongruous feature that draws undue
   attention.
9. While some properties within the wider area incorporate flat-roofed elements, these
   are generally limited in scale and remain clearly subordinate to the principal roof
   form of the dwelling. By contrast, the proposed development would introduce a
   substantial flat-roofed first-floor addition that would neither appear subservient nor
   integrate successfully with the existing roof structure
10. It is also acknowledged that development along Wharncliffe Road to the south
    exhibits a greater degree of architectural variety, including more contemporary
    designs and larger buildings. However, this character does not extend into
    Wharncliffe Gardens and Waterford Gardens to the same extent. There is a clear
    transition between these areas, with the appeal site forming part of a quieter, lower-
    scale enclave rather than the more varied frontage along Wharncliffe Road.
11. The appellant has drawn comparisons with nearby contemporary developments.,
    such as Needles Watch. Needles Watch is materially different, comprising a three-
    storey flatted development which occupies a distinct position. It does not reflect the
    established pattern of detached chalet bungalows within Wharncliffe Gardens and
    Waterford Gardens.
12. The proposed development would result in harm to the character and visual
    amenity of the street scene. The design, by virtue of its scale, bulk, height, and
    architectural form, would fail to integrate with its surroundings or reflect the
    established pattern of development. The adverse effects identified under the main
    issue substantially outweigh any benefits derived from the proposed development.
    As such, the proposal would conflict with Policy HE2 of the Christchurch and East
    Dorset Local Plan Part 1 Core Strategy (2014) and Policy H12 of the Borough of
    Christchurch Local Plan (2001).

Other Matters
13. It is noted that an alternative scheme has previously been approved and that no
    harm to residential amenity has been identified. However, these considerations do
    not outweigh the identified harm to character and appearance.
Conclusion and Recommendation
14. For the reasons given above and having had regard to all other matters raised, I
    recommend that the appeal should be dismissed.
