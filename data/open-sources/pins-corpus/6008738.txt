Application for costs
2.       An application for costs was made by Visao Ltd against Buckinghamshire Council.
         This application is the subject of a separate decision.
Background and Procedural Matter
3.       Planning permission for the demolition of existing dwellings and erection of six
         dwelling houses and open fronted carports alongside alterations to vehicular
         access was first granted on appeal on 20 December 20191. That decision (‘the first
         permission’) included a plans condition, imposed as condition 2, specifying the
         drawings to which the development must accord.
4.       Three further permissions were subsequently granted under Section 73 of the
         Town and Country Planning Act 1990, referenced PL/20/2547/VRC,
         PL/21/2713/VRC and PL/22/1847/VRC (‘the fourth permission’). Each authorised
         the development to proceed without compliance with conditions attached to the
         preceding permission, and each repeated the plans condition, now numbered as
         condition 13, requiring the development to be carried out in accordance with
         approved plans.
5.       The present appeal concerns an application made under Section 73 to vary
         condition 13 of the fourth permission by substituting a different set of plans.
         Although the fourth permission is a freestanding grant of planning permission, its
         description of development is not itself an act of development. It is nevertheless
         clear that it authorised an alternative version of the scheme permitted by the first
         permission. For that reason, and because the description appears on the
         application form and is confirmed in the appellant’s submissions, I have adopted
         the description from the first permission in the heading and formal decision. I am
         satisfied that doing so causes no injustice to any party.
Main Issue
6.       The main issue is the effect of the revised scheme on the living conditions of the
         occupiers of 54 The Warren, having regard to outlook.
Reasons
7.       The relationship between Plot 6 and No. 54 has already been examined in detail
         through two earlier appeal decisions, including the one referred to above. In the
         dismissed appeal2, the dwelling proposed at Plot 6 was of a broadly comparable
         size to that now before me. In contrast, the allowed appeal, referred to above,
         concerned a smaller dwelling occupying a similar footprint but with a reduced
         first-floor element.
8.       In the dismissed appeal, the Inspector found harm to the living conditions of the
         occupiers of No. 54 arising solely from the effect of the steeply pitched roof on the
         principal flank kitchen window. That window has since been removed and replaced
         with rear-facing openings. The only aspect of the earlier scheme that was judged
         harmful in terms of the neighbour’s living conditions has therefore fallen away. The
         present assessment must proceed on the basis of the physical relationship as it
         now exists, rather than on the circumstances that informed the previous decision.

9.    Plot 6 would continue to occupy the same established spatial relationship with No.
      54 as in the approved scheme. The two buildings sit in a configuration that has
      already been tested through successive permissions, and nothing in the present
      proposal alters that underlying arrangement. No. 54 benefits from a notably
      generous rear garden, both wide and deep, which extends well beyond the point at
      which Plot 6 is perceived. The principal outlook from the kitchen and living spaces
      is directed down that garden, rather than towards the flank of Plot 6. The openness
      of the garden, its depth, and the orientation of the buildings together ensure that
      the increased height now proposed would not materially change how Plot 6 is
      experienced from within No. 54.
10. The Council argues that the increased eaves height would raise the mass of the
    building at a level most readily perceived from ground-floor rooms and the patio
    area. While the eaves height would increase, the separation distance and the
    orientation of the dwellings mean that the additional height would not introduce a
    sense of undue enclosure. The mature tree on the boundary provides partial
    screening of oblique views, though I do not rely on vegetation for the purposes of
    this assessment. Even without it, the combination of distance, orientation and the
    depth of the neighbouring garden ensures that the increased height would not
    result in a materially harmful change in outlook.
11. The Council’s overlay drawing illustrates the difference between the dismissed
    appeal scheme and the current proposal. I acknowledge that the roof form is taller
    than that considered in the dismissed appeal. However, the Inspector in that
    appeal expressly found that the loss of outlook to the garden would not be
    significant. The only harm identified related to the flank kitchen window. That
    window no longer exists. The Council has not demonstrated that the modest
    increase in height now proposed results in a materially different or harmful effect
    on the rear-facing rooms or garden. The evidence before me does not show that
    the increased height would be perceived as visually intrusive or overbearing when
    experienced from the dwelling or its garden.
12. For these reasons, the revised scheme would not result in unacceptable harm to
    the living conditions of the occupiers of 54 The Warren, having regard to outlook.
    Consequently, it complies with Policies GC3, H3, H13 and H14 of the Chiltern
    District Local Plan (Adopted September 1997, with subsequent alterations), insofar
    as they seek to safeguard the amenities of neighbouring occupiers.
Other Matter
13. Concerns have been raised by an interested party regarding the presence of roof
    openings and compliance with a condition of the original appeal decision. The
    revised plans do not introduce any flank-facing openings that would give rise to
    overlooking of No. 54, and the same restriction would continue to apply to the
    varied permission. Nothing else raised alters my conclusion on the main issue.
Conditions
14. In light of my findings on the appeal, condition 13 shall be amended to reflect the
    updated suite of approved plans associated with the revised scheme. Furthermore,
    it is necessary to restate in full all conditions from the previous planning permission
    that remain applicable, as the effect of allowing the appeal is to grant a new and
    separate planning permission. I shall proceed accordingly.
