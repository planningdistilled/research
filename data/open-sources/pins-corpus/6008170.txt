Appeal Ref: 6008170
Burnham Grammar School, Hogfair Lane, Burnham, Buckinghamshire SL1 7HG
•    The appeal is made under Section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
     against a grant of planning permission subject to conditions.
•    The appeal is made by Dylan Featherstone against the decision of Buckinghamshire Council.
•    The application Ref PL/25/4954/FA was approved on 11 March 2026 and planning permission was
     granted subject to conditions.
•    The development permitted is erection of freestanding canopy and installation of hardstanding.
•    The condition in dispute is No 3 which states that: Prior to the commencement of development above
     ground level, details of ecological enhancements of bat and bird boxes, shall have been submitted
     to, and approved in writing by the Local Planning Authority. The development shall proceed in
     accordance with the ecological enhancements, which shall have been installed prior to the first
     occupation of the development and retained thereafter unless otherwise agreed in writing by the
     Local Planning Authority.
•    The reason given for the condition is: In accordance with NPPF and Core Policy 9: Natural
     Environment of the South Bucks Core Strategy and to provide roosting features to species of
     conservation concern.

Decision
1.    The appeal is allowed and the planning permission Ref PL/25/4954/FA for erection
      of freestanding canopy and installation of hardstanding at Burnham Grammar
      School, Hogfair Lane, Burnham, Buckinghamshire, SL1 7HG granted on 11 March
      2026 by Buckinghamshire Council, is varied by deleting condition 3.
Application for costs
2.    An application for costs was made by Dylan Featherstone against
      Buckinghamshire Council. This application is the subject of a separate decision.
Main Issue
3.    The main issue is whether the disputed condition (condition 3) is reasonable and
      necessary in the interest of the conservation of protected species.
Reasons
4.    The appeal relates to a freestanding canopy positioned between existing bike
      racks and fenced courts to the rear of the school building. The Council’s officer
      report describes the structure as lightweight, open sided and self-supporting, with
      no enclosed elements and no interaction with existing buildings or vegetation.
5.    The Council’s Ecology Officer raised no objection and did not identify any
      ecological constraints that would be affected by the works. The appellant
      submitted a Biodiversity Net Gain metric demonstrating a net gain through the
      planting of five trees, and the Council accepted this position. The officer report

      confirms that the proposal would not result in the loss of habitat or disturbance to
      species, and that there is no pathway by which ecological harm could arise.
6.    The reason given in the Council’s decision notice for condition 3 refers to Core
      Policy 9 of the South Bucks Core Strategy (2011) (CS) and to the National
      Planning Policy Framework (the Framework). The Ecology Officer relied on two
      paragraphs of the 2024 Framework which encouraged integrating opportunities to
      improve biodiversity, minimising impacts, providing net gains, strengthening
      ecological networks and incorporating features that support priority species such
      as swifts, bats and hedgehogs.
7.    The 2024 Framework has since been replaced by the 2026 Framework, published
      on 17 August 2026. Policy N2 requires development to conserve and enhance
      natural features, strengthen ecological networks, use nature-based solutions and
      minimise adverse impacts on biodiversity. It also expects development to include
      features supporting priority or threatened species unless compelling technical
      reasons prevent their use, and makes clear that biodiversity gains beyond
      statutory net gain should only be required where set out in up-to-date development
      plan policies for specific site allocations.
8.    Core Policy 9 is a general natural environment policy rather than a site-specific
      allocation, and when read alongside Policy N2 it cannot be relied upon to require
      biodiversity enhancements beyond statutory net gain. The statutory regime already
      provides the appropriate mechanism for securing biodiversity improvements where
      they are needed, and the Council has accepted that the proposal achieves a
      compliant net gain. No evidence indicates that the development affects ecological
      networks, priority habitats or species, or that any ecological harm arises which
      would necessitate mitigation or enhancement.
9.    The canopy’s lightweight polycarbonate roof, steel posts, limited height and the
      level of disturbance and artificial lighting associated with the school environment
      represent compelling technical reasons why bat or bird boxes would be unsuitable
      or ineffective when attached to the structure. In these circumstances, Policy N2
      does not require integrated provision, as the development itself cannot support
      such features and no ecological impact has been identified that would necessitate
      mitigation.
10. The Council suggested that bat or bird boxes could instead be installed elsewhere
    within the wider school grounds. However, Policy N2 expects ecological features
    to be incorporated into the development unless compelling technical reasons
    prevent their use, and it does not support off-site installation where the
    development has no material ecological impact. No evidence has been presented
    to show that enhancements on unrelated parts of the school site would be relevant
    to the acceptability of the canopy or proportionate to its effects.
11. In these circumstances, neither Core Policy 9 of the CS nor Policy N2 of the
    Framework requires ecological enhancements beyond the statutory net gain
    already secured, and the absence of any ecological impact means that no further
    measures are justified.
12. Policy DM6 of the Framework requires that conditions be imposed only where they
    are necessary to make the development acceptable, relevant to the development
    and to planning considerations generally, sufficiently precise to be capable of
    compliance and enforcement, and reasonable in all other respects. Applied here,

      condition 3 is not necessary, as no ecological impact has been identified, and it is
      not reasonable, as it imposes obligations disproportionate to the scale and nature
      of the proposal and unrelated to any identified harm. It therefore fails the tests set
      out in Policy DM6.
13. For these reasons, I find that disputed condition 3 is not reasonable or necessary.
    Its removal would not lead to material harm to biodiversity interests, nor would it
    undermine the measurable net gains already secured through the accepted
    Biodiversity Net Gain assessment. Without the condition, the development would
    remain compliant with Core Policy 9 of the CS, insofar as it requires proportionate
    conservation and enhancement of the natural environment. Moreover, there would
    not be a conflict with Policy N2 of the Framework, which expects development to
    minimise impacts on biodiversity, provide net gains and incorporate features that
    support priority or threatened species where appropriate.
Conclusion
14. For the reasons given above, the appeal should be allowed, and the planning
    permission varied in the terms I have explained.
