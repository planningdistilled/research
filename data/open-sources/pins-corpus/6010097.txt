Appeal Decision
Site visit made on 19 August 2026
by Lewis Condé BSc, MSc, MRTPI
an Inspector appointed by the Secretary of State
Decision date: 30 September 2026


Appeal Ref: 6010097
Pound Scots, Chinnor Road, Bledlow Ridge, Buckinghamshire HP14 4AJ
    The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
     against a refusal to grant planning permission.
    The appeal is made by Reading and Kemsley against the decision of Buckinghamshire Council.
    The application Ref is PL/25/4253/FA.
    The development proposed is the construction of a self-build 3-bedroom detached dwellinghouse,
     carport, shed/bike store and vehicular access together with associated hardstanding and
     landscaping works.

Decision
1.    The appeal is dismissed.
Preliminary Matters
2.    A new National Planning Policy Framework (the Framework) was published on
      17 August 2026. Main parties have been provided with an appropriate opportunity
      to comment on its implications, and where received comments have been
      considered in my determination of the appeal.
3.    The appellants’ names in the banner heading above are taken directly from the
      original planning application form.
Main Issue
4.    The main issue is the effect of the proposal on the significance of Pound Scots and
      the listed waterspout within its garden, both Grade II listed buildings.
Reasons
5.    Pound Scots is a Grade II listed building dating from the mid-to-late eighteenth
      century. Its significance derives principally from its age, vernacular character,
      historic fabric and detailing, including its brick construction, flint plinth, tiled roof
      and timber casements. Its spacious garden setting, containing formal planting and
      mature vegetation and allowing views across the surrounding Chiltern landscape,
      contributes to that significance. In particular, it allows the building to be
      appreciated as a substantial historic dwelling within generous, landscaped
      grounds.
6.    The appeal site forms part of the front garden of Pound Scots. Boundary
      treatment, planting and previous residential development have altered the wider
      plot and limit the site’s relationship with the road. Even so, its verdant garden
      character, mature planting and relationship with the dwelling continue to contribute
      appreciably to the spacious garden setting in which the listed building is

         experienced. This part of the garden therefore remains a meaningful component of
         the listed building’s setting.
7.       The Grade II listed waterspout dates from the early eighteenth century and
         comprises a finely worked lead feature mounted on a timber stand. Its significance
         derives from its age, craftsmanship and historic ornamental value. The available
         evidence indicates that the waterspout has previously been relocated,
         nonetheless, its domestic garden setting still provides an important context in
         which its historic and aesthetic interest is experienced.
8.       The proposal would introduce a dwelling, access and associated domestic activity
         into this part of the garden. It would diminish the spacious character of the frontage
         and alter the way in which Pound Scots is experienced within its grounds.
         Although the proposed dwelling would be positioned towards the roadside edge
         and sizeable parts of the wider garden setting would remain, the development
         would erode the spacious landscaped character that contributes to the listed
         building’s significance. It would therefore fail to preserve the building’s setting and
         would cause harm to its significance. Given the extent of the affected part of the
         garden and the retention of the wider landscaped setting, that harm would be
         modest in extent.
9.       No physical works are proposed to the waterspout. I do not, however, share the
         main parties’ view that the proposed works, including landscaping, would give it
         greater prominence or better reveal its significance. Its position in relation to the
         proposed built form and the likely presence of parked vehicles would limit any such
         benefit. Nevertheless, the waterspout would remain within a domestic garden and
         relatively close to the proposed vegetable garden, thereby retaining an appropriate
         functional relationship with its surroundings. Although the subdivision and
         development of the wider garden would alter the context in which the waterspout is
         experienced, that change would not detract from its historic or aesthetic interest.
         The proposal would therefore preserve its setting and significance.
10. The Framework requires substantial weight to be given to the conservation of
    designated heritage assets. It also establishes that any harm to such an asset is a
    matter of considerable importance and weight and must be weighed against the
    public benefits of the proposal. In carrying out that balance, I have had special
    regard to the desirability of preserving the setting of Pound Scots and the listed
    waterspout.
11. The proposal would provide one dwelling within Bledlow Ridge’s defined
    settlement boundary, in a broadly sustainable location where residential
    development is supported in principle. Although its numerical contribution would be
    modest, it would help to meet an evidenced need in circumstances where the
    Council has a significant housing land supply shortfall. In accordance with policy
    HO7 of the Framework, I attach substantial weight to this benefit. However, as
    there is no appropriate legal mechanism to secure the dwelling as self-build
    housing1, I attach no additional weight on that basis.
12. Limited economic and social benefits would arise from construction activity, future
    occupier spending and support for local services. The proposed biodiversity
    enhancements and incorporation of renewable and low-carbon emission measures

13. The preservation of the waterspout’s significance is a neutral consideration in the
    planning balance. Works to reinstate a historic pathway to Pound Scots fall outside
    of the appeal site’s boundary. Even if there were an appropriate mechanism before
    me to suitably secure these works, they would only offer a limited public benefit
    through providing glimpsed views of the heritage asset from public vantage points.
14. The proposal would cause harm to the significance of Pound Scots through the
    erosion of its spacious garden setting. Although that harm would be modest in
    extent, I give substantial weight to the conservation of the listed building and
    considerable importance and weight to the desirability of preserving its setting. The
    substantial weight attached to the additional dwelling, together with the other
    public benefits, would not outweigh that harm. The proposal would therefore
    conflict with policy HE6 of the Framework.
15. As the proposal is within a settlement, it benefits from the in-principle support
    provided by policies S3 and S4 of the Framework. However, that support is subject
    to assessment against the Framework’s national decision-making policies,
    including policy HE6. Given the substantial weight afforded to conservation and
    the considerable importance and weight attached to preserving the setting of
    Pound Scots, the adverse heritage effect would substantially outweigh the benefits
    of granting permission. The proposal would therefore conflict with policy S4 of the
    Framework.
16. Bringing the above together, whilst the proposal would preserve the significance of
    the listed waterspout it would cause harm to the significance of Pound Scots. The
    proposal therefore conflicts with Policies CP11 and DM31 of the Wycombe District
    Local Plan (adopted 2019) that seek to protect the historic environment, whilst it is
    also contrary to policies HE6 and S4 of the Framework.
Other Matters
17. The appeal site lies within the Chilterns National Landscape. The site contributes
    to the green and wooded character of Bledlow Ridge but is a garden plot within the
    existing village, rather than part of the open countryside that defines much of the
    National Landscape’s scenic qualities. The proposal would continue the dispersed
    linear pattern of development along the settlement’s roads and would be seen in
    the context of surrounding dwellings. A single dwelling in this location would not
    materially erode the landscape character, scenic beauty or tranquillity of the
    Chilterns National Landscape. I therefore find no conflict with the objective of
    conserving and enhancing the natural beauty of this nationally protected
    landscape.
18. The proposed development evolved through correspondence with the Council and
    incorporates amendments intended to address its concerns. Nevertheless, those
    changes do not overcome the harm to the significance of Pound Scots identified
    above.
19. I have been referred to other residential development in the village, including a
    nearby site adjoining Pound Scots. However, I do not have the full details or
    planning histories of those schemes. In any event, their circumstances and

20. The appeal site lies within the Green Belt, but the Council did not allege Green
    Belt harm. As the appeal is dismissed on the main issue, it is unnecessary to
    determine whether the proposal would constitute inappropriate development in the
    Green Belt because that assessment would not alter the outcome.
Conclusion
21. For the reasons given above, the proposal conflicts with the development plan and
    material considerations, including the approach of the Framework, do not justify a
    decision other than in accordance with it. The appeal is therefore dismissed.
