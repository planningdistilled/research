•   The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
    against a refusal to grant permission in principle.
•   The appeal is made by Mr Craig Gibbs against the decision of Coventry City Council.
•   The application reference is PL/2025/0001991/PIP.
•   The development proposed is Stage 1 Permission in Principle: erection of a single dwelling.

Decision
1. The appeal is allowed and permission in principle is granted for Stage 1 Permission
   in Principle: erection of a single dwelling at 236 Hawkes Mill Lane, Coventry CV5
   9FJ in accordance with the terms of the application, Ref PL/2025/0001991/PIP.
Preliminary Matters
2. The appeal relates to an application for Permission in Principle (PiP). This is the
   first stage of the process and considers only whether the site is suitable in principle
   for the amount of residential development proposed, having regard to the location,
   land use and amount of development. Matters of detailed design, appearance,
   layout, landscaping and other technical requirements would be considered at the
   technical details consent stage if permission in principle were granted.
3. Following the submission of the appeal, a new National Planning Policy Framework
   (the Framework) was issued on 17 August 2026. The main parties were given the
   opportunity to comment on the implications of the new Framework for this appeal.
   Where comments have been received, I have taken them into account in reaching
   my decision.
4. The Council has referred to the emerging Coventry Local Plan Review. I have
   taken account of the relevant policies within the Review insofar as they are a
   material consideration. However, as the Review has not been adopted, the weight
   that can be given to those policies depends on their stage of preparation, the extent
   to which there are unresolved objections to them, and their degree of consistency
   with the Framework. Given that the Review remains at examination stage, I have
   afforded the relevant emerging policies limited weight and have determined the
   appeal having regard primarily to the adopted development plan and the
   Framework.
Main Issue
5. The main issue relevant to the appeal is whether the proposed development would
   be inappropriate development in the Green Belt, having regard to the Framework
   and local development plan policies.

Reasons
Whether Inappropriate Development
6. Policy GB6 of the Framework states that development in the Green Belt is
   inappropriate unless it falls within one of the categories in Policy GB7. Policy
   GB7(1)(g) provides that development is not inappropriate where it would utilise grey
   belt land and would not fundamentally undermine the purposes, taken together, of
   the remaining Green Belt across the area of the plan; where there is an evidenced
   unmet need for the type of development proposed; and where the development
   would be in a sustainable location, with particular reference to Policy TR3.
7. Grey belt is defined in the Framework glossary as land in the Green Belt
   comprising previously developed land and/or any other land that, in either case,
   does not strongly contribute to purposes (a), (b), or (d) in Policy GB2. The relevant
   purposes are to check the unrestricted sprawl of large built-up areas, to prevent
   neighbouring towns merging into one another, and to preserve the setting and
   special character of historic towns.
8. The appeal site is bound by a row of houses on one side and by Ted Pitts Lane on
   the other, which takes access from Hawkes Mill Lane. These features enclose the
   site and create hard boundaries. Beyond Ted Pitts Lane is farmland and associated
   farm buildings, followed by a continuation of ribbon development. In that context,
   the site is located alongside existing development with built form around it, and the
   proposal would not extend built form beyond existing hard boundaries. I therefore
   find that the site does not strongly contribute to purpose (a).
9. As the site is enclosed by houses and Ted Pitts Lane, the proposal would not
   extend development towards nearby towns or materially reduce the separation
   between them. The appeal site does not subsequently strongly contribute to
   purpose (b). Nor is the site located so as to form part of the setting of a historic
   town, and so it does not strongly contribute to purpose (d).
10. Having regard to those matters, I consider that the appeal site is grey belt land. The
    proposal would be for a single dwelling on a modestly sized, enclosed plot
    alongside existing built form and with Ted Pitts Lane forming a clear physical
    boundary to the open land beyond. I acknowledge the Council’s concern that
    development would encroach into the countryside. However, given the site’s
    contained character, its relationship with adjoining houses and the limited scale of
    the proposed development, it would not undermine the purpose of safeguarding the
    countryside from encroachment. It would also not affect the Green Belt’s role in
    encouraging urban regeneration, as there is no substantive evidence that allowing
    this small-scale development would prejudice the use of urban land or previously
    developed land elsewhere. Given its limited scale, enclosed character and
    relationship with existing development, the proposal would not fundamentally
    undermine the purposes, taken together, of the remaining Green Belt across the
    area of the plan.
11. The Housing Delivery Test (HDT) is the Government’s published measurement for
    assessing housing delivery and forms part of the basis for national policy. I
    acknowledge the Council’s submissions that evidence supporting the emerging
    Local Plan Review has questioned the ONS figures that informed the HDT, that the
    emerging plan is progressing through examination, and that adoption may lead to a
    different position in due course. However, the Local Plan Review has not yet been

     adopted and the HDT remains the published measure for the purposes of applying
     national policy at the time of this decision. I have not been provided with
     substantive evidence that would justify disregarding the published HDT result or
     giving it limited weight in this appeal. The appeal must therefore be determined
     having regard to the currently adopted development plan and the material
     considerations that apply now. In that context, the published HDT result remains a
     relevant and important consideration.
12. For applications involving the provision of housing, footnote 41 to Policy GB7
    explains that an evidenced unmet need exists where the HDT result was below
    75% of the housing requirement over the previous three years. On the evidence
    before me, and having regard to the published HDT result, I find that there is an
    evidenced unmet need for the proposed dwelling.
13. Policy TR3 of the Framework addresses the relationship between new
    development, movement and transport infrastructure. It promotes locations where
    day-to-day travel can be undertaken by a choice of modes, where access is
    suitable for different users and where existing or planned transport provision can be
    used effectively. It also seeks to reduce reliance on the private car, while
    recognising that opportunities to improve walking, wheeling, cycling and public
    transport in rural areas will depend on what is practicable and capable of being
    supported by the scale and nature of the development.
14. The appeal site is located in Hawkes End, which itself contains few services or
    facilities. Nevertheless, the appeal site is located near Allesley, a settlement where
    many day-to-day needs could be met, as it contains a broad range of services and
    facilities and can be reached from the site by footpath. I recognise that the section
    closest to the appeal site is not fully street lit. Even so, much of the route is lit and it
    would provide a realistic pedestrian option for future occupiers. While there is an
    absence of dedicated cycle routes to Allesley, the site is also within a distance at
    which cycling would be practicable. Nearby bus services would add a further
    alternative to travel solely by private car to Coventry, which has a wide variety of
    services and facilities.
15. Taking these factors together, occupiers of the proposed dwelling would have
    access to services, facilities and transport choices, including walking, cycling and
    public transport options, such that day-to-day travel would not be wholly dependent
    on the private car. The proposal would therefore accord with Policy TR3 of the
    Framework. I therefore find that it would be in a sustainable location. As the site is
    grey belt land, there is an evidenced unmet need and the location is sustainable,
    the proposal would meet the relevant requirements of Policy GB7(1)(g).
16. Given that conclusion, it is not necessary for me to reach a separate finding on
    whether the proposal would amount to limited infilling in a village or within an
    existing ribbon development. Those matters formed part of the parties’ cases,
    including references to judgments and other appeal decisions, but they would not
    alter my finding that the proposal would not be inappropriate development under
    the grey belt provisions of the Framework.
17. I have also had regard to the 1979 appeal decision relating to the appeal site. That
    decision is a material consideration because it concerned the same land and
    referred to its relationship with nearby ribbon development. However, it was made
    under a different national policy context and before the current grey belt provisions

18. It follows that the location and land use of the development would not be
    inappropriate development in the Green Belt. In reaching that conclusion, I have
    had regard to Policies GB1 of the Coventry Local Plan 2017 as well as Policy GB1

    Belt from inappropriate development, with proposals to be considered against
    national Green Belt policy. For the reasons set out above, the proposal would
    accord with those policies and the Framework.
Other Matters
19. The appeal site lies outside of, but near to, the Brownshill Green Conservation
    Area. Its significance derives from its special architectural and historic interest as a
    former rural settlement on the edge of Coventry, including its agricultural heritage,
    historic buildings, traditional materials, trees and views that contribute to its
    distinctive character. I have had special regard to the desirability of preserving or
    enhancing the character or appearance of the Conservation Area, as required by
    section 72(1) of the Planning (Listed Buildings and Conservation Areas) Act 1990.
    However, there is existing built form between the appeal site and the Conservation
    Area. This intervening development limits the visual and spatial relationship
    between them. Consequently, a dwelling on the appeal site would not harm the
    features that contribute to the area’s significance. Subject to matters to be
    determined at the technical details consent stage, the scheme would preserve the
    character and appearance of the Conservation Area and the elements of its setting
    that make a positive contribution to its significance.
20. The appellant’s personal circumstances, including the identified need for accessible
    accommodation, have been advanced as part of the case. I have had due regard to
    the Public Sector Equality Duty under section 149 of the Equality Act 2010.
    However, as I have found the proposal acceptable in principle, having regard to the
    development plan and the Framework, it has not been necessary to consider these
    circumstances in this instance.
Conditions
21. As set out in the Planning Practice Guidance, it is not possible for conditions to be
    attached to a grant of permission in principle. Any detailed matters would instead
    be considered at the technical details consent stage.
Conclusion
22. The proposed development would be in accordance with the development plan,
    and the material considerations do not indicate that a decision should be taken
    otherwise than in accordance with it. Therefore, for the reasons given above, I
    conclude that the appeal should be allowed.
