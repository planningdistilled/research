 Appeal Decision
 Site visit made on 10 September 2026
 By SJ Desai BSc MSc MRTPI
 an Inspector appointed by the Secretary of State
 Decision date: 25 September 2026


 Appeal Ref: 6013792
 29 The Covert, Petts Wood, Orpington, Bromley BR6 0BT
 •    The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
      against a refusal to grant planning permission.
 •    The appeal is made by Ms Krejcirove & Mr Grant against the decision of the Council of the London
      Borough of Bromley.
 •    The application Ref is 26/00971/HPA.
 •    The development proposed is loft conversion with rear dormer and side roof window.

Decision
1. The appeal is dismissed.
Preliminary Matters
2. I have determined the appeal on the basis of the Council’s description of the
   development, as set out on the decision notice. I have used that description in the
   banner heading above because it accurately describes the development proposed.
3. A new National Planning Policy Framework (the Framework) was published on 17
   August 2026. However, I am satisfied that the policy approach relevant to this
   appeal has not fundamentally changed and that no party would be prejudiced by
   my having regard to it. Accordingly, I have determined the appeal having regard to
   the current Framework.
Main Issue
4. The main issue is the effect of the proposed development on the character and
   appearance of the host dwelling and the surrounding area, including the special
   residential character of the Petts Wood Area of Special Residential Character
   (ASRC), and whether it would preserve or enhance the character or appearance of
   The Covert Conservation Area (CA).
Reasons
5. The appeal relates to a two-storey semi-detached dwelling within a predominantly
   residential area. The dwelling forms part of an attractive group of inter-war houses
   characterised by traditional architectural detailing, prominent front-facing gables,
   pitched roofs and a generally verdant setting.
6. The appeal site is located in the CA. The terms of Section 72 of the Planning
   (Listed Buildings and Conservation Areas) Act 1990 (the Act), requires me to pay
   special attention to the desirability of preserving or enhancing the character or
   appearance of conservation areas.

7. As identified in The Covert Conservation Area Appraisal and Management Plan
   (2025), the significance of the CA derives principally from its garden-suburb
   character and its consistent pattern of inter-war detached and semi-detached
   dwellings, including their prominent front gables, traditional detailing, spacing,
   gardens, trees and landscaping. Together, these features provide a cohesive and
   distinctive residential character. The appeal property exhibits many of these
   characteristics and contributes positively to the significance of the CA.
8. The site also lies within the ASRC. Its special qualities overlap with those of the CA
   and include its low-density garden-suburb layout, spacious and verdant character,
   traditional architectural forms and the established rhythm and symmetry between
   individual and neighbouring pairs of dwellings.
9. The proposal would introduce a dormer into the rear roof slope, together with a
   side-facing roof window. The Council have raised no concerns in respect of the
   side-facing window subject to a condition requiring obscure glazing. I see no
   reason to disagree. The proposed dormer would be positioned below the main
   ridge and would retain margins between it and the sides of the roof. Nevertheless, it
   would extend across a substantial proportion of the rear roof slope.
10. As a result of its considerable width, scale and box-like form, the dormer would
    appear as a bulky and visually dominant addition rather than a subordinate roof
    alteration. Although the original ridge and eaves would remain apparent, the
    dormer would substantially reduce the prominence and legibility of the original rear
    roof slope. It would consequently detract from the architectural integrity of the host
    dwelling and disrupt the established balance and symmetry of the semi-detached
    pair with 27 The Covert (No.27).
11. The appellant sets out that the dormer would remain confined to the rear roof slope
    and would avoid any alteration to the principal elevation or the dwelling's footprint.
    However, these factors do not overcome the harm to the architectural balance and
    symmetry of the semi-detached pair resulting from the dormer’s considerable width
    and bulk. It would consequently detract from the established rhythm and symmetry
    which contribute to the special character of the ASRC.
12. I accept that the dormer would not be visible from The Covert and that the principal
    elevation, prominent front gable and front roof line would remain unaffected.
    However, the significance of the CA and character of the ASRC are not confined
    solely to public views or front elevations. The architectural form of buildings,
    including the established rhythm and symmetry of semi-detached pairs, also
    contributes to their character. The absence of an effect on the street-facing
    elevation therefore does not overcome the harm identified.
13. The dormer would be visible principally from the adjoining garden at No.27 and
    from a limited number of dwellings to the rear. Although intervening trees and
    vegetation would limit wider views, particularly when in leaf, its considerable width
    and bulk would nonetheless be apparent. Its rear position and screening would limit
    the extent of its effect but would not overcome the harm arising.
14. The appellant refers to rear dormers at 19 and 44 The Covert and has provided
    photographs of these examples. However, I have limited information regarding their
    detailed circumstances or the reasoning which led to those decisions. Moreover,
    the existence of other dormers does not demonstrate that the particular scale and

     form of the proposal before me would be acceptable. I therefore afford these
     examples limited weight and have determined the appeal on its individual merits.
15. Planning permission was previously granted at the appeal property under Ref
    21/04960/FULL6 for development which included a rear dormer. The approved
    dormer was appreciably narrower and more contained within the roof slope. This
    establishes that a suitably designed rear dormer can be acceptable at the property.
    However, it does not establish that a dormer of materially greater width and bulk
    would also be acceptable. The material differences in scale and form mean that the
    previous permission does not lead me to a different conclusion.
16. The Council also raises concern about the proposed external materials, including
    the rendered dormer cheeks. The appellant is willing to accept a condition requiring
    tile-hung finishes and roof tiles matching or closely corresponding with those of the
    existing dwelling. I consider that suitable materials could be secured by condition
    and would assist in integrating the addition with the existing roof. However, such a
    condition would not overcome the fundamental harm arising from the dormer’s
    scale and form.
17. I therefore find that the proposal would cause less than substantial harm to the
    significance of the CA. The harm would be limited by the dormer's rear position and
    the relatively restricted extent of views of it. Nevertheless, the Framework requires
    great weight to be given to the conservation of designated heritage assets. I attach
    great weight to the harm identified.
18. The proposal would provide additional accommodation and enable the occupants
    to adapt the dwelling to meet their needs. However, these are principally private
    benefits. Although the additional accommodation may make more efficient use of
    the existing housing stock, the proposal would not provide an additional dwelling or
    materially increase housing provision. I therefore attach limited weight to the public
    benefits of the proposal.
19. The limited public benefits would not outweigh the less than substantial harm to the
    significance of the CA, to which I attach great weight. Accordingly, the proposal
    would fail to preserve the character or appearance of the CA.
20. Overall, I conclude that the proposal would harm the character and appearance of
    the host dwelling, fail to preserve the character or appearance of the CA and
    detract from the special residential character of the ASRC, including the
    established balance and symmetry of the semi-detached pair. It would therefore
    conflict with the design requirements of Policies 6, 37, 41 and 44 of the Bromley
    Local Plan 2019 which, amongst other things, require development to complement
    the scale, proportion and form of the host dwelling and surrounding development,
    and protect the distinctive character and heritage significance of the area.
Other Matters
21. The Council found no unacceptable effects on neighbouring living conditions,
    protected trees, biodiversity or highway safety. These are neutral matters which do
    not weigh in favour of the appeal.

Conclusion
22. The proposal does not accord with the development plan when considered as a
    whole and there are no material considerations of sufficient weight, including the
    Framework, to indicate that the development should be determined otherwise than
    in accordance with it. The appeal should therefore be dismissed.
