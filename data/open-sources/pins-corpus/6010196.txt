Site visit made on 14 August 2026

Appeal Ref: 6010196
Land at OS 7540 6621 Stourport Road, Great Witley WR6 6JP
•   The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
    against a refusal to grant planning permission.
•   The appeal is made by Mr Karl Lockley against the decision of Malvern Hills District Council.
•   The application Ref is M/25/01626/FUL.
•   The development proposed is 4no. dwellings and associated works.

Decision
1. The appeal is dismissed.
Preliminary Matters
2. The planning application form used a description of the site location rather than a
   full site address. The appeal form included the grid reference and postcode, so I
   have included those for clarity in the heading above.
3. Since the application was determined, the Council adopted the South
   Worcestershire Development Plan Review 2021-2041 (Adopted March 2026)
   (SWDPR). This superseded the policies against which the appeal proposal had
   originally been determined. The main parties addressed this in their appeal
   statements. I have taken the updated policies into account in making my decision.
4. Upon adoption of the SWDPR the Council was able to demonstrate a five-year
   supply of deliverable housing with the required buffer. During the appeal, the
   Council provided evidence about its housing land supply, and the appellant has
   been given the opportunity to comment on this during the appeal.
5. During the course of the appeal, a revised National Planning Policy Framework (the
   Framework) was published on 17 August 2026. The main parties were given an
   opportunity to comment on the implications of the revised Framework for the
   appeal, and I have taken those comments into account in reaching my decision.
   References to the Framework in my decision are to the August 2026 version.
6. The Council's second reason for refusal related to the absence of a completed
   planning obligation to secure mitigation including affordable housing and provision
   for maintenance of public open space. During the appeal, an executed planning
   obligation to which the Council is a party dated 30 June 2026 was submitted. The
   Council confirmed that the executed obligation would address its concerns and I
   have no reason to disagree. Accordingly, I am satisfied that the proposal makes
   appropriate provision in these respects and that the second reason for refusal has
   been overcome.

7. The Council’s third reason for refusal arose from concerns regarding visibility,
   pedestrian connectivity, sustainability, and gradients. The second reason for refusal
   also referred to the need for highway-related contributions, although neither the
   decision notice nor the officer report explained what mitigation was required.
   Additional transport information submitted with the appeal has been considered by
   the highway authority, which advised that the remaining matters can be addressed
   by conditions. Therefore, this matter has been resolved.
8. Updated ecological information was submitted during the appeal and the Council's
   ecologist is satisfied that the statutory biodiversity gain objective can be secured
   through conditions. The planning obligation secures the necessary biodiversity
   monitoring contribution and requires submission of a Habitat Management and
   Monitoring Plan. Consequently, this matter has also been resolved.
9. Accordingly, although these matters formed part of the Council's reasons for
   refusal, they no longer represent substantive areas of dispute between the parties
   and do not weigh against the proposal, so I have not treated them as main issues.
10. In considering these submissions, I have been mindful that the appeal process
    should not be used to evolve a scheme. Having regard to the judgment in Holborn
    Studios Ltd1, I consider that the additional material provides clarification and
    mechanisms to secure matters relevant to the development, rather than materially
    altering the proposal. As the material was submitted with the appeal, the Council
    has had the opportunity to comment on it, and I am satisfied that no interested
    party has been prejudiced.
Main Issues
11. The main issues are:
             •    Whether the proposed development would make effective and efficient use
                  of the land, having regard to the character of the area; and
             •    Whether the proposal could provide satisfactory drainage arrangements.
Reasons
Background
12. The appeal site comprises part of an agricultural field fronting Stourport Road, on
    the edge of Great Witley. The settlement boundary is across the road from the
    appeal site.
13. Great Witley is identified in the development plan settlement hierarchy as a village
    with a range of services and facilities. The appeal site is therefore reasonably
    accessible to the village’s services notwithstanding its location outside the
    settlement boundary. These circumstances were also recognised when permission
    in principle (PiP)2 was granted on appeal for up to nine dwellings on the appeal
    site.
14. The Council's refusal reason refers to former Policy SWDP 2 of the South
    Worcestershire Development Plan, now replaced by Policy SWDPR 03 of the
    SWDPR, which sets out the development plan's spatial development strategy and

Effective and efficient use of land
15. The appeal site is undeveloped and forms a break in built form between Turnpike
    Close and dwellings beyond an access track from Stourport Road. There is also
    development opposite the site, and a large new housing scheme is being built
    further along the opposite side of Stourport Road. The appeal proposes four
    relatively large, detached dwellings in substantial individual plots. Together with the
    internal access and other supporting infrastructure, this would produce a loose
    layout with appreciable distances between the buildings.
16. Consideration of the extant PiP was limited to location, land use, and the amount of
    development. It does not mean that nine dwellings must be provided, nor what
    would be an appropriate density or layout. Nevertheless, it establishes that the site
    is capable in principle of accommodating up to nine dwellings, subject to
    acceptable technical details.
17. The main parties use different density measures, with the Council referring both to
    4 dwellings per hectare (dph) and 4 dwellings per acre (dpa), while the appellant
    refers to 4 dpa. As the appellant's figure is supported by the submitted evidence, I
    have used that density calculation in my assessment.
18. Policy SWDPR 16 of the SWDPR requires housing development to make the most
    effective and efficient use of land and that density should enhance the character
    and quality of the local area while being commensurate with scheme viability and
    infrastructure capacity. Greater densities are expected in more accessible
    locations. For villages, the policy identifies 30 dph as the minimum net density for
    allocated sites, subject to considerations including character, accessibility, and
    other site-specific constraints. Windfall development is to be assessed against the
    density criteria relevant to its locality and the character of its built and natural
    environment.
19. As the appeal site is not an allocated housing site, the proposal must be assessed
    in its particular context. Nevertheless, the policy provides a clear indication of the
    density regarded as making effective use of land in villages. The proposal’s density
    as set out by the appellant would fall significantly below that benchmark.
20. The appeal site's prominent position at the countryside edge and its sloping
    topography support concentrating development on the lower, flatter part of the site.
    The surrounding area is characterised by a varied mix of dwellings, plot sizes, and
    densities. Development at Turnpike Close is arranged more closely, whereas
    properties on the opposite side of Stourport Road occupy larger and more varied
    plots. Consequently, whilst the proposal's spacious layout would not appear out of
    keeping with the character of the area, the local context does not demonstrate that
    the site's constraints require development at the very low density proposed. Nor
    does it suggest that a somewhat denser form of development on the lower part of
    the appeal site would necessarily be unacceptable in character and appearance
    terms.

21. Policy L3 of the Framework requires development proposals to make efficient use
    of land, taking account of the identified need for different types of housing, local
    market conditions, infrastructure, connectivity, and the importance of securing well-
    designed, attractive, and healthy places.
22. The Council’s Strategic Housing Market Assessment identifies a need for smaller
    affordable dwellings, whereas the proposal comprises a limited number of
    comparatively large homes. The site's topography, infrastructure requirements, and
    local character do not justify development at such a low density, nor do they
    indicate that a more efficient use of the appeal site could not be achieved whilst
    delivering an attractive, well-designed development.
23. Policy L3:2.b. of the Framework seeks to achieve appropriate densities and applies
    to land outside settlements, and to land at the edge of built-up areas which is
    allocated or has permission for development and so will form part of the settlement.
    The appeal site lies adjacent to the settlement boundary. Although the PiP does not
    authorise development until technical details consent is granted, it establishes
    permission in principle for residential development. Therefore, in accordance with
    Policy L3:2b. the proposal’s development footprint should make the best use of the
    site’s development potential. Policy L3:4. indicates that development proposals that
    do not make efficient use of land in accordance with paragraph 2 of the policy
    should be refused.
24. Drawing these matters together, the proposal’s low density has not been sufficiently
    justified by the appeal site’s context or constraints. It would not make the best use
    of the land where the principle of residential development has already been
    established. Therefore, it would conflict with Policy L3 of the Framework and Policy
    SWDPR 16 of the SWDPR, which seeks to secure the effective and efficient use of
    land while ensuring that density responds appropriately to local character,
    accessibility, and infrastructure capacity.
Drainage
25. The Council's concern in determining the proposal arose primarily from the
    absence of sufficient drainage information, including infiltration testing and
    evidence regarding foul drainage capacity. The appellant submitted a revised
    Water Management Statement with the appeal, including infiltration testing and a
    detailed drainage strategy. This indicates that surface water can largely be
    managed through infiltration, supported by attenuation storage designed to
    accommodate the 1-in-100 year storm event plus a climate change allowance, and
    that foul drainage can connect to the existing network. The Council's drainage
    engineer no longer disputes the feasibility of infiltration drainage, instead
    expressing a preference for additional Sustainable Drainage Systems (SuDS)
    features.
26. Although the Council's drainage engineer considers there is scope for additional
    SuDS features, the evidence before me indicates that surface water could be
    managed in line with the SuDS hierarchy by infiltration with any overflow in extreme
    events discharging to highway drainage. This would control run-off rates and
    volumes and would not increase flood risk on or off the site. I am therefore satisfied
    that the proposal would manage site drainage and run-off in a sustainable and co-
    ordinated way. If permission were granted, further details of a satisfactory drainage
    scheme could be required by appropriate conditions.

27. I therefore conclude that the proposal would provide satisfactory drainage
    arrangements. Accordingly, it would comply with Policy SWDPR 39 insofar as it
    expects all development proposals (as appropriate to their nature and scale) to,
    amongst other things, demonstrate that site drainage and run off will be managed
    in a sustainable and co-ordinated way, and to manage surface water through
    SuDS.
Other Matters
28. The appellant contends no opportunity was given to provide further information in
    respect of some matters before the application was determined. However, I must
    determine the appeal on the basis of the evidence now before me. That includes
    additional information submitted during the appeal which has enabled some of the
    Council's concerns to be resolved.
29. The Council accepts that, in accordance with Annex D paragraph 9 of the
    Framework, it is required to demonstrate a five-year supply of deliverable housing
    sites, including a 20% buffer. It has confirmed that the South Worcestershire
    Councils' Five Year Housing Land Supply Report, published in June 2026,
    demonstrates a 5.48-years housing land supply.
Planning Balance
30. The proposal conflicts with Policy L3: 4. of the Framework which states that
    development proposals which do not make efficient use of land in accordance with
    paragraph 2 of the policy should be refused. I have also found that the proposal
    would not make effective and efficient use of the site and would therefore conflict
    with Policy SWDPR 16.
31. Policy HO7 of the Framework states that substantial weight should be given to the
    benefits of providing homes which will contribute to meeting the evidenced
    accommodation needs of the community. The Council's evidence indicates a
    particular need for smaller homes which is not addressed by the proposal.
    Consequently, whilst the proposal would contribute towards housing delivery, this
    limits the weight I attach to that benefit.
32. I acknowledge that benefits would arise through construction activity and spending
    by future occupiers supporting local shops, services, and businesses. The proposal
    would also make provision for affordable housing and would deliver biodiversity net
    gain. These benefits weigh moderately in favour of the proposal.
33. Drawing these matters together, I find that the benefits of approving the proposal
    are substantially outweighed by the adverse effects arising from the failure to make
    efficient use of the appeal site and the conflict with the development plan.
Conclusion
34. The proposal does not accord with the development plan taken as a whole and the
    material considerations in this case are not sufficient to indicate that a decision
    should be made other than in accordance with the development plan. Accordingly,
    the appeal is dismissed.
