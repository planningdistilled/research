Decision
1.     The appeal is allowed and planning permission is granted for erection of two
       storey student accommodation consisting of 38 bedrooms, contained in 7 Cluster
       Flats with new vehicle entrance on Frederick Street at Former Rear Gardens to 77
       Frederick Street, 2,4,6,8,10,12,14 Arthur Street, Loughborough, Leicestershire
       LE11 3AY in accordance with the terms of the application, Ref P/24/0077/2,
       subject to the conditions in the attached schedule.
Preliminary Matters
2.     The description of the development, set out above, is taken from the decision
       notice to reflect that the proposed development was amended during the
       application process by reducing the number of bedrooms and cluster flats from 43
       and 8 to 38 and 7 respectively.
3.     The address of the appeal site indicated on the application form only referred to
       the street names and contained no postcode. For accuracy, I have taken the
       address from the appeal submission which indicates that the site is in
       Loughborough, Leicestershire and includes the postcode.
4.     The Council cited Policy H8 of the emerging Charnwood Local Plan 2021-2037
       (LP) in its decision notice. Following the Council’s decision, the LP was adopted on
       19 January 2026 replacing the Charnwood Local Plan Core Strategy and the
       Borough of Charnwood Local Plan. Accordingly, I have determined this appeal in
       accordance with the new LP.
5.     A new National Planning Policy Framework (the Framework) was published on
       17 August 2026 during the appeal process. However, as the policy content of the
       Framework, insofar as it relates to this case, has not significantly changed, there is
       no requirement for me to seek further submissions from the main parties. I am
       satisfied no party would be prejudiced if I determine the appeal accordingly.

Main Issue
6.     The main issue is the effect of the proposed development on the character of the
       area, with regards to the balance and mix of housing.
Reasons
7.     The appeal site is a vacant plot of land that previously formed part of the rear
       gardens of properties that adjoin the site. It lies within a predominantly residential
       area inside the Limits to Development of Loughborough Urban Centre.
8.     LP Policy H8 relates to purpose-built student accommodation (PBSA) and
       supports additional off-campus provision where a number of criteria are satisfied.
       In this case, there is no substantive disagreement, nor compelling evidence before
       me, that the proposed development would fail to comply with four of the five
       requirements of LP Policy H8. I will therefore address the Council’s concerns
       which relate to the requirement that PBSA development should avoid damage to
       the social and physical character and amenity of the local area, taking account of
       cumulative impacts of the development and other similar uses.
9.     Occupiers of both PBSA and HMOs typically occupy individual rooms and share
       communal facilities. Although PBSA often benefits from dedicated on-site
       management, as proposed in this case, and HMOs are not occupied exclusively by
       students, these differences do not alter the essential characteristics of both forms
       of development as shared accommodation. I therefore find that HMOs and PBSA
       are sufficiently comparable in character and function to be regarded as similar
       uses for the purposes of LP Policy H8. In reaching this conclusion, I note that
       neither the wording of the policy nor its supporting text suggests that the reference
       to ‘other similar uses’ was intended to be limited solely to other PBSA
       development.
10. A Study of Housing in Multiple Occupation (HMO) in Charnwood (December 2018)
    suggests that a high proportion of HMOs within the Southfield ward, where the
    appeal site is located, are occupied by students. However, the current proportion
    of properties near to the appeal site that are HMOs and occupied by students is
    unknown. Even so, the undisputed evidence indicates that 40.6% of the 106
    properties within a 100 metre (m) radius of the site are HMOs. Although this
    represents a slight reduction since the planning application was determined, the
    proportion remains more than four times the 10% threshold identified in the
    Houses in Multiple Occupation Assessment (July 2019) as the point at which local
    communities become unbalanced. That assessment informed LP Policy H7, which
    specifically relates to HMOs.
11. LP Policy H7 does not apply to the proposed development. Furthermore, LP Policy
    H8 does not prescribe a threshold for the concentration of PBSA and similar uses
    within any specified radius of a site. Nevertheless, the 100m radius provides a
    useful benchmark when considering cumulative effects. Given the significant
    concentration of HMOs in the vicinity of the site, I find it reasonable to conclude
    that, although the nearest existing or permitted PBSA is about 200m from the site,
    the proposed development would contribute to the existing imbalance within the
    local community through the provision of additional shared accommodation.
12. Nevertheless, there is no compelling evidence before me that it would materially
    exacerbate any harm to the area’s social characteristics arising from the more

       transient and less stable population associated with nearby HMOs. This is
       particularly so because some of the potential impacts are capable of being
       mitigated through the submitted Residence Management Plan and Waste

       Consequently, even when the local concentration of HMOs is taken into account, it

    amenity of the local area.
Planning Obligation
14. A signed unilateral undertaking made pursuant to section 106 of the Town and
    Country Planning Act 1990 (the UU) has been submitted. It contains two
    obligations that would come into effect if planning permission were granted. They
    relate to the following matters:
15. Health contribution – Leicester, Leicestershire and Rutland Integrated Care Board
    have calculated that the proposed development could generate demand equivalent
    to an additional 38 patients. The UU provides for a payment of £15,200 towards
    the cost of undertaking premises development works at a local GP practice to
    meet such additional demand. It is indicated that the contribution has been
    calculated using the cost per square metre for recent health estate works.
16. Open space – The UU secures contributions of £5,206 towards town-centre parks
    and amenity open space and £15,086 towards recreational facilities for young
    people.
17. Accordingly, the matters covered within the UU are to address the additional
    demand arising from the proposed student accommodation and so are necessary
    to make the proposed development acceptable in planning terms and are directly
    related to it. Furthermore, the amount of the contributions has not been disputed
    and, therefore, there is no reason for me to conclude that the amounts are not
    fairly and reasonably related in scale and kind to the proposed development. The
    UU therefore meets the tests set out at Regulation 122 of the Community
    Infrastructure Levy Regulations, 2010. It would also comply with LP policies EV9
    and INF1 which seek to ensure that open space, sport and recreation needs are
    met and infrastructure necessary to support development is provided.
Other Considerations
18. The appeal site directly adjoins, and is therefore within the setting of, the Queen’s
    Park Conservation Area (CA). I consider that the significance and special interest
    of the CA, insofar as relevant to this appeal, derives from the historic and
    architectural value of the densely developed terraced housing along Arthur Street
    and Frederick Street, which creates a strong sense of enclosure, rhythm and
    architectural unity. As an undeveloped gap enclosed by a tall fence, the site
    interrupts the otherwise continuous built frontage and lacks the characteristics that
    define the part of the CA that it adjoins. Consequently, it does not make a positive
    contribution to the setting of the CA.

19. The proposed development includes a two-storey building which fronts onto
    Frederick Street, substantially closing the existing gap in the street frontage. It has
    been designed as a block of three terraced properties set back from the road
    behind small front gardens enclosed by a low brick wall. By reinstating a built
    frontage that aligns with neighbouring properties and incorporating architectural
    detailing reflective of the surrounding terraces, the building would reinforce the
    rhythm, enclosure and visual coherence of the street scene. As such, it would
    represent an appropriate form of development within the setting of the CA.
20. Although simpler in design than the frontage building, the proposed rear building
    would incorporate a consistent palette of materials and complementary detailing.
    Furthermore, owing to its position behind the frontage building, it would have only
    a limited presence in public views and would not detract from the character or
    appearance of the CA or its setting.
21. Overall, I find that the proposed development would preserve the setting of the CA
    and would not harm the significance of this designated heritage asset, which I note
    is the conclusion reached by the Council.
22. The Grade II listed Carillon Tower (the Tower) occupies a prominent position
    within the centre of Queen’s Park. The Tower serves as a war memorial and
    museum and in part derives its significance from its historic and communal value
    as a memorial to those who lost their lives in the world wars, together with its
    architectural interest. Its immediate setting comprises the park, which facilitates its
    commemorative function as a place for gathering, reflection and remembrance. Its
    wider setting includes the surrounding terraced streets, from which the upper
    section of the Tower remains visible above the roofscape and tree canopy,
    reinforcing its landmark presence within the local area.
23. The proposed development would not alter the parkland setting in which the Tower
    is experienced and would not materially alter its prominence or visibility within
    wider views. Consequently, I find that the proposal would not harm the way that
    this listed building is experienced, and its setting and significance would be
    preserved, which is the conclusion reached by the Council.
24. The proposed development would be in a location that benefits from good access
    to public transport and is within convenient walking and cycling distance of the
    campuses of Loughborough University and Loughborough College, as well as the
    services and facilities available in the city centre. In addition, the Council cannot
    demonstrate a five-year supply of deliverable housing sites. As student
    accommodation forms part of the Council’s housing land supply calculation, the
    scheme would make a modest but positive contribution towards addressing this
    shortfall. Accordingly, these matters weigh in favour of the proposal.
25. The quantity of economic benefits arising from the construction and occupation of
    the proposed development that is set out within an addendum submitted with the
    appeal is not agreed. However, it is common ground that economic benefits would
    arise. Such benefits carry moderate weight in favour of the proposal.
Other Matters
26. I have had regard to the other matters raised by interested parties, including the
    lack of affordable and family homes, crime and antisocial behaviour, highway
    safety concerns relating to parking, congestion and access visibility, impacts on

Conditions
27. I have, in the interests of certainty, imposed a condition that specifies that the
    development is carried out in accordance with the approved plans.
28. In the interests of protecting the character and appearance of the area and the
    setting of the adjacent CA, I have imposed conditions controlling landscaping and
    its maintenance, external materials, the design of buildings and structures within
    the site, boundary treatments, and tree protection. A condition requiring the
    approval of the finished floor levels of the buildings is also necessary to safeguard
    the character and appearance of the area and the living conditions of adjoining
    residents. This matter is addressed through a pre-commencement condition
    because the opportunity to secure satisfactory levels could be compromised once
    development has commenced.
29. To minimise the risks of surface water flooding, I have imposed conditions to
    secure infiltration testing, a surface water drainage scheme, and a surface water
    management plan during the construction phase. These are pre-commencement
    conditions so that the management plan is in place when development starts on
    site and because satisfactory delivery of a drainage scheme could be prejudiced if
    resolved later. A condition is also imposed to ensure the maintenance of the
    surface water drainage system to ensure that it remains fit for purpose.
30. A condition requiring the installation of bat and bird boxes is necessary in the
    interests of biodiversity enhancement and habitat creation. To promote sustainable
    construction and transport, I have imposed conditions to secure a Sustainable
    Construction Statement and cycle storage. In addition, to protect the living
    conditions of adjoining residents, I have imposed a condition requiring specified
    windows to be obscure glazed.
31. Conditions have been imposed requiring the installation of a CCTV system and
    securing a Resident Management Plan in the interests of crime prevention and
    reducing anti-social behaviour. Appropriate measures to manage waste are also
    required in the interests of the appearance of the site, highway safety and to
    protect the living conditions of adjoining residents.
32. I have imposed, in the interests of highway safety, conditions relating to the
    provision of the access, parking and turning areas and visibility splays, and
    securing a car parking management plan.
33. To clarify and define the extent of the planning permission a condition is imposed
    to restrict occupation to students only to accord with the form of development upon
    which my assessment was based.
34. Where necessary the wording of the suggested conditions has been adjusted to
    ensure consistency and clarity. I have also amended the order of the conditions to
    accord with the structure set out in the PPG1.
