Land adjacent to Unit 17, Lee Bridge Industrial Estate, Lee Bridge, Halifax,

•       The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
        against a refusal to grant planning permission.
•       The appeal is made by Mr Sam Duggan, Sam Duggan Services Ltd against the decision of
        Calderdale Metropolitan Borough Council.
•       The application Ref is 25/01229/FUL.
•       The development is a proposed industrial unit.

This decision is issued in accordance with section 56 (2) of the Planning and
Compulsory Purchase Act 2004 as amended and supersedes that issued on 8
September 2026
Decision
1.     The appeal dismissed.
Preliminary Matters
2.     The application form identifies the site as 4 Lee Bridge Industrial Estate, however,
       the decision notice and appeal form refer to land adjacent to Unit 17. The latter
       description is consistent with the plans before me, and I have therefore used it for
       the purposes of the site address set out above.
3.     During the course of the appeal, a revised National Planning Policy Framework
       (the Framework) was published on 17 August 2026. The main parties were given
       an opportunity to comment on the implications of the revised Framework for the
       appeal, and I have taken those comments into account in reaching my decision.
       References in this decision to the Framework are to the August 2026 version.
Main Issue
4. The main issue is whether the proposed development would make appropriate
   provision for car parking and the effect of the development on highway safety and
   the free flow of traffic.
Reasons
5. The appeal site forms part of an established industrial estate and comprises an
   area of hardstanding adjacent to an existing industrial unit. The existing unit is
   occupied by a joinery business operated by the appellant. The proposed
   development would provide storage accommodation in the form of a detached L-
   shaped building to support the operation of that business.

6. The proposed building would occupy an area currently available for parking and
   operational activities associated with the existing business. Following construction
   of the building, parking spaces for two vehicles would remain within the appeal site.
   Whilst that level of provision would align with the Council’s advisory parking
   standards when considered solely by reference to the floor area of the proposed
   building, such an assessment does not take account of the parking and operational
   requirements associated with the existing business already operating from the site.
7. The appellant relies upon a parking survey which recorded a maximum of five
   vehicles present during the survey period. However, the evidence does not clearly
   identify the location of those vehicles or distinguish between activity associated with
   the appeal site and activity associated with neighbouring units. Indeed, the survey
   refers to a number of existing units that are used for storage and workshop
   purposes, suggesting that the area considered extends beyond the appeal site
   itself. Consequently, the evidence does not enable me to determine with sufficient
   certainty the existing parking demand generated by the appeal site.
8. Even if the survey were determinative of the parking associated with the business
   operating from the appeal site, limited information has been provided regarding the
   occupancy of neighbouring units and their associated parking requirements. As a
   result, I am unable to assess whether parking demand arising from the proposed
   development could reasonably be accommodated elsewhere within the estate
   without prejudice to these neighbouring units.
9. Moreover, the parking and manoeuvring arrangements relied upon by the appellant
   extend beyond the red-line boundary of the appeal site. Whilst there is no dispute
   that these areas physically exist, I have limited information about whether they are
   under the appellant's control or continuing availability to serve the development.
   Consequently, I attach limited weight to the parking provision and manoeuvring
   arrangements shown beyond the appeal site.
10. I acknowledge that the site lies within walking distance of Halifax town centre and is
    accessible by public transport. Nevertheless, the proposed development would be
    associated with an industrial use where vehicle movements, including employee,
    visitor and delivery trips, can reasonably be expected. The provision of dedicated
    delivery bays within the scheme itself indicates an operational need for regular
    vehicle access in addition to longer-stay parking requirements.
11. I have had regard to the flexible approach to parking provision embodied in Policy
    IM5(III) of the Calderdale Local Plan (CLP) and TR4(1)(e) of the Framework.
    However, the evidence before me does not satisfactorily demonstrate that the
    parking needs arising from the proposed development, when considered alongside
    the operational requirements of the existing business, could be accommodated
    within the appeal site. Nor has it been demonstrated that the parking and
    manoeuvring areas outside the appeal site are adequate to serve the needs of the
    development.
12. In the absence of evidence demonstrating adequate parking provision within the
    appeal site and given the uncertainty surrounding the availability of off-site areas
    relied upon by the appellant, it is likely that vehicles associated with the
    development would park within circulation areas serving neighbouring units or on
    estate roads. This would reduce the space available for vehicle manoeuvring and
    would undermine the safe and efficient movement of vehicles within the estate.

13. Accordingly, I conclude that the proposed development would fail to make
    appropriate provision for car parking which would be detrimental to highway safety
    and the free flow of traffic. The proposal therefore conflicts with Policies BT4 and
    IM5(III) of the CLP, insofar as they require development to provide appropriate
    parking provision and facilitate the safe and efficient movement of highway users. It
    also conflicts with TR4(1)(e) of the Framework, which seeks an appropriate level of
    parking provision reflecting the nature and operation of the development.
Other Matters
14. During the appeal, the appellant submitted further information in response to an
    objection from the Environment Agency which, the appellant states, was not drawn
    to their attention prior to the appeal and is not reflected in the Council’s reason for
    refusal. Whilst that information was intended to address the Agency's concerns, the
    objection had not been withdrawn at the time of my decision.
15. Given my conclusions on the main issue, the outcome of the appeal does not turn
    on this matter. Consequently, I have not found it necessary to seek further
    submissions or to reach a concluded view on the acceptability of the proposal in
    respect of the matters raised by the Environment Agency.
Planning Balance
16. Policy S4 of the Framework states that development proposals within settlements
    should be approved unless the benefits of doing so would be substantially
    outweighed by any adverse effects, when assessed against the national decision-
    making policies in the Framework.
17. The proposal would support the operation of an existing business and could
    provide modest economic benefits through increased business capacity and limited
    employment opportunities. I afford these benefits moderate weight.
18. Against this, I have identified conflict with the TR4(1e) Framework arising from the
    failure to demonstrate adequate parking provision and the resulting harm to the
    safe and efficient operation of the industrial estate. TR6(4) of the Framework states
    that development should be refused if it would have an unacceptable impact on
    highway safety. I afford this conflict significant weight.
19. Taking all matters into account, I find that the adverse effects of the proposal
    substantially outweigh its benefits. Accordingly, the presumption in favour of
    sustainable development established by Policy S3 of the Framework does not
    apply to this appeal.
Conclusion
20. The proposal conflicts with the development plan when considered as a whole and
    there are no material considerations that indicate a decision should be made
    otherwise than in accordance with the development plan.
21. For the reasons given above, I conclude that the appeal should be dismissed.
