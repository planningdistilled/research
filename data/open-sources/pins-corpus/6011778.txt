     Appeal Ref: 6011778
     234 Acklam Road, Middlesbrough TS5 8AA
     •    The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
          against a refusal to grant planning permission.
     •    The appeal is made by Mr Mohammed Siddique against the decision of Middlesborough Council.
     •    The application Ref is 26/0063/FUL.
     •    The development is described on the planning application form as a ‘Two storey side extension’.

     -




Decision
1. The appeal is dismissed.
Preliminary Matters
2. The appellant submitted an alternative drawing1 with the appeal evidence. I find the
   changes proposed to be substantially different from the drawings submitted with the
   original application in that the height and width of the extension have been altered.
   The appeal process should not be used to evolve a scheme, particularly where it
   would deprive those who should have been consulted on the changed development
   the opportunity of such consultation. Therefore, in the interests of fairness and
   transparency to all parties, I have determined the appeal on the basis of the plans
   considered by the Council.
3. The National Planning Policy Framework (the Framework) has been revised since
   the parties made their original submissions. The main parties have been invited to
   make further representations in relation to the Framework. Any representations
   received have been taken into consideration.
Main Issue
4. The main issue is the effect of the proposal on the character and appearance of the
   area.
Reasons
5. The appeal property is one half of a pair of semi-detached dwellings. The dwellings
   are set back some distance from the highway. The pair has a pleasing symmetry.
   On site I observed large pairs of similar semi-detached dwellings, set back within
   spacious plots. I noted clear gaps at first floor level that have been maintained
   between the majority of the dwellings within this area of Acklam Road. Although
   gaps have been disrupted in part, where some terracing effect is evident, the gaps

       remain a distinct feature that contribute positively to the character and appearance
       of the area by evoking a sense of spaciousness around dwellings.
6. The proposal would introduce a two-storey side extension, which, due to the
   presence of a two-storey side extension at the neighbouring property, would result
   in the closing of the gap between the properties when viewed from Acklam Road.
   Although the proposal is set back from the principal elevation, this would not
   overcome the harm caused by reducing the sense of spaciousness through losing
   the gap.
7. The proposal would be of appropriate materials, set back from the main elevation
   and would not be more than half the width of the main dwelling. However, due to
   the height of the proposal, being that of the ridge height of the main dwelling, it
   would not appear as a subservient addition and would therefore be at odds with the
   Middlesbrough Urban Design Guide (2013). It would thus appear incongruous in
   the street scene due to its height.
8. Overall, I find that the closing of the gap and the height of the proposal would cause
   substantial harm to the character and appearance of the area and the street scene.
9. Consequently, I find that the proposal conflicts with policies CS5 and DC1 of the
   Middlesbrough Core Strategy (2008), that, amongst other things, seek high quality
   design that contributes positively to the character and appearance of the area.
Other Matters
10. My attention has been drawn to an application and subsequent appeal2 concerning
    a similar proposal at the same site in 2024. That Inspector also found the loss of
    the gap between properties to harm the character and appearance of the area.
    While the scheme now before me has sought to address the harms found in that
    case, I have considered the current proposal on its own merits and found that
    despite the proposed reduction in width, it would result in substantial harm.
11. The proposal would provide additional floorspace within an existing plot in an
    established settlement. In such circumstances, Policy L2 of the Framework
    provides that substantial weight be given to the benefits where proposals are
    consistent with the overall street scene. Given the substantial harm that I have
    found to the street scene, it has not been necessary for me to afford substantial
    weight to the benefits of the additional floorspace in this case.
12. That there would be no unacceptable harm in relation to other matters including
    highway safety and the living conditions of existing and future occupiers is a neutral
    consideration that does not weigh in favour of allowing the appeal.
Planning Balance and Conclusion
13. There would be modest benefits resulting from the continued use and adaptation of
    an existing home within a settlement, along with improvements to the family
    accommodation. However, these benefits would be small, largely personal to the
    appellant, and would not outweigh the harm that I have found to the character and
    appearance of the area and the street scene.
