Decision
1.     Appeal A is allowed and planning permission is granted for the installation of a
       new BT street hub unit and associated display of advertisement panels to both
       sides of the unit at Footpath Outside 2 Benson Street, Middlesbrough, TS5 6JQ in
       accordance with the terms of the application, Ref 26/0113/FUL, subject to the
       conditions in the attached schedule.
2.     Appeal B is allowed and express consent is granted for the installation of a new BT
       street hub unit and associated advertisement panels on either side of the unit as
       applied for. The consent is for five years from the date of this decision and is
       subject to the five standard conditions set out in the Town and Country Planning
       (Control of Advertisements) (England) Regulations 2007 (as amended) and the
       additional conditions set out in the attached schedule.
Preliminary Matters
3.     The two appeals are for related proposals on the same site. I have considered
       each appeal on its individual merits, however, as they raise similar issues, I have
       combined both decisions into a single decision letter.
4.     The decision notice refers to the emerging Middlesbrough Publication Local Plan
       (March 2025) (the PLP). Despite being at an advanced stage of preparation, I
       have limited substantive information regarding the PLP, including whether there
       are any unresolved objections relating to relevant policies or any required

       modifications which limits the weight to be attached to them. Moreover, in respect
       of Appeal B, the Town and Country Planning (Control of Advertisements)

5.     Since the appeals were submitted a new version of the National Planning Policy

Main Issues
6.     The main issues in Appeal A are the effect of the proposal on the character and
       appearance of the area, with regard to street clutter; and highway safety. The main
       parties agree that the advertisement would not have a harmful effect on amenity.
       From the evidence, together with my site visit observations, I have no clear reason
       to disagree. Therefore, the main issue in Appeal B is the effect of the proposed
       advertisements on public safety.
Reasons
Street clutter
7.     The appeal site comprises an area of a pavement adjacent to Linthorpe Road
       within a local centre that has a commercial character. The pavement is generous
       in width and in combination with the adjacent grassed area with public seating
       contributes to the open qualities of the area. There are several existing items of
       street furniture nearby to the appeal site, including lighting column, CCTV and
       Cabinets, a bus stop and benches which are generally dispersed across the
       pavement and grassed area, and each have a modest footprint and scale and
       massing. As a result, I did not observe the existing situation at the appeal site
       location to be cluttered.
8.     The proposed hub would be set back from the roadside edge and its scale and
       massing would readily be accommodated within the wide pavement and open
       characteristics of the area. Consequently, even cumulatively with the existing
       street furniture the proposed hub would not result in visual clutter, nor would it
       undermine the existing unified and coherent public realm. It would therefore avoid
       over cluttering as advised in Middlesbrough’s Urban Design Supplementary
       Planning Document (2013).
9.     I therefore conclude that the proposal would not be unduly harmful to the character
       and appearance of the area, with regard to street clutter and would be in
       accordance with PLP Policy IN7 insofar as this policy requires digital infrastructure
       to not cause harm to the appearance of an area through visual intrusion or
       creating unacceptable clutter.
Highway safety and public safety
10. Linthorpe Road is a busy thoroughfare which at the appeal site location has
    several junctions with adjoining streets. There is a bus stop proximate to the
    appeal site and on the opposite side of the road there is an entrance to a carpark
    associated with ‘Ali’s Superstore’ and a taxi stand.

11. The Planning Practice Guidance1 states that advertisements at points where
    drivers need to take more care are more likely to affect public safety but there are
    less likely to be road safety problems if the advertisement is on a site within a
    commercial locality.
12. There is good visibility in either direction along the straight section of Linthorpe
    Road at the appeal site location and the proposal would be located away from the
    roadside edge on a wide pavement. As such, the hub would not harmfully reduce
    visibility. Nor would the illuminated advertisement screens distract motorists,
    considering the site’s commercial context and that luminance and changing of
    images can be controlled through the imposition of conditions. The hub would
    therefore not have an unacceptable impact on highway safety which Framework
    Policy TR6 seeks to avoid. It would also be in line with the provisions of
    Framework Policy TR4 in relation to the creation of places that minimise the scope
    for conflict between road users and avoids unnecessary street clutter.
13. To conclude, the proposal would not unacceptably harm highway safety and would
    be in accordance with Policy DC1 of the Core Strategy (2008) and PLP policies
    CR2 and IN7. Amongst other things, these policies require all development
    proposals to not have an adverse effect on highway safety and digital
    infrastructure equipment to be positioned to avoid a negative impact on highway
    safety. As such, it follows that the proposed advertisements would not harm public
    safety and would be in accordance with the above policies and PLP Policy CR8
    insofar as it requires advertisements to avoid reducing visibility or adding notable
    distraction for users of the highway or compromising highway safety.
Conditions
14. For Appeal A, a plans condition is necessary in the interests of certainty. To
    manage any misuse of the proposal a condition to secure the implementation of
    the anti-social behaviour management plan is necessary. However, there is no
    substantive evidence that additional measures, such as CCTV are reasonable in
    the particular circumstances of this case given that existing problems are related to
    town centre hubs whereas in this case the hub would be in a local centre. A
    condition to secure the implementation of the noise management plan is
    necessary in the interests of the living conditions of neighbouring occupiers.
15. For Appeal B, in addition to the five standard conditions, I have imposed conditions
    to secure operational controls and restrictions in relation to illumination, the images
    displayed and measures in the event of fault or malfunction which are necessary in
    the interests of amenity and public safety.
Conclusion
16. For the reasons given above, and having regard to all other matters raised, I
    conclude that Appeal A should be allowed and Appeal B should be allowed.

Appeal B
1) The intensity of the illumination of the digital display screens permitted by this
   consent shall be no greater than 600 candelas per square metre (cd/m2) between
   dusk and dawn; and
2) The minimum display time for each piece of content on the digital display screens
   permitted by this consent shall be 10 seconds and the rate of change between
   successive images shall be no greater than 1 second with no swiping or other
   animated transition methods between successive images. The digital display
   screens shall not display any moving, or apparently moving, images (including
   animation, flashing, scrolling three dimensional, intermittent or video elements) and
   the display shall include a mechanism to freeze or turn off the screen in the event
   of a malfunction.
