Appeal A Ref: 6009141
Footpath outside 1 Prince Street, Bridlington YO15 2NW
•   The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
    against a refusal to grant planning permission.
•   The appeal is made by Verity Cheyne (BT Group Plc) against the decision of East Riding of
    Yorkshire Council.
•   The application Ref is 26/00310/PLF.
•   The development proposed is installation of 1 No BT Street Hub.

Appeal B Ref: 6009153
Footpath outside 1 Prince Street, Bridlington YO15 2NW
•   The appeal is made under Regulation 17 of the Town and Country Planning (Control of
    Advertisements) (England) Regulations 2007 (as amended) against a refusal to grant
    express consent.
•   The appeal is made by Verity Cheyne (BT Group Plc) against the decision of East Riding of
    Yorkshire Council.
•   The application Ref is 26/00311/PAD.
•   The advertisements proposed are two digital 75 inch display screens, one on each side of the
    Street Hub unit.

Decisions
1. Appeal A is dismissed.
2. Appeal B is dismissed.
Preliminary Matters
3. Appeal A relates to the refusal of planning permission for a BT Street Hub unit.
   Appeal B relates to the refusal of express consent for the digital advertisements
   incorporated within that unit. As the proposals are physically and functionally linked
   and raise closely related issues, I have considered them together in a single
   decision letter.
4. A revised National Planning Policy Framework (the Framework) was published on
   17 August 2026. The main parties have had the opportunity to comment on the
   revised Framework, and I have taken the comments into account in determining
   the appeals.
5. The appeal site lies within the Bridlington Quay Conservation Area. I am therefore
   required to pay special attention to the desirability of preserving or enhancing the
   character or appearance of the conservation area pursuant to section 72(1) of the
   Planning (Listed Buildings and Conservation Areas) Act 1990 (the Act).

6. In relation to Appeal B, the Advertisement Regulations require that control may be
   exercised only in the interests of amenity and public safety. Development plan
   policies and guidance are therefore relevant only insofar as they inform those
   matters and are not determinative of Appeal B.
Main Issues
7. For Appeal A, the main issue is the effect of the proposal on the significance of the
   Bridlington Quay Conservation Area, having particular regard to its character
   and appearance.
8. For Appeal B, the Council did not raise any concern in relation to public safety, and
   I have no reason to conclude otherwise. Therefore, the main issue is the effect of
   the proposed advertisements on amenity, having particular regard to the character
   and appearance of the Bridlington Quay Conservation Area.
Reasons
Heritage significance
9. The Bridlington Quay Conservation Area (the CA) encompasses the historic core of
   the town and harbour. So far as it is relevant to this appeal, the significance of the
   CA derives in part from the breadth and quality of its eighteenth, nineteenth and
   early twentieth century building stock, and the predominantly traditional character of
   its streets and townscape. The Bridlington Quay Conservation Area Appraisal
   (2010) acknowledges the presence of some modern and unsympathetic features.
   However, it explains that notwithstanding those exceptions, it is the traditional
   character of the area that continues to define its special interest and significance. It
   also identifies inappropriate signage as one of the factors capable of detracting
   from the quality of the area.
10. The appeal site is part of a highly prominent area of public realm located at the
    junction of Prince Street and Cross Street. It is clearly visible from a number of
    directions along the surrounding streets and consequently forms part of the public
    experience of the CA.
Effect on significance and visual amenity
11. The proposal would replace an existing BT telephone kiosk and freestanding cash
    machine with a BT Street Hub. The existing telephone kiosk and cash machine
    make little positive contribution to the significance of the CA. Furthermore, the
    proposed Street Hub would occupy a smaller footprint than the structures it would
    replace. Their removal would therefore provide some improvement to the
    public realm.
12. Nevertheless, the replacement Street Hub would comprise a nearly three-metre
    high freestanding structure finished in contemporary black materials and
    incorporating large illuminated digital advertising displays on both principal
    elevations. Whilst the existing kiosk and cash machine are noticeable features,
    neither are as darkly coloured and neither contains illuminated digital screens. The
    dark monolithic appearance of the proposed structure, together with the illuminated
    displays and changing digital content, would draw attention to the installation and
    make it substantially more conspicuous and dominant within the streetscape than
    the features it would replace. Owing to the site's prominent corner position, those
    characteristics would be readily apparent in views from several approaches and

     within the surrounding public realm. This would be the case irrespective of potential
     controls over luminance, content or transitions of images on the display screens.
13. Whilst there is illuminated signage within the wider area, this generally forms part of
    individual buildings rather than freestanding structures occupying prominent
    positions within the public realm. In addition, although reference has been made to
    a digital advertisement associated with a nearby bus stop, that installation is not
    experienced within the same immediate views as the appeal proposal.
    Consequently, it provides only limited assistance in assessing the effects of the
    development before me.
14. I have carefully considered the appeal decisions submitted by the appellant.
    However, each of those decisions relate to different sites and different townscape
    contexts. Several involved findings that the proposed hubs would be less
    conspicuous than the structures they replaced, or would have neutral effects on
    heritage significance. Moreover, a number of the cases are not located within a
    conservation area. I have determined the appeals before me on their own
    site-specific circumstances.
15. In this case, notwithstanding the removal of the existing structures, the replacement
    hub would form a visually assertive feature within a highly prominent location. Its
    modern monolithic form, together with the presence of illuminated digital displays,
    would sit uncomfortably with the traditional townscape character that contributes to
    the significance of the CA. As a result, it would erode the appreciation of those
    qualities and detract from the character and appearance of the area. I therefore find
    that the proposal would fail to conserve or enhance the character or appearance of
    the CA and would cause harm to its significance.
16. For the same reasons, I find that the proposed advertisements would cause
    unacceptable harm to the visual amenity of the area, having particular regard to the
    character and appearance of the CA.
Heritage balance
17. In finding harm to the significance of a heritage asset, Policy HE5(2) of the
    Framework requires the effect on significance to be identified, including the degree
    of any harm. In this case, I find that the proposal would result in a low level of harm
    to the significance of the CA. Although the development would be visually assertive
    and readily perceptible within the street scene, its influence on the significance of
    the CA would be moderated by the wider commercial character of the surrounding
    townscape and the fact that the effects would be confined to a single element within
    it. Accordingly, whilst harmful, the proposal would not result in substantial harm to
    the significance of the CA.
18. Nevertheless, Policy HE6(1) of the Framework requires substantial weight to be
    given to the conservation of designated heritage assets. This is irrespective of
    whether any potential effect amounts to a positive effect, harm, substantial harm, or
    total loss of its significance. In addition, Policy HE6(3) states that any harm to a
    designated heritage asset is a matter of considerable importance and weight.
    Policy HE6(4) requires the effect on the asset and its significance to be weighed
    against any public benefits resulting from the proposal.
19. The appellant has advanced a number of public benefits, including free public
    Wi-Fi, emergency calling capability, charging facilities and other communications

     services. In accordance with Policy CO1 of the Framework, I attach substantial
     weight to the benefits of maintaining and improving network coverage, capacity,
     reliability and resilience, although the contribution made by this individual
     installation to those wider objectives would be relatively limited.
20. There would be other social and operational benefits arising from the scheme,
    including improved accessibility to communications services, device charging,
    emergency contact facilities, environmental monitoring, and community messaging
    functions. Given the modest scale of the development and the more general nature
    of these benefits, they attract limited weight in the circumstances of this case.
    Likewise, whilst the removal of the existing telephone kiosk and ATM installation
    would provide some improvement to the immediate public realm, that benefit is
    moderated by the fact that it forms part of a replacement scheme which itself would
    cause heritage harm. Consequently, I attach only limited weight to that benefit.
21. Drawing these matters together, I find that the proposal would generate a range of
    meaningful public benefits. However, having regard to the substantial weight that
    must be given to the conservation of designated heritage assets and the
    considerable importance and weight that must be attached to the identified heritage
    harm, I conclude that the public benefits would not outweigh the identified harm to
    the significance of the CA.
22. Accordingly, for Appeal A, I find that the proposal conflicts with the policies of the
    Framework which seek to achieve well-designed places and conserve or enhance
    the historic environment. Furthermore, there is conflict with Policies ENV1 and
    ENV3 of the East Riding Local Plan (2025), which seek, amongst other things, to
    safeguard local character, respond appropriately to context, and conserve the
    significance of heritage assets. These development plan policies remain broadly
    consistent with the design and heritage objectives of the Framework.
Overall Planning Balance (Appeal A)
23. Policy S4 of the Framework states that development proposals within settlements
    should be approved unless the benefits of doing so would be substantially
    outweighed by any adverse effects, when assessed against the national
    decision-making policies of the Framework.
24. As set out above, the proposal would provide benefits in the form of enhanced
    telecommunications infrastructure and improved digital connectivity. In accordance
    with Policy CO1 of the Framework those benefits attract substantial weight. There
    would also be additional social, operational and public realm benefits to which I
    attach limited weight.
25. Against this, I have found that the proposal would harm the significance of the CA
    and that the public benefits would not outweigh that harm through the application of
    Policy HE6 of the Framework. Furthermore, the proposal would conflict with
    Policy DP3 of the Framework, which expects development to integrate with and
    enhance its surroundings. Given the considerable importance and weight that the
    Framework attaches to the identified heritage harm, together with the conflict with
    the Framework's design policies, these adverse effects attract substantial weight
    in the planning balance. Furthermore, Policy S5(2) of the Framework indicates
    that conflict with a national decision-making policy requiring refusal, including
    Policy DP3, is a circumstance in which the benefits of development are likely to be
    substantially outweighed by its adverse effects.

26. Taking all matters into account, I find that the benefits of granting permission would
    be substantially outweighed by the identified adverse effects when assessed
    against the Framework’s national decision-making policies taken as a whole.
    Accordingly, the proposal fails to satisfy Policy S4 of the Framework.
Conclusions
27. For Appeal A, I conclude that the proposal conflicts with the development plan
    when read as a whole and that the material considerations, including the
    Framework, do not indicate that the appeal should be decided other than in
    accordance with the development plan. Appeal A is therefore dismissed.
28. For Appeal B, I have found that the proposed advertisements would cause
    unacceptable harm to amenity, having particular regard to the character and
    appearance of the CA. Consequently, Appeal B is also dismissed.
