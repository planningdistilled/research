•   The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
    against a refusal to grant planning permission.
•   The appeal is made by Mr S Brocklehurst of Mast Property Group against the decision of High Peak
    Borough Council.
•   The application reference is HPK/2026/0021.
•   The development proposed is proposed detached dwelling.


Decision
1. The appeal is dismissed.
Preliminary Matters
2. During the course of the appeal, a revised National Planning Policy Framework (the
   Framework) was published on 17 August 2026. I have determined the appeal having
   regard to the revised Framework. The parties were given the opportunity to comment
   on the implications of the revised Framework for the appeal; however, no comments
   were received.
3. The application form was not completed with a full postal address. For completeness
   I have included the Easting and Northing co-ordinates in the banner above for clarity.
Main Issues
4. The main issues are:-
     • the effect of the proposal on the living conditions of neighbouring occupiers, with
       particular regard to privacy and overlooking;
     • whether the proposal would provide satisfactory living conditions for future
       occupiers, with particular regard to private amenity space;
     • whether sufficient information has been provided to demonstrate compliance with
       biodiversity net gain requirements; and
     • the effect of the proposal on the character and appearance of the surrounding
       area.

Reasons
Living conditions of neighbouring properties
5. The appeal site forms part of the rear curtilage of Nos 124-126 Station Road (Nos
   124-126) and comprises a sloping parcel of land behind the existing frontage
   development along Station Road and Salisbury Lane, it is accessed via a lane from
   Salisbury Street. Development in the immediate area is predominantly residential
   accommodation with a mix of building types and architectural styles. This includes a
   number of existing dwellings fronting onto the lane on the opposite side of the road
   from the appeal site. Notwithstanding this, the prevailing pattern of development is
   characterised by dwellings that generally front directly onto the surrounding streets
   and lane, with the appeal site forming part of the rear garden areas associated with
   those properties.
6. The appeal scheme proposes a detached dwelling within a rear garden area with
   associated parking spaces to the front and a private rear garden. Given the variance
   in ground levels in the area, the effect of the proposal on the living conditions of
   neighbouring occupiers, particularly in relation to privacy and overlooking, requires
   careful consideration.
7. The elevated relationship between the site and surrounding residential properties was
   readily apparent during my site visit and is clearly reflected in the submitted plans and
   sectional drawings. Consequently, my assessment is informed not only by physical
   separation distances but also by the site's topography, the relative positioning of
   surrounding properties and the opportunities for direct and oblique views that would
   arise from the proposed siting and layout. Based on the evidence before me and my
   observations on site, the site's higher position relative to neighbouring properties
   would increase the opportunities for overlooking beyond those typically associated
   with the separation distances involved.
8. In relation to Nos 124-126, a 2-metre boundary fence is proposed. Whilst this may
   assist in limiting views at ground-floor level, its effectiveness would be diminished by
   the difference in ground levels between the appeal site and neighbouring properties.
   Consequently, it would not prevent views from the proposed dwelling towards
   neighbouring gardens and habitable room windows. As such, the proposed screening
   would not overcome the harmful overlooking and loss of privacy that would arise.
9. In addition, the side boundary with Nos 28-30 Salisbury Street (Nos 28-30) shares a
   direct relationship with neighbouring rear amenity areas. Although a 2-metre
   boundary fence is also proposed in this location, the site's sloping topography and the
   elevated position of the dwelling would limit its effectiveness as a privacy screen. In
   particular, views from the first-floor rear windows towards neighbouring gardens
   would remain readily perceptible, even at an oblique angle.
10. Drawing these matters together, I find that the proposal would result in an
    unacceptable degree of overlooking and loss of privacy for occupiers of neighbouring
    properties. The proposed boundary treatment would not overcome the adverse
    effects arising from the elevated siting of the dwelling, the site’s topography and the
    resulting relationship with neighbouring properties. As such, the proposal would
    conflict with Policy EQ6 of the High Peak Local Plan 2016 (HPLP), which seeks to
    secure a high standard of amenity for existing occupiers. For the same reasons, it
    would also conflict with Policy L2(1)(d)(ii) of the Framework, which requires

     development of this nature to maintain living standards for neighbours, and Policy

Living conditions of future occupiers
11. The private amenity area for the appeal proposal would be positioned within a
    relatively confined space surrounded by existing residential development occupying
    higher and lower ground. As a result, it would be subject to a significant degree of
    actual and perceived overlooking from neighbouring properties. Whilst some degree
    of overlooking is inevitable within residential areas, the constrained nature of the site,
    coupled with the pronounced level differences, would result in an outdoor space with
    a limited sense of privacy and seclusion. Consequently, the proposed garden would
    fail to provide the quality of private amenity reasonably expected to serve a dwelling.
12. I therefore conclude that the proposal would fail to provide satisfactory living
    conditions for future occupiers. Owing to the constrained nature of the site and the
    pronounced differences in ground levels, the proposed garden would lack a
    reasonable degree of privacy and seclusion. As such, the proposal would conflict with
    Policy EQ6 of the HPLP, which seeks to secure a high standard of amenity for future
    occupiers. For the same reasons, it would also conflict with Policy L2(1)(d)(ii) of the
    Framework, which requires acceptable living standards for residents and neighbours,
    and Policy DP3 of the Framework, which seeks to secure well-designed places.
BNG
13. The appellant contends that the site is exempt from biodiversity net gain requirements
    because it comprises hardstanding. However, whilst parts of the site contain areas of
    hard surface, the evidence before me, including site photographs, indicates that a
    material proportion of the site comprises modified grassland. This was also evident
    during my site visit.
14. No biodiversity metric, habitat assessment or other ecological evidence has been
    submitted to establish the site's baseline biodiversity value or demonstrate that the
    statutory biodiversity gain objective would be achieved. Nor has sufficient evidence
    been provided to substantiate the claimed exemption from biodiversity net gain
    requirements. Furthermore, no mechanism has been identified to secure and manage
    any biodiversity enhancements for the required period. In the absence of this
    information, I cannot be satisfied that the proposal would meet the statutory
    biodiversity gain requirements. Consequently, the proposal would conflict with
    Policies S1 and EQ5 of the HPLP, which seek to conserve and enhance biodiversity
    and ensure that ecological impacts are appropriately addressed.
Character and appearance
15. The appeal site lies within the settlement boundary of Hadfield. Policy EQ6 of the
    HPLP seeks high quality development that respects site characteristics and local
    distinctiveness. The proposal would provide an additional dwelling within an existing
    residential area and make effective use of an existing residential plot.
16. The proposed dwelling would be positioned behind the existing frontage development
    on Station Road and Salisbury Street. Whilst it would be visible from the adjoining
    lane, it would be largely screened from the principal street frontage. Furthermore,
    whilst the site occupies sloping ground and the resulting building form would be

     influenced by those constraints, the surrounding area displays a variety of dwelling

17. I acknowledge that the proposal would not fully reflect the prevailing pattern of
    development in the area, which is generally characterised by frontage development.
    Nevertheless, the dwelling would be located behind existing development and would
    be experienced principally within the lane itself rather than the wider street scene.
    Taking these matters into account, I find that any adverse effect on the character and
    appearance of the area would be limited.
18. Consequently, I find that the proposal would not result in conflict with Policy EQ6 of
    the HPLP insofar as it relates to character and appearance. Furthermore, having
    regard to the limited visibility of the dwelling from the principal street frontage, I find
    no conflict with Policy L2(1)(d)(i) or Policy DP3 of the Framework.
Other Matters
19. I have had regard to the appellant's reliance on No 19 Curtis Grove and the planning
    permission granted in 2006. However, that development relates to another site, was
    assessed against a different policy context, and has a distinct spatial relationship with
    surrounding buildings. It also has a different relationship with neighbouring occupiers
    and therefore does not provide a directly comparable example in respect of the effect
    of living conditions. As such, it does not demonstrate an established pattern of similar
    development that would justify the proposal before me. It therefore carries limited
    weight in my assessment.
20. It is acknowledged that the proposed scheme seeks to resolve concerns raised in
    relation to a previous proposal that was refused under reference HPK/2018/0353.
    Nonetheless, I have reached my conclusions on the basis of the evidence before me
    and my assessment of the appeal proposal against the development plan and other
    material considerations.
Planning Balance
21. The proposal would provide an additional dwelling within the settlement boundary of
    Hadfield. Furthermore, it would make effective use of an existing residential plot.
    Consequently, the proposal attracts support in principle from Policies S4 and L3 of
    the Framework. These are benefits to which Policy L2(1)(d) requires substantial
    weight to be given.
22. However, whilst I have found no unacceptable harm to the character and appearance
    of the area, I have found that the proposal would fail to provide acceptable living
    conditions for neighbouring occupiers and future residents. In particular, the proposal
    would result in unacceptable overlooking of neighbouring properties and would fail to
    provide future occupiers with a reasonably private and functional outdoor space.
23. Consequently, I have found conflict with Policy EQ6 of the HPLP. For the same
    reasons, the proposal would also conflict with Policy L2(1)(d)(ii) and Policy DP3 of the
    Framework.
24. In addition, insufficient information has been provided to demonstrate compliance
    with biodiversity net gain requirements. As a result, I have found conflict with Policies
    S1 and EQ5 of the HPLP.

25. Taking all matters into account, I have given substantial weight to the benefits arising
    from the provision of an additional dwelling and the effective use of land, as required
    by Policies S4, L3 and L2(1)(d) of the Framework. Nevertheless, I attach greater
    weight to the identified conflict with the development plan, in relation to the failure of
    the scheme to provide acceptable living standards for residents and neighbours in
    regard to privacy and external amenity space as well as the absence of sufficient
    information regarding biodiversity net gain. The proposal would therefore conflict with
    the development plan when read as a whole, including Policies S1, EQ5 and EQ6 of
    the HPLP. It would also conflict with the Framework when read as a whole, including
    Policies L2(1)(d)(ii) and DP3.
26. In the final balance, I find that the material considerations in favour of the proposal
    are not of sufficient weight to indicate that a decision should be made other than in
    accordance with the development plan. Accordingly, the support provided by Policies
    S4, L3 and L2(1)(d) does not indicate that planning permission should be granted in
    this instance.
Conclusion
27. For the reasons given above the appeal should be dismissed.
