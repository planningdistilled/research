Appeal Decision
Site visit made on 24 July 2026
by Elaine Moulton BA (Hons) BPl MRTPI
an Inspector appointed by the Secretary of State
Decision date: 15th September 2026


Appeal Ref: 6008848
Beech Grove, Brock Road, Great Eccleston, Lancashire PR3 0XD
•    The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
     against a refusal to grant planning permission.
•    The appeal is made by Mr Peter Woodhouse against the decision of Wyre Borough Council.
•    The application Ref is 25/00762/FUL.
•    The development proposed is conversion of agricultural building to form a live/work unit.

Decision
1.     The appeal is dismissed.
Preliminary Matters
2.     On 17 August 2026 a new National Planning Policy Framework (the Framework)
       was published. As this could be material to the outcome of this appeal, I consulted
       the parties and have taken the comments received into account in my decision.
Main Issues
3.     The main issues are:

       • The effect of the proposed development on highway safety with particular
         regard to access visibility and parking provision; and

       • Whether the site is a suitable location for the proposed development having
         regard to local and national policy.
Reasons
Highway safety
4.     The Council identified concerns relating to the absence of sufficient information in
       respect of the parking layout and the visibility splays achievable from the proposed
       access. In response, the appellant has submitted an amended plan with the
       appeal documents showing three parking spaces together with a Visibility Report
       (VR). The Council has confirmed that it is now satisfied that parking provision and
       internal site layout are acceptable, and I have no substantive evidence before me
       to reach a different conclusion.
5.     The Council accepts the results of the speed survey contained within the VR,
       which indicate that vehicle speeds on Brock Road are substantially below the
       speed limit of 60 miles per hour (mph) that applies to this road. Consequently, the
       Council agrees with the appellant that visibility splays designed to meet the Design
       Manual for Roads and Bridges standards for a 60mph speed limit are not

       necessary in this case. My observations during the site visit were broadly
       consistent with the speed survey results, and therefore I agree that visibility
       requirements should be based on actual vehicle speeds rather than the speed limit
       of the road.
6.     The evidence indicates that a visibility splay measuring 2.4 metres (m) by 58m can
       be achieved to the southwest of the access. While this is 2m less than the splay
       that should be provided, the Council regards the shortfall as negligible and raises
       no objection in this regard. My site observations confirmed that adequate visibility
       is available in the southwest direction, and I therefore reach the same conclusion.
7.     The parties agree that a visibility splay measuring 2.4m by 58m is required to the
       northeast of the access. The visibility splay that can be achieved within land under
       the appellant’s control falls significantly short of that.
8.     As I observed during my site visit, visibility for drivers exiting the proposed access
       is restricted by the boundary treatment of the adjoining property, which extends
       close to the carriageway edge. Overgrown vegetation along the front boundary of
       that property would further reduce visibility and may require the driver to edge into
       the carriageway to obtain adequate site of vehicles approaching from the right.
       Accordingly, while the evidence suggests that Brock Road currently operates
       safely, the limited visibility available to vehicles leaving the site would be likely to
       increase the risk of conflict with other road users, to the detriment of highway
       safety.
9.     The appellant has indicated that acceptable visibility to the northeast could be
       achieved through a binding legal agreement securing the provision and
       maintenance of the required splay over third party land. However, no such
       agreement is before me. In the absence of a mechanism to secure these
       measures, there is no certainty that the required visibility splays would be secured
       and maintained.
10. Although the proposed development would not give rise to severe residual
    cumulative impacts on the road network due to its limited scale, it would, for the
    reasons given above, have an unacceptable impact on highway safety.
    Accordingly, it would conflict with Framework Policy TR6.4.
11. Consequently, I conclude that, while adequate onsite parking could be provided,
    the development would have an unacceptable effect on highway safety due to the
    inadequacy of visibility at the access. As such it would conflict with Policy CDMP6
    of the Wyre Local Plan (LP), adopted 26 January 2023, which, amongst other
    matters, seeks to ensure safe vehicular access would be provided. It would also
    conflict with the similar objectives of the Framework.
Suitable location
12. LP Policy SP1 establishes the settlement hierarchy for the borough. It directs new
    development to sites within the defined settlement boundaries. Outside defined
    settlements, where the appeal site is located, new development is strictly limited.
13. LP Policy SP4 identifies forms of development that may be acceptable outside
    defined settlement boundaries. These include the conversion of existing buildings,
    provided the proposal complies with the Core Development Management Policies
    and demonstrates that the specified order of priority has been considered. Within

       that hierarchy, live/work units rank third, after employment uses appropriate to the
       rural area and tourism destination or local commercial uses. Residential use is the
       fifth and final priority, provided the proposal enhances its immediate setting.
14. Framework Policy S5.1c supports the re-use of existing buildings outside
    settlements in certain circumstances irrespective of their intended use. Although
    Policy SP4 is consistent with the Framework in many respects, its hierarchy of
    uses for the conversion of existing buildings imposes greater restrictions than
    those contained in Framework Policy S5.1c and is therefore inconsistent with
    national policy.
15. It is not disputed that the building is lawful in planning terms and of permanent and
    substantial construction, and the evidence before me provides no basis for
    reaching a different conclusion. Furthermore, no extensions are proposed.
    Accordingly, the proposed development satisfies the requirements of Framework
    Policy S5.1c.
16. Even if I were to agree with the Council that the appellant has failed to
    demonstrate that higher priority uses have been considered in accordance with LP
    Policy SP4.4, paragraph 2 of Annex A of the Framework indicates that only very
    limited weight should be afforded to any conflict with that requirement because of
    its inconsistency with national policy. Furthermore, Framework Policy S5.1c
    supports the re-use of the building irrespective of whether the proposed live/work
    unit includes a separate or substantial work element.
17. The Council has suggested that the residential element of the proposed use would
    be dominant and that the business floorspace could readily revert to residential
    use without further permission, due to the lack of physical or functional separation
    between the two elements. Nevertheless, I am required to determine the
    development as submitted, namely a live/work unit. Furthermore, any future
    conversion of the work floorspace to residential use could be controlled through
    the imposition of a condition should I be minded to allow the appeal.
18. LP Policy EP8 supports proposals that help diversify the rural economy, and/or
    expand existing businesses where specified criteria are met. The Council has not
    identified any harm arising from the scale or nature of the proposed business use
    in relation to the character of the area and, based on the evidence before me, I
    agree. Furthermore, the policy contains no requirement for an applicant to
    demonstrate a functional need for the business to be located in this rural area, nor
    does it require evidence that the proposed development would make a meaningful
    contribution to the rural economy. Whilst the proposal would consolidate rather
    than expand an existing business, it would nonetheless assist in diversifying the
    rural economy and therefore attracts a degree of support under LP Policy EP8.
19. Framework Policy TR3 indicates that development proposals should reflect certain
    principles so that development supports sustainable patterns of movement,
    enables good accessibility for different users and makes the most of existing and
    proposed transport infrastructure. Given the limited scale of both the residential
    and business elements of the proposed development, it is reasonable to conclude
    that it would not generate a significant amount of movement, even having regard
    to its rural location. Consequently, the proposed development is not a type to
    which Framework Policy TR3.1a is principally directed when seeking to locate

       development in places that limit the need to travel, particularly by private car, and
       provide a genuine choice of transport modes.
20. The Council has referred to Framework policies TR1 and TR8. However, TR1 is
    directed towards plan-making policy and is therefore of limited relevance in this
    case. Policy TR8 relates to public rights of way which, amongst other things,
    requires opportunities to be taken to extend, link or improve the quality of existing
    routes. No such opportunity arises in this case and, given that the proposed
    development would not adversely affect an existing public right of way, it would not
    conflict with Framework Policy TR8.
21. Accordingly, I find that the appeal site is in a suitable location for the proposed
    development and it therefore accords with LP Policy SP2. Any conflict with LP
    Policy SP4 attracts limited weight due to its inconsistency with Framework Policy
    S5.1c, which supports the re-use of existing buildings outside settlements. The
    proposal also accords with LP policy EP8 as it would facilitate the diversification of
    the rural economy through the consolidation of an existing business within a
    countryside area. Furthermore, having regard to its limited scale, the proposed
    development would not be contrary to the Framework objectives of meeting
    development needs through sustainable patterns of development.
Other Matters
22. As set out above, the proposed development would not harm the landscape
    character of the area. Furthermore, there would be no harm to the living conditions
    of nearby residents. However, these are neutral factors that would not weigh in
    favour of, or against, the proposed development.
23. The appellant states that no opportunity was given to respond to the comments of
    the Highway Authority. However, notwithstanding the provisions of Framework
    Policy DM3.1a and e, consideration of the Council’s handling of the planning
    application falls outside the scope of this decision.
Planning Balance and Conclusion
24. For the reasons set out above, the proposal is one of the forms of development
    which Framework Policy S5.1 states should be approved outside settlements
    unless the benefits of doing so would be substantially outweighed by any adverse
    effect when assessed against the national decision-making policies in the
    Framework.
25. Policy S5.2 identifies the circumstances in which the benefits of development are
    likely to be substantially outweighed by adverse effects. These include, but are not
    restricted to, situations where the development proposal would fail to comply with
    one of the national decision-making policies which state that development
    proposals should be refused in specific circumstances.
26. The Council accepts that the proposal would secure the re-use and long-term
    preservation of an existing building that has some heritage value. It would also
    support a small-scale rural business. These are benefits to which I attribute
    moderate weight in favour of the proposed development. The proposed live/work
    unit would also reduce the need to commute. However, this benefit is tempered by
    the likelihood that occupants would remain reliant on the private car to meet their
    day-to-day needs. Therefore, this is a benefit to which I attach limited weight.

27. The proposal would, however, fail to comply with one of the national decision-
    making policies, namely Policy TR6.4. This policy indicates that proposed
    development should be refused where it would have an unacceptable effect on
    highway safety. For the reasons given above, I have found that this would be the
    case.
28. As such, the material considerations do not indicate that in this case the appeal
    should be decided other than in accordance with the development plan. The
    proposed development conflicts with the development plan as a safe vehicular
    access would not be provided. Therefore, for the reasons given above the appeal
    should be dismissed.
