Decision date: 15th September 2026


Appeal Ref: 6012060
108 Ridgeway Drive, Bromley BR1 5DD
•    The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
     against a refusal to grant planning permission.
•    The appeal is made by Mr Magnus Norense against the decision of the Council of the London
     Borough of Bromley.
•    The application Ref is 26/00620/HPA.
•    The development proposed is Single-storey rear extension.

Decision
1.     The appeal is allowed and planning permission is granted for single-storey rear
       extension at 108 Ridgeway Drive, Bromley BR1 5DD in accordance with the terms
       of the application, Ref: 26/00620/HPA, and the plans submitted with it, subject to
       the following conditions:
     1)       The development hereby permitted shall begin not later than 3 years from the
              date of this decision.
     2)       The development hereby permitted shall be carried out in accordance with
              the following approved plans: EE01; EFP01; PE01; PFP01; proposed block
              plan and site location plan.
Preliminary Matter
2.     The National Planning Policy Framework (the Framework) was updated on 17
       August 2026. Although I have made my determination against the updated
       national policy context, the relevant changes to the Framework are not
       fundamental to matters which are determinative to the outcome of this appeal.
Main Issue
3.     The main issue is the effect of the proposal on the living conditions of occupiers of
       106 Ridgeway Drive (No 106), with particular reference to outlook and light.
Reasons
4.     The appeal site is a two storey end of terrace house. It is located within a short
       terrace which is set away from the public highway at an elevated level above the
       road. As a result of the sloping ground on this side of the street the gardens of
       properties within the terrace slope downwards towards the rear elevations of the
       houses.
5.     At present, the appeal property’s rear elevation is in line with the rear elevation of
       the adjoining property, No 106. The shared boundary between the properties is
       marked by a wood panel fence, which is approximately 2 metres in height. In its

       rear elevation at ground floor level, No 106 has a pair of glazed doors, which are
       positioned close to the shared boundary with the appeal site. It appears that these
       serve a habitable room. A further window is positioned at the opposite end of the
       elevation, away from the appeal site.
6.     The single storey extension would be located adjacent to the boundary with No
       106. It would extend 4 metres in depth and feature a flat roof design, which would
       help to minimise its overall height. In addition, the submitted drawings indicate
       that, due to the site's sloping topography, the ground floor level would be
       excavated into the ground, creating a ‘sunken’ floor level. As a result, the flat roof
       would project only a limited distance above the existing shared boundary fence.
       Consequently, only a modest section of the extension’s side wall and roof would
       be visible from the rear ground floor windows of No 106. In this context, the
       extension would not appear unduly imposing, nor would it create an oppressive
       sense of enclosure for the occupiers of that neighbouring property.
7.     Given the extension's limited height and flat roof form, only a modest section of the
       side wall would be visible from No 106. This arrangement would not result in
       unacceptable overshadowing of the nearest ground floor windows in the rear
       elevation or the rear garden.
8.     I conclude that the development would not cause significant harm to the living
       conditions of the occupiers of No 106. Consequently, the proposal would comply
       with Policy 37 of the Bromley Local Plan (2019) and Policy D3 of the London Plan
       (2021), insofar as they require that proposals respect the amenity of occupiers of
       neighbouring buildings.
Conditions
9.     I have had regard to the planning conditions that have been suggested. I have
       considered them against the tests in the Framework and the advice in the Planning
       Practice Guidance (PPG). I have made such amendments as necessary to comply
       with those documents and for clarity and consistency.
10. In addition to the standard time limit, I have imposed an approved plans condition
    in the interests of certainty.
Conclusion
11. For the above reasons, and having had regard to all other matters raised, I
    conclude that the proposed development would accord with the development plan
    and the Framework. The appeal is therefore allowed.
