Decision
1.     The appeal is allowed and planning permission is granted for installation of three
       wall mounted condensers with an accompanying acoustic wall to 110’s courtyard
       and one ground-level condenser contained within an acoustic housing at 106
       courtyard, at 106-110 Belgrave Road, London SW1V 2BJ, in accordance with the
       terms of the application, Ref 25/04302/FULL, subject to the following conditions:
       1)      The development hereby permitted shall begin not later than three years
               from the date of this decision.
       2)      The development hereby permitted shall be carried out in accordance with
               the following approved plans:
               OS Map and Block Plan – 6248-P-001
               Proposed Basement Plan – 6248-P-020 Rev A
               Proposed Rear Elevation – 6248-P-030 Rev A
       3)      Prior to the approved condenser units being brought into use, all acoustic
               mitigation measures identified within the ‘Noise Impact Assessment’
               (Revision 02, 02/10/2025, prepared by Scotch Partners) and shown on the
               approved plans, including any acoustic enclosures, acoustic screening,
               acoustic absorbers and specified operating restrictions, shall be fully
               installed. The approved mitigation measures shall thereafter be retained and
               maintained for the lifetime of the development.
       4)      The approved condenser units shall at all times be operated in accordance
               with the operational recommendations set out in the ‘Noise Impact
               Assessment’ (Revision 02, 02/10/2025, prepared by Scotch Partners).

Main Issue
5.     The main issue is the effect of the proposed development on the living conditions
       of nearby occupiers, with particular regard to noise.
Reasons
6.     Policies 7 and 33 of Westminster City Plan 2019-2040 (the CP) collectively seek to
       protect residential amenity and prevent adverse effects arising from noise. The
       Environmental Supplementary Planning Document (ESPD) provides prescriptive
       criteria against which proposals should be determined, underpinned by World
       Health Organisation (WHO) figures and data. The ESPD is formally adopted
       planning guidance, and in the absence of more specific criteria for assessing noise
       impacts in the development plan, I afford it significant weight.
7.     The planning application was accompanied by a Noise Impact Assessment (NIA)
       based on measured background and ambient noise data collected over several
       days at the site.
8.     The highest mean daytime (07:00-23:00) ambient noise level recorded by the NIA
       was 55 dB and the highest mean night-time (23:00-07:00) ambient noise level
       recorded was 53 dB. The NIA concludes that daytime ambient noise levels do not
       exceed the World Health Organisation (WHO) daytime guideline value of 55 dB,
       whereas the WHO night-time guideline value of 45 dB is exceeded during that
       period.
9.     The ESPD prescribes separate requirements for assessing noise generated by
       plant and machinery depending on whether the existing external ambient noise
       climate exceeds the relevant WHO guideline values. The NIA adopts this approach
       in determining the effects of the proposed condensers. During the daytime, the
       NIA applies a plant noise limit of 34 dB, derived by applying a 10 dB reduction to
       the minimum measured background sound level during periods when ambient
       noise levels did not exceed the WHO daytime guideline value. During the night-
       time, where the WHO guideline value was exceeded, a more stringent limit of 29
       dB is applied, representing a 15 dB reduction from the same minimum measured
       background sound level.

10. Taking account of the proposed mitigation measures, including acoustic
    enclosures, acoustic treatment to the courtyard walls and restricted operating
    modes, the NIA predicts that noise from the proposed plant would not breach
    these limits at the nearest noise-sensitive receptors. The Council has not identified
    any shortfall in the methodology of the NIA, the selection of receptors, the
    background noise measurements or the prediction calculations.
11. The Council's objection rests substantially on its interpretation that a daytime
    ambient noise level of 55 dB should be treated as exceeding the WHO daytime
    guideline value of 55 dB. However, the ESPD distinguishes between
    circumstances where ambient noise "exceeds" WHO guideline levels and where it
    "does not exceed" those levels. In the absence of any alternative definition being
    provided, I am not persuaded that a value equal to a threshold can reasonably be
    said to exceed it. A daytime ambient noise level of 55 dB is equal to, rather than
    greater than, the 55 dB WHO guideline value.
12. The Council's position is explained further within the accompanying informative on
    the decision notice, which states that daytime ambient noise levels of 55 dB mean
    that the WHO guideline levels are exceeded during both daytime and night-time
    periods, such that the more onerous ESPD criterion of 15 dB below background
    should apply throughout. On that basis the Council considers that the proposed
    mitigation would be insufficient.
13. Nevertheless, I am not persuaded that this assists the Council's case. The
    evidence before me demonstrates that the daytime ambient noise environment
    does not exceed the WHO daytime guideline value, while the night-time ambient
    environment exceeds the corresponding night-time guideline value. Accordingly, I
    find that the less onerous ESPD criterion should apply during the daytime period
    and the more onerous criterion during the night-time period.
14. Concerns were also raised by several nearby occupiers regarding the potential
    noise impacts of the proposed condensers, particularly in relation to low-frequency
    hum, tonal noise, vibration, sleep disturbance and the effect on the peaceful
    enjoyment of neighbouring homes. Objectors also questioned the methodology
    and conclusions of the NIA, arguing that it relied on assumed receptor locations,
    failed to adequately assess the nearest residential properties, underestimated the
    acoustic effects of the enclosed courtyard environment and allowed insufficient
    margin between predicted noise levels and Westminster's guideline limits.
    Concerns were additionally expressed regarding cumulative noise impacts, the
    proposed operating modes and the experience of previous unauthorised plant at
    the site.
15. Whilst those concerns are noted, they are not supported by compelling evidence
    that would lead me to depart from the findings of the NIA. The assessment has
    been prepared by suitably qualified acoustic consultants, applies the methodology
    prescribed by the ESPD, and reaches acceptable conclusions based on proposed
    mitigation measures. In the absence of any substantive evidence contradicting the
    findings of the NIA, I am not persuaded that the proposal would give rise to
    unacceptable noise impacts or otherwise result in material harm to the living
    conditions of neighbouring occupiers.
16. Given my findings regarding the applicable criteria, I am satisfied that the
    submitted acoustic evidence demonstrates that the proposed condenser units,

Other Matters
17. The appeal site lies within the Pimlico Conservation Area (the CA), which derives
    its significance principally from its extensive survival as a planned mid-nineteenth
    century residential district characterised by stucco-fronted classical terraces,
    formal street layouts and a strong degree of architectural uniformity. Section 72(1)
    of the Planning (Listed Buildings and Conservation Areas) Act 1990 requires that
    special attention be paid to the desirability of preserving or enhancing the
    character or appearance of the CA.
18. The proposed equipment would be located within enclosed rear courtyard areas at
    basement level. I have had regard to the Council's design and heritage
    assessment within the officer report and the concerns raised in representations.
    Owing to the positioning of the equipment within the utilitarian rear service
    courtyard, its limited visibility and the absence of any significant effect on the
    architectural qualities that contribute to the significance of the CA, I find that the
    proposal would preserve the character and appearance of the CA. Accordingly, the
    proposal would satisfy the statutory duty under section 72(1) of the Act.
19. The appeal property forms part of 78-130 Belgrave Road, a Grade II listed mid-
    nineteenth century stucco terrace (the LB). The list description identifies the
    special interest of the building group as arising from its balanced terrace
    composition, projecting bays, classical detailing, Doric porches, balconies,
    cornices, sash windows and its contribution to the wider townscape of Belgrave
    Road and St George's Square.
20. Section 66(1) of the Planning (Listed Buildings and Conservation Areas) Act 1990
    requires special regard to be had to the desirability of preserving a listed building
    and its setting or any features of special architectural or historic interest which it
    possesses.
21. I note that listed building consent has already been granted for the works
    proposed. Although that decision does not determine the outcome of this appeal, it
    is nonetheless a material consideration and, having regard to the nature and
    location of the proposed equipment, I have seen no substantive evidence to lead
    me to a different conclusion concerning the effect of the development on the LB.
    Consequently, I find that the proposal would preserve the LB and its setting, would
    result in no harm to its significance, and would accord with the statutory duty under
    section 66(1) of the Act.
22. Several representations express concern regarding potential ground-borne
    vibration from the equipment, heat emissions, energy use, carbon emissions, the
    urban heat island effect and the absence of a thermal impact assessment.
    However, there is insufficient substantive evidence before me to indicate that
    these matters would give rise to unacceptable impacts.

23. Objectors further contend that alternative locations for the equipment, including
    roof-level installations or internal plant rooms, have not been adequately
    investigated and that the need for the equipment itself has not been demonstrated.
    However, the purpose of this appeal is to determine the scheme before me, not to
    identify or consider speculative alternatives.
24. Representations also refer to alleged previous unauthorised works and concerns
    regarding planning compliance history at the property, including existing rooftop
    air-conditioning equipment. Any such matters would fall to be considered
    separately under the Council's enforcement powers and do not affect my
    assessment of the planning merits of the appeal proposal. Similarly, each proposal
    must be determined on its own specific considerations, and the absence of
    identified harm in this case does not lead me to find against the proposal.
25. I have also taken account of concerns regarding maintenance activity, loss of
    green space, biodiversity impacts, the relationship between the planning and listed
    building applications and potential effects on property values. Property values are
    not a material planning consideration and, in any event, none of the remaining
    matters has been demonstrated to result in material planning harm.
26. Taken together, these matters do not lead me to a different conclusion from that
    reached on the main issue, nor to a different overall finding on the appeal.
Conditions
27. Neither main party has suggested any specific conditions to be imposed in the
    event that the appeal succeeds. Nevertheless, having regard to the submitted
    details and the tests set out in the Framework and the Planning Practice Guidance
    (the PPG), I have considered what conditions are necessary and reasonable.
28. Conditions relating to the statutory time limit and approved plans are necessary in
    the interests of certainty.
29. The NIA identifies a number of operational controls and mitigation measures that
    are integral to its conclusions regarding the acceptability of the proposal in relation
    to neighbouring living conditions. I shall therefore impose a condition requiring the
    condenser units to be operated in accordance with the operational
    recommendations set out in the approved NIA.
30. A further condition requiring all acoustic mitigation measures identified within the
    NIA and shown on the approved plans to be installed prior to the first operation of
    the approved condenser units and retained thereafter is also necessary in the
    interests of safeguarding the living conditions of nearby occupiers.
Conclusion
31. The proposed development would accord with the development plan and there are
    no material considerations to indicate that the appeal should be determined other
    than in accordance with the development plan. For the reasons given above, I
    therefore conclude that the appeal should be allowed subject to the conditions
    listed in my formal decision.
