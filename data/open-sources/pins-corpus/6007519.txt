Appeal Ref: 6007519
Laurels, Salisbury Terrace, Mytchett, Camberley, Surrey GU16 6DB
    The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
     against a refusal to grant planning permission.
    The appeal is made by A Belnik (Centaur Estates Ltd) against the decision of Surrey Heath Borough
     Council.
    The application Ref is 26/0077.
    The development proposed is the change of use from residential annex (Class C3) to children's day
     nursery (Class E (f)).

Decision
1.    The appeal is dismissed.
Applications for costs
2.    An application for costs was made by the appellant. This application is the subject
      of a separate decision.
Preliminary Matters
3.    A revised version of the National Planning Policy Framework (the Framework) was
      published on 12 December 2024. Although I have made my determination against
      the updated national policy context, the relevant changes to the Framework are
      not fundamental to matters which are determinative to the outcome of this appeal.
      On this basis, it has not been considered necessary to request any related
      observations from the main parties.
4.    Surrey Heath Borough Council is currently preparing a new Local Plan. It
      submitted the Pre-Submission Surrey Heath Local Plan (2019-2038) (Regulation
      19) for independent examination by the Secretary of State on 10 December 2024.
      Changes to the new Local Plan, or Main Modifications, were subject to public
      consultation between 28 May and 9 July 2026. The emerging policies set out
      within the new Local Plan and referred to by the Council in the decision notice and
      other submissions have been taken into account in my decision making. However,
      given the new Local Plan is not yet formally adopted, the weight attached to these
      emerging policies is limited.
Main Issue
5.    The main issue is whether the site is appropriate for the proposed development in
      respect of its effect on the living conditions of neighbouring occupiers, with
      particular regard to noise and disturbance.

Reasons
6.    The appeal site comprises a residential annex known as ‘Laurels’ attached to a
      neighbouring dwelling known as ‘Mystrou’. It is located within Salisbury Terrace, a
      small residential cul-de-sac serving fewer than 20 properties characterised by a
      quiet residential environment with relatively little traffic or pedestrian activity
      beyond that ordinarily associated with the occupants of the surrounding dwellings.
7.    I have had regard to the updated Acoustic Impact Assessment submitted in
      support of the appeal and note that the Council's Environmental Health officers
      raised no objections. The acoustic evidence indicates that average noise levels
      generated by the development, associated with children's play, vehicle
      movements, parking activity and arrivals and departures, would not be
      unacceptable. I have no reason to depart from that technical assessment, and I
      therefore accept that the proposal would not give rise to unacceptable acoustic
      impacts when assessed using the methodology adopted within the submitted
      report.
8.    However, the assessment of living conditions is not confined solely to whether
      average noise levels exceed particular acoustic thresholds. The Planning Practice
      Guidance (PPG) recognises that the impact of noise is not determined solely by
      noise levels and requires consideration of matters such as the frequency, pattern
      and character of noise events, cumulative impacts and the existing character of
      the area.
9.    The appellant's transport evidence forecasts between 57 and 64 two-way vehicle
      movements per day associated with the nursery. It also forecasts up to 23 two-way
      vehicle movements during the morning peak period and up to 13 two-way vehicle
      movements during the evening peak period. Whilst those figures may not be
      unacceptable in highway capacity or safety terms, they would nevertheless
      represent a substantial increase in activity when compared with the existing
      residential use of the site.
10. Moreover, the operation of the nursery would generate frequent pedestrian
    movements, parents accompanying children between vehicles and the nursery
    building, conversations associated with drop-off and collection periods, doors
    opening and closing, and regular movements between the parking area and the
    entrance to the premises.
11. A nursery accommodating up to 18 children would generate a level, frequency and
    intensity of activity beyond that normally associated with a dwellinghouse. This
    would include outdoor play, interactions between children and staff, and
    occasional intermittent noise events such as crying and shouting. Although such
    events may be short-lived and may not materially alter average noise levels, they
    are nevertheless capable of contributing to disturbance experienced by
    neighbouring occupiers.
12. I am particularly mindful that neighbouring occupiers would not simply experience
    occasional noise events. Rather, they would experience the cumulative effects of a
    commercial childcare use operating within a confined residential environment. The
    proposal would introduce a recurring pattern of activity that would be materially
    different from that ordinarily associated with residential occupation.

13. The appellant argues that the impacts can be adequately managed through
    operational measures, including allocated drop-off and collection slots and wider
    traffic management arrangements. Whilst such measures may assist in
    moderating activity, I am not persuaded that they would overcome the fundamental
    issue. Even if fully implemented, the appellant's own evidence demonstrates that a
    substantial number of vehicle and pedestrian movements would continue to occur
    throughout the day. Furthermore, the effectiveness of the proposed arrangements
    would depend to a significant degree upon the behaviour of parents, visitors and
    other third parties over the lifetime of the development. The evidence before me
    does not persuade me that such arrangements would reliably preserve the
    tranquillity currently experienced within Salisbury Terrace.
14. I have also taken account of local residents' concerns regarding increased activity,
    traffic and disturbance. Whilst such concerns must be considered alongside the
    technical evidence, I find that they broadly accord with my own observations
    regarding the subdued and tranquil character of the area and the likely effects of
    the proposal. In particular, they reinforce my conclusion that the principal harm
    arises not from excessive noise levels in an acoustic sense, but from the
    cumulative disturbance associated with the scale and pattern of activity that would
    be introduced into this quiet residential cul-de-sac.
15. Drawing these matters together, I find that although the proposal would not result
    in unacceptable average noise levels, it would nevertheless introduce a significant
    intensification of vehicular movements, pedestrian activity, arrivals and departures,
    and nursery-related activity within an environment presently characterised by
    limited traffic and relative tranquillity. The resulting cumulative disturbance would
    materially erode the quiet residential character of Salisbury Terrace and would
    harm the living conditions of neighbouring occupiers.
16. Accordingly, I conclude that the proposal conflicts with Policy DM9 of the Surrey
    Heath Local Plan1, insofar as it seeks to ensure new development proposals
    respect the amenities of occupiers of neighbouring properties. I have also had
    regard to emerging Policy DH1 of the Pre-Submission Surrey Heath Local Plan
    (2019-2038) (Regulation 19) which seeks to ensure that development does not
    adversely affect the amenities enjoyed by the occupants of neighbouring
    properties.
Other Matters
17. I recognise that the proposal would provide additional childcare places and that
    there is a need for such facilities. However, I find that the provision of childcare
    would not outweigh the harm that I have identified to the living conditions of
    neighbouring occupiers. Accordingly, this consideration does not alter my overall
    conclusion.
18. I also recognise that the proposal would provide employment opportunities and
    generate some economic activity. However, given the modest scale of the
    proposed operation, I attach very limited weight to those benefits.

Conclusion
19. The proposal conflicts with the development plan and the material considerations
    do not indicate that the appeal should be decided other than in accordance with it.
20. For the reasons given above the appeal should be dismissed.
