Appeal Ref: 6008713
Eastlands, Bull Moor Lane, Flaxton YO60 7RP
•   The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
    against a failure to give notice within the prescribed period of a decision on an application for
    planning permission.
•   The appeal is made by Mr and Mrs M Johnson against North Yorkshire Council.
•   The application Ref is ZE25/01413/House.
•   The development proposed is demolition of existing single storey rear extension and erection of
    replacement single storey extension.

Decision
1. The appeal is allowed and planning permission is granted for demolition of existing
   single storey rear extension and erection of replacement single storey extension at
   Eastlands, Flaxton YO607RP in accordance with the terms of the application, Ref
   ZE25/01413/House, and the plans submitted with it, subject to the following
   conditions:
     1)       The development hereby permitted shall begin not later than 3 years from the
              date of this decision.
     2)       The development hereby permitted shall be carried out in accordance with
              the following approved plans:
              Site Location Plan;
              Plans and Elevations Proposed M84 01 AR50 01 C
              -001 Rev-C
     3)       The external materials of the development hereby permitted shall be
              constructed in the materials shown on plan no. M84 01 AR50 01 C.
Appeal Procedure
2. The site visit was undertaken by a representative of the Inspector whose
   recommendation is set out below and to which the Inspector has had regard before
   deciding the appeal.
Preliminary Matters
3. The appeal relates to a planning application that was not determined by the Council
   within the prescribed period. I note that the Council would have refused planning
   permission should it have made a decision on the proposal. I have had regard to
   the parties’ submissions in establishing the main issue which I set out below.
4. The Council considers that the scheme would not give rise to any adverse impacts
   upon neighbouring living conditions. However, it considers that the proposal would

     be out of keeping with the character and appearance of the host dwelling and the
     Flaxton Conservation Area (FCA).
5. A revised National Planning Policy Framework (the Framework) was published on
   17 August 2026. There have been some layout and subsequent changes to
   incorporate paragraphs into a policy-style format, as well as minor relevant
   alterations to the ways in which heritage matters are considered. I am satisfied that,
   having reviewed the relevant changes, they do not have a material bearing on the
   substance of the main issue or indeed the arguments of the main parties thereon.
   Since the parties would not therefore be prejudiced, I have proceeded without
   further consultation.
Main Issue
6. Having regard to the above, the main issue is the effect of the proposed
   development on the character and appearance of the host dwelling and area,
   including whether it would preserve or enhance the character or appearance of the
   FCA.
Reasons for the Recommendation
7. Eastlands is a Victorian detached, double-fronted two-storey dwelling, prominently
   situated fronting the principal road through the FCA. Constructed of textured
   brickwork beneath a slate roof with paired chimney stacks, the building possesses
   an attractive symmetrical front elevation, enhanced by contrasting buff string
   courses and decorative surrounds to its arched sash windows. Similarly shaped
   windows openings, including a larger centrally positioned half-landing opening, are
   also present on the rear elevation. However, this elevation is comparatively plain
   and more irregular in appearance, also incorporating a modestly sized single-storey
   addition and a broad box dormer roof extension.
8. An open gated gap between the appeal dwelling and its associated detached
   garage provides the main external access to the rear garden. In addition, I noted a
   secondary access route via the driveway of the adjacent dwelling, Kelfield House,
   through a solid gate within the boundary wall connecting between the existing rear
   extension and an outbuilding serving that property.
9. The statutory duty set out in Section 72 of the Planning (Listed Buildings and
   Conservation Areas) Act 1990 (the Act) requires special attention to be paid to the
   desirability of preserving or enhancing the character or appearance of a
   Conservation Area.
10. During my visit, I observed that, while the appeal dwelling’s essential form, scale
    and materials bear similarities with many surrounding properties, it is nevertheless
    of an individual design within the FCA. In the absence of a Conservation Area
    Appraisal, I find that its positive contribution to the significance of the FCA derives
    principally from the architectural interest and quality of its principal elevation, which
    reflects the period of its construction, and from its positioning within a streetscape
    characterised by dwellings exhibiting a cohesive range of traditional features. A
    further defining characteristic of the FCA, evident from properties such as
    Eastlands and Kelfield House, is the prevalence of substantial ancillary
    development positioned to the rear of the road-fronting main buildings.

11. The proposed replacement extension, which would extend to adjoin the
    neighbouring outbuilding, would feature a largely glazed side elevation facing the
    garage, a projecting central bay with flanking roof projections, and a further raised
    glazed section partly above. The design adopts a purposefully contemporary
    approach intended to complement the character of the host dwelling. This approach
    is also reflected in the proposed materials, comprising a natural oak frame structure
    with matching brickwork and slates, combined with dark grey aluminium
    fenestration and fibreglass roofing.
12. The proposal would occupy a larger footprint and reach a greater height than the
    existing extension. Nevertheless, by virtue of its fundamentally single storey scale
    and limited overall proportions, it would still present as a clearly subordinate
    addition to the host building. Whilst the lower canopy and mono-pitched upper roof
    forms would connect to the building close to the rear central landing window, they
    would not compromise the retention of this notable original feature. Any reduction in
    the visibility of any of the dwelling’s arched rear openings would be very limited and
    would not detract from the dwelling’s overall appearance. Furthermore, although
    the extension would adjoin the neighbouring outbuilding, the markedly lower
    roofline and lesser width of the connecting section would break up its massing,
    ensuring that the distinct scale and form of the respective buildings remains readily
    discernible.
13. Due to the spacing between the respective dwellings, the rebuilt boundary wall to
    Kelfield House would be seen from the roadside. However, this view would be
    largely unchanged from the current extension, while because of its discreet
    positioning, there would be only very limited public views possible of solely the rear-
    most section of the opposing glazed elevation.
14. The extension would introduce architectural features not present on the host
    dwelling and, as such, its design would not be strictly in keeping with its traditional
    character. However, this is not an explicit requirement of the development plan and
    each development should be assessed on its own design merits.
15. The Council raises concerns regarding the design of the proposed bay window and
    canopy roof, and I acknowledge that these would introduce different features.
    Nevertheless, as integral components of a deliberately contemporary yet clearly
    subordinate extension, I do not consider them to be inappropriate in design terms.
    Together with the high-quality palette of materials proposed, the scale, form and
    appearance of the development would represent a contextually responsive design
    solution that would add architectural interest to the rear of the property, while
    preserving the significance and value associated with its characteristic principal
    elevation.
16. On this basis, I conclude that the proposed development would not harm the
    character and appearance of the host dwelling and area, and would preserve or
    enhance the character or appearance of the FCA. It would therefore comply with
    the requirements of the Act, together with Policies SP12 and SP16 of the Ryedale
    Local Plan Strategy. Taken together, amongst other things, these policies require
    development to conserve the significance of designated heritage assets, including
    Conservation Areas, and for extensions and alterations to historic buildings to be
    appropriate in scale, form, materials and architectural style, whilst complementing
    the traditional character of the main building.

17. By preserving the significance of the FCA, the proposal would also accord with the
    Framework, which requires great weight to be given to the conservation of
    designated heritage assets when considering the impact of development on their
    significance.
Other Matters
18. Third party representations raise concerns regarding the lack of detail about how
    the rear wall of the extension would connect to the adjoining structure. However,
    the determination of this appeal under section 78 of the Act operates within a
    separate regulatory framework from the Party Wall etc. Act 1996 and does not
    remove or override any obligations arising under that legislation. For the purposes
    of this appeal, I am satisfied that the submitted plans provide sufficient detail to
    allow the proposal to be properly assessed.
19. I acknowledge that the proposal would alter the existing arrangement by converting
    the garden access gate from Kelfield House’s driveway into a direct entrance to the
    extended appeal dwelling. However, there is no substantive evidence before me to
    indicate that either the nature or frequency of use of this access would materially
    increase. Consequently, I am satisfied that the living conditions of the occupants of
    Kelfield House would be adequately safeguarded.
Conditions
20. In addition to conditions related to the commencement of development and the
    development being carried out in accordance with the approved plans, the Council
    suggests a condition requiring the external materials to be in accordance with the
    details as expressed on the plans. I am satisfied that these conditions are
    necessary for certainty and to secure good quality design that preserves the
    character or appearance of the FCA. Where necessary, I have made minor
    amendments to the Council’s suggested wording.
Conclusion and Recommendation
21. For the reasons given above and having had regard to all other matters raised, I
    recommend that the appeal should be allowed and planning permission granted.
