   The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
    against a refusal to grant planning permission.
   The appeal is made by Mrs Suzanne Harrison & Mrs Catriona Harrison against the decision of
    Tandridge District Council.
   The application Ref is TA/2024/1226.
   The development proposed is described as "Erection of two storey new detached dwelling. A+
    energy efficiency. Exceptional quality. M4(3) disabled living."

Decision
1. The appeal is dismissed.
Applications for costs
2. An application for a partial award of costs has been made by Mrs Suzanne
   Harrison & Mrs Catriona Harrison against Tandridge District Council. That
   application will be the subject of a separate decision.
Preliminary Matters
3. Since the appeal was lodged, a revised National Planning Policy Framework (the
   Framework) was published. The parties were given the opportunity to comment on
   any implications for this appeal arising from the revised Framework, and I have
   taken those comments into account in determining the appeal.
4. The Council amended both the description of development and the site address
   during the application process. In the absence of any evidence that these
   amendments were agreed by the appellants and having regard to their clear
   preference for the appeal to be determined on the basis of the original application, I
   have proceeded on that basis.
5. During the appeal, the appellants relied upon additional transport evidence,
   including a Transport Note and visibility information relating to the existing access.
   To the extent that this material had not previously been considered by the Council,
   the main parties have had the opportunity to comment upon it and I am satisfied
   that taking it into account would not cause procedural unfairness. I have therefore
   taken it into account in determining the appeal. The Council has subsequently
   withdrawn its objection relating to visibility at the access. The main issues reflect
   that position.

Reasons
Whether inappropriate development
7. Policy DP10 of the Tandridge Local Plan Part 2: Detailed Policies 2014 (TLP)
   provides that inappropriate development in the Green Belt will only be permitted
   where very special circumstances exist that clearly outweigh the harm arising from
   inappropriateness and any other harm. The policy reflects the over-arching aims of
   Green Belt policy and, whilst it predates the revised Framework and does not refer
   to grey belt land, I find it remains broadly consistent with the Framework insofar as
   it seeks to protect the openness of the Green Belt and resist inappropriate
   development.
8. The Framework similarly identifies inappropriate development as, by definition,
   harmful to the Green Belt. However, it provides a number of circumstances where
   development is not inappropriate. In the present case, the parties' cases have
   primarily focused on whether the appeal site comprises grey belt land and whether
   the proposal satisfies the relevant requirements of Policy GB7.
9. The appeal site comprises an area of grassed land adjacent to, but physically
   separate from, Frith Manor. The appellants contend that it comprises previously
   developed land. Whilst the site shares ownership with Frith Manor, would utilise the
   existing access, and appears to be maintained as part of the wider grounds, the
   evidence before me does not clearly demonstrate that it forms part of the
   residential curtilage of that property. Moreover, the Council has not advanced a
   case that the site constitutes residential curtilage. Having regard to my site
   observations, I noted limited characteristics typically associated with domestic
   curtilage. As the Framework definition of previously developed land includes land
   within the curtilage of developed land, I am not persuaded that the appeal site
   comprises previously developed land. Even if it did, Policy GB7 of the Framework
   requires several criteria to be met before development on grey belt land is not
   inappropriate.
10. I am satisfied that the proposal would not strongly contribute to Green Belt
    purposes (a), (b) or (d). It is not adjacent to a large built-up area, would not prevent
    neighbouring towns from merging, and would not affect the setting or special

       into the countryside, that does not preclude the site from being grey belt. Given the
       modest scale of the proposal and the limited extent of land involved, I have no
       substantive evidence that one dwelling would fundamentally undermine the
       purposes, taken together, of the remaining Green Belt across the plan area. The
       Council’s most recent submissions appear to reach a similar conclusion.
11. Policy GB7 also requires there to be a demonstrable unmet need for the type of
    development proposed. The Council's evidence indicates that it is unable to
    demonstrate a five-year housing land supply and refers to a supply of
    approximately 1.92 years. Consequently, there is undisputed evidence of a
    significant unmet housing need within the district. Whilst the appellant has
    additionally relied upon evidence of demand for self-build housing, given the
    acknowledged shortfall in housing supply more generally, I need not reach a firm
    conclusion on the extent of any specific self-build need for the purposes of this part
    of my assessment.
12. All of the relevant grey belt requirements in Policy GB7 must be met for the
    proposal not to be inappropriate development. As I am not persuaded that the site
    is previously developed land, that would itself render the proposal inappropriate.
    Nonetheless, I go on to consider whether the sustainability requirements of Policy
    GB7, with particular reference to Policy TR3, are met.
Location
13. The proposal would be situated outside any defined settlement boundary. I
    therefore recognise that it conflicts with Policy CSP1 of the Tandridge District Core
    Strategy 2008 (CS), which seeks to direct development towards existing
    settlements. However, the Council accepts that CSP1 is now out of date in the
    context of its housing land supply position and I attach it only limited weight.
    Furthermore, I note that this policy approach does not fully reflect the current
    Framework, which requires a more nuanced and site-specific assessment of
    sustainability rather than one based solely upon settlement boundaries.
    Nevertheless, the conflict with the development plan remains relevant.
14. I have had regard to the previous appeal decisions relating to the site1. The most
    recent Inspector found that there would be alternatives to the private car to access
    local services and facilities, and I see no substantive evidence before me that the
    distances to those facilities have materially changed. Indeed, East Grinstead town
    centre and its range of services are approximately 2 km from the site, whilst
    facilities at Baldwins Hill are closer still. Distances of this order could, in principle,
    be capable of being undertaken on foot by some users. Nevertheless, the quality of
    the walking environment is an important consideration. Whilst Lingfield Road
    benefits from street lighting and a continuous footway, the footway is relatively
    narrow and runs immediately adjacent to a busy road. Consequently, although
    walking is possible, the route is not one that would be particularly attractive or
    convenient for many users.
15. The site also benefits from good proximity to public transport. The nearest bus
    stops are located a short distance from the site and are comfortably within walking
    distance. I am satisfied that occupiers would have a practical means of accessing
    those stops. The stops are served by two routes, providing connections to East

     Grinstead and other nearby destinations. However, the public transport offer is
     relatively limited. One service operates only on weekdays, neither service operates
     on Sundays, and frequencies are modest even during periods of operation. Whilst
     these services provide an alternative to private car use, they are unlikely to

16. I also note that East Grinstead railway station lies approximately 2 km from the
    appeal site. This provides access to the wider rail network and offers a further
    alternative to the private car. However, for the reasons already set out regarding
    the quality of the pedestrian environment, the station would not be conveniently
    accessible for all users, particularly those with reduced mobility.
17. Drawing these matters together, I find that the site provides some choice of
    transport modes. Walking, cycling, bus and rail travel are available in principle.
    However, the attractiveness and convenience of those alternatives is constrained
    by the quality of the pedestrian environment, the limited frequency of public
    transport services and the distances involved. Whilst Policy TR3(a) does not
    require private car use to be eliminated, particularly in rural areas, I am not
    persuaded that future occupiers would have a genuinely attractive choice of
    transport modes such that reliance on the private car would be significantly
    reduced. Consequently, whilst this is a finely balanced matter, I conclude that the
    proposal would not satisfy Policy TR3 and therefore the site would not be in a
    sustainable location for the purposes of Policy GB7 of the Framework.
18. I note finally that Policy TR3(2) requires use of the Connectivity Tool alongside
    other qualitative and quantitative evidence. No Connectivity Tool assessment has
    been provided by either party. Whilst this does not prevent me reaching a
    conclusion on the evidence before me, it does mean that there is no objective
    connectivity score available to assist in resolving the dispute between the parties.
19. The proposal would fail to meet the requirements of Policy GB7 of the Framework
    and would therefore be inappropriate development in the Green Belt. Such
    development is, by definition, harmful and attracts substantial weight. Even if the
    site were previously developed land, the proposal would still fail to satisfy Policy
    GB7 because of the shortcomings I have identified in relation to the site’s location
    and Policy TR3. Consequently, that would not alter my conclusion that the
    development is inappropriate.
Openness
20. Having found that the proposal would constitute inappropriate development,
    substantial weight should be given to that harm in accordance with Policy GB6(2). I
    have also considered whether the proposal would cause any other harm to the
    Green Belt, including harm to openness.
21. I recognise that the site has a degree of enclosure and benefits from screening
    from Lingfield Road, Frith Manor and Farm View Cottage. However, that enclosure
    is achieved primarily through vegetation, the effectiveness of which is liable to
    change over time and seasonally. From my site inspection, I am satisfied that
    glimpsed views of the site and any future dwelling would remain possible from
    surrounding areas and the access serving adjacent properties.
22. I also accept that the land has a domestic character to some extent, being
    maintained as garden land rather than used for agriculture. Nevertheless, it

        remains free from built development and forms part of an area where development
        is relatively sporadic. Consequently, notwithstanding its managed appearance, the
        site presently contributes to Green Belt openness through its undeveloped
        character. I have also had regard to the Beacon Platt decision2 relied upon by the
        appellant. However, unlike the present proposal, the Inspector concluded that all of
        the relevant requirements of the grey belt policy had been satisfied. In particular,
        the site was found to be in a sustainable location. Whilst I have taken that decision
        into account, it does not persuade me to reach a different conclusion on the facts
        before me.
23. Whilst any harm would be localised, the proposal would introduce a substantial
    permanent two-storey dwelling onto land that is currently undeveloped, together
    with the domestic activity, vehicles and paraphernalia ordinarily associated with
    residential occupation. Regardless of whether the site can properly be regarded as
    residential curtilage, it does not presently function as the immediate private garden
    of a dwelling and is not currently subject to those domestic influences.
24. Accordingly, despite the limited scale of the proposal in the context of the wider
    Green Belt, I find that it would result in a notable reduction in both the spatial and
    visual openness of the site. The openness of this part of the Green Belt would be
    significantly eroded.
25. I have had regard to the findings of the Inspector in the previous appeal relating to
    the site3. Whilst that decision was made under a different policy framework, I find
    the Inspector's conclusions regarding the effects of developing this parcel of land
    remain relevant. In particular, I see no reason to disagree with the earlier finding
    that the introduction of a dwelling on this currently undeveloped land would reduce
    spatial openness and diminish the perception of openness. Accordingly, I attach
    significant weight to those findings in my assessment.
26. For these reasons, I conclude that the proposal would cause significant harm to the
    openness of the Green Belt, to which I attach substantial weight in accordance with
    Policy GB6(2). It would also conflict with Policy DP13 of the TLP, insofar as that
    policy seeks to ensure that development in the Green Belt preserves its openness.
Ashdown Forest SPA
27. The appeal site lies within the zone of influence of the Ashdown Forest Special
    Protection Area (SPA), an internationally important nature conservation site
    designated for its populations of ground-nesting birds. The proposal would
    introduce an additional dwelling and therefore increase the number of people
    permanently residing within the zone of influence. Whilst the effect of a single
    dwelling would be limited, the principal concern is the cumulative effect of additional
    residential development on recreational pressure at the SPA. Mitigation is normally
    provided through a combination of Suitable Alternative Natural Greenspace
    (SANG) and Strategic Access Management and Monitoring (SAMM) measures.
28. The appellant has raised concerns regarding the Council's assessment of effects
    on the SPA and referred to other developments where mitigation was accepted.
    However, I am required to determine the appeal on the basis of the evidence
    before me, including the executed unilateral undertaking.

29. The UU would secure the SAMM contribution and provide two alternative
    mechanisms intended to address the SANG requirement. Whilst I am satisfied that
    the SAMM contribution would be secured, this would not in itself mitigate the likely
    significant effect identified.
30. The uplift contribution route remains uncertain. No receiving site has been identified
    and there is insufficient information before me regarding how the contribution would
    be used to provide mitigation. Similarly, whilst the evidence indicates that capacity
    may be available at the Imberhorne Lane SANG, no capacity has been purchased
    and no agreement with Mid Sussex District Council is before me. The proposed
    mitigation therefore remains dependent upon future actions by third parties.
31. I have also had regard to the other examples relied upon by the appellant, including
    the Charters Towers development. However, those cases were determined on the
    basis of the specific mitigation proposed and secured. In the case of Charters
    Towers, this included specialist extra-care accommodation and substantial on-site
    recreational provision. By contrast, the mitigation before me remains dependent
    upon future actions by third parties and has not been secured with sufficient
    certainty. Consequently, those examples do not alter my conclusion that the
    proposal would fail to adequately mitigate its effects on the SPA.
32. Whilst there may be a reasonable prospect of securing the necessary mitigation, a
    reasonable prospect falls short of the certainty required when considering the
    integrity of a European site. Consequently, I am not satisfied that the likely
    significant effect on the SPA would be adequately mitigated. I therefore cannot
    conclude that the proposal would avoid an adverse effect on the integrity of the
    Ashdown Forest Special Protection Area. The proposal consequently conflicts with
    the requirements of the Habitats Regulations, the Framework and Policy DP19 of
    the TLP.
Other considerations
33. The proposal would constitute inappropriate development in the Green Belt and
    would cause significant harm to its openness. It would also fail to demonstrate that
    it would avoid an adverse effect on the integrity of the Ashdown Forest Special
    Protection Area. Policy GB6 of the Framework makes clear that substantial weight
    should be given to any harm to the Green Belt. Very special circumstances will not
    exist unless the harm to the Green Belt by reason of inappropriateness, and all
    other harm resulting from the proposal, is clearly outweighed by other
    considerations.
34. The Council is unable to demonstrate a five-year housing land supply, with an
    indicated supply of around 1.92 years. There is therefore undisputed evidence of a
    significant unmet housing need within the district. The proposal would deliver one
    additional dwelling and therefore make a contribution towards addressing that
    shortfall. I have had regard to the Framework's strong support for the delivery of
    housing which contributes towards identified needs. However, I have already taken
    that unmet need into account when considering Policy GB7 and, whilst the proposal
    satisfies that element of the policy, it nevertheless fails the overall requirements of
    GB7 and remains inappropriate development in the Green Belt. Having regard to
    the limited contribution that a single dwelling would make towards addressing the
    district's housing needs, I attach moderate weight to this benefit.

35. I also have regard to the fact that the proposal would deliver a self-build dwelling.
    From the evidence before me, I have no reason to dispute that there is an unmet
    need for self-build housing within the district. Furthermore, the UU contains robust
    provisions requiring the dwelling to be constructed and first occupied as a self-
    build/custom-build dwelling. However, the proposal would deliver only a single self-
    build dwelling and would therefore make only a very limited contribution towards
    meeting that need. Whilst this adds some weight beyond the delivery of a market
    dwelling, that additional benefit is limited. I therefore attach limited weight to this
    consideration.
36. The appellant also relies upon the proposed dwelling's energy efficiency
    credentials, accessible design, biodiversity benefits and design quality. However,
    there is no mechanism before me securing Passivhaus certification or compliance
    with the M4(3) standard, and there is limited evidence of any specific unmet need
    for accessible accommodation. Similarly, compliance with biodiversity net gain
    requirements is generally expected of development and there is no substantive
    evidence that ecological benefits materially beyond those required by legislation
    and policy would be delivered. Whilst the proposal would be a bespoke dwelling
    incorporating a number of positive features, there is limited evidence before me that
    it would achieve a standard of design quality that is genuinely exceptional or
    materially beyond that expected of well-designed development. Collectively, I
    attach only limited weight to these considerations.
Green Belt Balance
37. Taken together, there are considerations in this case which weigh in favour of the
    proposal, principally the contribution it would make towards meeting housing needs
    in a district with a significant housing land supply shortfall. There would also be
    more limited benefits arising from the provision of a self-build dwelling and the other
    matters identified above.
38. However, the proposal would constitute inappropriate development in the Green
    Belt, which is harmful by definition. It would also cause significant harm to the
    openness of the Green Belt. Policy GB6 of the Framework makes clear that
    substantial weight should be given to any harm to the Green Belt, including harm to
    its openness. Taking all matters into account, I find that the other considerations
    advanced in favour of the proposal do not clearly outweigh the harm to the Green
    Belt by reason of inappropriateness and the significant harm to openness that I
    have identified. Consequently, the very special circumstances necessary to justify
    development in the Green Belt do not exist.
39. The proposal consequently conflicts with Policies DP10 and DP13 of the TLP and
    with the Framework's policies for the protection of the Green Belt.
Other Matters
40. In line with the statutory duty under section 66(1) of the Planning (Listed Buildings
    and Conservation Areas) Act 1990, I have considered the effect of the proposal on
    the setting of the Grade II listed Frith Manor. Given the physical separation
    between the appeal site and the listed building, together with the limited
    intervisibility between them, I find that the proposal would preserve its setting and
    would not harm its significance as a designated heritage asset.

41. The Council is satisfied that the proposal would not result in unacceptable effects
    in respect of residential amenity, trees, drainage, parking provision or highway
    safety. Having regard to the evidence before me, I see no reason to reach a
    different conclusion. However, the absence of harm in these respects is a neutral
    factor and does not weigh in favour of the proposal.
42. The appellant raises a number of concerns regarding the Council's handling of the
    application, including suggestions that the Council assessed the wrong site and
    that limited weight should therefore be attached to its conclusions. However, I am
    satisfied that the appeal site and proposal were sufficiently understood by the
    Council during the determination of the application.
43. In any event, I am required to determine the appeal on its planning merits and have
    reached my own conclusions on the basis of the evidence before me.
    Consequently, any criticisms of the Council's decision-making process do not
    materially alter my findings on the main issues.
44. The appellant has also referred to a number of appeal decisions, including the
    previous appeal relating to this site4, the Beacon Platt decision5 and the Frith
    Manor Farm scheme6. I have had regard to those decisions, giving weight to them
    only insofar as they are relevant to the facts, evidence and policy context before me
    in this appeal.
45. The Beacon Platt decision was determined under the revised Framework and is
    therefore of greater relevance. However, in that case the Inspector concluded that
    the site satisfied all of the relevant grey belt criteria, including the sustainability
    requirements. I have reached a different conclusion on the evidence before me.
46. Similarly, whilst the Frith Manor Farm scheme demonstrates that cross-boundary
    mitigation can be acceptable in principle, the mitigation proposed in that case was
    materially different and more advanced than that before me. Accordingly, none of
    the appeal decisions relied upon causes me to reach a different conclusion on the
    main issues in this appeal.
Conclusion
47. For the reasons given above, I have found that the proposal would constitute
    inappropriate development in the Green Belt and would result in significant harm to
    its openness. I have also found that the site would not satisfy the requirements of
    Policy GB7 of the Framework and that the proposal would fail to demonstrate that it
    would avoid an adverse effect on the integrity of the Ashdown Forest Special
    Protection Area.
48. Whilst the proposal would provide some benefits, including the delivery of an
    additional dwelling in an area of significant housing need, those benefits do not
    amount to the very special circumstances necessary to justify inappropriate
    development in the Green Belt.
49. The proposal therefore conflicts with Policy CSP1 of the CS and Policies DP10,
    DP13 and DP19 of the TLP. For the reasons given above, the material
