Appeal Decision

Appeal Ref: 6006805
60 Marine Road West, Morecambe, Lancashire LA4 4ET
•    The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
     against a refusal to grant planning permission.
•    The appeal is made by Mrs Robyn Holtham against the decision of Lancaster City Council.
•    The application Ref is 25/00874/FUL.
•    The development proposed is conversion from combined commercial and residential to combined
     commercial and House of Multiple Occupation.

Decision
1.     The appeal is allowed and planning permission is granted for conversion from
       combined commercial and residential to combined commercial and House of
       Multiple Occupation at 60 Marine Road West, Morecambe LA4 4ET in accordance
       with the terms of the application, Ref 25/00874/FUL, subject to the following
       conditions:
       1) The development hereby permitted shall begin not later than 3 years from the
          date of this decision.
       2) The development hereby permitted shall be carried out in accordance with
          drawings: Location Plan; 804 Revision A; and 805 Revision A.
       3) The House in Multiple Occupation hereby permitted shall not be occupied until
          the details of a homeowner pack have been submitted to, and approved in
          writing by, the local planning authority. The pack shall include details of the
          nearby sites designated for their nature conservation interest (and the wider
          Morecambe Bay coastline), their sensitivities to recreational pressure and shall
          promote the use of alternative areas for recreation, in particular dog walking
          areas. The pack shall be issued to the property prior to its first occupation as a
          House in Multiple Occupation and a copy shall be retained in a location
          accessible to all residents for the lifetime of the development.
       4) The overnight occupancy of the House in Multiple Occupation hereby permitted
          is limited to a maximum of 8 residents.
Applications for costs
2.     An application for costs was made by Mrs Robyn Holtham against Lancaster City
       Council. This application is the subject of a separate Decision.

       considered in accordance with the ‘Procedural Guide to Planning Appeals –

         England’. Although these plans were not before the Council when it determined
         the application, the changes are limited in scope, comprising the replacement of a

4.       The appeal site lies within a 3.5-kilometre buffer zone of European Designated
         Sites: The Morecambe Bay Special Area for Conservation (SAC); the Morecambe
         Bay and Duddon Estuary Special Protection Area (SPA); and the Morecambe Bay
         Ramsar Site. Such sites are afforded protection under the Conservation of
         Habitats and Species Regulations 2017 as amended. Although not forming part of
         the Council’s reason for refusal, it is incumbent upon me as a competent authority
         to consider whether the proposed development would be likely to have a
         significant effect on the integrity of such designated sites. It is therefore necessary
         to consider this matter as a main issue.
5.       A new National Planning Policy Framework (the Framework)1 was published on 17
         August 2026, during the appeal process. However, as the Framework’s policy
         content insofar as it relates to the main issues in this case has not been
         significantly changed there is no requirement for me to seek further submissions
         from the main parties. I am satisfied no party would be prejudiced by determining
         the appeal accordingly.
Main Issues
6.       The main issues are:
          • whether the proposed development would provide adequate living conditions
            for the occupants with particular regard to the size of bedrooms 5 and 8, and
            refuse and bicycle storage;
          • the effect of the proposed development on the local housing mix and the
            character of the area; and
          • the effect of the proposed development on the integrity of the SAC, SPA and
            Ramsar Site.
Reasons
Living conditions
7.       The Council identified concerns regarding the size of Bedrooms 5 and 8,
         considering them inadequate when assessed against the standards set out in the
         Residential Conversions and Houses in Multiple Occupation Supplementary
         Planning Document (SPD), adopted 8 December 2020. The SPD sets a minimum
         floor space requirement for single occupancy bedrooms and those to be occupied
         by couples and specifies that certain pieces of furniture and adequate circulation
         space should be accommodated.

8.     In response, the appellant submitted the amended plans showing how the required
       furniture could be accommodated within both rooms. The plans show that
       Bedroom 5, now proposed for single occupancy, could accommodate a bed, desk
       and chair, wardrobe, chest of drawers, and the circulation space specified by the
       SPD. It is therefore evident that Bedroom 5 would provide an acceptable standard
       of accommodation for a single occupant, which the Council accepts.
9.     The Council remains concerned about the adequacy of Bedroom 8, which the
       amended plans identify as capable of accommodating either one or two
       occupants. The SPD requires additional space and furniture within rooms occupied
       by couples, including duplicated furniture and a small sitting area with two easy
       chairs. The plans do not demonstrate that these requirements could be
       accommodated. Accordingly, when assessed against the SPD requirements,
       Bedroom 8 would be unsuitable for double occupancy. However, it would satisfy
       the SPD’s requirements for a single bedroom. This could be secured by a
       condition restricting the HMO to a maximum of eight occupants, thereby ensuring
       that Bedroom 8 is occupied by only one person.
10. The appellant indicates that Bedroom 8 exceeds the floorspace requirements for
    two persons contained in the Council’s HMO licensing standards. Contrary to the
    Council’s position, I consider those standards to be a material planning
    consideration because they provide an established benchmark for assessing
    residential accommodation. However, I afford greater weight to the SPD, which
    has been prepared specifically for planning purposes and provides planning advice
    as to how the Council will apply the HMO requirements of LP22 Policy DM13.
    Accordingly, compliance with licensing standards alone does not demonstrate that
    acceptable living conditions would be provided for two occupants of Bedroom 8.
11. The amended plans show the provision of cycle stands for eight bicycles within an
    existing storage area, which would be accessible from both the ground-floor
    lounge and the rear yard. Refuse storage containers are also shown within the
    rear yard. I am satisfied that the proposed cycle and refuse storage provision
    would be adequate, and there is no substantive evidence before me to suggest
    otherwise.
12. I conclude that the proposed development would provide adequate living
    conditions for the occupants, including regarding the size of bedrooms 5 and 8,
    subject to both rooms being for single occupants, and the provision of refuse and
    bicycle storage. It would therefore accord with LP2 policies DM13, DM29 and
    DM61, which seek to protect residential amenity, secure appropriate waste and
    recycling facilities, and prioritise cycling. It would also accord with the policy
    objectives of the Framework to ensure healthy living conditions for occupiers.
Housing mix
13. In its first reason for refusal, the Council cites conflict with LP2 Policy DM1 which
    supports proposals that promote balanced communities and meet evidenced
    housing needs in accordance with the Council’s latest Strategic Housing Market
    Assessment (SHMA).

14. The Council also cites conflict with LP13 Policy EC5 which provides that
    regeneration, reuse and redevelopment proposals within Regeneration Priority
    Areas (RPA) are supported in principle, subject to compliance with other relevant
    Local Plan policies. In its decision, the Council identified the appeal site as falling
    within Morecambe West End RPA, where a historical over-supply of HMOs and
    poor quality one-bedroom units of accommodation has been identified.
15. The Council has subsequently confirmed that the appeal site is outside the
    Morecambe West End RPA. Accordingly, LP1 Policy EC5 does not apply to the
    proposed development. Although the policy context is of some relevance given the
    site’s proximity to the designation, this does not demonstrate that the housing
    market characteristics or historic over-supply identified within the RPA extends
    beyond its defined boundary to include all the area immediately surrounding the
    appeal site.
16. While not cited in the reason for refusal relating to this main issue, LP2 Policy
    DM13 has been referred to by the parties. It addresses HMOs and is therefore
    clearly relevant to the determination of this appeal. The policy seeks to maintain an
    appropriate housing mix and safeguard the character of residential areas. It
    establishes a presumption against new HMOs, indicating that a concentration
    exceeding 10% within a 100-metre radius will not be considered acceptable,
    although proposals may exceptionally be acceptable where criteria V to IX are
    satisfied.
17. The appellant relies on information obtained from the Council’s online mapping
    tool and HMO licensing register, which indicates that there are no HMOs within a
    100-metre radius of the appeal site. The Council disputes the reliability of that
    evidence, noting that publicly available data may not be fully up to date and that
    not all HMOs require licensing and registration. The Council has not provided a
    figure for the proportion of HMOs in the locality and instead relies on the Lancaster
    Strategic Housing Market Assessment (Part II) 2018 (SHMA), specifically table 4.7
    which compares current housing stock against market expectations. The Council
    highlights that, within Morecambe West, where the appeal site is located, there is a
    surplus of one- and two-bedroom apartments relative to the expected household
    demand and contends that the proposed development would therefore exacerbate
    an existing imbalance in the housing mix.
18. However, self-contained apartments are not directly comparable to HMOs, where
    occupiers typically rent individual rooms and share facilities. It is more appropriate
    to regard HMOs as falling within the SHMA’s ‘Other 1 Bed’ type category. The
    SHMA indicates that there is an adequate supply, rather than an oversupply, of
    this type of accommodation within Morecambe West.
19. The central test within LP2 Policy DM13 concerns the concentration of HMOs
    within a 100-metre radius of a proposed development. The Council has not
    quantified the existing proportion of HMOs within that area or demonstrated that
    the proposed development would cause the 10% threshold to be exceeded.
    Moreover, the SHMA evidence relates to the wider Morecambe West housing
    market area, which is substantially larger than the area to be assessed under LP2
    Policy DM13. In addition, the ‘Other 1 Bed’ category is not confined to HMOs.
    Consequently, whilst the SHMA provides useful strategic evidence at a district-

22. In addition, I have found that adequate refuse and bicycle storage is proposed and
    that, subject to an occupancy restriction, future occupiers would enjoy acceptable
    living conditions. No harmful external alterations are proposed. The increased
    occupancy of the property would be compatible with the character of the
    surrounding area and there is no evidence that it would result in unacceptable
    parking pressure, noise or disturbance. I therefore find that the proposed
    development satisfies criteria V to IX of LP2 Policy DM13 and, as such, falls within
    the circumstances in which HMO development may be acceptable.
23. Accordingly, I find that the proposed development would not adversely affect the
    local housing mix or the character of the area. Although LP2 Policy DM13
    establishes a general presumption against new HMOs, the Council has not
    demonstrated that the proposed development would exceed the policy’s
    concentration threshold, and I have found that it satisfies the relevant exception
    criteria. Consequently, there would be no demonstrable harm to community
    balance, housing mix or local character. The proposed development accords with
    LP2 policies DM1, DM13 and DM29, as well as the policy objective of the
    Framework to support an appropriate mix of accommodation for the local
    community.
SAC, SPA and Ramsar Site
24. The SAC, SPA and Ramsar Site comprise an important marine foraging area that
    support many breeding and non-breeding birds, including waterbirds. Residential
    activities undertaken by residents and visitors to Morecambe Bay have the
    potential to disturb and adversely affect the habitats and species for which the
    sites are designated. The proposed development would introduce a use likely to
    accommodate a greater number of residents than a single dwellinghouse, resulting
    in a modest increase in recreational activity. In combination with other
    development in the area, this is likely to have a significant effect on the SAC, SPA
    and Ramsar Site.
25. A condition has been suggested requiring the provision of a homeowner
    information pack for future occupiers of the development. The pack would inform
    residents about the ecological importance of the designated sites, promote
    responsible behaviour, and encourage the use of alternative recreational
    opportunities which would ensure that harm arising from any increased residential
    activity would be appropriately mitigated. Subject to appropriate wording, the

Other Considerations
27. The appeal site is located within the Morecambe West End Conservation Area
    (CA). As such, I have had regard to the statutory duty to pay special attention to
    the desirability of preserving or enhancing the character or appearance of that
    area, as set out at section 72(1) of the Planning (Listed Buildings and
    Conservation Areas) Act 1990. The significance and special interest of the CA,
    insofar as it is relevant to this appeal, derives from its associative and historic
    value as a Victorian seaside resort popular with mill workers.
28. The proposed development includes only limited external alterations, involving the
    insertion of a door into the rear outrigger to replace a window, which I have found
    is not harmful. It is also for the change of use from one type of residential use to
    another, and the nature of activity on the appeal site would therefore be similar
    albeit more intensive. I therefore find that the proposed development has a neutral
    effect and, as such, preserves the character and appearance of the CA, a
    conclusion I note was also reached by the Council.
Conditions
29. To ensure proper planning and provide certainty, conditions are necessary to
    secure the standard time limit for the commencement of development and to
    specify the approved plans.
30. A condition requiring the provision of a homeowner pack has also been imposed.
    The wording has been amended to ensure that a copy is retained on the premises
    and remains available to both the initial and all subsequent residents.
31. I have imposed a condition limiting occupancy to a maximum of eight persons in
    order to safeguard the living conditions of the future residents, as the bedrooms
    are only suitable for single occupancy for the reasons set out above.
Conclusion
32. For the reasons given above, having regard to the development plan as a whole
    and all relevant material considerations, I conclude that the appeal should be
    allowed.
