    Site visit made on 17 August 2026

    Appeal Ref: 6013044
    2 Boileau Road, Barnes, Richmond Upon Thames, London SW13 9BL
    •   The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
        against a refusal to grant planning permission.
    •   The appeal is made by Mr Calvin Barretto against the decision of the Council of the London Borough
        of Richmond Upon Thames.
    •   The application Ref is PA26/1133.
    •   The development proposed is front first floor infill.

Decision
1. The appeal is dismissed.
Preliminary Matters
2. I have also considered an appeal relating to a corresponding first-floor front infill
   extension at the adjoining property, 4 Boileau Road (No. 4)1. The proposals are
   closely related and have been considered together. Nevertheless, they concern
   separate properties, and each appeal must be determined on its individual planning
   merits. The appeal relating to No. 4 is the subject of a separate decision.
3. Since the Council determined the application and the appeal was submitted, a
   revised National Planning Policy Framework (the Framework) was published on 17
   August 2026. I have determined the appeal having regard to the Framework as
   now published. Insofar as relevant to this appeal, its provisions concerning good
   design, development responding appropriately to its context and the use of
   safeguarding conditions do not materially alter the main considerations in this case.
   I am therefore satisfied that neither party would be prejudiced by my proceeding on
   that basis.
Main Issue
4. The main issue is the effect of the proposed development upon the character and
   appearance of the host dwelling and the surrounding area.
Reasons
5. The appeal property is a two-storey semi-detached dwelling forming a pair with No.
   4. The pair has projecting two-storey elements positioned towards its outer sides
   and corresponding recessed sections at first-floor level between them. This
   arrangement provides articulation to the frontage and gives the pair a strong
   degree of visual balance.

6. The surrounding area is predominantly residential and contains a mixture of
   terraced and semi-detached properties. There is some variation in the detailed form
   and appearance of dwellings along Boileau Road. Nonetheless, the appeal dwelling
   and No. 4 are clearly perceived together as a single semi-detached composition.
   Their broadly symmetrical proportions, including the corresponding first-floor
   recesses, are readily apparent from the street and contribute positively to the street
   scene.
7. The proposal would construct a first-floor extension above the existing garage,
   substantially infilling the existing recess. The extension would remain below the
   principal eaves, have a flat roof and be marginally set behind the projecting two-
   storey element.
8. If implemented independently, the proposal would infill the recess on one side while
   leaving the corresponding recess at No. 4 unchanged. Although relatively limited in
   scale, it would erode the balanced composition of the pair and result in an
   appreciably asymmetric, unbalanced frontage. Given the strong visual relationship
   between the two dwellings that change would be readily apparent from Boileau
   Road and would harm the character and appearance of the semi-detached pair and
   the street scene.
9. I acknowledge that the extension would not project forward of the principal two-
   storey element and would remain below the principal eaves and ridge. I also note
   the proposed use of brickwork, fenestration and other detailing intended to match
   the existing dwelling. These factors would limit the prominence of the extension.
   However, they would not overcome the more fundamental effect of substantially
   infilling one of the corresponding first-floor recesses, which are important to the
   articulation and balanced composition of the semi-detached pair.
10. If both developments were implemented in the manner shown on the respective
    drawings, the matching first-floor infills would retain a symmetrical treatment of the
    pair. Furthermore, given the varied built development along Boileau Road, paired
    infill extensions would not appear overly prominent within the established street
    scene.
11. However, the proposal at No.4 is the subject of a separate planning application
    and appeal. There is no certainty that the corresponding development would be
    implemented. I have therefore considered whether this matter could be addressed
    by a condition. National policy and guidance require conditions to be necessary to
    make the development acceptable in planning terms, relevant to the development
    and to planning considerations generally, sufficiently precise to make them capable
    of being complied with and enforced, and reasonable in all other respects.
12. Conditions requiring the development to be carried out in accordance with the
    approved drawings, or requiring matching materials and detailing, would control the
    form and appearance of the development at the appeal property but would not
    secure implementation of the corresponding development. A condition seeking to
    prevent or control development at the appeal site by reference to the
    implementation of a separate planning permission at No.4 would extend beyond the
    development which is the subject of this appeal and would not provide sufficient
    certainty that the corresponding development would take place. I am therefore not
    satisfied that such a condition would meet the relevant tests. Nor is there a
    planning obligation or other mechanism before me which would provide that

13. For the reasons set out above, I conclude that the proposal would cause
    unacceptable harm to the character and appearance of the host dwelling and
    surrounding area. It would therefore conflict with Policy 28 of the Richmond upon
    Thames Local Plan ‘The best for our borough’ 2024-2039 (2025), which, amongst
    other things, requires development to be of high design quality and compatible with
    local character, including in respect of its proportions and form. It would also be
    contrary to the design objectives of the Council’s House Extensions and External
    Alterations Local Plan Supplementary Planning Document (2015) which seeks
    development that responds positively to the character of existing buildings and their
    surroundings.
Other Matters
14. I have had particular regard to planning permission Ref 04/0273/HOT, granted in
    March 2004 for first-floor front infill extensions at both the appeal site and No.4.
    That permission concerned a single proposal encompassing corresponding
    development at both properties and was determined under the development plan
    and design guidance applying at that time, which have subsequently changed. I
    acknowledge that, on the evidence before me, it was not subject to a condition or
    other mechanism requiring the two extensions to be implemented together.
    Nevertheless, the earlier permission related to a materially different decision-
    making context, whereas the appeal before me concerns development only at the
    appeal site. Having regard to those differences, and to the harm I have identified if
    the appeal scheme were implemented independently, the previous permission does
    not lead me to a different conclusion.
15. I have also had regard to the three other appeal decisions referenced by the
    appellant. Based on the evidence provided, these demonstrate that alterations to
    one half of a semi-detached pair do not necessarily result in unacceptable harm
    simply because asymmetry would arise. However, each case turns on the particular
    context, architectural characteristics and effects of the development proposed. In
    this case, the prominence of the corresponding central recesses, their contribution
    to the composition of the pair and the strong visual relationship between the two
    dwellings distinguish the appeal property from the examples referred to. Those
    decisions do not therefore alter my findings.
16. The Council raised no concerns in regard to neighbouring amenity, outlook,
    daylight or sunlight, parking, highways, trees or flood risk. Based on the evidence
    before me I have no reason to disagree with the Council in regard to these matters.
    Nonetheless, an absence of harm is a neutral factor weighing neither for not
    against a development.

Conclusion
17. For the reasons given above, the development conflicts with the development plan
    and there are no material considerations, including the Framework, of sufficient
    weight to indicate that the proposed development should be determined otherwise
    than in accordance with the development plan. The appeal should therefore be
    dismissed.
