Site visit made on 3 August 2026
by C Cooper-Young MSc MRTPI

Appeal Ref: 6005999
Flat 45, Rozel House, 42 Birnbeck Road, Weston-super-Mare BS23 2BU
•    The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
     against a refusal to grant planning permission.
•    The appeal is made by Mr Clive Wright against the decision of North Somerset Council.
•    The application reference is 25/P/1464/FUL.
•    The development is the erection of pergola and decking to roof terrace.

Decision
1.     The appeal is dismissed.
Preliminary Matters
2.     For the avoidance of doubt, I observed at my site visit that the development had
       already been carried out, and in accordance with the plans before me.
3.     I have had regard to the duty set out in Section 66(1) of the Planning (Listed
       Buildings and Conservation Areas) Act 1990. This requires, with respect to a listed
       building or its setting, special regard to the desirability of preserving the building or
       its setting or any features of special architectural or historic interest which it
       possesses. The evidence indicates that the site is located nearby to a number of
       Listed Buildings1. The Council conclude that the development does not affect the
       setting of these heritage assets. On the evidence before me and following my site
       visit, I see no reason to disagree.
4.     A revised version of the National Planning Policy Framework (the Framework) was
       published in August 2026. In the interest of natural justice and to ensure neither of
       the main parties were prejudiced by this matter, I consulted both parties on the
       changes to the Framework. Any comments received have been considered in my
       determination of the appeal. Any reference to the Framework in this decision is to
       the most recent version.
Main Issues
5.     The main issues are:
       1) whether the development preserves or enhances the character or appearance
          of the Seafront Character Area of the Great Weston Conservation Area (CA),
          and the effect of the development on the character and appearance of the host
          building.

Reasons
6.       The appeal site comprises the roof terrace to Flat 45, atop a 7-storey apartment
         block within the CA. The CA Appraisal and Management Plan (2018) (CAAMP)
         explains that the CA has four constituent parts and sets out that the significance of
         the CA in part derives from its great aesthetic and architectural value. The appeal
         site is located within the Seafront Area. The CAAMP identifies the Birnbeck Road
         sub-area as a historic, winding, and topographically distinctive seafront sub-area
         centred on Birnbeck Pier and characterised by its terraces, views, and intimate
         townscape character. The CAAMP seeks to prevent unsympathetic roof
         extensions that erode the architectural integrity of buildings.
7.       This part of the CA is characterised by imposing sea facing buildings comprising
         residential apartments, hotels, B&Bs and Victorian houses looking across the bay.
         Its significance lies in the strong relationship between the built environment and
         the seafront setting, where historic hotels, terraces, villas and public spaces
         combine to create a distinctive coastal townscape.
8.       The statutory requirement2 demands that special attention be paid to the
         desirability of preserving or enhancing the character or appearance of a
         conservation area.
9.       The development carried out is comprised of a stepped composite deck area on a
         wooden sub-frame, with a powder coated steel framed gazebo attached to the
         deck. At my site visit I noted restraining straps further anchoring the gazebo to the
         deck. I acknowledge that the colour of the materials used in construction of the
         development are sympathetic to elements of the existing materials of the host
         building.
10. Rozel House is a prominent and distinctive seafront building occupying a highly
    visible position on Weston-super-Mare’s seafront. Its contemporary design is
    characterised by its strong symmetry and composition that creates visual interest
    through strong horizontal lines, and uniform proportions. The articulated corner
    features provide landmark qualities within the streetscape. As a modern
    intervention within the CA, Rozel House contributes a confident contemporary
    architectural expression that reflects the continuing evolution of the seafront whilst
    maintaining a strong relationship with its coastal context and surrounding built
    form.
11. At my site visit I noted a number of historic roof alterations along the seafront
    considered by the CAAMP to be detrimental to the character of the host building.
    Notwithstanding these additions, I observed a broad consistency to the rhythm of
    seaward facing roof heights from the host building toward Knightstone Road.
12. The development introduces a conspicuous vertical element that competes with,
    and detracts from, the purposefully balanced composition of the principal
    elevation. Positioned off-centre and above the established roofline, it is not
    integrated into the underlying architectural order of the building. Consequently, it
    interrupts the intended symmetry of the elevation, creates a visually discordant
    feature within the roofscape, and undermines the coherence and legibility of the
    original design.

13. These harmful effects are exacerbated by the development's elevated position and
    clear visibility against the skyline. In both immediate and longer-range views from
    the seafront and surrounding public realm, the structure appears as a prominent
    and intrusive addition rather than a subordinate feature. Its visual prominence
    draws attention away from the building's principal architectural features, resulting
    in an incongruous focal point within the roofscape. The resultant effect is a
    cluttered and unbalanced roof form that detracts from the appearance of the host
    building and diminishes its positive contribution to the character and visual quality
    of the wider seafront townscape.
14. Consequently, the development does not preserve or enhance the character or
    appearance of the CA, and results in visual harm to the character and appearance
    of the host building.
15. I find the development would cause harm to the significance of the CA as a
    designated heritage asset. The Framework sets out that substantial weight should
    be given to the conservation of Heritage Assets and advises that harm should be
    weighed against the public benefits of the development.
16. The development enhances the living accommodation available to the present and
    future occupiers of Flat 45. As required by Framework, I have had regard to any
    public benefits of the development. However, none have been identified and
    therefore this matter cannot outweigh the harm caused.
17. For the above reasons, I therefore find that the development fails to preserve or
    enhance the character or appearance of the Great Weston CA and harms the
    character and appearance of the host building. The proposal therefore conflicts
    with the aims of Policies DM3 and DM32 of the North Somerset Sites and Policies
    Plan (Part 1) (2016) and Policies CS5 and CS12 of the North Somerset Core
    Strategy (2017) which seek to ensure a high standard of design and development
    that preserves or enhances the character and appearance of the CA and
    contributes to local distinctiveness.
Other Matters
18. The Council has identified the site as lying within Horseshoe Bat Consultation
    Zone C. Whilst Consultation Zone C is not itself a European designated site, it
    identifies land where development may affect habitats used by Greater and Lesser
    Horseshoe Bats associated with the North Somerset and Mendip Bats Special
    Area of Conservation (SAC), a site protected under the Conservation of Habitats
    and Species Regulations 2017 (as amended). In the event that I had found no
    harm in respect of the main issue, the competent authority (in this case myself)
    would need to carry out an Appropriate Assessment in respect of the potential
    effects of the proposal on the designated site. However, as I have found against
    the appellant on other substantive grounds, this matter does not need to be
    considered any further.
19. I note the comments of the Interested Parties, the matters relating to the CA and
    visual appearance are considered above. Other matters such as potential damage,
    fire, ownership and risks associated with the development fall outside of the scope
    of the matters I am able consider.
20. The appellant outlines that the development has been inspected by a structural
    engineer and has the approval of the management company. However, no

21. I have been made aware of the personal circumstances of the appellant, but no
    substantive evidence has been provided in this regard. Moreover, it has not been
    shown that there is no feasible alternative to the development in place. As such, I
    place limited weight on the personal circumstances of the appellant as a
    justification for the development.
Conclusion
22. The proposal conflicts with the development plan and the material considerations,
    including the Framework, do not indicate that the appeal should be decided other
    than in accordance with it.
23. For the reasons given above the appeal should be dismissed.
