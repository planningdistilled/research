Appeal Ref: 6011913
2 Wadham Gardens, London NW3 3DP
•   The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
    against a refusal to grant planning permission.
•   The appeal is made by Barbara Ross against the decision of the Council of the London Borough of
    Camden.
•   The application Ref is 2026/1369/P.
•   The development proposed is proposed modifications to the front elevation, comprising the erection
    of a first-floor bay window and alterations to the central window.

Decision
1. The appeal is dismissed.
Appeal Procedure
2. The site visit was undertaken by a representative of the Inspector whose
   recommendation is set out below and to which the Inspector has had regard before
   deciding the appeal.
Preliminary Matter
3. A Revised National Planning Policy Framework (the Framework) was published on
   17 August 2026. There have been some layout and subsequent paragraph
   numbering changes, as well as minor relevant alterations to the ways in which
   heritage matters are considered. I am however satisfied that, having reviewed the
   relevant changes, they do not have a material bearing on the substance of the main
   issue, the arguments of the main parties thereon or the outcome of the appeal.
   Since they would not therefore be prejudiced, I have proceeded without further
   consultation.
Main Issue
4. The main issue is whether the proposed development would preserve or enhance
   the character or appearance of the Elsworthy Conservation Area (CA).
Reasons for the Recommendation
5. The significance of the CA derives in part from its well-preserved residential
   character, established through development between 1840 and 1914. It is
   characterised by terraced townhouses, substantial semi-detached villas and large
   detached dwellings arranged within a garden suburb layout, reflecting its historic
   role as an attractive and spacious residential area for affluent families within easy
   reach of central London.

6. The host building is not listed but was designed by Horace Field and is
   characterised by the underlying architectural style of William Willett. During his
   development of Wadham Gardens, Willett adapted the Arts and Crafts movement
   and developed the Free Style and the eclectic Queen Anne style which can be
   described as an attractive medley of picturesque features including Dutch gables
   and irregular windows with small panes and white glazing bars.
7. The existing front elevation features larger openings at ground-floor level and
   smaller windows at first-floor level setting out a clear visual and traditional
   hierarchy. Of particular importance is the relationship between the first-floor
   windows and the prominent gables above. Whilst the windows are positioned to
   align with the gables, the ground-floor openings are deliberately offset. This
   alignment creates an asymmetrical but carefully balanced and intentional
   composition that is characteristic of the Arts and Crafts and Free Style architectural
   approach. Consequently, the relationship between the first-floor windows and the
   gables forms a defining element of the building's design and contributes positively
   to its architectural interest and appearance within the CA.
8. The alterations would realign the first-floor windows with the openings below.
   However, this would come at the expense of the relationship between the first-floor
   windows and the gables above, which form a key feature of the elevation. The
   increase in size would also result in the fenestration being top heavy at first floor
   level, ignoring the traditional hierarchy. Whilst the existing facade features windows
   in irregular places, this reflects the deliberate design approach associated with the
   Arts and Crafts and Free Style traditions, where balance and visual interest are
   achieved through irregular composition rather than strict symmetry in every case.
   By disrupting the existing hierarchy of windows and the established alignment
   between the first-floor windows and the gables, the proposal would erode an
   important aspect of the building's architectural character and unacceptably diminish
   the positive contribution it makes to both the character and appearance of the CA.
9. With this and the above in mind, the proposal would fail to either preserve or
   enhance the character or appearance of the CA. Accordingly, it would conflict with
   Policies D1 and D2 of the Camden Local Plan (2017) which require development to
   respect local context and character and to preserve or enhance the historic
   environment and heritage assets.
10. The Framework explains that any harm to a designated heritage asset will be a
    matter of considerable importance and weight and should be weighed against any
    public benefits resulting. Beyond private ones and the personal desire behind the
    changes to the elevation concerned, I can see no further public benefits of the
    proposed development. They would accordingly attract very limited weight.
Conclusion and Recommendation
11. The appeal scheme would conflict with the development plan and material
    considerations, including the approach of the Framework in this particular case with
    specific regard to heritage matters, do not indicate a decision other than in
    accordance therewith. I therefore recommend that the appeal should be dismissed.
