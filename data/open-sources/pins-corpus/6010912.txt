Appeal Decision
Site visit made on 4 August 2026
by Gary Deane BSc (Hons) DipTP MRTPI
an Inspector appointed by the Secretary of State
Decision date: 27 August 2026


Appeal Ref: 6010912
979 Tyburn Road, Birmingham B24 0TJ
•   The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
    against a refusal to grant planning permission.
•   The appeal is made by Mr Mohammed Nanu against the decision of Birmingham City Council.
•   The application reference is 2025/06515/PA.
•   The development is the erection of a veranda to the front of 979 Tyburn Road.

Decision
1. The appeal is allowed, and planning permission is granted for the erection of a
   veranda to the front of 979 Tyburn Road, Birmingham B24 0TJ in accordance with
   the terms of the application reference 2025/06515/PA and the plans submitted with
   it, which are drawing numbers 03 A, 08 and 09.
Procedural and preliminary matters
2. The veranda has been erected on the site. It appears to be broadly in accordance
   with the submitted plans. Planning permission is therefore sought retrospectively.
3. A new National Planning Policy Framework (the Framework) was issued on
   17 August 2026. The Framework replaces the previous version published in
   December 2024 that was in force when the Council determined the application. I
   have had regard to the new Framework in reaching my decision. It does not raise
   any new matters that are determinative to the outcome of this appeal.
Main issues
4. The main issues are the effect of the veranda on the character and appearance of
   the host building and the local area, and on highway safety particularly for users of
   the footway adjacent to the site.
Character and appearance
5. The appeal property is a substantial two-storey building that occupies a prominent
   corner at the junction of Tyburn Road and Kingsbury Road within a predominantly
   commercial area. Its individual design, upper brick walls and hipped roof, together
   with the orientation of its principal elevation at an offset angle towards the road
   junction, set the appeal building apart from the adjoining commercial terrace that
   stands broadly parallel to Tyburn Road.
6. The veranda extends across the front elevation and projects outwards from the
   host building. It is visible from numerous public vantage points and is a
   conspicuous addition to the local streetscape. Nevertheless, the appeal property

     terminates the built frontage along Tyburn Road and, unlike the remainder of the
     adjacent terrace, the elevation to which the veranda attaches directly faces a large,
     open road junction. Consequently, the established building line along Tyburn Road
     is less influential here than it would be within a continuous uniform frontage.
7. There is no directly comparable structure nearby. Nevertheless, the modest height,
   extensive glazing and relatively slender black frame give the veranda a lightweight
   general appearance. Its transparency also allows views through to the host
   building from the footway, with its upper elevations and hipped roof, which
   positively contribute to the distinctive style of the property, pre-eminent.
8. The veranda has a contemporary design that contrasts with the more traditional
   design, materials and detailing of the existing building. City Note LW-30 of the
   Healthy Living and Working Places City Manual of the Birmingham Design Guide
   (BDG) allows an alternative approach where there is an architectural rationale. In
   this case, the clear distinction between the more traditional building and the
   modern, lightweight glazed addition provides an appropriate contrast that
   complements rather than competes with the architecture of the host building.
9. The veranda has a noticeable presence in the local street scene. Having regard to
   its transparent design, limited height and orientation to face the open junction, it is
   not incongruous, unduly dominant or intrusive. Nor does the veranda add
   significantly to street clutter, as it is seen as a coherent part of the host building
   rather than as an unrelated feature within the street scene.
10. On the first main issue, I conclude that the veranda does not cause harm to the
    character and appearance of the host building or the local area. It therefore
    accords with Policy PG3 of the Birmingham Development Plan 2031 and Design
    Principle 2 of the Birmingham City Manual. It is also consistent with the relevant
    guidance in the BDG and the National Design Guide. These policies and guidance
    expect new development to demonstrate high design quality. I also find no conflict
    with Policy DP3 of the Framework, which requires development to respond to the
    context of its site so that it integrates with and enhances its surroundings, while
    expressly allowing for innovation or change where appropriate.
Highway safety
11. The information submitted with the planning application did not clearly establish the
    boundary of the public highway. The Council's Transport Team considered that the
    veranda appeared to encroach onto highway land maintainable at public expense
    and sought confirmation of the precise boundary from the Council's Highways
    Information Team. That confirmation was not received before the application was
    determined. The issue is therefore whether the evidence now before me is
    sufficient to assess the planning effects of the development.
12. Further evidence submitted with the appeal includes a Land Registry title plan and
    photographs showing the relationship between the veranda and the adjacent
    surfaced areas. The title plan shows only the general position of the land title
    boundaries and does not establish the precise extent of the public highway.
    Moreover, as the Council's Public Rights of Way Officer noted, land accessible to
    the public may be subject to highway rights irrespective of its registered ownership.
    I therefore attach limited weight to the land title plan in establishing the precise
    boundary of the public highway.

13. Nevertheless, it is not necessary to determine the exact legal boundary to assess
    the planning effects of the development. The relevant consideration is whether the
    veranda, as constructed, materially affects highway safety including the movement
    of users of the adjacent footway. From my observations, a substantial area of
    unobstructed hard surfacing remains around the veranda sufficient to allow
    pedestrians to circulate around the building and pass each other safely. I saw no
    obvious restriction that would hinder or impede pedestrian movement or otherwise
    compromise the safe use of the adjoining footway with the veranda in place.
14. While the information available at application stage explains why the Council was
    unable to fully assess the proposal, I am satisfied that the veranda does not have
    an unacceptable effect on highway safety, pedestrian movement or the function of
    the adjoining public realm. I therefore conclude that there is no conflict with BDP
    Policies PG3 and TP44 or with Policy TR6 of the Framework. Together, these
    policies seek to ensure that development can proceed without an unacceptable
    impact on highway safety.
Other matters
15. A third-party representation raises concern about the use of the appeal premises as
    a restaurant, vehicle parking and the number of food outlets in the area. The
    appeal before me concerns the veranda. I have nevertheless taken these matters
    into account insofar as they relate to the development before me, but they do not
    alter my conclusions on the main issues.
Conditions
16. As the development has already been carried out, a condition requiring
    implementation within a specified time-period is unnecessary. The description of
    development and the plans identified in the formal decision define the development
    for which planning permission is granted. A separate condition that requires
    compliance with the plans is therefore unnecessary.
17. The Council's Environmental Pollution Control Officer raised no objection in
    principle but suggested that use of the veranda might be time limited to protect the
    amenity of first-floor residential occupiers within the adjoining buildings. However,
    in the absence of details relating to the veranda's actual pattern of use and its
    specific relationship to any windows of nearby residential properties I am unable to
    conclude that such a restriction is necessary to make the development acceptable.
    Therefore, I have not imposed this condition.
Conclusion
18. There are no material considerations that indicate that the development should be
    determined other than in accordance with the development plan. For the reasons
    given above, I conclude that the appeal should be allowed.
