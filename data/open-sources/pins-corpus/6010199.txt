Appeal Decision
Site visit made on 2 September 2026
by G Sibley MPLAN MRTPI
an Inspector appointed by the Secretary of State
Decision date: 2 October 2026


Appeal Ref: 6010199
Grass Verge at Ferrers Close, Coventry CV4 9RA
•    The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
     against a refusal to grant approval required under Article 3(1) and Schedule 2, Part 16, Class A of
     the Town and Country Planning (General Permitted Development) (England) Order 2015 (as
     amended).
•    The appeal is made by MBNL (EE UK Ltd and H3G UK Ltd) against the decision of Coventry City
     Council.
•    The application Ref is PL/2025/0001852/PAEC.
•    The development proposed is the proposed installation of a telecommunications base station
     comprising a 20m monopole supporting 9 no. antennas and 2 no. dishes with wraparound cabinet at
     the base, together with 6 no. ground based equipment cabinets and ancillary development thereto.

Decision
1.    The appeal is dismissed.
Preliminary Matters
2.    The provisions of the GPDO, under Article 3(1) and Schedule 2, Part 16, Class A,
      paragraph A.3(4), require the local planning authority, and therefore the Secretary
      of State, to assess the proposed development solely on the basis of its siting and
      appearance, taking into account any representations received.
3.    The principle of development is established by the GPDO and the provisions of
      Schedule 2, Part 16, Class A of the GPDO do not require regard to be had to the
      development plan. I have therefore had regard to the policies of the development
      plan and the National Planning Policy Framework (the Framework) only insofar as
      they are a material consideration relevant to matters of siting and appearance.
4.    A new Framework was issued on 18 August 2026. The main parties were given
      the opportunity to comment on the implications of the new Framework for this
      appeal. Where comments have been received, I have taken them into account in
      reaching my decision.
5.    The appellant has submitted revised plans showing the monopole coloured black
      and the cabinets coloured green. These drawings do not fundamentally alter the
      siting, scale or design of the scheme. The parties had the opportunity to comment
      on the revised plans and, therefore, no party would be prejudiced by my
      consideration of them.
Main Issues
6.    The main issues relevant to this appeal are the effect of the siting and appearance
      of the proposed installation on the character and appearance of the area and, if

Reasons
Siting and Appearance
7.    The appeal site forms part of an area of grassed open space close to the junction
      of Ferrers Close and Faseman Avenue. It is seen in the context of a residential
      street scene, where the grassed areas and nearby trees make a positive
      contribution to the relatively open and suburban character of the locality. The
      immediate surroundings do not contain tall structures or a concentration of
      substantial street furniture; however, there are taller apartment blocks within views
      further afield.
8.    Whilst there is vertical infrastructure nearby, such as telegraph poles, the
      approximately 20-metre-tall monopole would be appreciably taller than the nearby
      infrastructure and would be seen above much of the surrounding tree cover.
      Although trees would provide some filtering and backdrop in certain views, they
      would not assimilate the mast into the street scene. Its height and engineered form
      would make it a prominent vertical feature in views from the nearby roads,
      footways and open spaces.
9.    The submitted photomontages indicate that the degree of visibility would vary and
      that trees would provide some screening or backdrop in certain views. However,
      they also show that the monopole would remain clearly visible, including in views
      where it would rise above, or be seen separately from, the surrounding tree cover.
      The photomontages therefore do not alter my finding that the proposal would
      appear as a prominent and incongruous feature in the street scene.
10. The associated cabinets would also have a noticeable presence at ground level.
    Their number, bulk and utilitarian appearance would introduce a more urbanising
    form of development onto the area of open space. Taken together with the
    monopole, the equipment would appear as a visually assertive and urbanising
    addition that would contrast with the softer and more informal qualities of this part
    of the street scene.
11. The revised colour scheme, with a black monopole and green cabinets, would
    reduce the degree of visual contrast to some extent. I also recognise that
    telecommunications infrastructure is commonly found within residential and
    highway environments and that the use of a slimline monopole would reduce its
    apparent bulk. However, these matters would not overcome the harm arising from
    the specific siting and appearance of the installation in this location, including the
    height and prominence of the monopole and the number, bulk and cumulative
    presence of the associated cabinets.
12. I have also had regard to the appellant’s point that officers recommended approval
    of the proposal. However, the Council reached a different decision, and I have
    determined the appeal on the basis of the evidence before me and my own
    assessment of the site and its surroundings. For the reasons set out above, I find
    that the siting and appearance of the proposal would be harmful.
13. For these reasons, I conclude that the siting and appearance of the proposed
    installation would cause unacceptable harm to the character and appearance of

      the area. Insofar as they are relevant, the scheme would conflict with Policies C2
      and DE1 of the Coventry Local Plan 2017. Amongst other things, these policies
      support telecommunications infrastructure where the siting and appearance of
      equipment are acceptable and seek to ensure that development is of high-quality
      design, responds positively to local character and creates a high-quality, attractive

14. Although the appeal site is within an area of open green space, the footprint of the
    equipment would be limited, and insofar as it is relevant, I have not found separate
    conflict with Policy GE2 of the Coventry Local Plan 2017 on that basis. This matter
    does not, however, overcome the harm I have identified to the character and
    appearance of the area due to the siting and appearance of the equipment.
Alternative Sites
15. Given my finding that the appeal proposal would cause visual harm, the evidence
    relating to alternative sites is relevant to the acceptability of its siting. The
    Framework supports high-quality communications and recognises the importance
    of electronic communications networks. Policy CO1 indicates that proposals
    should use existing masts, buildings and other structures already employed for
    telecommunications purposes, or upgrade or replace existing infrastructure, unless
    there is no reasonable opportunity to do so.
16. The appellant’s evidence identifies a need for telecommunications equipment
    within the search area, and I do not dispute the importance of providing suitable
    coverage in the area. I also recognise that the search area is constrained by the
    need to replace the existing installation and maintain coverage. However, that
    does not remove the need for clear and persuasive evidence to explain why a
    potentially less harmful alternative has been rejected.
17. The car park site off Jardine Crescent, identified as D20 in the appellant’s table of
    discounted sites, was discounted on the basis that the necessary equipment would
    result in the loss of at least five parking spaces and would affect the operation of
    the car park. However, there is limited substantive evidence before me about the
    extent of that operational effect or why it would make the site unavailable, unviable
    or clearly less suitable than the appeal site.
18. I do not need to determine whether the D20 car park site would itself be
    acceptable. That would depend on the details of any proposal. The issue is
    whether clear and persuasive evidence demonstrates that it was properly
    discounted as a potentially less harmful siting option. On the evidence before me, I
    am not satisfied that it has been discounted on a clear and persuasive basis.
19. The appellant has also referred to the D15 Faseman Avenue/Langley Croft option.
    I acknowledge the appellant’s position that this site would have a more open siting,
    less screening and a closer relationship with residential properties. Those matters
    provide a reasoned basis for discounting D15. However, that does not overcome
    the separate evidential deficiency in relation to D20.
20. I have had regard to the wider site search, including the appellant’s assessment of
    the 22 alternative sites. The Council identified D15 and D20 as options which it
    considered had not been convincingly discounted, but it did not demonstrate that
    either site would be suitable or preferable. Nevertheless, that does not remove the

      need for the appellant’s evidence to demonstrate with sufficient clarity why
      potentially less harmful options were rejected.
21. I have taken account of the appellant’s wider explanation for discounting other
    sites, including the search area, structural capacity, height, operational
    requirements and other constraints. However, that wider evidence does not clearly
    and persuasively demonstrate that D20 was properly discounted as a potentially
    less harmful option. This evidential deficiency weakens the justification for the
    chosen siting, in circumstances where I have found that the appeal proposal would
    cause visual harm.
Conclusion
22. The Framework supports high-quality communications and the provision of
    electronic communications networks. I also recognise the appellant’s evidence that
    the proposal would assist in maintaining and improving mobile telecommunications
    coverage and capacity in the area. However, this is a prior approval appeal where
    the relevant considerations are confined to siting and appearance. The general
    need for electronic communications infrastructure is reflected in the permitted
    development right itself.
23. I have also had regard to Policy CO1 of the Framework, including the statement
    that substantial weight should be given to the benefits of maintaining or improving
    network coverage, capacity, reliability and resilience. In the context of this prior
    approval appeal, that policy support is relevant insofar as it informs the need for
    the installation to be sited as proposed, taking into account the availability of
    realistic alternatives.
24. For the reasons given above, the siting and appearance of the proposed monopole
    and associated cabinets would cause unacceptable harm to the character and
    appearance of the area. That harm arises from the height, prominence and
    cumulative urbanising presence of the equipment within an area of open space
    whose verdant appearance makes a positive contribution to the character and
    appearance of the area.
25. It has not been demonstrated with clear and persuasive evidence that the D20 car
    park site was properly discounted as a potentially less harmful siting option.
    Consequently, I am not satisfied that the need for the installation to be sited as
    proposed outweighs the harm arising from its siting and appearance.
26. For the reasons given above, I conclude that the appeal should be dismissed.
