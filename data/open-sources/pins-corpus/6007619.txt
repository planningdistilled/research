Appeal A Ref: 6007619
Eden Grove, 17-51 London Road, Staines-upon-Thames TW18 4EX
•   The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
    against a refusal to grant planning permission under section 73 of the Town and Country Planning
    Act 1990 (as amended) for the development of land without complying with conditions subject to
    which a previous planning permission was granted.
•   The appeal is made by Berkeley Homes (West London) Ltd against the decision of Spelthorne
    Borough Council.
•   The application Ref is 25/01245/RVC.
•   The application sought planning permission for erection of six buildings to provide 467 residential
    homes (Use class C3) and flexible commercial space at ground and first floors (Use Classes A1-A3,
    B1a, D1 or D2), car parking, pedestrian and vehicle access, landscaping and associated works,
    without complying with conditions attached to planning permission Ref 23/00273/RVC dated 5 June
    2023.
•   The conditions in dispute are Nos 3 and 20.
•   Condition 3 states that: The parking spaces for motor vehicles and bicycles for each block shown on
    the approved plans shall be constructed and laid out prior to the occupation of each block within the
    development, as set out within the Parking Phasing Plan (September 2021), and shall be retained
    thereafter for the benefit of the occupiers of the development as approved and shall not be used for
    any other purpose without the express written consent of the Local Planning Authority.
    The reason given for the condition is: To ensure that the proposed development does not prejudice
    the free flow of traffic or the conditions of general safety along the neighbouring highways and to
    ensure that the cycle parking spaces are provided are reserved for the benefit of the development for
    which they are specifically required, in accordance with policy CC3 of the Spelthorne Borough Core
    Strategy and Policies Development Plan Document 2009.
•   Condition 20 states that: The 86 car parking spaces within the multi storey car park as identified on
    plans L(LE)001 and L(LE)003 shall be retained in perpetuity for such use by the residential occupiers
    of the proposed development, unless otherwise agreed by the Local Planning Authority in writing.
    The reason given for the condition is: The condition above is required in order that the development
    should not prejudice highway safety, nor cause inconvenience to other highway users, and to accord
    with the National Planning Policy Framework 2019 and policy CC2 of Spelthorne Borough Council's
    Core Strategy and Policies Development Plan Document February 2009.

Appeal A: Decision
1.     The appeal is allowed and planning permission is granted for erection of six
       buildings to provide 467 residential homes (Use class C3) and flexible commercial
       space at ground and first floors (Use Classes A1-A3, B1a, D1 or D2), car parking,
       pedestrian and vehicle access, landscaping and associated works at Eden Grove,
       17-51 London Road, Staines-upon-Thames, TW18 4EX in accordance with the
       terms of the application, Ref 25/01245/RVC, subject to the conditions in the
       attached schedule.
Appeal B: Decision
2.     The appeal is allowed and planning permission is granted for erection of two
       buildings to provide 173 residential homes (Class C3) and flexible commercial
       space at ground and first floors (Class A1, A2, A3, B1, D1 or D2), with landscaping
       and associated works, to be delivered as an extension to the wider redevelopment
       of 17-51 London Road under application 19/00290/FUL comprising an additional
       22 homes above those within application 19/00290/FUL at Eden Grove, 15-51
       London Road, Staines-upon-Thames, TW18 4EX in accordance with the terms of
       the application, Ref 25/01244/RVC, subject to the conditions in the attached
       schedule.
Preliminary Matters
3.     As set out above, there are two appeals, which relate to different parts of the site
       but in all other respects are identical. I have considered each proposal on its
       individual merits, but to avoid duplication I have dealt with the two appeals
       together.
4.     Planning permission was originally granted respectively under Ref 19/00290/FUL
       and 19/01051/FUL for the developments described in the banner heading, and
       subsequently varied by permissions Ref 23/00273/RVC and 22/00766/RVC to
       amend the wording of Conditions 1 and 2 respectively. For clarity, I have used the
       original description of development for each banner heading and decision.
5.     Planning Practice Guidance (PPG) allows for approved proposals to be modified
       where new issues arise. In this case, the appellant has cited a lack of uptake of
       parking spaces and is proposing to reduce the number of residential parking
       spaces. The Council considers this to be a substantial alteration which requires the
       submission of new planning applications under s70 of the Town and Country
       Planning Act 1990 are required. However, by reference to the judgement in John
       Leslie Finney v Welsh Ministers & Carmarthenshire County Council, Energiekontor
       (Uk) Limited, the proposals can be dealt with under s73 of the Town and Country
       Planning Act 1990 since neither description of development states the number of
       parking spaces, meaning that there would be no variation to the terms of the
       operative parts of the planning permissions.
6.     The Spelthorne Local Plan 2024-2039/40 March 2026 (SLP) was adopted after the
       determination of the two applications, and consequently carries full weight in
       decision-making.

7.     Following the submission of the appeals, the revised National Planning Policy

Main Issue
8.     The planning permissions granted under Ref 23/00273/RVC and 22/00766/RVC
       were subject to a number of conditions, including No 3, which was the same on
       both permissions, and No 20 on Ref 23/00273/RVC and No 21 on Ref
       22/00766/RVC, which shared the same wording.
9.     Condition 3 required parking spaces for motor vehicles and bicycles to be
       constructed and laid out as set out within the Parking Phasing Plan and retained
       for the benefit of the occupiers of the development and for no other purpose. The
       reason given for the condition was to ensure that the proposed development did
       not prejudice the free flow of traffic or the conditions of general safety along
       neighbouring highways, and to reserve cycle parking spaces for the benefit of the
       development.
10. The proposal would vary Condition 3 to read: The parking spaces for motor
    vehicles and bicycles shown on the approved plans shall be constructed and laid
    out and shall be retained for the benefit of the occupiers of the development as
    approved and shall not be used for any other purpose without the express written
    consent of the Local Planning Authority.
11. Condition 20/21 required 86 car parking spaces within the multi storey car park to
    be retained in perpetuity for use by the residential occupiers of the development.
    The reason given for the condition was so that the development did not prejudice
    highway safety or cause inconvenience to other highway users.
12. The proposal would vary Condition 20/21 to read: The 27 car parking spaces
    within the multi storey car park as identified on plan L(LE)003 Rev B shall be
    retained in perpetuity for such use by the residential occupiers of the proposed
    development, unless otherwise agreed by the Local Planning Authority in writing.
13. The main issue is the effect on highway safety, including the free flow of traffic and
    the convenience of other highway users, of varying the conditions to allow a further
    reduction in parking spaces against the Council’s parking standards.
Reasons
Policy position
14. The planning permissions granted in 2019 were for a total of 489 homes and 5
    commercial units across the two sites, and that development is now nearing
    completion. Under the terms of the permissions, 346 car parking spaces were
    provided, 341 for the homes and 5 for the commercial units. The spaces were split
    between a basement car park under the sites containing 255 spaces, and an
    adjacent multi-storey car park with 86 spaces, plus 5 on street car club spaces.
15. The proposal would reallocate 35 residential spaces in the basement car park to
    commercial use, and retain only 27 residential spaces in the multi-storey car park
    which are allocated for affordable units, whilst retaining the 5 car club spaces. In

       total, the number of residential spaces would be reduced to 247 and the number of
       commercial spaces would increase to 40.
16. By retaining 27 spaces for the affordable units, the proposal would accord with the
    terms of the S106 Agreement attached to 19/00290/FUL and the supplemental
    deed attached to both it and 19/01051/FUL. A second deed of variation in respect
    of 22/00765/RVC and 22/00766/RVC inserted a new clause binding any
    subsequent s73 applications to the covenants contained in the original agreement,
    as a consequence of which there is no requirement for a further deed of variation
    to tie the proposals to the original agreement.
17. Both the original planning permissions, and the subsequent applications to vary
    conditions which are the subject of these appeals, were assessed against the
    Spelthorne Borough Core Strategy and Policies Development Plan Document
    2009, and specifically policies CC2 and CC3. That plan has been replaced by the
    SLP, Policy ID2 of which provides guidance on sustainable transport for new
    developments. It requires development proposals to incorporate opportunities to
    facilitate sustainable and active modes of transport, which will be achieved through
    various means including the provision of vehicle parking standards set out in a
    Parking Supplementary Planning Document (SPD).
18. The Council confirmed that, although the Parking Standards Supplementary
    Planning Guidance (SPG) against which the original planning permissions were
    assessed is no longer in force, the SPD referred to in Policy ID2 has yet to be
    adopted. In the interim, the Council is using Surrey County Council’s Vehicular,
    Electric Vehicle and Cycle Parking Guidance for New Developments (PGND). The
    PGND guides that reduced or even nil residential parking may be appropriate in
    support of demand management and the most efficient use of land, which broadly
    aligns with the footnote to Policy ID2 which advises that a reduction in vehicle
    parking standards may be appropriate in locations with good access to public
    transport and local services.
19. Whilst the PGND is not the SPD envisaged by Policy ID2, and it has not been
    formally adopted by the Council, its degree of alignment with Policy ID2 means
    that it is afforded moderate weight as a material consideration. It moreover
    accords with Policy TR2(2) of the Framework, which encourages the setting of
    maximum car parking standards where they will support efforts to encourage the
    use of sustainable transport modes and shared transport.
20. A footnote to Policy ID2 sets out the criteria for assessing a site’s accessibility:
    these include the site’s distance from public transport nodes, the frequency and
    quality of services, the availability and quality of pedestrian and cycle routes, and
    the provision of car clubs for developments of 50 or more residential units. The
    sites are located within walking and cycling distance of the centre of Staines,
    which contains a range of retail and community facilities. They are also within
    walking distance of the train station, which benefits from a regular service to
    London Waterloo and other destinations, and within walking distance of bus stops
    providing services to Slough, Hounslow and Sunbury amongst other destinations.
    Furthermore, the scheme includes a commitment to introducing a car club prior to
    the occupation of the final block.
21. The range of alternative modes of transport to the private car satisfy the criteria
    laid down in the footnote to Policy ID2. This finding is reinforced by the sites’ score

        of between 79 and 82 using the Department for Transport’s Connectivity Tool,
        which derives from its very high public transport connectivity and high walking and
        cycling connectivity, giving it a score well above the median score of 50 and thus
        denoting good accessibility.
Assessment against parking standards
22. The sites were originally assessed against the SPG, which was issued in 2001 and
    then reissued in 2011. The SPG was described in a 2022 appeal decision for the
    Old Telephone Exchange1 as ‘rather elderly’, as it pre-dated both the publication of
    the Framework and the preparation of supplementary planning documents through
    the planning system. The SPG set minimum parking standards for residential
    development, which generated a notional requirement of 646.5 parking spaces,
    but in the event the minimum standard was flexed on the basis that the SPG
    allowed for reduced car parking in town centre locations. 346 parking spaces were
    provided, at a ratio of 0.7 spaces per unit.
23. The PGND sets maximum vehicular parking levels, which in the case of residential
    development vary depending on a site’s location. Whilst the supporting text to
    Policy ID2 only refers to maximum parking standards being used in town centres, it
    does not preclude their use in appropriate locations elsewhere. I find no reason to
    disagree with the Council’s assessment that the sites are in an edge of centre
    location, based on their relative proximity to the centre of Staines, and I consider
    that the use of maximum standards is therefore appropriate.
24. For edge of centre locations, the PGND sets a maximum parking requirement of 1
    space per unit, with 1 additional space for each 3 bed unit where space permits.
    The Council has calculated, and the appellant has not disputed, that the
    development as a whole therefore generates a maximum parking requirement of
    489 spaces, 36 of which are additional spaces to serve the 3 bed units.
25. Both the current provision of 341 residential spaces and the proposal for 247
    spaces are below the maximum level set by the PGND. The PGND advises that
    parking proposed at levels below the maximum standard will not be objected to,
    other than in exceptional circumstances where there are significant implications for
    highway safety. Even so, the Council takes the view that insufficient information
    has been provided to justify reducing parking provision to the extent now
    proposed.
26. According to the appellant’s data, more than 90% of the flats have been sold.
    Around half the flats have been sold as private homes, with a take up of 0.47
    parking spaces per flat. A smaller number of flats have been sold to a rental
    operator, with a take up of 0.13 spaces per flat, whilst the affordable units have
    had a take up of 0.63 spaces per flat. The outliers in terms of take up are the units
    where an operator – the rental operator or affordable housing operator – has been
    the primary driver of demand. The homes which have been sold privately are
    therefore likely to provide the best indicator of parking demand.
27. Under the proposal, the residential parking ratio would reduce from 0.7 to 0.51
    spaces per unit. The proposed ratio is similar to the ratio of 0.54 spaces per unit
    on the recently-approved residential scheme at the Renshaw Industrial Estate2,

28. Census data from 2021 shows that 38.4% of flats in the central Staines area did
    not have a car, highlighting the extent to which occupiers of smaller households
    locally may be utilising alternative modes of travel. Since the pandemic, working
    from home has become more prevalent, reducing the need to travel. The appellant
    has pointed to data which shows that car clubs reduce private car ownership, with
    each car club vehicle taking between 14 and 32 cars off the road. The
    development includes a car club to be operated by Enterprise Car Club, initially
    with one car but increasing to two once utilisation of the first car reaches 30%.
    First occupiers of the flats are given one’s year free membership.
29. In accordance with Policy ID2, the development incorporates measures to reduce
    reliance on the private car, including a travel plan which, amongst other things,
    contains modal split targets and a commitment to encouraging car sharing.
    Although the Council has raised a concern that the travel plan has not been fully
    implemented to date, it is in place and capable of being enforced. The
    development also incorporates secure cycle racks in the basement car park which
    can accommodate 494 bicycles, at a ratio of one space per flat.
30. The Council has queried whether the occupancy rate of the development is indeed
    90%, given the rate of construction to date and the number of consultation letters it
    sent out which were returned as undeliverable. It is likely that occupancy has
    lagged behind sales, particularly in the case of the rental flats, but the appellant
    has confirmed that since parking spaces are allocated at the point of sale, the
    demand for parking spaces would not alter once occupancy rates had caught up
    with sales.
31. There is no dispute between the main parties about the price at which parking
    spaces have been offered for sale, and it has not been put to me that the price
    was artificially high or has dampened the demand for parking spaces. The
    appellant has explained that it would not be in their interests to discourage
    occupiers from purchasing available parking spaces by inflating the price.
Highway safety
32. Roads in the immediate vicinity of the sites incorporate restricted parking zones,
    which have not changed since the planning permissions were granted, having
    most recently been reviewed in 2024 when no requirement was identified for
    additional parking restrictions. In London Road and the first section of Fairfield
    Avenue, no waiting or loading is allowed at any time, whilst in the remainder of
    Fairfield Avenue waiting is allowed at specific times. Adjoining streets and those
    on the opposite side of London Road have a mixture of parking restrictions. The
    resultant scarcity of available on street parking within easy walking distance of the
    sites would act to discourage residents from parking on nearby roads.
33. Interested parties have pointed to indiscriminate parking on Fairfield Avenue. It is
    unclear whether such parking is occurring in the section of Fairfield Avenue where
    waiting is allowed at certain times, in which case parking outside the restricted
    times would not be in breach of parking restrictions. If indiscriminate parking is


https://www.gov.uk/planning-inspectorate   6
Appeal Decisions 6007619 and 6007620


       occurring where waiting is not allowed, it is capable of being controlled through
       appropriate enforcement action.
34. The effect of parked cars on the two-lane section of Fairfield Avenue is to reduce it
    to a single lane, which is said to result in regular congestion and disputes between
    drivers. I have no reason to doubt the evidence put forward, but congestion and
    nuisance, whilst potentially disruptive to adjacent residents, do not in themselves
    mean that highway safety has been comprised. Similarly, indiscriminate parking
    within private parking areas attached to nearby developments such as Linden
    Place, whilst causing a nuisance, would not directly impact on highway safety.
35. The PGND advises that Surrey County Council (SCC) as highway authority will
    only raise objections in relation to parking if there is a shortfall that would lead to
    danger on the adjoining highway. SCC has confirmed in its statement of common
    ground with the appellant that the proposed reduction in car parking spaces does
    not raise any issues of highway safety. Were overflow parking to occur in future,
    there is no compelling evidence to indicate that it would impede the free flow of
    traffic or inconvenience other highway users to the extent that highway safety
    would be impaired. In the event that a requirement for further parking restrictions
    to limit overflow parking arose in the future, the appellant’s previous contributions
    towards the management of parking remain unspent.
Benefits
36. The appellant has stated that commercial units within the development remain
    unoccupied, which their commercial agents believe is partly the result of a lack of
    dedicated parking in comparison with competing office developments nearby. In
    these circumstances, it is a pragmatic response to reallocate residential spaces for
    commercial use, which could potentially create jobs and strengthen the local
    economy. There is no inherent contradiction between identifying a latent demand
    for commercial spaces, and the lower-than-expected demand which has arisen for
    residential spaces.
37. The appellant has indicated that the remaining surplus residential spaces could
    provide parking for future developments within the site, but as there is no
    mechanism to link the loss of the spaces to future development, such a benefit
    would not necessarily occur. I therefore cannot give weight to such a potential
    outcome.
Findings
38. The evidence demonstrates that, in accordance with Policy ID2 of the SLP and
    Policy TR2(2) of the Framework, the development has successfully incorporated
    opportunities to facilitate sustainable and active modes of travel. Partly as a result
    of encouraging the use of alternative modes of transport, the take up of residential
    parking spaces has been lower than anticipated. A reduction in residential parking
    provision to the level sought by the appellant, below the maximum provision
    required by the PGND, is therefore justified.
39. Policy TR6(4) of the Framework guides that development proposals should only be
    refused if they would have an unacceptable impact on highway safety, taking into
    account any mitigation measures proposed, including measures to support
    sustainable patterns of movement. The evidence before me does not indicate that

       the proposal would have an unacceptable impact on highway safety, and therefore
       a refusal is not justified.
40. I conclude that varying the conditions to allow a further reduction in parking spaces
    against the Council’s parking standards would not impede the free flow of traffic or
    inconvenience other highway users to the extent that highway safety would be
    adversely affected. The proposal would accord with Policy ID2 of the SLP, the
    aims of which I have outlined above.
Other Matters
41. Interested parties have raised a concern about the loss of allocated parking
    spaces within the development. Spaces which have already been allocated are not
    included in the proposal, meaning that those residents with parking spaces would
    not be affected.
Conditions
42. The PPG makes clear that decision notices for the grant of planning permission
    under section 73 should restate the conditions imposed on earlier permissions that
    continue to have effect. The Council has provided a list of suggested conditions for
    both appeals which reflect those imposed on the previous planning permissions. I
    have reviewed these conditions and agree that they are still relevant. I have
    included previous conditions which the Council suggested could be omitted, so
    that control is retained over these matters.
43. As the development has been implemented, it is unnecessary for me to attach the
    commencement of development condition. I have amended conditions 3 and 20/21
    to reflect the reduced residential parking provision and to set a limit on the
    provision of commercial parking. Where appropriate I have revised the pre-
    commencement conditions to reflect details which have subsequently been
    approved.
Conclusion
44. As I have concluded that the conditions can be varied without harm to highway
    safety, the appeals are allowed.
