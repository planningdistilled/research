•      The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
       against a refusal to grant planning permission.
•      The appeal is made by Benedict Boker-Ingram against the decision of Horsham District Council.
•      The application Ref is DC/25/0657.
•      The development proposed is Construction of a detached coach house containing two residential
       units in the grounds of the former public house The Lamb Inn.

Decision
1. The appeal is allowed and planning permission is granted for Construction of a
   detached coach house containing two residential units in the grounds of the former
   public house The Lamb Inn at The Lamb Inn, Lambs Green, Rusper, West Sussex
   RH12 4RG in accordance with the terms of the application, Ref DC/25/0657,
   subject to the conditions in the attached schedule.
Preliminary Matters
2. Since the appeal was lodged, a revised version of the National Planning Policy
   Framework (the Framework) was published (17 August 2026). Comments have
   been sought from the parties on any implications for this case, and I have had
   regard to those comments in the determination of this appeal.
3. Planning permission has been granted for the conversion of, and extensions to the
   existing public house to 6 residential flats1. At the time of my site visit, the
   development had been implemented and construction was underway. Copies of the
   approved plans and decision notice have been provided with the appeal, and I
   have had regard to these in my determination.
4. The appeal scheme is accompanied by a drainage strategy prepared by BP Civils
   dated February 2026 (the drainage strategy). This introduces additional technical
   information which seeks to directly respond to the Council’s third reason for refusal.
5. As the appeal timetable allowed the main and interested parties to comment, I am
   satisfied that accepting the drainage report would not cause procedural unfairness.
   I have therefore taken the drainage report and the representations received into
   account in reaching my decision.
Main Issues
6. The main issues for this appeal are:
             •   whether the appeal site is suitable for new residential development, having
                 regard to the spatial strategy and proximity to services.

Reasons
Suitability for new residential development
7. The appeal site lies outside any defined built-up area boundary and Lambs Green
   is not identified within the settlement hierarchy in Policy 3 Horsham District
   Planning Framework 2015 (HDPF). The proposal would therefore amount to new
   residential development in the countryside. It would not comprise development
   within a settlement identified for growth, nor would it meet the circumstances for
   settlement expansion anticipated by policy (4) of the HDPF. Policy 26 also seeks to
   protect the countryside from inappropriate development. On that basis, the
   proposal conflicts with the locational strategy of the development plan.
8. The Council’s Advice Note recognises the current housing land supply position and
   indicates that some unallocated sites outside built-up area boundaries may be
   considered positively. However, paragraph 5.12 is directed towards sites adjoining
   defined built-up area boundaries. The appeal site does not do so, and Lambs
   Green is not a classified settlement. The proposal therefore does not fall within the
   category of development to which that guidance gives particular support.
9. I recognise that the appeal site is previously developed land, that the proposal is
   modest in scale, and that it would be physically associated with an existing cluster
   of development. It would also be seen in the context of the former public house,
   nearby commercial buildings and existing residential development. These matters
   reduce the extent of harm and mean that the proposal would not be isolated.
   However, they do not bring the scheme into compliance with policies (3), (4) and
   (26) of the HDPF.
10. The earlier permission for residential conversion of the former public house is also
    a relevant consideration. However, that scheme involved the reuse of an existing
    building and was justified in part by heritage-related benefits. The appeal proposal
    would introduce a new, free-standing building and is therefore materially different.
11. Policy S5 of the Framework provides support for the redevelopment of previously
    developed land outside settlements, unless the benefits would be substantially
    outweighed by adverse effects when assessed against the Framework as a whole.
    The proposal would redevelop an existing area of hardstanding and therefore
    attracts support under that policy. However, this support sits alongside, rather than
    displaces, the conflict with the development plan.
12. The site’s accessibility is limited. Although a railway station lies around 2 miles
    away, Lambs Green itself appears to contain few, if any, day-to-day services. The
    local highway environment lacks footways and street lighting, and the public right of
    way alongside the site is narrow, uneven in places and unlit. Future occupiers
    would therefore be likely to rely heavily on private cars for everyday needs. This
    reinforces the conflict with the locational strategy of the development plan.
13. I therefore conclude that the site is not a particularly sustainable location for
    additional housing when assessed against the development plan, and the proposal

Character and appearance
14. The Council's concern is that the proposal would introduce additional built form
    unrelated to the original justification for the redevelopment of the site and would
    result in the over-intensification of development, extending built form further west
    into a more rural setting. I acknowledge that the proposal would increase the
    density of development on the site beyond that previously approved and would
    result in a limited loss of openness within the former public house grounds.
    Nevertheless, the spacing between the appeal building and surrounding
    development would remain appropriate, and the proposed coach house would
    appear as a subservient building that relates well in scale, form and appearance to
    the former public house, which is currently being converted to residential use.
15. Whilst the appeal building would extend built form further west, I observed that it
    would be surrounded on three sides by existing residential and commercial
    development. In these circumstances, I do not find that it would appear as an
    isolated encroachment into the countryside. Rather, the building would visually read
    as a logical infilling of the space between existing buildings within the developed
    site. The proposal would therefore appear contained within the existing pattern of
    development and would not materially alter the settlement pattern of Lambs Green.
16. I recognise that, unlike the earlier redevelopment scheme, the proposal would not
    deliver any specific heritage benefits associated with securing the future of the
    former public house. However, I am satisfied that the appeal proposal is acceptable
    on its own merits in terms of its effect on character and appearance. The appeal
    site comprises previously developed land in the form of part of the former public
    house car park, and I find that the proposal would not result in wider harm to the
    intrinsic character, beauty or rural qualities of the surrounding countryside.
17. Taking all of the above into account, I conclude that the proposal would preserve
    the character and appearance of the site and its surroundings. Accordingly, I find
    no conflict with policies (25), (32) and (33) of the HDPF insofar as they seek to
    protect local character, landscape quality and the intrinsic beauty of the
    countryside.
Drainage
18. The Council’s concern relates primarily to the absence of sufficient information to
    demonstrate that a suitable surface water and foul drainage scheme could be
    achieved.
19. I note that the earlier conversion scheme2 (DC/24/0874) was approved subject to a
    pre-commencement condition requiring the submission of a drainage strategy.
    However, the circumstances before me are not directly comparable. The appeal
    proposal involves a new building in a different part of the site and the Council's
    concerns relate specifically to the proposed building plot, which it identifies as being

        at risk of surface water flooding. Moreover, unlike the Council when determining the
        application, I have the benefit of detailed drainage and flood risk information. My
        conclusions, including whether any outstanding matters could appropriately be
        secured by condition, are therefore informed by that evidence.
20. The appeal is accompanied by a detailed drainage strategy, together with
    supporting calculations and drawings. The strategy identifies the site as lying within
    Flood Zone 1 and concludes that the proposed development would remain safe
    from flooding, with finished floor levels set above identified flood levels. It further
    indicates that the drainage system would accommodate the 1 in 100-year rainfall
    event, including a 40% allowance for climate change, and that the development
    would not increase flood risk on or off the site. I note that the Council has not
    commented on the drainage strategy within its appeal submissions.
21. Surface water would be managed through permeable paving with underground
    storage and a restricted discharge to the adjacent watercourse. The proposed
    discharge rate of 2.8 l/s would be significantly lower than the existing site runoff
    rate of 17.7 l/s, thereby reducing the rate of runoff leaving the site. Whilst this would
    not achieve the calculated greenfield runoff rate, the drainage strategy explains that
    a lower restriction was not considered practical due to the risk of blockage.
22. Whilst achieving greenfield runoff rates is desirable, the Council's drainage advice
    recognises that this will not always be possible. In such circumstances, flows
    should be reduced as close as reasonably practicable to greenfield rates and
    should achieve at least a 50% improvement over the existing brownfield situation.
23. Policy 38 of the HDPF and the Framework do not require greenfield runoff rates in
    all circumstances. Rather, the focus is on managing surface water in accordance
    with the drainage hierarchy, providing a sustainable drainage solution and ensuring
    that flood risk is not increased elsewhere. On the basis of the information before
    me, the drainage strategy appears to address those requirements.
Other Matters
24. I have considered an appeal decision relating to The Royal Oak, Friday Street,
    Rusper3, which similarly concerned development associated with a former public
    house in a rural location. Whilst the Inspector in that case found the location to be
    unsustainable due to its reliance on private car travel, the decision also identified
    less than substantial harm to a designated heritage asset, such that Footnote 7 of
    the then Framework was engaged.
25. The appeal before me falls to be considered under a different national policy
    context and I have not found comparable harm to character, appearance or
    heritage interests. Furthermore, the proposal involves the redevelopment of
    previously developed land, which now attracts support under Policy S5 of the
    Framework. For those reasons, I do not consider the Royal Oak decision to be
    determinative and have assessed the present appeal on its own merits.

    development acceptable, and those matters are addressed through other

Planning Balance and conclusion
33. The parties both recognise that there is a severe housing shortfall within the district.
    The Council's Shaping Development in Horsham District Planning Advice Note
    2025 records that Horsham can demonstrate only around 1.7 years of housing land
    supply and applies a 20% buffer due to its Housing Delivery Test performance.
    Consequently, there is a significant unmet need for housing within the district.
34. The proposal would provide two additional dwellings. Whilst modest in scale, I have
    regard to the extent of the district's housing need and to Policy HO7 of the
    Framework, which requires substantial weight to be given to the provision of homes
    which contribute towards evidenced accommodation needs. I therefore attach
    substantial weight to this benefit.
35. I have also found that the proposal would redevelop previously developed land. As
    such, it falls within Policy S5(d) of the Framework, which identifies the
    redevelopment of previously developed land as a form of development that should
    be approved outside settlements unless the benefits of doing so would be
    substantially outweighed by any adverse effects when assessed against the
    national decision-making policies of the Framework. This weighs significantly in
    favour of the proposal.

36. Against this, I have found that the site has limited accessibility and that future
    occupiers would be likely to rely heavily on private cars for most day-to-day needs.
    As a result, the proposal conflicts with the locational objectives of policies (3), (4)
    and (26) of the HDPF. That conflict attracts weight against the proposal. However,
    those policies were prepared in a different policy context and, insofar as they
    restrict housing development outside settlement boundaries, I find that they attract
    reduced weight in the circumstances of this appeal, having regard to the approach
    now taken by the Framework towards the redevelopment of previously developed
    land outside settlements.
37. Taking these matters together, I find that the proposal conflicts with the
    development plan and therefore does not accord with it. However, substantial
    weight should be given to the provision of additional housing and significant weight
    to the redevelopment of previously developed land under policy S5(d).
38. Having regard to the Framework as a whole, I find that the limited adverse effects
    arising from the proposal do not substantially outweigh its benefits. Consequently,
    whilst the proposal conflicts with the development plan, there are material
    considerations of considerable importance, particularly the provision of housing and
    the redevelopment of previously developed land, which indicate that a decision
    should be made otherwise than in accordance with it.
39. Accordingly, the appeal is allowed and planning permission is granted, subject to
    the conditions set out below.
