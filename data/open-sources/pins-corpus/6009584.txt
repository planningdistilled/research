Appeal Ref: 6009584
42 Fountayne Road, Hunmanby, North Yorkshire YO14 0LU
•    The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
     against a refusal to grant planning permission.
•    The appeal is made by Mr M Jackson against the decision of North Yorkshire Council.
•    The application reference is 26/00747/FUL.
•    The development proposed is ‘two-storey side extension’.

Decision
1. The appeal is dismissed.
Applications for Costs
2. An application for cost was made by Mr M Jackson against the North Yorkshire
   Council. This is the subject of a separate decision.
Preliminary Matter
3. Since the appeal was lodged, the National Planning Policy Framework (the
   Framework) has been updated. I have determined the appeal on the basis of the
   current Framework. The parties, through the evidence submitted with the appeal,
   have had the opportunity to address the planning merits of the proposal. I am
   satisfied that the changes to the Framework do not alter the substance of the main
   issue in this appeal or disadvantage either party.
Main Issue
4.     The main issue is the effect of the proposed development on the character and
       appearance of the surrounding area.
Reasons
5. The appeal property occupies a corner plot at the junction of Fountayne Road and
   Constable Road. The surrounding area is characterised by semi-detached
   bungalows of similar scale and proportions set within modest plots. Along the same
   side of Fountayne Road as the appeal property, the dwellings are dormer
   bungalows, whereas those on the opposite side generally do not incorporate
   dormers. Nevertheless, the scale, proportions and spacing between pairs of
   dwellings results in a coherent and consistent rhythm and pattern of development
   within the street scene.
6. Although a conservatory currently occupies part of the side garden to the appeal
   property, the openness retained around the junction makes a positive contribution
   to the character of the area. In particular, the gap between the built form of the
   appeal property and Constable Road mirrors that found adjacent to 40 Fountayne

     Road on the opposite side of the junction, reinforcing the spacious character of this

7. The proposed extension would substantially increase the overall length and mass
   of the building. In doing so, it would markedly reduce the existing gap alongside
   Constable Road and would result in a dwelling that appears appreciably larger than
   others in the immediate vicinity. As a consequence, the building would appear
   visually dominant within its plot and would erode the harmonious pattern of
   development and an important component of the spacing and openness that
   presently characterises this part of the street scene.
8. Furthermore, the resulting building would appear disproportionately elongated
   when viewed from both Fountayne Road and Constable Road. Rather than
   appearing as one of a group of similarly proportioned dwellings, it would present as
   a noticeably larger and more dominant building that sits uncomfortably within its
   surroundings. In this respect, the proposal would appear incongruous and would
   disrupt the balanced relationship that currently exists between the appeal property,
   adjoining dwellings and the wider street scene.
9. The use of matching materials and continuation of the existing roof form would
   assist in integrating the proposed extension with the existing dwelling. However,
   these matters would not overcome the visual effects arising from the substantially
   increased scale of development. The harm that I have identified arises principally
   from the proposal's effect on the proportions of the building and the erosion of
   openness around the corner plot rather than the detailing of the extension itself.
10. I have also been referred to the Scarborough Borough Council Residential Design
    Guide Supplementary Planning Document (August 2021). While that document
    indicates that side extensions should typically be set back from the principal
    elevation and below the existing ridge line, I do not regard the absence of those
    features as determinative in itself. Rather, my concerns arise from the effect of the
    completed development on the character and appearance of the area. Even
    applying the SPD flexibly, the scale and disposition of the proposed extension
    would remain harmful for the reasons given above.
11. Similarly, whilst the surrounding area exhibits some variation in detailed design, I
    saw little evidence during my site visit of extensions or alterations that materially
    depart from the prevailing pattern of development. Moreover, I have been provided
    with no substantive examples of development comparable in scale or effect to that
    now proposed. Consequently, this matter does not alter my conclusions.
12. Drawing these matters together, I conclude that the proposed development would
    have a harmful effect on the character and appearance of the surrounding area. It
    would therefore conflict with Policy DEC1 of the Scarborough Borough Local Plan
    (July 2017), which seeks, among other things, to ensure that development reflects
    the local environment and responds positively to the local context.
Other Matters
13. There is no dispute between the parties that the proposed development would not
    result in harm to the living conditions of occupants of neighbouring properties, and I
    have no reason to find otherwise. However, the absence of harm to residential
    amenity is not a benefit of the scheme. Rather, it is simply a characteristic of

14. I have also considered the concern raised regarding the extent of engagement
    undertaken during the determination of the application. However, my role is to
    determine the appeal on the basis of the proposal before me and the evidence
    submitted by the parties. Consequently, irrespective of the process that led to the
    decision, that matter does not affect my assessment of the planning merits of the
    proposal.
15. The proposal would provide additional living accommodation for the occupants of
    the dwelling. While I have taken that matter into account, it would predominantly
    amount to a private benefit for the appellant and does not lead me to a different
    conclusion regarding the planning merits of the proposal.
Conclusion
16. The proposal conflicts with the development plan and the material considerations
    do not indicate that the appeal should be decided other than in accordance with it. I
    therefore conclude that the appeal should be dismissed.
