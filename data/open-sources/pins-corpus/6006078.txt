Appeal Decision
Site visit made on 19 August 2026
by Lewis Condé BSc, MSc, MRTPI
an Inspector appointed by the Secretary of State
Decision date: 22 September 2026


Appeal Ref: 6006078
Land South West Of Poachers Pocket, St Columb, TR9 6JA
•    The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
     against a refusal to grant planning permission.
•    The appeal is made by Tim Barritt against the decision of Cornwall Council.
•    The application Ref is PA25/01031.
•    The development proposed is an agricultural barn for the storage of arable crops, supplements and
     nutrients for restoration of the land.

Decision
1.     The appeal is dismissed.
Preliminary Matters
2.     A new National Planning Policy Framework (the Framework) was published on
       17 August 2026. Main parties have been provided with an appropriate opportunity
       to comment on its implications, and where received comments have been
       considered in my determination of the appeal.
Main Issues
3.     The main issues are: i) the effect of the proposal on the character and appearance
       of the area; ii) whether an overriding locational or business need has been
       demonstrated for the proposal.
Reasons
       Character and Appearance
4.     The appeal site forms part of a sizeable agricultural field within the countryside. It
       is bounded by Cornish hedge banks and vegetation but is experienced within a
       wider gently undulating rural landscape where agriculture is the dominant use. The
       background evidence places the site within Cornwall Character Area CCA25,
       which is characterised by wide rolling pastoral and arable land, Cornish hedges,
       dispersed settlement and a generally uncluttered, quiet rural character. The
       undeveloped field contributes positively to those qualities.
5.     There are other agricultural and commercial buildings in the wider locality,
       including some visible from the nearby highway network. However, they are not
       within the appeal field and do not form a strong visual or functional grouping with
       the proposal. Accordingly, the presence of other rural buildings nearby does not, in
       itself, make the proposed siting of the development acceptable.

6.     Despite the proposed building having an inherently rural design and appearance, it
       would extend built form away from the appellant’s existing group of farm buildings.
       The barn and hardstanding would increase the spread and prevalence of
       development in the rural landscape and would appear as an unsympathetic
       addition, diminishing the openness of the field and its contribution to the area’s
       landscape character.
7.     Although public viewpoints would be limited, that does not mean the development
       would be without harm. The proposed bund would screen some views, but would
       also introduce an artificial landform. Existing and proposed vegetation would
       provide some containment, but landscaping would soften rather than remove the
       effect of the building and hardstanding within the field.
8.     Consequently, while the harm would be localised, the proposal would have a
       clearly adverse effect on the character and appearance of the area. It would
       increase the spread of built form within a rural field and fail to respond positively to
       its landscape context. The proposal would therefore conflict with Policies 1, 2, 12
       and 23 of the Cornwall Local Plan Strategic Policies 2010–2030 and Policy C1.7 of
       the Climate Emergency Development Plan Document. Taken together, these
       policies seek to ensure that development is appropriately located and designed,
       responds positively to its context, and safeguards local character, landscape
       character and distinctiveness.
       Overriding Locational or Business Need
9.     Local Plan Policy 5 concerns the supply of appropriate business space and
       proposals for new employment land. In the countryside and smaller rural
       settlements, it requires development to be of a scale appropriate to its location, or
       to demonstrate an overriding locational and business need for that location. The
       supporting text identifies agriculture as a key sector within Cornwall and states that
       its continued growth will be critical to Cornwall’s long-term prosperity. It also
       emphasises support for indigenous businesses, including agriculture.
10. Although Policy 5 was not cited on the Council’s decision notice, it has been raised
    by the Council during the appeal. I find it relevant to the appeal proposal, and the
    parties have had an opportunity to comment on the development plan and national
    policy context.
11. Policy 5 of the Cornwall Local Plan supports business development in rural areas
    where, amongst other matters, an overriding locational and business need is
    demonstrated. Whilst the August 2026 National Planning Policy Framework adopts
    a positive approach towards supporting rural economic growth and the
    diversification of agricultural enterprises, it also seeks to ensure that development
    is sustainable and respects the character of the countryside. In the circumstances
    of this appeal, the objectives of Policy 5 are substantially aligned with those aims.
    Consequently, I find the policy to be broadly consistent with national policy and I
    afford it significant weight. The key issue is therefore whether the evidence
    demonstrates a genuine operational need for the proposed building in this
    particular location.
12. The proposal would provide storage for arable crops, supplements and nutrients
    associated with the restoration and use of agricultural land. There may be practical
    and financial advantages in bulk purchasing supplements and nutrients, creating
    some storage requirement. However, the likely extent of that need has not been

       clearly explained. The field has previously produced approximately 250 bales of
       silage in a year, but the likely yield of arable crops from the restored land has not
       been suitably quantified.
13. The appellant’s evidence is also not sufficiently detailed about the nature and
    extent of existing or proposed agricultural operations, including the location of
    other land farmed by the appellant, the level of storage space required and the use
    and capacity of existing farm buildings. Nor is there robust evidence to show that
    the existing buildings and yard could not be used more efficiently, or appropriately
    expanded, even taking account of any nearby utilities or services infrastructure.
    The onus is on the appellant to justify the appeal proposal.
14. The existing farm buildings are located alongside a public bridleway, creating
    some potential for conflict between farm vehicles, operations and users of the
    bridleway. However, such conflicts are likely to continue because the proposal
    would not replace the existing buildings. Based on the evidence before me, and
    my observations on site, I am also not persuaded that the nature or extent of the
    conflicts, including those involving the appellant’s own vehicles and operations,
    could not be suitably managed or that they justify the proposed development.
15. The proposal may reduce some vehicle movements between the existing farm
    buildings and the appeal site. However, given the relatively short distance involved
    and the generally quiet nature of the adjacent highway, this benefit would be very
    modest.
16. Whilst the proposal is likely to require significant financial investment, this does not
    itself demonstrate a need for the proposed building. The delegated officer report’s
    finding that the existing three barns were sufficient for a smaller holding does not,
    of itself, justify a further building for a larger holding without clear evidence of the
    operational requirements. Such evidence has not been suitably provided.
17. Accordingly, neither an overriding locational need nor an overriding business need
    for the proposed development has been demonstrated. The appeal scheme
    therefore conflicts with Local Plan Policy 5.
Other Matters
18. Planning permission has recently been granted for a sizeable storage building
    opposite the appeal site. I do not have the full background details of that
    permission, including the full application submissions or any detailed justification.
    However, on the evidence before me, that building is materially different in scale,
    context and purpose from the appeal proposal, and was approved as ancillary to
    an existing dwelling. It is therefore not directly comparable.
19. The appellant indicates concerns with the Council’s approach to determining the
    application, including in respect of its site visit procedure and the level of
    justification required compared to other developments. Nonetheless, the appeal
    has been determined on its own merits.
Planning Balance and Conclusion
20. The proposal would provide additional agricultural storage for arable crops,
    supplements and nutrients associated with the restoration and use of agricultural
    land. It would support the country’s food production, whilst it may also offer some
    operational efficiencies, including potential reductions in vehicle movements

       between the appeal site and the appellant’s existing buildings. There are also likely
       to be economic benefits associated with the construction of the building. However,
       these benefits have not been clearly quantified and, given the scale and nature of
       the proposal, attract only modest weight.
21. Set against those benefits, the proposal would cause localised but clearly adverse
    harm to the character and appearance of the area. Neither an overriding locational
    need nor an overriding business need for the building in this countryside location
    has been demonstrated. Consequently, the appeal scheme conflicts with the
    development plan, including the policies identified above which seek to safeguard
    local character and ensure that rural business development is appropriately
    justified in its location.
22. I have considered whether the revised Framework indicates that the appeal should
    be determined otherwise than in accordance with the development plan. The
    Framework supports certain forms of development outside settlements, including
    where they are appropriate to a rural area, but that support is subject to the
    benefits of doing so not being substantially outweighed by adverse effects when
    assessed against the national decision-making policies in the Framework.
23. In this case, the adverse effects include the identified localised but clearly adverse
    harm to the character and appearance of the area and the absence of a
    demonstrated overriding locational and business need for the proposed building.
    Although the proposal would deliver some agricultural and operational benefits,
    those benefits are modest and are substantially outweighed by the identified
    adverse effects. As such, when assessed against the national decision-making
    policies in the Framework, the proposal does not benefit from support sufficient to
    justify granting permission.
24. For the reasons outlined above, the appeal scheme conflicts with the development
    plan and there are no material considerations, including the provisions of the
    Framework, that justify a decision other than in accordance with it. The appeal is
    therefore dismissed.
