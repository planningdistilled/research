•   The development proposed is Two storey rear extension (Part retrospective).

Decision
1. The appeal is dismissed.
Appeal Procedure
2. The site visit was undertaken by a representative of the Inspector whose
   recommendation is set out below and to which the Inspector has had regard before
   deciding the appeal.
Preliminary Matters
3. During my site visit, I observed that the rear extension has already been
   constructed. The appeal therefore relates in part to development that has been
   carried out and is consequently part retrospective in nature. I also noted that the
   development as built differs from both the as-built and the proposed plans before
   me.
4. In particular, the as-built drawings omit a rooflight that has been installed on the
   single-storey rear projection. In addition, on the as-built plans the ground floor rear
   elevation includes double glazed doors on the right-hand side, which were not
   present at the time of the site visit. The proposed scheme also introduces a
   different roof form from that which currently exists on site.
5. Therefore, in the interests of clarity and fairness, I have made my assessment
   based on the submitted plans, as that it what has been applied for. The description
   taken from the council’s decision notice reflects this.
Main Issue
6. The main issue is the effect of the proposal on the character and appearance of the
   appeal property and surrounding area.
Reasons for the Recommendation
7. The appeal site is located within a predominantly residential area characterised by
   detached properties of a similar scale and form. The houses are generally set back
   from the highway behind front gardens, many of which have been adapted for

     off-street parking. Mature trees line the street, contributing positively to its suburban
     character.
8. The surrounding dwellings display a coherent architectural rhythm. Most are
   finished in a mixture of red brick and white render, with red tiled features and
   elements of mock-Tudor detailing, creating a consistent visual identity along the
   street. The host property reflects some aspects of this character, while being
   finished in white render with slate roof tiles.
9. There is a clear and established pattern of development in the locality, including the
   presence of front and side dormers as well as rear extensions of varying forms.
   These additions form part of the evolving character of the area and contribute to the
   mixed roofscape visible from both the street and neighbouring plots.
10. Due to the size and scale of the first floor addition, extending across almost the full
    width of the host dwelling, it would appear as a dominant feature. When viewed
    alongside the existing rear dormer, the cumulative massing would result in a
    visually bulky and incongruous development. As a consequence, the extension fails
    to read as a subservient addition to the original dwelling and instead appears
    incongruous in both scale and proportion.
11. The proposed roof design compounds these concerns. Its relationship with the
    existing rear dormer would appear awkward, giving an incongruous and disjointed
    appearance with poor integration between the dwelling and the proposed
    development.
12. Overall, the combined width, massing, and roof configuration of the proposed
    development would materially harm the appearance of the dwelling and its rear
    elevation. The adverse effects identified under the main issue substantially
    outweigh any benefits derived from the proposed development. Therefore, the
    proposed development conflicts with Policies CDH01 and CDH05 of the Barnet
    Local Plan 2021 – 2036 (adopted 2025), Policy D3 of the London Plan (March
    2021) and the Barnet Residential Design Guidance SPD (2016). These policies,
    amongst other things, collectively seek to ensure new development compliments
    the existing building, remains subordinate and is high quality design.
Other Matters
13. The appellant has referred to an approved first-floor extension at a neighbouring
    property (No. 42) which would extend to the shared boundary. However, I have
    been provided with no further details of that scheme, its context, or the
    considerations that informed its approval. In the absence of this information, I am
    only able to afford this limited weight in my assessment.
14. The appellant has also highlighted a scheme within the area that had similar
    aspects (No. 55). I do not have the full details of the circumstances that led to these
    developments being approved and cannot therefore be certain that they represent
    a direct parallel to the appeal proposal. Consequently, I have assigned limited
    weight to this particular scheme.
