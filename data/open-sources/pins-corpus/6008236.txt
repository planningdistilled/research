Appeal Decision
Site visit made on 5 August 2026
by J Buxton BA(Hons) DipArch ARB RIBA
an Inspector appointed by the Secretary of State
Decision date: 26 August 2026
Appeal Ref: 6008236
1 Goods Yard Street, Manchester M3 3BG
•   The appeal is made under Regulation 17 of the Town and Country Planning (Control of
    Advertisements) (England) Regulations 2007 (as amended) against a refusal to grant express
    consent.
•   The appeal is made by Permanently Unique against the decision of Manchester City Council.
•   The application reference is 145010/AO/2026.
•   The advertisement proposed is proposed single fascia sign and projecting signs x 2.


Decision
1. The appeal is dismissed.
Preliminary Matters
2. The Government published a revised National Planning Policy Framework (the
   Framework) on 17 August 2026. It does not change the planning policy context in
   respect of the main issues, and I have determined the appeal accordingly.
3. Regulation 3 of the Town and Country Planning (Control of Advertisements)
   (England) Regulations 2007 (as amended) requires that local planning authorities
   control the display of advertisements in the interests of amenity and public safety,
   taking into account the provisions of the development plan, in so far as they are
   material, and any other relevant factors.
4. The Council’s reason for refusal relates specifically to matters of amenity. There
   was no objection raised on the grounds of public safety, and I find no reason to take
   a different view on this matter.
Main Issue
5. The main issue is the effect of the proposed advertisements on the visual amenity of
   the host building and the surrounding area.
Reasons
6. The appeal site forms part of Manchester Goods Yard; a recently completed mixed-
   use development delivered as part of the wider Enterprise City regeneration project
   at a key gateway location to Manchester City Centre. The surrounding area
   comprises a mix of commercial, leisure, hospitality, office, residential and cultural
   uses within a contemporary urban setting. The appeal premises occupy a ground
   floor commercial unit within a multi-storey building, with office accommodation at the
   upper levels. The building exhibits a coherent architectural design, characterised by
   high-quality materials and carefully considered elevations. The site lies within the
   Castlefield Conservation Area (CCA). An approved signage strategy has been
   established for the wider Enterprise City development which seeks to maintain the

     architectural quality and cohesive appearance of the buildings through a restrained
     and coordinated approach to signage and wayfinding.
7. The proposed advertisements comprise an internally illuminated fascia sign
   positioned on the corner feature of the building together with two internally
   illuminated projecting signs. At my site visit it was apparent that the corner feature
   canopy forms a prominent architectural feature of the host building, contributing to
   its modern design and appearance within the wider development. It is also a
   recurring feature which is repeated at other corners on the development, helping to
   establish a consistent architectural language across the site. As such, the feature
   plays an important role in the overall appearance of the building and the wider
   development.
8. The proposed fascia sign would be positioned on this architectural feature and, by
   reason of its siting, scale and illumination, would appear unduly prominent within the
   street scene. Rather than complementing the architectural composition of the host
   building, it would draw attention away from the design of the corner canopy feature
   and diminish the clarity of the building's architectural form. Furthermore, the
   proposed projecting signs, whilst acceptable in principle, would appear overly bulky
   and conspicuous as a consequence of their depth and method of construction. The
   proposed materials would not reflect the high quality detailing of the host building
   and would appear as visually intrusive additions to the façade. As a result, they
   would not reflect the restrained and coordinated approach to signage promoted by
   the approved signage strategy and would appear at odds with the building’s design.
   Taken together, the proposed advertisements would fail to integrate successfully
   with the host building and would detract from its appearance and that of the wider
   development, causing harm to visual amenity.
9. As part of my assessment, I have considered the Manchester Shopfront and
   Signage Design Guide (2024). Whilst the document is supplementary guidance
   rather than development plan policy, it provides a relevant framework for assessing
   the impact of advertisements on visual amenity. Amongst other things, the guidance
   seeks to ensure that signage responds appropriately to the architectural character of
   its host building and avoids overly prominent, bulky or visually intrusive forms of
   advertisement. In this case, I find that the proposed fascia sign and projecting signs
   would not accord with the principles of the guidance.
10. In reaching my decision, I have had regard to the appeal site's location within the
    CCA and to the statutory duty under Section 72(1) of the Planning (Listed Buildings
    and Conservation Areas) Act 1990, which requires special attention to be paid to the
    desirability of preserving or enhancing the character or appearance of conservation
    areas. I note that the Council raised no heritage objection to the proposal and
    concluded that the advertisements would have a neutral effect on the CCA. I have
    no reason to disagree with that assessment and therefore find that the proposal
    would preserve the character and appearance of the CCA.
11. For the reasons given above, I conclude that the proposed advertisements would
    have an unduly harmful effect on the visual amenity of the building, its high-quality
    façade and the wider development. The advertisements would undermine the strong
    architectural form of the host building and conflict with the approved signage
    strategy. Consequently, the proposal conflicts with Policies SP1, EN1 and DM1 of
    the Manchester Core Strategy (2012) which among other things seek to ensure that
    development in Manchester is located in the right places, is well designed, and is

Other Matters
12. The appellant has drawn my attention to the approved signage at Mollie’s, 2
    Atherton Street. I have carefully considered this comparison. However, I do not
    consider that this approval demonstrates that the appeal proposal would be
    acceptable. Whilst the premises are located in close proximity to one another, the
    circumstances of the two sites differ. In particular, the façade at the Mollie’s is set
    back behind boundary treatments which obscure the ground floor elevation. By
    contrast, the appeal premises occupies a prominent position within a building whose
    ground floor façade is highly visible in the street scene. As a result, the
    circumstances of the two sites are materially different and the approval at Mollie's
    carries little weight in my assessment of the appeal proposal.
13. The appellant also argues that the presence of informal advertisements and
    promotional material elsewhere within the wider development demonstrates that
    occupiers require greater visibility than provided by the approved signage strategy
    and is a consequence of the Council's approach to signage control. However,
    regardless of the reasons for their presence, other signage displays within the area
    do not justify the appeal proposal and do not alter my assessment of its effect on
    visual amenity.
14. I acknowledge the appellant's submissions that the proposed illuminated signage is
    necessary to attract passing trade and contribute to the commercial success and
    viability of the restaurant. However, the advertisement control regime limits the
    assessment of proposals to considerations of amenity and public safety. Whilst
    some degree of signage is clearly necessary to identify and advertise the premises,
    commercial competition, business requirements and the desire to maximise visibility
    are not determinative considerations. In this case, I have not been provided with
    convincing evidence that the scale, siting and design of the proposed
    advertisements are necessary to achieve effective identification of the unit, nor that
    alternative signage solutions more closely aligned with the approved signage
    strategy and design guidance would be incapable of providing an appropriate level
    of visibility. Consequently, the commercial benefits identified by the appellant do not
    outweigh the harm to visual amenity arising from the proposed advertisements.
15. The appellant further argues that non-illuminated versions of the projecting signs
    may benefit from deemed consent. However, the appeal before me relates to the
    specific advertisements as proposed. The acceptability or otherwise of a different
    scheme is not a matter that falls to be determined in this decision.
Conclusion
16. For the reasons given above the appeal should be dismissed.
