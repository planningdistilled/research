Appeal Decision
Site visit made on 18 August 2026
by A Caines BSc (Hons) MSc MRTPI
an Inspector appointed by the Secretary of State
Decision date: 8 September 2026


Appeal Ref: 6009197
18 Finden Gardens, Hampsthwaite, Harrogate HG3 2EN
•    The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
     against a refusal to grant planning permission.
•    The appeal is made by Alex Adamson against the decision of North Yorkshire Council.
•    The application Ref is 26/00434/FUL.
•    The development proposed is construction of a two-bedroom, two-storey detached house.

Decision
1.     The appeal is allowed and planning permission is granted for construction of a
       two-bedroom, two-storey detached house at 18 Finden Gardens, Hampsthwaite,
       Harrogate HG3 2EN in accordance with the terms of the application,
       Ref 26/00434/FUL, subject to the conditions in the attached schedule.
Preliminary Matters
2.     A revised version of the National Planning Policy Framework (the Framework) was
       published on 17 August 2026. The main parties have provided comments
       regarding the implications of the updated national policy and I have had regard to
       those submissions.
3.     During the appeal process a signed unilateral undertaking, dated 12 June 2026,
       has been provided which secures the occupation of the dwelling for self-build
       purposes. I am satisfied that the obligation meets the tests set out in Regulation
       122 of the Community Infrastructure Levy Regulations 2010 (as amended). The
       Council confirmed that, in light of that obligation, it no longer seeks to rely upon the
       third reason for refusal. I have determined the appeal accordingly.
Main Issues
4.     The main issues are:
           •    the effect of the development on the character and appearance of the area;
           •    whether the development would provide satisfactory living conditions for its
                occupiers, with particular regard to the quality of the garden space.
Reasons
Character and appearance
5.     The appeal site comprises a former side garden to 18 Finden Gardens and
       benefits from its own vehicular access. The site occupies a prominent corner
       position within the residential cul-de-sac and is enclosed by boundary hedging and
       fencing. However, beyond the immediate context, its wider visibility is very limited.

6.     Policy HP3 of the Harrogate District Local Plan (2020) (HLP) requires development
       to respond positively to local character and distinctiveness and to respect the
       spatial qualities of the area, including building density, plot size and the
       relationship between buildings and surrounding spaces.
7.     I have had regard to the previous appeal decision relating to the site
       (APP/E2734/W/20/3265492). Whilst that appeal was dismissed for other reasons,
       the Inspector concluded that the proposed detached dwelling would not harm the
       character and appearance of the area. That finding attracts significant weight in
       my assessment.
8.     The current proposal differs from the previously considered scheme in that the
       dwelling would be somewhat larger, with increased width and height. However,
       whilst these changes would increase the scale of the building, the evidence before
       me does not demonstrate that they would fundamentally alter the relationship
       between the dwelling, the plot and the surrounding development.
9.     The surrounding area is characterised by a varied suburban pattern of
       development. The proposed dwelling would be of a conventional design, would
       utilise materials reflecting surrounding development and would retain existing
       boundary landscaping which would soften its appearance within the street scene.
10. The site would undoubtedly be developed more intensively than at present and
    the dwelling would occupy a relatively compact plot. However, having regard to
    the previous appeal decision, the varied character of the surrounding estate and
    the limited nature of the differences between the two appeal schemes, I am not
    persuaded that the development would appear unduly cramped or discordant
    within its surroundings.
11. I therefore conclude that the development would preserve the character and
    appearance of the area and would comply with Policy HP3 of the HLP.
Living conditions/garden space
12. The proposal would provide approximately 82 square metres of outdoor garden
    space. This exceeds the 75 square metre guideline identified within the
    Council's Landscape Design Guide for a dwelling of this size. However, the
    adequacy of garden space cannot be determined solely by reference to overall
    area. Policy HP4 of the HLP seeks well-designed and appropriately located private
    amenity space, whilst the Landscape Design Guide also places emphasis on
    matters including layout, usability, sunlight and the ability of outdoor space to
    function as a coherent private garden.
13. In this case, the front of the site would be largely occupied by access and parking.
    The principal garden area would be a side garden to the north of the dwelling, with
    a limited amount of space to the rear.
14. I recognise that the arrangement would have certain shortcomings. The principal
    garden area would be likely to experience periods of shade owing to its position
    relative to the dwelling, while the narrow rear garden would have limited
    functionality. Taken together, the arrangement would provide a less attractive and
    less versatile outdoor environment than that typically associated with many
    surrounding dwellings.

15. Nevertheless, I am not persuaded that the resulting deficiencies would be so
    significant as to render the garden unusable or incapable of serving its intended
    function. The garden area would still be capable of accommodating sitting out,
    drying clothes and other ordinary domestic activities. The boundary hedge would
    provide some privacy from passers-by. Whilst I accept that the quality of the space
    would fall short of the ideal sought by Policy HP4 and the Landscape Design
    Guide, the garden would retain some practical utility for the occupiers.
16. Drawing these matters together, I conclude that the development would give rise
    to a limited degree of harm to the living conditions of future occupiers arising from
    the quality and configuration of the garden space. Whilst the garden areas would
    retain some practical utility, their layout and constrained nature would fall short of
    the quality of environment sought by Policy HP4 of the HLP. As such, the proposal
    would not fully accord with that policy.
Other Matters
17. I have noted the concerns raised by local residents regarding parking, traffic
    conditions and disruption during construction. However, the Highway Authority
    raises no objection to the proposal and is satisfied that it would not result in an
    unacceptable impact on highway safety or operation of the highway network. I
    have seen no persuasive evidence that would lead me to a different conclusion.
Conditions
18. I have had regard to the conditions suggested by the Council and have assessed
    them against the tests set out in the Framework and Planning Practice Guidance.
    Where necessary I have amended the wording in the interests of clarity and
    precision, and I have only imposed conditions where they meet the relevant tests.
19. In addition to the standard time limit and approved plans conditions, a condition
    requiring the approved parking provision to be laid out before first occupation and
    retained thereafter is necessary in the interests of highway safety. I have also
    imposed an additional condition requiring the provision and retention of the
    approved bin and cycle storage facilities before occupation to secure appropriate
    facilities for sustainable travel and waste storage.
20. Given the contaminated land advice submitted to the Council, I have imposed a
    condition requiring appropriate investigation and remediation should previously
    unidentified contamination be encountered during development.
21. However, I have not imposed conditions relating to external materials, construction
    hours, drainage systems, or removing permitted development rights for additional
    windows. Having regard to the evidence before me, I am not satisfied that such
    conditions would be necessary to make the development acceptable in planning
    terms. The approved plans adequately establish the proposed materials, no
    unacceptable impacts arising from construction activity have been demonstrated,
    there is no technical objection in relation to drainage, and the evidence does not
    justify the specific withdrawal of permitted development rights for windows.
Planning Balance
22. The appeal site lies within a recognised settlement where the principle of
    residential development is accepted. The Council further acknowledges that it
    cannot currently demonstrate a five-year supply of deliverable housing sites, with

       the evidence indicating a significant shortfall. The provision of an additional
       dwelling therefore attracts meaningful weight in favour of the proposal.
23. The proposal would also generate modest economic benefits through construction
    activity and future expenditure associated with occupation of the dwelling. In
    addition, the scheme would make a limited contribution towards self-build housing
    provision. These considerations attract further weight in favour of the proposal.
24. I have found that the proposal would preserve the character and appearance of
    the area. The only adverse effect identified relates to the quality and configuration
    of the proposed garden space. Whilst that harm attracts weight against the
    proposal and results in conflict with Policy HP4 of the HLP, I have also found that
    the space would retain meaningful utility and would not be unacceptable in all
    respects. This reduces the weight I attach to the conflict with Policy HP4.
25. Policies S3 and S4 of the Framework establish a presumption in favour of
    development within settlements and direct that such proposals should be approved
    unless the benefits of doing so would be substantially outweighed by any adverse
    effects when assessed against the national decision-making policies of the
    Framework. In this case, the proposal would provide an additional dwelling in a
    sustainable location, contribute towards housing supply at a time of significant
    shortfall, make effective use of a vacant plot within a settlement, generate modest
    economic benefits associated with construction and future occupation, and secure
    a self-build dwelling. These outcomes are consistent with the Framework's
    objectives and policies in relation to boosting housing delivery and choice, making
    effective use of land and directing development towards sustainable locations.
    Taken together, these benefits attract substantial weight in favour of the proposal.
26. Against those benefits, I have identified some harm to the living conditions of
    future occupiers arising from the quality and configuration of the proposed garden
    space. This engages the Framework's objectives and policies in relation to
    securing a high standard of amenity for existing and future users and creating
    well-designed places. However, I have found that the identified shortcomings
    would result in only a limited degree of harm. Assessed against the national
    decision-making policies of the Framework as a whole, I find that this limited
    adverse effect would not substantially outweigh the benefits of the proposal.
27. Accordingly, having regard to Policies S3 and S4 and the national decision-making
    policies of the Framework taken as a whole, I conclude that the proposal would
    represent sustainable development.
Conclusion
28. For the reasons given, the development would result in limited conflict with
    Policy HP4 of the HLP and therefore would not fully accord with the development
    plan when read as a whole. However, material considerations, particularly the
    support afforded by Policies S3 and S4 of the Framework to housing development
    in settlements, the Council’s significant housing land supply shortfall, and the
    benefits identified, indicate that planning permission should nevertheless be
    granted. Accordingly, the appeal is allowed.

SCHEDULE OF CONDITIONS
     1)       The development hereby permitted shall begin not later than 3 years from the
              date of this decision.
     2)       The development hereby permitted shall be carried out in accordance with
              the following approved plans: 300 rev A; 301 rev B; 302 rev A; 304 rev A.
     3)       The areas shown on the approved plans for the parking of vehicles shall be
              made available for use prior to the first occupation of the dwelling hereby
              permitted and shall thereafter be retained and kept available for parking at all
              times. The parking areas shall not be used for any purpose other than the
              parking of vehicles associated with the development.
     4)       The bin storage and cycle storage facilities shown on Drawing No 304 rev A
              shall be provided prior to the first occupation of the dwelling hereby permitted
              and shall thereafter be retained and kept available for those purposes.
     5)       If contamination not previously identified by the developer prior to the grant of
              this planning permission is encountered during the development, all works in
              the affected area (save for site investigation works) shall cease immediately
              and the local planning authority shall be notified in writing within 2 working
              days. Works in the affected area shall not recommence until either (a) a
              Remediation Strategy has been submitted to and approved in writing by the
              local planning authority or (b) the local planning authority has confirmed in
              writing that remediation measures are not required. The Remediation
              Strategy shall include a timetable for the implementation and completion of
              the approved remediation measures. Thereafter, remediation of the site shall
              be carried out and completed in accordance with the approved
              Remediation Strategy.
              Following completion of any measures identified in the approved
              Remediation Strategy, a Verification Report shall be submitted to the local
              planning authority. No part of the site shall be brought into use until such time
              as the site has been remediated in accordance with the approved
              Remediation Strategy and a Verification Report in respect of those works has
              been approved in writing by the local planning authority. Where verification
              has been submitted and approved in stages for different areas of the whole
              site, a Final Verification Summary Report shall be submitted to and approved
              in writing by the local planning authority.
