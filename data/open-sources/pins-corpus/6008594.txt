•   The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended) against
    a refusal to grant planning permission.
•   The appeal is made by Ana Ureche-Johnson against the decision of Tameside Metropolitan Borough
    Council.
•   The application Ref is 25/00921/FUL.
    The development proposed is removal of existing garage and the siting of two linked modular buildings
    for use in connection with the church and its community outreach.

Decision
1. The appeal is allowed and planning permission is granted for removal of existing
   garage and the siting of two linked modular buildings for use in connection with the
   church and its community outreach in accordance with the terms of the application,
   reference 25/00921/FUL, and the plans submitted with it, subject to the following
   conditions:
     1)       The development hereby permitted shall begin not later than three years from
              the date of this decision.
     2)       The development hereby permitted shall be carried out in accordance with
              drawing nos:
              •    Location Plan;
              •    As Existing - Location Plan, Site Plan, Block Plan and Existing Floor and
                   Elevation Plans - 4094.1;
              •    As Proposed - Location Plan, Site Plan, Block Plan and Proposed Floor and
                   Roof Plans - 4094.2;
              •    Elevations - OPP1377242-09 Rev A;
              •    SL051 Linked Solus Buildings - OPP1377242 Rev A;
              •    Foundation Loading Drawing - OPP1377242-100 Rev 01;
              •    Portakabin Specification Detail Document
     3)       No demolition, construction, or installation works, including deliveries and the
              movement of plant and machinery, shall take place outside the hours of 07:30
              and 18:00 Mondays to Fridays and 08:00 to 13:00 Saturdays. No work shall take
              place on Sundays and Bank Holidays.

3. I have adopted a shorter description of development suggested by the appellant at the
   appeal stage and which the Council had the opportunity to comment upon. I am
   satisfied that the description accurately reflects that upon which the planning
   application was made, and no party would be prejudiced by my use of this.
Main Issue
4. The main issue is the effect of the proposed modular buildings on the character and
   appearance of the host building and the surrounding area.
Reasons
5. The appeal site forms part of an established developed site used by New Life Church
   Tameside in connection with its community activities. The host building is a substantial
   brick structure with variation in its roofscape and built form. The main church building is
   positioned on a corner and, by virtue of its size and siting, screens much of the rear of
   the site from the street, with only limited views of the existing garage available from St
   James Street and Hall Street. The appeal site itself is located to the rear of the main
   church building. The surrounding area is predominantly residential and contains a
   range of building types, forms, and ancillary features. There is an ancillary car park to
   the west and south of the site.
6. The appeal proposal would cater for a variety of community needs within the church
   and would replace the existing garage with modular buildings occupying the same
   footprint and using the existing foundation. The Council does not object to the principle
   of development at the site. Rather, its concerns relate to the suitability of the modular
   buildings in this location and its effect on the character and appearance of the host
   building and the surrounding area, including its visibility from the street.
7. Policy C1 requires development proposals to respect the distinct settlement pattern,
   townscape character and surrounding fabric of the area, whilst paying particular
   attention to the relationship between buildings and their setting. Similarly, Places for
   Everyone Policy JP-P1 seeks amongst other things high quality development that
   responds positively to its context and contributes to the creation of attractive and
   sustainable places. In this case, the proposed modular buildings would be sited to the
   rear of the church within the existing developed curtilage and would remain physically
   and visually subordinate to the principal building. Its location reflects the prevailing
   pattern of development on the site and avoids encroachment into more prominent
   areas that contribute to the character of the street scene.
8. In assessing the proposal, I have had regard to the relationship between the proposed
   building, the existing church and the wider streetscape. From my site visit, it was
   evident that views of the proposed development from St James Street and Hall Street
   would be largely screened by the existing church building and boundary treatments.
   Consequently, the proposal would have only a limited presence within the public realm
   and would not detract from the visual qualities of the church. As a result, the scheme
   would preserve the appreciation of the church's principal elevations and architectural
   features, which would remain the dominant elements within public views. Accordingly,
   the proposal would respect the character of the surrounding fabric and the relationship
   between the building and its setting, consistent with the objectives of Policy C1.
9. Whilst the modular buildings would be of a standardised design and would not
   replicate the architectural detailing of the church, neither Policy C1 nor Policy JP-P01
   requires ancillary development to mirror existing buildings. Rather, they seek to ensure

     that proposals respect the surrounding fabric and maintain an appropriate relationship
     with their setting. In this case, the modular buildings would be located to the rear of the
     church within an established group of ancillary features and would remain clearly
     subordinate to the principal building. Its presence would therefore not appear
     discordant within the site, nor would it detract from the character of the surrounding
     area.
10. In addition, the finished floor level would remain unchanged and the proposed
    structure would sit at a noticeably lower level than the adjacent car park. This
    relationship would lessen the apparent scale and visibility of the building and reinforce
    its subordinate role within the site. Taken together with the existing screening afforded
    by the church building itself, the proposal would have a limited visual impact and would
    accord with the objectives of Policy C1 in respecting the character of the site and its
    setting.
11. Whilst the Council's officer report refers to the architectural interest of the church and
    suggests that it may exhibit characteristics of a non-designated heritage asset, no
    substantive evidence has been presented regarding its significance or the contribution
    made by the area in which the modular building is proposed. On the evidence before
    me, the proposal would not materially harm the appreciation of the building's
    architectural qualities.
12. Drawing these matters together, the development would not appear visually intrusive
    or unduly prominent. Any visual effects would be limited and localised, with the church
    remaining the dominant feature of the site. Consequently, I find that the proposal would
    not harm the character or appearance of the host building, its setting or the wider area.
    As such, it would accord with Tameside Unitary Development Policy C1, which seeks
    among other things development that respects local townscape and urban form, and
    Places for Everyone Policy JP P1, which seeks well-designed development that
    responds appropriately to its context and contributes to the creation of sustainable
    places.
Other Matters
13. I note that the Council acknowledges the social, economic and environmental benefits
    associated with the proposal. The development would provide additional operational
    space to support the continued functioning of the church, and its community outreach
    works. It would provide accommodation for individuals with reduced mobility, enable
    small group activities and increase the flexibility of the church’s facilities which it
    provides to the local community. It would also generate a modest level of economic
    activity through its installation and use. Furthermore, the proposal would utilise a
    prefabricated structure capable of reuse and avoid the extent of construction activity
    associated with a more permanent form of development. These benefits are consistent
    with the aims of Policy JP-P1 in supporting the creation of sustainable and inclusive
    places that respond to community needs. Taken together, these benefits weigh in
    favour of the proposal.
14. The appellant has raised concerns regarding the Council's handling of the application,
    including changes in the officer's position and the extent of discussions regarding
    amendments to the proposal. Whilst I have had regard to these submissions, the
    appeal has been determined on its planning merits, and these matters do not affect my
    conclusions on the main issue.

15. The Council concluded that the proposal would not result in any unacceptable effects
    through overlooking, overshadowing or overbearing impacts. No objections were
    received from neighbouring occupiers, and Environmental Health raised no objection
    to the development. I therefore find no substantive conflict with development plan
    policies seeking to safeguard residential amenity.
16. The Council's specialist consultees raised no objections in respect of highway safety,
    trees, ecology, drainage, flood risk or contaminated land, and I have no reason to
    conclude otherwise. Consequently, I find that the proposal would not cause harm in
    any of these respects and these matters weigh neither for nor against the proposal.
Conditions
17. I have considered the Council's suggested conditions in light of the National Planning
    Policy Framework and Planning Practice Guidance. In addition to the standard time
    limit and approved plans conditions, a condition restricting demolition and construction
    hours is necessary and reasonable in the interests of safeguarding the living conditions
    of nearby residents.
18. The appellant indicates a willingness to amend the colour of the portacabin following
    discussions during the application process. However, I am satisfied that the appeal can
    be determined on the basis of the submitted proposal with the cabin in its standard
    colour palette and no additional condition is necessary in this regard.
Conclusion
19. For the reasons given above, I conclude that the proposal would not harm the
    character or appearance of the host building or the surrounding area. Furthermore, it
    would provide additional ancillary accommodation in support of an established
    community facility without giving rise to unacceptable impacts in any other respect.
    Accordingly, the proposal accords with the development plan when read as a whole
    and the appeal should be allowed.
