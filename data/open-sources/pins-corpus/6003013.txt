Decision
1.       The appeal is dismissed.
Background and Main Issue
2.       Planning permission was granted for a hip to gable roof enlargement with rear
         dormer roof extension and front rooflights, replacement roof over existing two
         storey garage and window alteration, first floor rear extension and new first floor
         side extension, proposed part ground floor rear and side extensions and proposed
         front and rear fence, under Council ref. 24/0507/HOT.
3.       A subsequent application was granted for the variation of condition U0181865 of
         planning approval 24/0507/HOT to allow an altered front rooflight location,
         fenestration alterations, revised roof eaves height and altered materials, under
         Council ref. 24/2105/VRC.
4.       This appeal now seeks a further variation to the previously approved development.
         In particular, a change is proposed to a window within the approved first floor rear
         extension, labelled as ‘W1’, which wraps around the corner of the room labelled as
         ‘principal bedroom’. Described as a glazed bay, it stands to almost full floor-to-
         ceiling height. The glazed side of the bay facing 8 Park Gate Gardens was
         approved as a window that would be non-openable and obscurely glazed below a
         minimum height of 1.7 metres above the floor level, as required by Condition No.
         U0190640.
5.       The appeal proposal, however, is to retain the as-built window, which is openable
         and features clear glazing over its entire surface. The evidence indicates that other
         changes, to the approved bathroom windows, are not in dispute. As such, and
         notwithstanding that the application form refers to all of the conditions imposed on
         the planning permission Ref. 24/2105/VRC, not all of those clearly relate to the
         matters discussed in the submissions. Accordingly, I have proceeded on the basis
         that the appeal seeks the removal of Condition No U0190640 as unnecessary, and
         to vary Condition No U0190638 in order that it refers to the amended drawings
         which accompany the appeal.
6.       At the time of my visit, an obscure film had been applied to glazing at the side of
         W1 facing 8 Park Gate Gardens. For clarity, I have assessed the proposal on the
         basis of the drawings, rather than the development that has been carried out.

7.     Having regard to the above, the main issue is the effect of the proposal on the
       living conditions of the occupiers of 8 Park Gate Gardens (‘No 8’), with regard to
       privacy.
Reasons
8.     The appeal site and its counterpart at No 8 form a pair of semi-detached properties
       with front and rear gardens, occupying a similar position within their plots. The
       properties are 2 storeys and both feature 2 storey rear projections across part of
       their width, the upper parts of which are well set away from the shared boundary.
       Whilst both properties have windows at first floor in the rear elevation, only the
       appeal dwelling has a window to the side elevation following the erection of the
       approved extensions. The subject window, W1, is located at the end of its first-
       floor addition which projects out a short distance from the semi-detached pair's
       main rear wall.
9.     No 8 has a paved terrace area between the shared boundary and the side wall of
       its ground floor extension. The paved terrace is accessed off a set of glazed doors
       and is the most private part of the garden and is therefore likely to be used most
       intensively. The glazed doors, as well as large windows in the side wall, and a
       further small window, all provide light and outlook to a habitable kitchen / dining
       room.
10. Due to the window W1’s orientation, substantial size and elevated position, a
    person standing at it, potentially for an extended period, would be able to look
    directly towards the ground-floor kitchen / dining room side windows of No 8. In
    addition, whilst the massing of the ground floor extension would partly limit views
    of the paved terrace area, W1 would nevertheless afford direct views of parts of
    that space. Consequently, occupiers of No 8 would be aware that their activities
    within both the kitchen/dining room and the terrace area could readily be observed
    from W1. The opportunity for sustained and direct views from W1 means occupiers
    of No 8 could reasonably feel under observation when using these areas. This
    would give rise to a heightened perception of overlooking and would significantly
    erode their sense of privacy and seclusion, resulting in a harmful loss of privacy to
    the occupiers of No 8.
11. The existing soft landscaping along the shared boundary could not be relied upon
    in perpetuity to provide the same level of screening at all times as that shown in
    the photographs provided. Moreover, from what I have seen the existing
    landscaping would not be sufficient to entirely address the experience of perceived
    overlooking for occupiers using the kitchen / dining room of No 8, from where W1
    is seen above the height of the brick side wall of the appeal property’s ground floor
    extension. Therefore, this would not overcome the harm identified.
12. From the window of bedroom 2 (‘W2’) at the appeal site, I was able to see parts of
    the end of the rear garden at No 8 and a lateral, angled view over part of its paved
    terrace area. The extent of lateral overlooking over the rear gardens possible from
    W2, and its counterpart in the rear elevation of No 8, reflect an established
    standard of privacy between this pair. The views available from there, similar to
    those which would be available out of window W3 in the dormer above, result in a
    degree of overlooking that is typical of suburban areas. However, those openings
    are not directly comparable to the orientation of W1 which is in a side elevation
    and projects out beyond the position of the rear wall, offering a different and more

       direct field of view over the kitchen / dining room windows at No 8, as well as its

13. For the reasons given, I conclude that the proposal would have a harmful effect on
    the living conditions of the occupiers of No 8, with regard to privacy. This would
    conflict with the relevant provisions of Policy 46 of the Richmond upon Thames
    Local Plan ‘The best for our borough’ (2024 to 2039) (2025), which seeks to avoid
    unacceptable levels of overlooking, including perceived overlooking, in all
    development.
Other Matters
14. To a limited degree the proposal would provide a more harmonious design
    solution. Nevertheless, as the design is not in dispute I attach only limited weight
    to that matter. Also, although the proposal would provide improved ventilation to
    the room, it has not been demonstrated that there is no other way to provide this
    benefit without causing the harm identified. Accordingly, these factors would not
    adequately justify the harm that I have identified.
Conclusion
15. For the reasons given, the proposal conflicts with the development plan and the
    material considerations do not indicate that the appeal should be decided other
    than in accordance with it. I therefore conclude that the appeal should be
    dismissed.
