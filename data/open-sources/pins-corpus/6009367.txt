Appeal Decision
Site visit made on 11 August 2026
by Chris Mayes CMLI
an Inspector appointed by the Secretary of State
Decision date: 3rd September 2026


Appeal Ref: 6009367
Carlow House Barn, Woodman Lane, Cowan Bridge, Lancashire LA6 2HT
•   The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
    against a refusal to grant planning permission.
•   The appeal is made by Mr & Mrs Fraser against the decision of Lancaster City Council.
•   The application reference is 26/00093/FUL.
•   The development proposed is change of use of existing studio/office to dwelling.

Decision
1. The appeal is allowed and planning permission is granted for change of use of
   existing studio/office to dwelling at Carlow House Barn, Woodman Lane, Cowan
   Bridge, Lancashire LA6 2HT in accordance with the terms of the application,
   reference 26/00093/FUL, subject to the conditions in the attached schedule.
Preliminary Matter
2. Since the appeal was submitted, the National Planning Policy Framework (the
   Framework) has been updated. The main parties have been provided with the
   opportunity to comment on the implications of that change. I have taken any
   comments into consideration. I have determined the appeal on the basis of the
   current Framework.
Main Issue
3. The main issue is whether the proposal would be suitably located having regard to
   relevant development plan policies concerning the location of residential
   development.
Reasons
4. The development plan seeks to direct new housing towards the most sustainable
   locations within the district. Policy SP2 of the Local Plan for Lancaster District
   2020-2031 Strategic Policies and Land Allocations Development Plan Document
   (climate emergency review) (January 2025) (SPLA DPD) identifies a settlement
   hierarchy, while Policy DM4 of the Local Plan for Lancaster District 2020-2031
   Development Management Development Plan Document (climate emergency
   review) (January 2025) (DM DPD) supports residential development outside the
   main urban areas where it reflects sustainable patterns of development and
   accords with that hierarchy.
5. The appeal site lies outside any settlement identified for housing growth and
   occupies a rural location beyond the villages identified as suitable locations for new

     housing development. The proposal would create an additional dwelling in that
     location.
6. While the proposal would not involve the construction of a new building, it would
   secure the residential use of an existing building that already forms part of a small
   group of development at Overtown. The building exists, is physically separate from
   neighbouring dwellings and is already in lawful use as a studio/office. These factors
   are relevant to my assessment of the proposal and are consistent with the
   objectives of Policy DM49 concerning the re-use and conversion of rural buildings.
7. Policy DM13 of the DM DPD seeks, among other matters, to ensure that residential
   conversions provide accommodation that addresses local housing needs and
   imbalances in the local housing market. The evidence before me does not
   demonstrate that the proposal would address an identified local housing need or
   housing market. However, I find no conflict with the remaining relevant objectives of
   Policy DM13.
8. Moreover, there is no substantive dispute regarding the acceptability of the
   proposal in other respects. No external alterations are proposed, and the Council
   accepts that the proposal would preserve the character and appearance of the
   building and its surroundings. Furthermore, the evidence before me does not
   indicate that the proposal would result in harm to heritage interests, the living
   conditions of neighbouring occupants, highway safety, parking provision, drainage
   or flood risk.
9. Drawing these matters together, I find that the proposal would introduce a dwelling
   in a location that is not identified for housing growth within the settlement hierarchy.
   As such, it would conflict with Policies SP2 and DM4, which seek to direct
   residential development towards the district's most sustainable locations and
   ensure development reflects sustainable patterns of growth. Furthermore, the
   proposal has not been shown to address local housing needs or imbalances in the
   local housing market, as sought by Policy DM13. Accordingly, having regard to
   relevant development plan policies concerning the location of residential
   development, I conclude that the proposal would not be suitably located and would
   conflict with the development plan when read as a whole.
Planning Balance
10. I have found that the proposal would not be suitably located having regard to
    relevant development plan policies concerning the location of residential
    development and that it would conflict with the development plan when read as a
    whole. I attach moderate weight against the proposal arising from that conflict.
11. Set against this, the proposal would provide an additional dwelling. While a single
    dwelling would make only a modest contribution in numerical terms, it would
    nevertheless contribute towards housing supply at a time when the Council cannot
    demonstrate a five-year supply of deliverable housing sites. I attach moderate
    weight to that benefit.
12. The proposal would also secure the residential re-use of an existing lawful building
    without the need for substantial new construction. Therefore, I find that the proposal
    is consistent with Policy S5(c) of the Framework, which supports the re-use of
    existing buildings outside settlements in appropriate circumstances. I attach
    moderate weight to that benefit.

13. Taking all relevant considerations into account, I find that the adverse effects
    arising from the proposal would not substantially outweigh its benefits when
    assessed against the national decision-making policies in the Framework.
Other Matters
14. Overtown Farm, to the east of the appeal site, is a Grade II listed building (listed
    building number 1071619). In accordance with section 66(1) of the Planning (Listed
    Buildings and Conservation Areas) Act 1990, I have had special regard to the
    desirability of preserving the listed building, its setting and any features of special
    architectural or historic interest which it possesses. The appeal building lies within a
    group of buildings associated with the listed building. The evidence indicates that
    the significance of the listed building derives principally from its historic interest and
    architectural detailing. However, its setting is also informed by the presence of a
    group of complementary buildings of broadly similar scale, materials and
    proportions which retain a clear hierarchy in terms of their historic function and
    architectural order. The proposal would not entail any external alterations to the
    appeal building and its relationship with the listed building and surrounding
    buildings would remain materially unchanged. Consequently, I find that the
    proposal would preserve the setting of the listed building and would not harm its
    significance.
15. The appellants also rely upon Policy L2 of the Framework. While I accept that the
    building no longer meets the appellant's requirements as a studio/office, that does
    not in itself demonstrate that it is vacant, redundant or under-utilised in planning
    terms. In the absence of evidence to that effect, I am unable to conclude that the
    proposal would deliver the benefits identified by Policy L2. Accordingly, this matter
    weighs neither for nor against the proposal.
16. The appellants also explain that occupation of the appeal property would enable
    the release of a larger family dwelling nearby. While I have had regard to that
    submission, the extent to which such an outcome would occur cannot be
    guaranteed through the appeal proposal.
Conditions
17. The Council has provided a list of suggested conditions, which I have considered in
    terms of the requirements in Policy DM6 of the Framework, and advice contained in
    the Planning Practice Guidance. Where appropriate, I have amended the wording
    of the suggested conditions for clarity and reasonableness.
18. To meet legislative requirements, a condition shall be imposed to address the
    period for commencement. For the reasons of certainty and clarity, I have imposed
    a condition that requires the development to be carried out in accordance with the
    submitted drawings.
19. I have imposed a condition requiring the retention of the parking and manoeuvring
    area shown on the approved plans. This is necessary in the interests of highway
    safety and to ensure that satisfactory parking provision is available to serve the
    dwelling.
20. I have also imposed a condition requiring the development to be carried out in
    accordance with the flood mitigation measures identified within the submitted Flood

     Risk Assessment. This is necessary to ensure that the development remains safe
     for its intended users and to minimise the potential impacts of flooding.
21. The Council has suggested the removal of a broad range of permitted development
    rights. I have not identified evidence which would justify such a wide-ranging
    restriction. However, based on my findings regarding the contribution made by the
    scale, proportions and hierarchical relationship of the buildings within the setting of
    the listed building, I am satisfied that uncontrolled upward extensions could harm
    the setting of the listed building and its significance. Consequently, I have imposed
    a condition that removes permitted development rights under Schedule 2, Part 1,
    Class AA only.
22. The Council has suggested a condition requiring details of a foul drainage scheme
    to be submitted and approved. However, the evidence before me indicates that the
    appeal building already benefits from foul drainage through an existing system
    shared with the adjacent barn. Moreover, no substantive evidence has been
    provided to demonstrate that the existing arrangements are inadequate or that
    additional drainage works are required to make the development acceptable.
    Consequently, I am not satisfied that such a condition would be necessary, and I
    have not imposed it.
Conclusion
23. For the reasons given above, I have found that the proposal would conflict with
    Policies SP2 of the SPLA DPD and DM4 of the DM DPD. While the proposal would
    not be supported by the element of Policy DM13 which seeks to address local
    housing needs and imbalances in the local housing market, I have found no conflict
    with the remaining relevant objectives of that policy or with Policy DM49.
24. The proposed development would conflict with the development plan when read as
    a whole but the material considerations, including the Framework, indicate that a
    decision should be made other than in accordance with it. I therefore conclude that
    the appeal should be allowed.
