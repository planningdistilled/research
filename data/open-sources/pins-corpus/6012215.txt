    The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
     against a refusal to grant planning permission under section 73 of the Town and Country Planning
     Act 1990 (as amended) for the development of land without complying with conditions subject to
     which a previous planning permission was granted.
    The appeal is made by Wang Kin Chow against the decision of the Council of the London Borough of
     Barnet.
    The application Ref is 26/0127/S73.
    The application sought planning permission for ‘part single, part two storey side extension following
     the demolition of existing garage/side extension. Single storey rear extension. Single storey front
     extension and new front porch’ without complying with a condition attached to planning permission
     Ref 24/4150/HSE, dated 12 December 2024.
    The condition in dispute is No. 1 which states that: The development hereby permitted shall be
     carried out in accordance with the following approved plans: 6338-AL-001, 6338-EX-001 A, 6338-
     EX-002, 6338-EX-003, 6338-EX-004, 6338-EX-005, 6338-EX-006, 6338-EX-007, 6338-PL-101 A,
     6338-PL-102 A, 6338-PL-103 A, 6338-PL-104 A, 6338-PL-105, Location Plan, Design and Access
     Statement.
    The reason given for the condition is: For the avoidance of doubt and in the interests of proper
     planning and so as to ensure that the development is carried out fully in accordance with the plans
     as assessed in accordance with Policies CS NPPF and CS1 of the Local Plan Core Strategy DPD
     (adopted September 2012) and Policy DM01 of the Local Plan Development Management Policies
     DPD (adopted September 2012).

Decision
1.    The appeal is dismissed.
Preliminary Matters
2.    I have taken the address of the site from the Council’s Decision Notice, which is
      more concise than that used in the application form. I am satisfied that no party
      would be prejudiced as a result.
Background and Main Issues
3.    Planning permission was granted in December 2024 for extensions and alterations
      to the existing dwelling. These included a rear single storey extension. On site, an
      extension has been constructed, together with a terraced area and steps. The
      Council and the appellant dispute whether the extension has been built from a
      baseline consistent with the approved plans, and whether a rear terraced area was
      authorised by the December 2024 permission. The appellant seeks to vary the
      approved plans set out in condition 1. The changes include alterations to the
      height of any approved rear terrace, and to the flat roof of the extension.

4.    Section 73 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 as amended (s73) relates
      to minor material amendments. It permits an application to be determined for the
      same development, but with different conditions. As confirmed by case law in
      Finney1, an application under s73 cannot be used to obtain a permission which
      would require a variation to the terms of the operative part of the previous
      permission. This includes the description of the development.
5.    At application stage, the Council considered that the proposal would fall within the
      scope of a s73 application. Nonetheless, I must consider the appeal application as
      if it had been made to me in the first instance. The main issue is whether the
      proposal would result in a conflict with the approved description of development,
      and therefore whether it would fall within the scope of the powers under s73.
Reasons
6.    The approved proposed east and west side elevations (104A) show lines which
      could represent a projection or projections of some form to the rear of the dwelling.
      However, as side elevations, they do not show width. The approved rear elevation
      (103A) shows a continuous line extending horizontally beyond the sides of the
      dwelling. Nevertheless, it shows nothing below this line. The appellant argues that
      the rear elevation drawing is taken from the terrace level itself, rather than from
      further back and lower. Even so, this plan does not conclusively show a new
      terrace to the rear.
7.    No rear terraced area is shown on the approved ground floor plan (101A), or on
      any other plan. Moreover, there is no reference in the description of development
      to a terrace, platform or similar works. Consequently, and as a matter of planning
      judgement, I am not satisfied that the December 2024 permission authorised the
      construction of a rear terrace.
8.    The plans submitted with the application before me show a terrace of appreciable
      width and depth, and the erection of steps down to the garden. Given my
      conclusions above, this would amount to an additional form of development
      beyond that approved by the December 2024 permission. As such, in light of the
      Finney judgement, it would fall outside of the scope of the powers under s73 and
      so the appeal cannot proceed any further.
Other Matters
9.    The Council and the appellant dispute whether the single-storey rear extension
      has been built from the approved baseline. They also disagree about the planning
      merits of the development. Given my findings above, and as a full planning
      application is not before me, it would be inappropriate for me to conclude on these
      matters.
Conclusion
10. As no further action can be taken with the appeal, it must be dismissed.
