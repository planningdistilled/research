•    The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
     against a refusal to grant approval required under Article 3(1) and Schedule 2, Part 3, Class MA of
     the Town and Country Planning (General Permitted Development) (England) Order 2015 (as
     amended).
•    The appeal is made by Ski World Ltd against the decision of the Council of the London Borough of
     Hounslow.
•    The application Ref is PAC/2026/0218.
•    The development proposed is described as the change of use from Class E (commercial, business
     and service) to Class C3 (dwellinghouse).

Decision
1.     The appeal is allowed and prior approval is granted under the provisions of Article
       3(1) and Schedule 2, Part 3, Class MA of the Town and Country Planning (General
       Permitted Development) (England) Order 2015 (as amended) for the change of
       use from Class E (commercial, business and service) to create 10 residential units
       Class C3 (dwellinghouse) at 14 Turnham Green Terrace Mews, Chiswick, London
       W4 1QU in accordance with the application PAC/2026/0218, the details submitted
       with it and subject to the following condition:
         1) In the event that any contamination that is found during the course of
            construction of the approved development that was not previously identified,
            it shall be reported immediately to the local planning authority.
              Development on the part of the site affected shall be suspended and a risk
              assessment carried out and submitted to and approved in writing by the local
              planning authority. Where unacceptable risks are found remediation and
              verification schemes shall be submitted to and approved in writing by the
              local planning authority. These approved schemes shall be carried out before
              the development is resumed or continued.
Preliminary Matters
2.     The Council changed the description of development as stated in the application
       form and as set out in the banner heading above. The revised description is the
       change of use from Class E (commercial, business and service) to create 10
       residential units Class C3 (dwellinghouse). The appellant has also used this
       revised description in the appeal form. It more accurately defines the scheme, and
       I have therefore determined the appeal on this basis.
3.     A new National Planning Policy Framework (the Framework) was published on 17
       August 2026. As this could affect the issues and matters in this case, the Council

       and the appellant were invited to make further comments. The Framework is a
       material consideration.
4.     Article 3(1) and Schedule 2, Part 3, Class MA of the Town and Country Planning
       (General Permitted Development) (England) Order 2015 as amended (the GPDO),
       permits a change of use of a building and any land within its curtilage from a use
       falling within Class E (commercial, business and service) to a use falling within
       Class C3 (dwellinghouses).
5.     Development under Class MA is permitted subject to compliance with specific
       limitations and requires under MA.2 that an application to the local planning
       authority for a determination as to whether prior approval is required in relation to
       the conditions set out in Paragraph MA.2 (2).
6.     There is no dispute between the parties that the proposal would comply with the
       limitations in MA.1 of the GPDO and MA.2 (2) (a) to (c) and (f) to (i). From the
       evidence before me I see no reason to disagree. Having regard to the above, the
       matters in dispute relate to prior approval matters set out in MA.2 (2) (e) and (f).
Main Issues
7.     The main issues are:
           •    the effect of the proposed development on the character and sustainability
                of the Conservation Area; and
           •    whether the proposed development would provide satisfactory living
                conditions for future occupiers, with particular regard to noise from nearby
                commercial premises.
Reasons
8.     The appeal site comprises a three-storey, modern office building with a basement,
       providing approximately 1,442m² (GIA) of office floorspace within Class E use. It is
       situated within a pedestrianised mews area, which is close to Turnham Green
       Terrace, a busy shopping street. It is bounded by the rear gardens of residential
       properties on Mayfield Avenue to the south and east, the Organ Works office
       building to the west, and office premises at 1-12 Turnham Green Terrace Mews to
       the north.
9.     The surrounding area is characterised by a mix of commercial and residential
       uses, close to Chiswick Primary Shopping Area and Chiswick Town Centre. The
       site is located around 130 metres south of Turnham Green Station and benefits
       from a PTAL rating of 4, indicating good accessibility by public transport.
Character and Sustainability of the Conservation Area
10. The appeal site lies within the Chiswick High Road Conservation Area (the
    CHRCA). Therefore, in reaching my decision, I have had regard to the Section
    72(1) duty to preserve or enhance the character or appearance of the Chiswick
    High Road Conservation Area, insofar as that duty falls within the scope of the
    prior approval considerations prescribed by Class MA of the GPDO.
11. The significance of the Chiswick High Road Conservation Area derives principally
    from its well-preserved late nineteenth and early twentieth century commercial
    townscape, reflecting the area's development following the arrival of the railway

       and suburban expansion of Chiswick. Within this, the Turnham Green Terrace
       Character Area is of particular importance as a distinctive gateway between
       Chiswick High Road and Turnham Green Station, characterised by its varied
       scale, surviving single-storey shop terraces, railway-related heritage features and
       high-quality historic buildings which contribute positively to the special architectural
       and historic interest of the conservation area.
12. The Council is concerned that the loss of the commercial use would harm the
    character and sustainability of the CA. However, the appeal site occupies a modern
    building within the Mews, a secondary area that is physically and functionally
    separate from the principal commercial frontages of Turnham Green Terrace and
    Chiswick High Road. The Mews are characterised by a more varied and informal
    pattern of development and substantially lower levels of pedestrian activity than
    those primary commercial streets. Consequently, its contribution to the significance,
    character and appearance of the CA is derived from its distinctive townscape
    character and spatial relationship with the surrounding development, rather than
    the presence of active commercial uses.
13. In these circumstances, the proposed residential use would not erode the active
    commercial frontage, vitality or public experience of the principal commercial areas
    that make an important contribution to the character and appearance of the CA.
    Given the site's limited visibility within the wider CA, its detached relationship from
    the main commercial streets and the absence of a traditional shopfront or other
    commercial frontage, I am not persuaded that the change of use would materially
    alter the character of the area or undermine the significance of the CA.
14. Accordingly, the residential use of this building would be compatible with the
    character of the surrounding area and would not harm the character or
    sustainability of the CA. For these reasons, I therefore conclude that the proposal
    would comply with Paragraph MA.2(2) (e) of the GPDO. Furthermore, I find that the
    proposal would preserve the character and appearance of the CA and, in doing so,
    preserve the significance of the designated heritage asset.
Noise from Nearby Commercial Premises
15. Class MA.2.(2)(d) requires consideration of the impacts of noise from commercial
    premises on the intended occupiers of the development. Whilst the Council's
    reason for refusal refers to noise from roads, Class MA of the GPDO limits
    consideration of noise impacts on future occupiers to those arising from existing
    commercial premises. I have therefore considered the appeal on this basis.
16. The appeal proposal would introduce residential accommodation within a mixed-
    use mews environment. In considering the prior approval matter relating to noise
    from commercial premises, I have had regard to the submitted acoustic evidence,
    including the additional commentary provided by the appellant's acoustic
    consultant at the appeal stage. The evidence indicates that the surrounding area is
    relatively tranquil, with the principal commercial noise source arising from activity
    associated with the nearby café's outdoor seating area.
17. Whilst periods of elevated speech noise were recorded, the assessment
    demonstrates that these occurrences are limited in duration and principally
    associated with normal daytime operating hours. Furthermore, the evidence
    indicates that the existing building fabric, including the external façade and glazing,
    is capable of providing sufficient sound insulation such that recognised internal

       residential noise standards can be achieved within the proposed dwellings. The
       assessment also concludes that acceptable internal noise levels would be
       achievable with windows open, allowing for natural ventilation.
18. I have considered the Council's concerns regarding the potential for future
    changes to neighbouring Class E premises that could result in greater levels of
    noise. However, such scenarios are speculative and relate to hypothetical future
    circumstances rather than the existing situation before me. Consequently, I attach
    limited weight to these concerns.
19. Taking all matters into account, the evidence before me does not demonstrate that
    noise from surrounding commercial premises would result in unacceptable living
    conditions for future occupiers. I am therefore satisfied that the proposal would
    provide an adequate standard of residential amenity in respect of noise and that
    the prior approval matter relating to noise from commercial premises weighs in
    favour of the development.
20. Therefore, I conclude that the proposed development would provide satisfactory
    living conditions for future occupiers, with particular regard to noise from nearby
    commercial premises. It would therefore comply with condition MA.2.(2)(f) of
    Schedule 2, Part 3, Class MA of the GPDO
Other Matters
21. I have had regard to the concerns raised by third parties regarding the loss of
    employment floorspace, the potential impact of the proposal on neighbouring
    businesses, and the overall quality and suitability of the surrounding environment
    as a location for residential accommodation. However, the scope of my
    assessment is restricted to the prior approval matters specified under Class MA of
    the GPDO.
22. The site is located within Flood Zone 3a, and the appeal is supported by a Flood
    Risk Assessment. The Environment Agency were consulted and raised no
    objection to the proposed change of use, and the Council raised no concerns
    regarding flood risk in their assessment. Based on the evidence before me, I see
    no reason to disagree with these conclusions.
Conditions
23. The approval is granted on the basis that the development must be completed
    within three years from the date of prior approval, as stipulated by MA.2(5), and
    thereafter the building must remain in use as a dwellinghouse under Class C3, in
    accordance with MA.2(6). It is also subject to compliance with paragraph W, which
    governs the prior approval procedure.
24. Paragraph W(13) of Schedule 2, Part 3 of the GPDO enables conditions to be
    imposed where they are reasonably related to the subject matter of the prior
    approval. In this case, the matters for prior approval are limited and I have
    considered the conditions suggested by the Council in that context.
25. The Council has identified the site's former industrial use as giving rise to the
    potential for contamination. However, the proposal relates to the conversion of an
    existing building and would involve only limited, if any, ground disturbance. In the
    absence of substantive evidence that contamination is present, I am not
    persuaded that the extensive programme of phased investigation, remediation and

       use of the site, a condition requiring the investigation and remediation of any
       unsuspected contamination encountered during the works is justified. Such a

       satisfies the relevant tests for planning conditions.
26. The suggested conditions relating to a Construction Logistics Plan, cycle parking,
    parking permits, a Delivery and Servicing Plan, and waste and refuse provision
    seek to address matters that either fall outside the scope of the prior approval
    assessment or have not been demonstrated to be necessary having regard to the
    matters before me. Consequently, I have not imposed those conditions.
Conclusion
27. For the reasons given above the appeal should be allowed and prior approval
    should be granted.
