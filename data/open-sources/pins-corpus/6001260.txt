Appeal Ref: 6001260
Paddock land adjoining 39a Stone Lane, Lydiard Millicent
•    The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
     against a refusal to grant planning permission.
•    The appeal is made by Hills Homes Developments against the decision of Wiltshire Council.
•    The application Ref is PL/2024/07426.
•    The development proposed is development of site to provide 9 dwellings, with associated
     landscaping, sustainable drainage and primary vehicular access from Stone Lane.

Decision
1.     The appeal is allowed and planning permission is granted for development of site
       to provide 9 dwellings, with associated landscaping, sustainable drainage and
       primary vehicular access from Stone Lane at Paddock land adjoining 39a Stone
       Lane, Lydiard Millicent, in accordance with the terms of application
       Ref. PL/2024/07426 and subject to the Schedule of Conditions to this Decision.
Preliminary Matter
2.     Following the submission of the appeal, a new National Planning Policy
       Framework (the Framework) was published on 17 August 2026. The main parties
       were afforded an opportunity to comment on any implications arising from this. I
       have taken those submissions into account and have determined the appeal
       having regard to the Framework in force at the date of this Decision.
Main Issues
3.     The main issues are:
         i)        whether the site is a suitable location for housing having regard to the
                   development plan and national policy; and
         ii)       the effect of the proposal on highway safety.
Reasons
Suitability of location
4. The appeal site lies outside any defined settlement boundary and is therefore
   located within the countryside for the purposes of the development plan. Core
   Policy 1 of the Wiltshire Core Strategy (WCS) establishes the settlement hierarchy
   and directs most development towards the settlements identified within that
   hierarchy. WCS Core Policy 2 reinforces that approach by limiting development in
   the countryside to forms of development specifically supported by policy, whilst


https://www.gov.uk/planning-inspectorate
Appeal Decision 6001260


     WCS Core Policy 19 seeks to deliver growth within the Royal Wootton Bassett and
     Cricklade Community Area in accordance with the overarching spatial strategy.
5. The proposal would comprise residential development on unallocated land outside
   any defined settlement boundary and would not fall within any of the exceptions
   envisaged by those policies. Consequently, it would conflict with the settlement
   strategy of the development plan.
6. Whilst primarily a design policy, Policy LM1 of the Lydiard Millicent Neighbourhood
   Plan (NP) expressly supports infill development within the built area of Lydiard
   Millicent in accordance with Core Policies 1 and 2. The proposal would not
   constitute infill development within the built area of the village and would instead
   extend built form beyond the existing settlement edge. It therefore does not attract
   the support anticipated by that policy.
7. In reaching this finding, I have also had regard to the site's relationship with
   surrounding development and services. Whilst future occupiers would be likely to
   rely to some degree upon private car travel, the appeal site is not isolated from
   existing built development. It adjoins residential development associated with
   Common Platt and would benefit from connections to the wider pedestrian network,
   including the proposed footway link and associated pedestrian improvements. The
   site therefore occupies a location that is reasonably connected to surrounding
   development. Nevertheless, it remains outside any defined settlement boundary
   and is not a location identified for housing growth under the development plan.
   These considerations do not alter my finding of conflict with the settlement strategy,
   although they are relevant to my assessment of national policy later in this decision.
8. Taken together, these policies seek to direct housing growth towards planned and
   sustainable locations whilst protecting the countryside from unjustified
   encroachment and maintaining the distinctive character of Lydiard Millicent. I
   therefore find that the site is not in a suitable location for housing having regard to
   the spatial strategy of the development plan, particularly Core Policies 1, 2 and 19
   of the WCS, and conclude that the proposal would not attract the support
   envisaged by Policy LM1 of the NP.
Highway Safety
9. Stone Lane is a narrow carriageway of varying width and opportunities for
   pedestrian movement are currently limited. Based on my observations, I also
   acknowledge that larger vehicles would occasionally encounter difficulties passing
   one another and that some vehicles may need to wait or manoeuvre to negotiate
   constrained sections of the carriageway.
10. I accept that the proposed access would utilise a constrained section of Stone
    Lane. However, following amendments to the access position, visibility
    arrangements and supporting swept-path analysis, the Local Highway Authority
    was satisfied that the proposal could be accommodated safely and I have been
    presented with no substantive technical evidence that would justify departing from
    that specialist advice.
11. I have also considered in detail concerns regarding the relationship between the
    proposed site access and the existing accesses serving 39 and 40 Stone Lane. A
    number of interested parties contend that the submitted plans either failed to
    accurately depict, or did not sufficiently acknowledge, the driveway serving 39

     Stone Lane and consequently understated the potential for conflict between
     vehicles entering and leaving the existing properties and those accessing the
     proposed development. Similar concerns have been raised regarding the
     constrained nature of this section of Stone Lane, its proximity to the Common Platt
     junction and the effect of those constraints on vehicle movements.
12. However, the existence and position of the accesses serving Nos 39 and 40 Stone
    Lane were identified during the application process and formed the subject of
    consultation responses. Further swept-path analysis was undertaken and the
    access arrangements amended, including revisions to the bell-mouth geometry and
    visibility provision. Following consideration of those matters, the Local Highway
    Authority withdrew its objection and concluded that the proposal was acceptable in
    highway terms. The revised arrangements were also intended to ensure that the
    necessary visibility splays and associated highway works could be accommodated
    within land under the applicant's control or within the public highway.
13. The pedestrian arrangements also evolved during the application process following
    concerns raised regarding the original footway proposal along Stone Lane. The
    final scheme omits part of the footway provision originally proposed.
14. Consequently, pedestrians travelling between the two footway sections would be
    required to walk within the carriageway for a short distance of about 43m before
    rejoining a segregated route. Nevertheless, the revised scheme retains footway
    provision where it can practicably be accommodated and incorporates dropped
    kerbs, corduroy tactile paving and pedestrian visibility splays at both transition
    points.
15. Furthermore, the evidence indicates that vehicle speeds on Stone Lane are
    relatively low at the point where pedestrians would be required to walk within the
    carriageway. Those conditions are an important factor in assessing the safety of
    the proposed pedestrian arrangements and weigh in favour of the proposal.
16. Traffic flows are also relatively modest. Whilst I recognise local residents' concerns
    regarding larger vehicles and occasional congestion, the available evidence does
    not indicate a heavily trafficked road environment.
17. The submitted transport evidence also indicates that the development would
    generate only modest levels of pedestrian activity. Whilst such forecasts are
    estimates, they support the conclusion that pedestrian movements associated with
    the proposal would be limited.
18. The available accident records identify very few recorded personal injury accidents
    within the study area over an extended period and no recorded pedestrian injury
    accidents. Whilst residents refer to incidents and near misses that may not have
    been formally recorded, I nevertheless attach considerable weight to the objective
    accident evidence before me.
19. In addition, gateway features, speed reduction measures, roundel markings and
    carriageway narrowing would reinforce the existing 30mph speed limit on Stone
    Lane, providing wider highway safety benefits for both future occupiers and existing
    users of the highway.
20. Drawing these matters together, I have had regard to the proposed site access,
    visibility provision, swept-path analysis, pedestrian arrangements, accident history,

     traffic conditions and the wider highway mitigation measures. Whilst a continuous
     segregated footway would be preferable, pedestrians would be required to walk
     within the carriageway only for a short distance in circumstances where vehicle
     speeds and traffic volumes are relatively low. Having regard to those factors, I am
     satisfied that safe and suitable access can be achieved for all users, including
     pedestrians and other vulnerable road users.
21. Accordingly, I am not persuaded that the proposal would result in an unacceptable
    impact on highway safety or severe residual cumulative impacts on the road
    network. The proposal would therefore comply with Core Policy 61 of the WCS and
    Policy TR4 of the Framework, which seek to ensure that development provides
    safe and suitable access for all users and that transport impacts can be
    satisfactorily addressed.
Other considerations
22. The revised Framework introduces a new structure for decision-making through its
    Policies. The appeal site lies outside any defined settlement boundary and
    therefore Policy S5 of the Framework is relevant. Policy S5(1)(j) expressly supports
    development outside settlements where there is evidenced unmet housing need,
    including where a local planning authority cannot demonstrate a five-year supply of
    deliverable housing sites, provided that the development is physically well-related
    to an existing settlement and is of a scale capable of being accommodated by
    existing or proposed infrastructure.
23. The Council accepts that it can currently demonstrate only approximately 2.03
    years of housing land supply and therefore cannot demonstrate a five-year supply
    of deliverable housing sites. Accordingly, the unmet housing need element of Policy
    S5(1)(j) is satisfied.
24. The Council argues that the site is not physically well-related to a settlement
    because it lies some distance from the main built form of Lydiard Millicent and lacks
    a continuous protected pedestrian route. Whilst I have taken those matters into
    account, I do not read Policy S5 of the Framework as requiring a site to adjoin the
    principal built form of a particular settlement or to be served by a continuous
    segregated footway. Rather, the policy requires an assessment of whether a site is
    physically well-related to an existing settlement.
25. In this respect, the appeal site adjoins established residential development
    associated with Common Platt and is closely related to the wider developed edge
    of western Swindon. Whilst the absence of a continuous segregated footway
    weighs against the proposal, I do not consider that factor alone demonstrates that
    the site is not physically well-related to existing settlement development. Taking
    account of its relationship with surrounding development, the proposed pedestrian
    improvements and the scale of the proposal, I am satisfied that the site is physically
    well-related to an existing settlement for the purposes of Policy S5(1)(j)(i).
26. Furthermore, no objection has been raised by any infrastructure provider or
    technical consultee and no substantive evidence has been presented to
    demonstrate that the development would exceed the capacity of existing or
    proposed infrastructure.
27. Accordingly, whilst the proposal conflicts with the development plan settlement
    strategy, it falls within the category of development identified by Policy S5(1)(j) of

     the Framework. That policy indicates that such development should be approved
     unless its benefits are substantially outweighed by adverse effects. I therefore
     attach significant weight to that national policy support in the overall planning
     balance.
28. Although I have found conflict with the spatial strategy of the development plan, I
    have also had regard to Annex A of the Framework and the parties' submissions
    regarding the weight to be afforded to relevant development plan policies. I
    acknowledge the Council's position, which I broadly accept, that aspects of Core
    Policies 2 and 19 which restrict development outside defined settlement boundaries
    are materially inconsistent with Policy S5 of the Framework and, to that extent,
    attract very limited weight. Nevertheless, those policies continue to form part of the
    development plan and the conflict identified above remains a matter weighing
    against the proposal.
29. Policy HO7 of the Framework states that substantial weight should be given to the
    benefits of providing homes which contribute towards meeting identified housing
    needs. In this case, the proposal would provide nine dwellings at a time when the
    Council is unable to demonstrate a five-year supply of deliverable housing sites.
    Whilst limited in scale, the proposal would nevertheless make a meaningful
    contribution towards addressing the significant identified housing shortfall and I
    attach substantial weight to that benefit.
30. Furthermore, the proposal would contribute towards the vitality of local services and
    facilities, generate economic benefits during both the construction and occupation
    phases of the development, and provide biodiversity enhancements. I attach
    moderate weight to these benefits collectively.
Other Matters
31. The appeal site lies within the outer Zone of Influence (4.2km-9.4km) of the North
    Meadow and Clattinger Farm Special Area of Conservation (SAC). Residential
    development within this zone has the potential to increase recreational pressure on
    the SAC, either alone or in combination with other plans and projects.
32. As the competent authority, I have undertaken an Appropriate Assessment
    pursuant to the Conservation of Habitats and Species Regulations 2017 (as
    amended). I am satisfied that likely significant effects on the SAC cannot be
    excluded in the absence of mitigation.
33. A Unilateral Undertaking dated 20 October 2025 has been submitted pursuant to
    section 106 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990. It secures a Strategic
    Access Management and Monitoring (SAMM) contribution of £2,984.49 towards
    implementation of the North Meadow and Clattinger Farm SAC Interim Recreation
    Mitigation Strategy 2023-2028. The contribution must be paid prior to
    commencement of development.
34. I am satisfied that the obligation meets the tests in Regulation 122 of the
    Community Infrastructure Levy Regulations 2010 (as amended) and I have
    therefore taken it into account in determining the appeal.
35. Based on the evidence before me, including the Council's ecological assessment,
    the Appropriate Assessment undertaken by the Council, the views of Natural
    England and the completed Unilateral Undertaking, I conclude that the proposed


https://www.gov.uk/planning-inspectorate   5
Appeal Decision 6001260


     development would not adversely affect the integrity of the North Meadow and
     Clattinger Farm SAC, either alone or in combination with other plans or projects.
36. Concerns have been raised regarding flood risk and the potential for soakaways to
    exacerbate flooding affecting neighbouring properties, particularly given the
    elevated position of the site and local ground conditions. However, the Council's
    drainage adviser raises no objection in principle, subject to conditions. Those
    conditions would require demonstration that flood risk would not increase
    elsewhere and that infiltrated flows would not adversely affect neighbouring land. In
    the absence of compelling technical evidence to the contrary, I have no basis to
    conclude that the proposal would result in unacceptable flood risk.
37. I recognise concerns that the proposal would erode the open land separating
    Common Platt, Lydiard Millicent and the western edge of Swindon. The
    development would reduce the extent of undeveloped land in this location and
    would represent a noticeable encroachment into the countryside. However, it would
    neither physically coalesce the settlements nor remove all of the remaining open
    land between them. Whilst concerns regarding precedent are understandable, any
    future development proposals would be assessed on their own merits. I therefore
    attach only limited weight to concerns regarding future development beyond the
    identified development plan conflict. The proposal's encroachment into currently
    undeveloped land nevertheless attracts limited adverse weight, which I have taken
    into account as part of the overall conflict with the development plan.
38. The proposed dwellings would inevitably alter the outlook of neighbouring
    occupiers who currently adjoin open land. However, changes in outlook and the
    introduction of additional activity are normal consequences of residential
    development. From the evidence before me, and taking account of the separation
    distances and layout shown on the submitted plans, I am not persuaded that the
    proposal would result in unacceptable overlooking, loss of privacy or other living
    condition impacts sufficient to justify dismissal of the appeal. I have also considered
    concerns regarding vehicle headlights from traffic using the proposed access.
    However, having regard to the modest scale of the development, the anticipated
    level of vehicle movements and the existing highway environment, I am satisfied
    that any light intrusion would be limited and would not materially harm the living
    conditions of neighbouring occupiers.
39. The Council's Housing Enabling Team confirms that the application proposes
    between five and nine dwellings on a site measuring less than 0.5 hectares and
    therefore falls below the threshold at which affordable housing provision is required
    by adopted policy. On the evidence before me, I have no basis to conclude that the
    proposal fails to comply with the relevant affordable housing policy requirements.
40. The site is of relatively limited ecological value. The proposal incorporates
    ecological mitigation and enhancement measures, including habitat creation,
    ecological management, biodiversity enhancement features and biodiversity net
    gain. Whilst interested parties have sought additional measures, including
    enhanced swift nesting provision, such matters can be appropriately secured
    through the detailed discharge of planning conditions and the biodiversity measures
    proposed. Subject to the imposition of suitable conditions relating to ecological
    mitigation, habitat management and external lighting, I am satisfied that the
    proposal would not result in unacceptable harm to biodiversity or ecological
    interests and that these matters do not weigh against the proposal.

41. A number of interested parties express concern regarding the ability of children and
    other pedestrians to travel safely to schools, bus pick-up points and nearby
    facilities, particularly given the constrained nature of Stone Lane and the absence
    of a fully segregated pedestrian route throughout. I understand those concerns.
    However, neither the Highway Authority nor the Education Authority has identified
    the proposal as giving rise to unacceptable impacts in this regard and no objection
    has been raised on school transport, school accessibility or education capacity
    grounds. Whilst the absence of a continuous footway has been emphasised by
    interested parties, the evidence before me does not demonstrate that journeys to
    schools, school transport pick-up points or nearby facilities would be unsafe. In the
    context of a development of only nine dwellings, and having regard to my
    conclusions on highway safety generally, I attach limited weight to these concerns.
42. Whilst these matters have informed my assessment, none lead me to a different
    conclusion on the main issues before me.
Conditions
43. I have considered the conditions suggested by the Council having regard to the
    advice contained within the Framework and Planning Practice Guidance. In
    addition to the standard time limit for implementation, I have imposed a condition
    identifying the approved plans and documents in the interests of certainty.
44. Conditions are imposed requiring biodiversity enhancement measures, a
    Landscape and Ecology Management Plan, and the approval and control of
    external lighting. These conditions are necessary in the interests of biodiversity
    conservation and enhancement and to ensure that the mitigation and enhancement
    measures relied upon in reaching my decision are secured and maintained for the
    lifetime of the development.
45. A condition requiring archaeological investigation and recording before
    development commences is necessary in order to safeguard and appropriately
    record any archaeological remains which may be affected by the development.
46. Having regard to local concerns regarding drainage and flooding, I am satisfied that
    the surface water drainage condition is necessary to ensure that flood risk is
    appropriately managed, infiltrated flows do not adversely affect neighbouring land
    and flood risk does not increase elsewhere.
47. Conditions are imposed requiring the provision of the approved footway link,
    visibility splays, estate roads, footpaths, turning facilities, parking areas and
    gateway feature, together with the submission and implementation of a
    Construction Management Statement. These measures are necessary to ensure
    that the development is served by a safe and suitable means of access for all
    users, including pedestrians, and to minimise disruption and risk during the
    construction phase.
48. Conditions requiring the submission of details of external materials, an updated
    Arboricultural Impact Assessment including tree protection measures, and the
    implementation and maintenance of the approved landscaping scheme are
    necessary to safeguard the character and appearance of the area, secure a high
    standard of design, protect retained trees and ensure the successful integration of
    the development within its surroundings.

Planning Balance and Conclusion
52. Section 38(6) of the Planning and Compulsory Purchase Act 2004 requires
    planning applications and appeals to be determined in accordance with the
    development plan unless material considerations indicate otherwise.
53. For the reasons given above, I have found that the proposal conflicts with the
    spatial strategy of the development plan. The proposal also does not fall within the
    form of development supported by Policy LM1 of the NP. It would also result in the
    loss of a modest area of undeveloped land. Whilst the site would benefit from the
    proposed pedestrian improvements, future occupiers would nevertheless be likely
    to rely to some extent on private car travel. These matters weigh against the
    proposal.
54. However, the Council cannot demonstrate a five-year supply of deliverable housing
    sites. The proposal would provide nine dwellings and thereby contribute towards
    addressing an identified housing shortfall. Consistent with my findings above in
    relation to Policy HO7 of the Framework, I attach substantial weight to that benefit.
55. I have also found that the proposal falls within the category of development
    expressly supported by Policy S5(1)(j) of the Framework. That policy support
    attracts significant weight in favour of the proposal and weighs heavily in the overall
    planning balance.
56. Additional benefits would arise through support for local services and facilities,
    economic activity, and biodiversity enhancements. I attach moderate weight to
    these benefits collectively.
57. Taking all matters into account, I find that the material considerations in favour of
    the proposal, including the support provided by Policy S5(1)(j), the contribution
    towards housing delivery in the context of a significant housing land supply shortfall
    and the associated social, economic and environmental benefits, outweigh the
    identified conflict with the development plan. Accordingly, material considerations
    indicate that planning permission should be granted notwithstanding that conflict.
58. I, therefore, conclude that the appeal should be allowed.
