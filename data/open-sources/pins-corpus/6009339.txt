Newbiggin Farm West, Scarborough Road, Filey, North Yorkshire YO14 9PG
•   The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
    against a refusal to grant approval required under Article 3(1) and Schedule 2, Part 3, Class Q of the
    Town and Country Planning (General Permitted Development) (England) Order 2015 (as amended).
•   The appeal is made by Mr Jaspal Singh of Singhs 3 Ltd against the decision of North Yorkshire
    Council.
•   The application reference is ZF25/01158/P3Q.
•   The development proposed is ‘conversion of an existing steel framed agricultural building into a
    residential dwelling.’

Decision
1. The appeal is allowed and prior approval is granted under the provisions of Article
   3(1) and Schedule 2, Part 3, Class Q of the Town and Country Planning (General
   Permitted Development) (England) Order 2015 (as amended) (the 2015 GPDO) for
   conversion of an existing steel framed agricultural building into a residential
   dwelling at Newbiggin Farm West, Scarborough Road, Filey, North Yorkshire YO14
   9PG in accordance with the application ZF25/01158/P3Q dated 5 September 2025.
   The approval is subject to the condition that the development must be completed
   within a period of 3 years from the date of this decision in accordance with
   paragraph Q.2 (3) of the 2015 GPDO and subject to the following additional
   conditions:
         1) The development hereby permitted shall be carried out in accordance with
            the following approved plans: Ground Floor Plan as Proposed Drawing No.
            05 and Site Plan as Proposed Drawing No. 08 Rev B.
         2) Other than works necessary to undertake the investigation required by this
            condition, no development shall take place until a Phase 2 intrusive site
            investigation has been undertaken in accordance with the recommendations
            of the Phase 1 Desk Study Site Investigation Report (Geoinvestigate Ltd,
            December 2025) and the results submitted to and approved in writing by the
            local planning authority.
              If contamination is identified, no further development shall take place until a
              remediation strategy has been submitted to and approved in writing by the
              local planning authority. The approved remediation measures shall be
              implemented in full, and a verification report shall be submitted to and
              approved in writing by the local planning authority before the dwelling is
              occupied.
              If contamination not previously identified is found during development, work
              shall cease in the affected area until additional remediation measures have
              been submitted to and approved in writing by the local planning authority.

Background and Main Issues
2. The proposal comprises the change of use of an agricultural building to a dwelling
   together with the building operations shown on the submitted plans. The Council
   raises no objection to the proposal in relation to the applicable limitations and
   conditions in paragraph Q.1 of Schedule 2, Part 3, Class Q of the Order. On the
   evidence before me, I have no reason to reach a different conclusion.
3. The Council refused prior approval because insufficient information had been
   submitted concerning contamination risks. It also considered that the relationship
   between the proposed dwelling and nearby holiday accommodation, including an
   outdoor amenity area and hot tubs then situated between the buildings, would
   result in unacceptable noise and disturbance, light intrusion and loss of privacy for
   future occupants.
4. As part of the appeal, the appellant submitted a Phase 1 Desk Study Site
   Investigation Report (December 2025) (Phase 1 Report). I have taken this
   additional evidence into account. At my site visit, I also observed that the hot tubs
   and the fencing that had enclosed the associated amenity area had been removed.
5. The main issues are therefore whether:
           •    contamination risks can be satisfactorily addressed; and
           •    the location or siting of the building would make its change to residential
                use impractical or undesirable, having particular regard to the living
                conditions of future occupants in respect of noise and disturbance, light
                intrusion and privacy.
Reasons
Contamination
6. The appeal building is a steel-framed agricultural structure within a working
   agricultural holding. Agricultural activities can give rise to contaminants associated
   with, among other things, fuels, machinery, asbestos-containing materials and
   agricultural chemicals. The absence of a formal investigation at the application
   stage therefore left uncertainty as to whether the site would be suitable for
   residential occupation.
7. The Phase 1 Report identifies the agricultural use of the site, former buildings near
   the appeal building and a nearby storage tank as potential sources of
   contamination. However, the site walkover found no obvious odours, staining or
   residues indicative of chemical or hydrocarbon contamination, and no indication of
   substantial made ground.
8. The report explains that the anticipated low-permeability clay deposits would
   restrict the migration of contaminants into or out of the site. On a precautionary
   basis, it assesses the potential for harmful contamination from historical sources as
   low. It assesses the risks from hazardous gas as very low, although that conclusion
   would require reconsideration if significant made ground were encountered.

9. These findings do not establish that the site is free from contamination. The report
   recommends a Phase 2 intrusive investigation, including soil analysis, to establish
   the actual ground conditions and any need for remediation. Nevertheless, no
   source or pathway has been identified that indicates contamination of such a nature
   or extent that residential use would be unlikely to be achievable. The remaining
   uncertainty can instead be resolved through further investigation and, if necessary,
   remediation before the proposed dwelling is occupied.
10. Paragraph W of Schedule 2, Part 3 of the Order permits prior approval to be
    granted subject to conditions reasonably related to the subject matter of the prior
    approval. A condition requiring further investigation, any necessary remediation and
    verification would be directly related to contamination risks and would ensure that
    the site is suitable for its intended residential use.
11. I therefore conclude that contamination risks can be satisfactorily addressed by a
    suitably worded condition.
Location and Siting
12. The appeal building forms part of a loose group of development that includes
    agricultural buildings, former agricultural buildings in use as holiday
    accommodation and a farmhouse. The holiday accommodation lies to the south of
    the appeal building, with a separation of more than 13m between the facing
    elevations.
13. When the application was determined, part of the intervening land was enclosed
    and used as outdoor amenity space for the holiday accommodation. This included
    hot tubs positioned considerably closer to the appeal building than the holiday
    accommodation itself. The Council was concerned that activity associated with the
    use of that area, together with the proximity of the holiday accommodation and its
    parking area, would expose future occupants of the proposed dwelling to
    unacceptable noise, disturbance, light intrusion and loss of privacy.
14. The Council’s Environmental Health Officer identified the potential for noise
    associated with the hot tubs, especially during evenings and weekends, as well as
    disturbance from vehicle movements and the direct relationship between the uses.
    However, no formal objection was raised, and it was considered that the identified
    effects could be addressed through design measures or conditions.
15. At the time of my site visit, the hot tubs had been removed. The fencing that had
    enclosed the associated outdoor amenity space had also been removed.
    Consequently, the arrangement that principally informed the Council’s concerns no
    longer existed. Although those former circumstances provide relevant background
    to the decision under appeal, I have determined the appeal on the basis of the
    plans provided and the physical relationship that now exists.
16. The space immediately behind the holiday accommodation is narrow and lies below
    the level of the adjoining yard. Glazed doors and windows serving the holiday units
    face onto that space. Given its confined form, it functions as a passage providing
    access to and around the holiday accommodation, rather than as an appreciable
    area for outdoor recreation or congregation.
17. Beyond that passage is an open yard or hardstanding extending towards the
    appeal building. The absence of the former enclosure means that there is no longer

     a defined outdoor amenity area for the holiday accommodation positioned close to
     the proposed dwelling. The intervening yard, together with the separation of more
     than 13m between the facing elevations, provides a clear physical distinction
     between the buildings.
18. The submitted plans show openings in the appeal building facing generally towards
    the holiday accommodation. Nevertheless, the separation between the buildings
    and the character of the intervening space would prevent an unacceptably direct or
    oppressive relationship. Future occupants may be aware of people entering or
    leaving the holiday accommodation, and there may be some intervisibility between
    the buildings, but this would not result in an unacceptable loss of privacy.
19. Vehicle movements and activity associated with the holiday accommodation would
    generate some noise and light, particularly during guest arrivals and departures.
    However, such activity would be intermittent and limited in duration. The proposed
    dwelling would also be situated within an established farmstead containing
    agricultural, residential and holiday accommodation uses, where a degree of
    vehicular activity and associated disturbance would reasonably be expected.
20. The fact that the adjoining accommodation is occupied by holidaymakers does not,
    of itself, establish that it would be an incompatible neighbour to a permanent
    dwelling. There is no substantive evidence of persistent noise, unusually intensive
    occupation or other activity associated with the holiday accommodation that would
    be likely to cause unacceptable disturbance to future occupants of the proposed
    dwelling.
21. Taking account of the present relationship between the buildings, including their
    separation, the narrow passage to the rear of the holiday accommodation and the
    intervening yard or hardstanding, future occupants of the proposed dwelling would
    not be exposed to unacceptable noise or disturbance, light intrusion or loss of
    privacy. I therefore conclude that the location or siting of the appeal building would
    not make its change to residential use impractical or undesirable.
Other Matters
22. Concern has been expressed about the suitability of the access, the availability of
    parking and provision for emergency vehicles. However, the proposal would use
    the established access serving the farmstead, and the submitted plans identify two
    parking spaces for the proposed dwelling. The Council did not identify transport or
    highway impacts as a reason for refusal, and there is no substantive evidence
    before me that the limited additional movements associated with one dwelling
    would result in unacceptable effects.
23. It is also suggested that the building remains necessary for agricultural purposes.
    However, no detailed evidence has been provided to demonstrate that its loss
    would prejudice the operation of the agricultural holding. In any event, the
    continuing agricultural need for an otherwise qualifying building is not a matter for
    prior approval under paragraph Q.2 of Class Q.
24. Reference has also been made to a nearby dyke and land drain. The Phase 1
    Report records a nearby watercourse but identifies negligible surface-water flood
    risk and low groundwater flood risk. There is no technical evidence before me to
    demonstrate that the proposed dwelling would be at unacceptable flood risk.

Conditions
25. For certainty, I have imposed a condition requiring the development to be carried
    out in accordance with the approved plans. I have also imposed a contamination
    condition because the Phase 1 Report identifies a low preliminary risk of
    contamination and recommends intrusive investigation to establish the actual
    ground conditions and any remediation requirements.
26. The parties have suggested that planting or screening could be provided between
    the proposed dwelling and the holiday accommodation. However, the submitted
    plans do not identify land upon which such measures would be provided. In any
    event, given my findings concerning the separation and relationship between the
    uses, such measures are not necessary to make the development acceptable. I
    have therefore not imposed a condition requiring boundary treatment or
    landscaping.
Conclusion
27. For the reasons given above, the appeal should be allowed.
