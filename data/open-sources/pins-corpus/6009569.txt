Background and Main Issue
3. In 2004, the Council granted planning permission for conversion of the building
   known as School House Barn to form two dwellings, along with a single storey
   extension1 (“the 2004 planning permission”). This development has lawfully
   commenced, as formally confirmed by the Council through the issue of a lawful
   development certificate.
4. Appeal A relates to a section 78 planning appeal following refusal of planning
   permission for the installation of a pedestrian gateway to Circular Road within the
   existing stone wall, and external alterations to the School House Barn elevation
   facing High Street. Such alterations would extinguish the existing entrance door off
   High Street, and the appellants present all above works as a betterment to highway
   safety. Appeal B follows the Council’s refusal of a section 73 application seeking to
   remove a condition relating to the approved High Street entrance door, and to vary
   a condition that restricts additional vehicle/pedestrian access to the appeal site.
5. Both the above applications are clearly interrelated and dependent on each other. I
   have thus combined both decisions into a single decision letter.
6. I am aware that the Council refused permission in 2022 and 2024 for installation of
   a pedestrian doorway off Circular Road, and that the earlier application was
   dismissed at appeal2 in September 2022. Whilst this planning history is a significant
   material consideration, I must also have regard to the other submitted evidence
   before me. The 2022 appeal decision also relates to a materially different type of
   development in terms of layout. I have determined the current appeals accordingly.
7. On 17 August 2026, the Government issued a revised version of the National
   Planning Policy Framework (the Framework). I do not consider that there is any
   need to revert to the main parties regarding this. This is because its new policies for
   decision making do not substantially alter the policies in the previous Framework
   relevant to the main issue of this appeal, which is:
            •   the effect of the proposed development on highway safety, with particular
                regard to users of Circular Road and High Street.
Reasons
8. The Council refers to the visibility requirements set out in table E/5.2 within CD 143
   (Designing for walking, cycling and horse-riding) of the Design Manual for Roads
   and Bridges (DMRB). However, the DMRB requirements typically apply to
   motorways and trunk roads. Moreover, the DMRB sets out that on roads that are
   not part of the trunk road network, use of the DMRB could result in significant over-
   specification. In such circumstances, alternative documents such as the Manual for
   Streets (MfS) may be used.

9. I therefore give very little weight to CD143’s recommended absolute minimum X
   distance3 of 1.5m. MfS however does not provide specific guidance regarding
   pedestrian access visibility requirements. Neither does Manual for Streets 2 (MfS2),
   its subsequent companion guide. Whether or not a pedestrian access proposal will
   be inherently unsafe is therefore a matter of judgement for me as the decision
   maker, taking account of the evidence before me and my site visit observations.
10. Circular Road has a two-way vehicle layout and is of narrow width in the vicinity of
    the proposed gate location. Although there is significant double yellow line
    coverage along this road, there is unrestricted parking in the vicinity of the
    proposed gate access, effectively reducing the carriageway to single vehicle width
    when vehicles are parked. All available on-street parking space in that area was
    occupied at time of my site visit, which was during a Friday late morning period
    within the main summer holiday period. Although my site visit was a snapshot in
    time, vehicular traffic levels and speeds along this road were low.
11. Circular Road is subject to a 30mph speed limit. However, the automatic traffic
    count (ATC) surveys before me (undertaken in February 2023 and August 2024)
    recorded the 85th percentile vehicle speed4 on this road as being between 13mph
    and 15.4mph. Average weekday traffic flows recorded by the August 2024 ATC
    were almost double those recorded in February 2023. This seems to reflect the
    area’s popularity during summertime, and could also account for the slightly lower
    vehicle speeds than as recorded in February 2023.
12. Based on the evidence before me and my site visit observations, Circular Road in
    the vicinity of the appeal site is a low-speed traffic environment. Traffic frequency is
    also generally low. The narrow width of Circular Road undoubtedly reduces
    vehicular speeds, including those entering from High Street, in particular when
    vehicles are parked. Whilst vehicles could enter or leave the on-street parking area
    adjacent to the proposed gate access, this would typically occur at very low speed.
13. The proposed gate would be set back slightly from the carriageway edge, on
    account of the thickness of the stone wall opening to each side. Additionally, an
    access protection marking (or ‘H’-bar marking) would be provided across the gate
    access to ensure that vehicles do not block it. This would run slightly behind the
    double yellow lines approaching the High Street crossroads junction.
14. The above design features would offer some protection for pedestrians when
    stepping onto the highway from the gate access, including when manoeuvring a
    child with a stroller. This is because even when vehicles are not parked, the
    boundary wall, along with the existing double-yellow lines, externally illuminated
    highway sign, electricity pole and affixed public bin, would keep vehicle movements
    away from the immediate carriageway edge across the proposed gate, including
    vehicles manoeuvring to park or exiting from a parked position.
15. The appellants accept that pedestrians crossing a road should be seen by, and be
    able to see, approaching vehicles as far as possible. In this respect, the proposed
    gate would not be served by a footway as a refuge. I also observed poor visibility
    beyond the line of parked vehicles on the right-hand side of the proposed egress
    location. Due to the temporary and varying nature of vehicle parking, that cannot
    always be relied upon to provide refuge for nearby pedestrians.

16. The gate access would thus not achieve an entirely safe environment for all users.
    This is due to potential conflict between future access users and vehicle
    movements on Circular Road, in particular children and those with restricted
    mobility or other physical impediments. For the above reasons, I envisage such
    highway safety risk to be low in terms of severity and probability. Nonetheless, as a
    standalone proposal, the gate access conflicts with Policy DM2 of the Island Plan5
    insofar as it expects development proposals to provide a safe environment.
17. The proposed external alterations to the western elevation of School House Barn
    would however extinguish its existing pedestrian access off High Street. This
    existing access has significantly better visibility than the proposed gated access,
    when measured approximately 0.5m back from the carriageway edge. The main
    parties nonetheless accept that this access door and crossing point on to High
    Street is substandard and following my site visit, I have no reason to disagree.
18. I observed that whilst vehicles tended to traverse along the middle of the one-way
    High Street carriageway, such movements were still in close proximity to this
    existing access that is not served by a segregated footway. This proximity between
    those exiting the site and vehicles is similar to that which would typically arise from
    the proposed gate access. This existing access is thus also not safe for all users.
19. Additionally, I observed High Street to have significantly more vehicular and
    pedestrian traffic. Whilst my observations were only a snapshot in time, they
    correlated with the ATC survey recordings showing average weekday traffic flows
    along High Street to be several times greater than those travelling in either direction
    along Circular Road, including outside of the summertime period.
20. Moreover, the ATC surveys recorded the 85th percentile vehicle speed on High
    Street to range from 14.6mph (August 2024) to 17.8mph (February 2023). Both
    these averages are higher than those recorded on Circular Road. I also note that
    the August 2024 ATC location on Circular Road was on a straight section of
    highway and considerably further away from the High Street crossroads junction
    than the proposed gate access location. This suggests that average vehicle speeds
    immediately passing the proposed gate access would likely be significantly lower
    than as recorded in that ATC.
21. Taking all above matters into account, I conclude that replacing the existing High
    Street access with the proposed gated access off Circular Road would provide a
    minor betterment to highway safety, in terms of reducing risk of conflict between
    pedestrians and vehicles. In this respect, the overall proposed scheme complies
    with Policy DM17 of the Island Plan insofar as it seeks development proposals to
    improve accessibility for pedestrian transport.
22. Given the above Policy DM17 compliance, and that the extent of conflict with Policy
    DM2 that I have identified is minor, the overall proposal complies with the
    development plan taken as a whole. As the residual highway safety risk would be
    minor, the proposed scheme would not amount to an unacceptable impact on
    highway safety. There is thus no conflict with the Framework in this respect.

Other Matters
23. I do not view the lack of officially recorded vehicle accidents in recent years, at
    either the existing High Street access location or the proposed Circular Road gate
    access location, as a reliable indicator of highway safety. This is because only
    crashes which involve personal injuries are recorded by the police. This matter has
    thus had no bearing on my determination of this appeal.
24. At the time of my site visit, I observed a bicycle and a motorcycle parked by the
    proposed gate. The proposed site plan indicates that there would be sufficient
    space retained between the proposed gate and that of Pear Tree Cottage for three
    smaller cars to park in. Two larger cars were parked within this section of highway
    at the time of my visit, with no space for additional cars. The above indicates that
    the proposal would result in a minor loss of car and cycle parking, with the precise
    loss dependent on the size and location of vehicles parked on any given day. I also
    observed limited parking availability on nearby roads.
25. However, the above does not amount to an adverse level of parking loss to warrant
    dismissal of these appeals. Alteration to existing speed limits, or installation of
    speed control measures, are beyond the scope of these appeals, as these would
    require a separate Traffic Regulation Order to be administered by the Council as
    highway authority.
26. The appeal site is within Seaview Conservation Area (SCA), specifically within Area
    1 (The Historic Core) as set out in the SCA Appraisal (adopted 2007). The location
    of the proposed pedestrian gate is within the setting of the Grade II Listed Building
    of “Alton House Shirley Cottage”6 (LB), which faces High Street. The tall stone wall
    enclosing the site along Circular Road and High Street is also identified by the
    Council as a non-designated heritage asset, and following my site visit, I have no
    reason to disagree.
27. Section 72 of the Planning (Listed Buildings and Conservation Areas) Act 1990
    requires special attention be paid to the desirability of preserving or enhancing the
    character or appearance of a conservation area. From what I have seen and read,
    the significance of the SCA as a whole is derived from, amongst other things, its
    historic status as a coastal resort. This is still appreciated today in terms of use,
    movement and visual character, in part by the narrow streets and the decorative
    commercial and residential buildings.
28. The proposed pedestrian gateway would have a modest width and height. Its scale
    and appearance, including lintel and quoin detailing along its surrounds, would be
    broadly similar to the existing gate serving Pear Tree Cottage that is within the
    same wall. Although the proposal would involve a small loss of historic fabric that
    positively contributes to the SCA’s significance, it would not change the wall’s
    function or appearance to a meaningful degree. The retained wall would still
    provide an enclosed and traditional feel, as identified by the SCA Appraisal.
29. The character and appearance of the wall, and the SCA as a whole, would
    therefore be preserved, and its significance as a designated heritage asset would
    not be harmed. Allowing these appeals therefore avoids conflict with the statutory
    presumption under s72(1) of the Act.

30. Section 66(1) of the Listed Buildings and Conservation Areas Act 1990 requires me
    to have special regard to the desirability of preserving the listed building or its
    setting or any features of special architectural or historic interest which it
    possesses. Intervisibility between the proposed gate and the LB is limited due to
    the angled distance and intervening buildings along High Street. The proposal
    would thus not detract from or harm the LB’s special character, historic interest and
    setting, either in visual or spatial terms. There is no cogent evidence before me to
    demonstrate that the wall would be physically compromised by the proposed works.
31. The lack of harm in the above respects neither weighs in favour nor against
    allowing these appeals. Although concern has been expressed that the proposed
    gate may be widened for vehicles in future, that would be a materially different
    development type that would require additional express planning permission.
    Allowing these appeals does not set any sort of precedent for the above, noting
    also that “off street parking altering the rhythm of the street” is listed in the SCA
    Appraisal as one of several negative elements within the SCA.
32. Construction works, including those in relation to the 2004 planning permission,
    would be temporary. Any statutory nuisance arising from such works would be
    subject to action from the Council under separate legislation, if deemed necessary.
    Any change to the value of the appeal site following these appeal decisions, when
    compared to its previous sale, has no bearing on my determination of these
    appeals, as property prices can change for a wide variety of reasons.
Conditions
33. For both appeals, I have considered the Council’s suggested conditions against the
    tests in the Framework and the Planning Practice Guidance, and have amended
    their wording, where necessary. For Appeal A, condition No 1 is necessary in the
    interests of planning certainty. Condition No 2 is necessary to clarify the approved
    plans. Condition No 3 is necessary in the interests of the character of the SCA and
    the settings of surrounding heritage assets.
34. Condition Nos 4 and 5 are necessary in the interests of highway safety, the special
    character of the SCA and surrounding heritage assets. I have deleted reference in
    condition No 5 to “unless otherwise approved in writing by the Local Planning
    Authority” as it is superfluous.
35. The Council has suggested a condition governing any entrance door on the
    western building elevation. However, as the above referenced condition No 5 will
    remove permitted development rights for new windows and doors, this suggested
    condition is rendered superfluous, as the proposed scheme does not include such
    an entrance door. For the same reason, I have deleted the Council’s suggested
    condition no 3 for Appeal B.
36. Turning to the newly imposed conditions for Appeal B, condition Nos 1-3 are
    necessary in the interests of the special character of the SCA and the settings of
    heritage assets. Condition No 4 is necessary in the interests of highway safety, the
    character of the SCA and the settings of surrounding heritage assets.
Conclusion
37. For the reasons set out above, I have found that the overall proposed scheme is in
    accordance with the development plan as a whole, and the material considerations
