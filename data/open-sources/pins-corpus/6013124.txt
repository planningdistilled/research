Decision
1. The appeal is dismissed.
Preliminary Matter
2. The National Planning Policy Framework (the Framework) has been revised since
   the parties made their original submissions, however the revisions are of limited
   relevance to this appeal.
Main Issue
3. The main issue is the effect of the proposal on the living conditions of the current or
   future occupiers of 57 St James Avenue (No 57), particularly in relation to outlook
   and light.
Reasons
4. The appeal property is a detached dwelling with first floor accommodation in the
   roof space. It is within a residential area characterised by bungalows and two-
   storey dwellings of varied design. As with other properties in the area, the design of
   the appeal property means that its side bathroom dormer window directly faces the
   side bedroom dormer window of No 57, thus avoiding habitable room windows
   facing each other.
5. The proposal would introduce a two-storey side extension with a front facing
   dormer window. On site I observed that the side elevation to No 57 includes a door
   and windows at ground floor level. The room served by the larger of these windows
   is described as a utility room in recent sales particulars. However, it was previously
   a kitchen and is of a size capable of being used as a study. The occupiers state it is
   presently used as a craft room. I have considered the application on the basis that
   this window serves a main habitable room (Habitable Room). The window is
   designed as clear glazed, but the evidence suggests that at some point it has been

     covered in a temporary opaque film. I have assessed the proposal on the basis that
     the window is designed as clear glazed.
6. The Chester West and Chester Council - House Extensions and Domestic
   Outbuildings Supplementary Planning Document (2021) (SPD) states that a
   habitable room can include a study and would usually have one clear glazed
   window. The SPD also states that although a separation distance of 13 metres from
   a habitable room window to a blank wall should be sought, the existing orientation
   of land and buildings will be taken into account.
7. The present outlook from the Habitable Room is already compromised to some
   extent as it faces the side elevation of the appeal property and does not have the
   separation distance sought in the SPD. However, even with the proposed roof
   central ridge design, the height and proximity of the two-storey appeal proposal to
   the Habitable Room window of No 57 would create a sense of overbearing and
   worsen the outlook further to an unacceptable level.
8. The appellant has not submitted a technical sunlight/daylight assessment.
   Nevertheless, having regard to the orientation of the dwellings and the proximity
   and height of the two-storey extension I consider there would be an unacceptable
   loss of light to the Habitable Room. Overall, I find that the proposal would cause
   unacceptable harm to the living conditions of the current or future occupiers of No
   57 by way of loss of outlook and light.
9. Consequently, I find that the proposal conflicts with policies DM2 and DM21 of the
   Cheshire West and Chester Local Plan (Part Two) (2019) and the SPD that seek to
   secure an appropriate standard of residential amenity and avoid unacceptable
   harm to neighbouring occupiers.
Other Matters
10. My attention has been drawn to a purported fallback position, comprising a single
    storey side extension that could be closer to No 57 than the proposal. Limited
    details of such a fallback have been provided, and it is not advanced as anything
    more than a theoretical possibility. In any event, due to the height of the appeal
    proposal, I consider it would be likely to have a worse effect on the outlook and light
    available to No 57 than the purported fallback position.
11. The Council’s Officer Report refers to the proposal having been designed to meet
    the complex medical needs of the appellant which should be considered in the
    context of the Public Sector Equality Duty contained in the Equality Act 2010. There
    is no evidence before me to substantiate that the proposal is needed for these
    reasons, or that it is the only way such needs could be met. I am therefore not in a
    position to assess whether such needs would outweigh the harm caused by the
    proposal.
12. The proposal would provide additional floorspace within the curtilage. Policy L2 of
    the Framework provides that substantial weight be given to this benefit. However,
    the Framework also states that such proposals should maintain acceptable living
    standards for residents and neighbours in terms of, amongst other things, access to
    daylight and sunlight. Given the harm I have found that would be caused to the
    living conditions of the current or future occupiers of No 57 by way of loss of
    outlook and light it has not been necessary for me to afford substantial weight to
    the benefits of the additional floorspace in this case.

13. There would be no unacceptable harm in relation to other matters including
    highway safety and the character and appearance of the area. These are neutral
    considerations that do not weigh in favour of allowing the appeal.
Planning Balance and Conclusion
14. There would be modest benefits resulting from the continued use and adaptation of
    an existing home within a settlement, along with improvements to the family
    accommodation. However, these benefits would be small, largely personal to the
    appellant, and would not outweigh the harm that I have found to the living
    conditions of the current or future occupiers of No 57.
15. Accordingly, I find that the proposal conflicts with the development plan and the
    material considerations do not indicate that the appeal should be decided other
    than in accordance with it. I therefore conclude that the appeal should be
    dismissed.
