•    The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
     against a refusal to grant approval required under Article 3(1) and Schedule 2, Part 3, Class Q of the
     Town and Country Planning (General Permitted Development) (England) Order 2015 (as amended).
•    The appeal is made by Mr & Mrs Sansom against the decision of Cheshire East Council.
•    The application reference is 26/1059/PRIOR-3Q.
•    The development proposed is prior approval for change of use to a 3 bedroom single storey dwelling
     with the addition of a single storey extension to the rear elevation.

Decision
1.     The appeal is allowed and prior approval is granted under the provisions of Article
       3(1) and Schedule 2, Part 3, Class Q of the Town and Country Planning (General
       Permitted Development) (England) Order 2015 (as amended) for prior approval for
       change of use to a 3 bedroom single storey dwelling with the addition of a single
       storey extension to the rear elevation at Paddock House, Paddock Lane, Audlem
       CW3 0DP, in accordance with the application Ref 26/1059/PRIOR-3Q and the
       details submitted with it.
Preliminary Matters
2.     I have utilised the description of development given on the decision notice, as this
       is a greater reflection of the scheme before me, rather than what is given in the
       application form.
3.     Under Schedule 2, Part 3, Class Q of the General Permitted Development
       (England) Order 2015 (as amended) (GPDO), development is permitted for the
       change of use of a building and any land within its curtilage, from use as an
       agricultural building to a use falling within Class C3 (dwellinghouses) together with
       building operations reasonably necessary to convert that building, subject to
       limitations and conditions. Development under Class Q is subject to a number of
       limitations and conditions, as set out in Paragraph Q.1. These include restrictions
       relating to the nature and extent of building operations proposed.
Main Issue
4.     The main issue is whether the extent of building operations proposed would fall
       within the scope of being reasonably necessary for conversion.
Reasons
5.     The Council is concerned that the proposed works would go beyond that which
       could be deemed as reasonably necessary to convert the building. The Planning
       Practice Guidance (PPG) states that the intention of Class Q is not to allow

         rebuilding works that would go beyond that reasonably necessary for the
         conversion of the agricultural building to residential use.
6.       Class Q1(j) of the GPDO permits building operations, provided those operations
         are limited to the installation or replacement of windows, doors, roofs, exterior
         walls, and services, to the extent reasonably necessary for the building to function
         as a dwellinghouse. The PPG clarifies that the permitted development right
         assumes the agricultural building is capable of functioning as a dwelling, and that it
         is not the intention of the right to allow rebuilding work which would go beyond
         what is reasonably necessary for conversion. In this context, the Hibbitt1 judgment
         held that the building must be capable of conversion to residential use without
         operations amounting to complete or substantial re-building of the pre-existing
         structure or, in effect, the creation of a new building.
7.       A Structural Report (prepared by C2C Consulting Engineers Ltd, dated 7 January
         2026), was submitted in support of the application. The report concludes that the
         building is capable of conversion, subject to the completion of certain works. In
         particular, the report identifies the need for insulated blockwork to provide an
         internal thermal envelope behind the existing timber cladding. The PPG states that
         internal works do not generally constitute development. Furthermore, where
         necessary to enable a building to function as a dwelling, internal structural works,
         including the installation of internal walls, may be undertaken and are not
         prohibited under Class Q.
8.       Given that the building is proposed for human habitation, the installation of
         insulated blockwork is considered to be a form of work that would reasonably be
         expected as part of the conversion of a timber agricultural building to residential
         use. The primary purpose of the blockwork is to provide a thermal envelope,
         ensuring the building meets modern standards where human habitation is
         concerned. Whilst the Structural Report notes that the blockwork may also provide
         supplementary structural support and assist with load distribution, these functions
         are incidental and secondary to its principal role in facilitating the building's use as
         a dwelling. As such, the inclusion of this element does not, in itself, indicate that
         the works extend beyond those reasonably necessary to enable the building's
         conversion under Class Q.
9.       During my site visit, I noted that whilst only constructed of timber, the roof trusses
         and walls each looked to be in a relatively adequate condition for a building of its
         age, which were clearly supporting the existing building in its current form. This
         inspection illustrated to me that the provision of internal blockwork would not be
         necessary to support the existing building. But, its primary provision in providing an
         internal thermal envelope would also provide extra support. In construction terms,
         these functions are not mutually exclusive, and the blockwork can reasonably
         serve both purposes simultaneously. My observations at my site visit therefore
         broadly align with the conclusions of the structural report.
10. At this point, I acknowledge that, during the appeal process, the Council noted that
    no assessment had been provided to demonstrate that the existing floor slab is
    capable of supporting the proposed internal blockwork. During my site visit, I
    observed that the floor slab appeared to be in good condition overall, with no
    obvious signs of significant structural deterioration. The Structural Report also

11. My attention now turns to the concern regarding the replacement of external timber
    cladding. The Structural Report identifies a number of localised areas where
    cladding would require replacement due to damage or loss. During my site visit, I
    observed that these areas were generally limited in extent and related to sections
    of missing or deteriorated cladding. In the context of the building as a whole, the
    affected areas represented only a small proportion of the overall external
    envelope.
12. Importantly, Class Q expressly permits the partial replacement of exterior walls
    where reasonably necessary to facilitate the conversion of an agricultural building
    to residential use. In this case, the extent of replacement required is limited to a
    number of small, localised repairs and does not amount to substantial rebuilding of
    the structure. I am therefore satisfied that the proposed replacement of damaged
    or missing cladding would fall within the scope of works that could reasonably be
    described as a conversion, rather than a rebuild, and would not exceed the
    limitations of Class Q.
13. Accordingly, I find that these proposed works fall within the scope of development
    permitted under Class Q. Having regard to the evidence submitted and my
    observations at my visit, the building appears to be robust and in reasonable
    condition. The main structural elements would be retained and utilised in the
    conversion, and the proposed works would not involve the substantial replacement
    of the building’s fabric. Having regard to the provisions of the GPDO, the PPG and
    Hibbitt, the cumulative extent of the works would not amount to the creation of a
    new building, and would instead fall within the scope of operations reasonably
    necessary to facilitate the building’s conversion to residential use.
14. The Council has referred to previous appeal decisions2 in support of its position.
    However, notwithstanding that none of these cited decisions lie within Cheshire
    East, the full details of those decisions and the specific development proposals
    involved have not been provided. Each building in these cases, and the building
    before me, will have their own characteristics and will be in a different condition to
    one another. Therefore, these considerations do not alter my previous findings.
15. Accordingly, the proposed development complies with the limitations set out in the
    GPDO and the PPG.
Conditions
16. Paragraph Q.2(4) of Schedule 2, Part 3, Class Q of the GPDO states that
    development under Class Q is permitted subject to the condition that it must be
    completed within a period of 3 years starting with the prior approval date.
    Paragraph W (12) of Schedule 2, Part 3 of the GPDO requires the development to
    be carried out in accordance with the approved details. Therefore, it is not
    necessary to repeat these requirements as conditions.

Conclusion
18. For the reasons given above, I conclude that the appeal should be allowed, and
    prior approval granted.
