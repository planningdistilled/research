Decision
Appeal A
1. The appeal is dismissed.
Appeal B
2. The appeal is dismissed and express consent is refused for the advertisements as
   applied for.
Preliminary Matters
3. There are two appeals at the site. Appeal A relates to the refusal of planning
   permission, while Appeal B is against the refusal of advertisement consent. They
   are intrinsically linked and raise similar issues. Therefore, to avoid repetition, I have
   detailed the findings under a single reasoning section. Notwithstanding this, I
   confirm that I have determined each appeal separately and on their own merits in
   accordance with the respective processes and requirements that I am required to
   undertake.
4. Since the determination of the application the National Planning Policy Framework
   (2024) has been superseded by the National Planning Policy Framework (2026)

Main Issue
5. The main issue in Appeal A is the effect of the proposed development on highway
   and public safety, with particular regard to the safety of pedestrians and cyclists
   using the footway and nearby cycle route.
6. The main issue in Appeal B is the effect of the advertisements on public safety.
Reasons
7. The National Planning Practice Guidance1 confirms that all advertisements are
   intended to attract attention but proposed advertisements at points where road
   users need to take more care are more likely to affect public safety. For example, at
   junctions, roundabouts, pedestrian crossings, on the approach to a low bridge or
   level crossing or other places where local conditions present traffic hazards.
8. The appeal site is an area of pavement outside commercial premises on Hull Road.
   The proposed hub would be located within the adopted highway in a pedestrian
   area where cyclists enter a short off-road cycle track leading from the eastbound
   carriageway of Hull Road towards Millfield Avenue.
9. Although the footway is relatively wide in general terms, the relevant safety issue is
   not limited to the remaining width available to pedestrians. The hub would occupy a
   position close to where the cycle route meets the pedestrian environment. Given
   its height, width and orientation, it would form a substantial obstruction in the line of
   sight between cyclists entering the off-road cycle track and pedestrians moving
   across or emerging from behind the unit.
10. The appellant argues that the footway would remain sufficiently wide, that the
    nearby highway junction is some distance away, and that the advertisements would
    be static images changing only at set intervals. However, those points do not
    overcome the more direct concern arising from intervisibility between cyclists and
    pedestrians at the point where their movements may conflict.
11. I acknowledge that there is other street furniture in the vicinity, including bollards
    and a bus shelter. Nevertheless, the proposed structure would introduce a large,
    fixed object at a sensitive point on the footpath.
12. For Appeal A, I conclude that the proposed development would have an
    unacceptable effect on highway and public safety. The proposed development
    would conflict with Policies D13 and T1 of the City of York Local Plan, which seek,
    among other things, to ensure that development positively reflect public safety and
    provide safe access for pedestrians and cyclists. It would also conflict with the
    public safety objectives of the Framework.
13. With regard to Appeal B, for the above reasons the proposal would harm public
    safety. I have taken into account Policies D13 and T1 of the Local Plan which
    seeks to ensure that advertisements positively reflect public safety and so are
    material in this case. Given I have concluded that the proposal would harm public

Other Matters
14. The appellant has drawn my attention to appeal decisions relating to similar
    developments elsewhere which I have taken into account. Notwithstanding this
    each case is determined on its own site specific circumstances, including the width
    and layout of the footpath, the relationship with cyclist and pedestrian movements
    and the surrounding highway context. The referenced appeal decisions do not lead
    me to a different conclusion in this case.
15. I have also considered the appellant’s submission including photographs,
    photomontages and comments regarding the absence of accident data. While
    these matters are relevant, the absence of recorded collisions does not mean that a
    proposal cannot give rise to an unacceptable risk. My assessment is based on the
    likely effects of the development on visibility and the safe movement of pedestrians
    and cyclists, having regard to the conditions and characteristics of the site and its
    surroundings.
16. The appellant contends that the proposed development would deliver public
    benefits. Included in these benefits are removal of existing payphones, delivery of
    public/emergency messaging, air quality and CO2 sensors, providing in-direct
    street lighting, providing access to contact emergency services, and phone
    charging ability and connection to Wi-Fi with no charge. I give weight to those
    benefits in relation to Appeal A. However, they do not outweigh the harm I have
    identified to highway and public safety at this particular location.
17. Notwithstanding the appellants claims relating to public benefits that the proposed
    advertisements would create, the Regulations2 and Planning Practice Guidance3
    are clear that the advertisement appeals shall be considered with regard to amenity
    and public safety. Even if these benefits were demonstrated they would not
    outweigh the harm I have found in relation to public safety in relation to Appeal B.
Conclusion
18. Appeal A is dismissed.
19. Appeal B is dismissed and express consent is refused for the advertisements as
    applied for.
