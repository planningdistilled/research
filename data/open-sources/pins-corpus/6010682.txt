Appeal Ref: 6010682
Land North East Of 7 Redmoor Lane, Wisbech, Cambridgeshire PE14 0RN
•   The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
    against a refusal to grant planning permission.
•   The appeal is made by Zena Livesey against the decision of Fenland District Council.
•   The application Ref is F/YR26/0184/F.
•   The development proposed is Change of use of land for domestic purposes, and the siting of a static
    caravan and erection of a shed (retrospective).

Decision
1. The appeal is dismissed.
Preliminary Matters
2. At the time of my site visit I observed the static caravan and shed to be present on
   site.
3. On 17 August 2026 a new Framework was published. As this could affect the issues
   and matters in this case, the Council and the appellant were invited to make further
   comments. My decision reflects the latest version of the Framework, and the
   responses received on it.
Main Issues
4. The main issues are:


       i)       whether a suitable location for development having regard to local and
                national planning policies;
       ii)      the effect of the proposal on character and appearance; and
       iii)     whether future occupiers of the proposed development would be safe from
                flooding and whether the proposal would comply with national and local
                planning policies which seek to steer new development away from areas at
                the highest risk of flooding.
Reasons
Location
5. Policy LP1 of the Fenland Local Plan 2014 (LP) seeks to deliver sustainable
   growth. Policy LP3 of the LP sets out the district-wide spatial strategy where the
   focus for the majority of growth to be in and around its four market towns. The
   application site is located in an ‘Elsewhere’ location, where development is

        restricted to that which is essential for agriculture, or other uses requiring a rural
        location. This LP policy is broadly consistent with the Framework insofar as it seeks
        to avoid isolated homes in the countryside unless in accordance with certain
        circumstances including where there is an essential need for a rural worker to live
        permanently at or near their place of work in the countryside.
6. Part D of Policy LP12 of the LP sets out that when considering proposals for
   residential uses in areas away from the market towns and villages the applicant
   should provide supporting evidence and explain a number of criteria a) – f) to justify
   development in such circumstances. Part E of the said Policy states that
   applications for mobile homes will be considered in the same way as permanent
   homes, including subject to flood risk considerations.
7. The Planning Practice Guide (PPG) advises considerations which may be taken
   into account when determining whether there is an essential need to live on, or
   near, the site can include to ensure the effective operation of the enterprise for
   instance, where animals require on-site attention 24-hours a day and where,
   otherwise, there would be a risk to animal health, or from crime.
8. In their appeal submission the Appellant advances justification that was not
   previously before the Council. Initially the proposal was advanced as ancillary
   accommodation whereas now it is presented that the static caravan provides
   accommodation functionally connected to, and in support of, a genuine land-based
   use of the holding, which falls squarely within the categories of agriculture and
   horticulture that Policy LP3 expressly supports in ‘Elsewhere’ locations.
9. The Procedural Guide for Planning Appeals is clear that the appeal process should
   not be used to evolve a scheme, and it is important that what is considered is
   essentially what was considered by the Council, and on which interested people’s
   views were sought. Under the principles established by the Courts in Holborn
   Studios Ltd1 to consider such a modification could procedurally prejudice interested
   parties by depriving those who should have been consulted on the change the
   opportunity of such consultation. However, I note ‘ancillary’ was not included in the
   description of development and that the Council has commented on this reasoning
   in terms of its response to the appeal. I shall therefore consider the justification now
   put forward.
10. The Appellant cites the keeping of livestock requires daily attendance, and that in
    times of lambing/kidding and in adverse weather, attendance at unsocial hours and
    short notice is required and that a presence on the holding is of practical value to
    animal welfare and the proper management of the stock and the land. It is also
    advanced that there is a well-documented and increasing problem of live-stock theft
    across the Fenland areas and that a presence on site would be a meaningful
    deterrent against theft of stock and equipment.
11. Whilst I do not dispute that an on-site presence would aid animal welfare and
    management of the holding, in the absence of any robust evidence in respect of the
    holdings needs and current support it is afforded, it has not been sufficiently
    demonstrated that there is an essential need for a worker to live on-site.
    Furthermore, having regard to the extent of the red-line application site, which
    seeks the change of use of the land to domestic purposes, it is unclear to what
    extent any agricultural use or function of the holding would remain.

12. While the Appellant indicates their agreement to the imposition of planning
    conditions to secure the rural-enterprise link, I do not consider that such conditions

13. Consequently, the proposal does not fall within the list of exceptions for
    development in such locations. Whilst the appeal site being proximate to existing
    built form would not be in an isolated location in the context of the Framework this
    does not overcome the conflict with Policies LP1, LP3 and LP12 of the LP.
14. I therefore conclude that the proposal would conflict with the district-wide spatial

    Policies LP1, LP3 and LP12 of the LP.
Character and appearance
15. The appeal site forms part of a land holding used for small livestock and mixed-
    species fruit orchard. Whilst the Appellant submits that the static caravan is located
    on mown land forming part of their garden, no substantive evidence has been
    provided to demonstrate that the site forms part of a lawful residential curtilage.
    Based on my observations, the land has the appearance of agricultural land, being
    physically separate from the main residential area (circa 106 metres) and lacking
    the defining characteristics typically associated with domestic garden land.
    Furthermore, the description of development includes the change of use of land for
    domestic purposes. I therefore afford limited weight to the Appellant's assertion in
    this regard. Nevertheless, nothing in this decision would preclude the Appellant
    from applying for a Certificate of Lawfulness or otherwise seeking to establish the
    lawful status of the existing caravan and shed through the appropriate statutory
    procedures should they consider that sufficient evidence exists to do so.
16. Notwithstanding its limited height, the mobile home would be of considerable width
    and would display a distinctly domestic appearance by virtue of its design and
    external finish. Moreover, the introduction of a residential unit and associated
    activity into an undeveloped field would result in the domestication of the site,
    causing harm to its rural character and appearance. While the appeal site benefits
    from heavily vegetated boundaries the development is, nonetheless, visible from
    both Redmoor Lane and the A47, which lies immediately to the north of the appeal
    site. My attention has been drawn to other static caravans in the locality, stating
    that they form part of the established character of the area. However, I do not know
    if they are lawful or the circumstances in which they are sited.
17. Furthermore, the red line boundary encompasses an extensive area of land that is
    sought for residential purposes. The use of this land would be likely to introduce
    domestic features and paraphernalia, such as planting, garden furniture, storage
    and other domestic alterations, some of which may not require planning
    permission. The cumulative effect of such changes would be to impart a
    suburbanising influence that would be at odds with the site's existing agrestic
    character. Consequently, the proposed development, including the extent of the
    land, would undermine the rural character and appearance of the area.
18. I therefore conclude that the proposal would be harmful to the character and
    appearance of the area in conflict with Policies LP12 and LP16 of the LP. Amongst
    other things these policies seeks development that makes a positive contribution to
    the local distinctiveness and character of the area, enhances its local setting,

Flood Risk
19. The appeal site lies within Flood Zone 3a. Policy LP14 of the LP requires
    development in areas at risk of flooding to follow a sequential approach. Where
    applicable, proposals must pass the Sequential Test and Exception Test,
    demonstrate an identified need for the development, and be supported by a site-
    specific Flood Risk Assessment showing that the development can be made safe
    for its lifetime, would not increase flood risk elsewhere, and incorporates
    appropriate flood risk mitigation measures without reliance on emergency services.
    This stance is supported by the guidance contained within the Cambridgeshire
    Flood and Water SPD 2016.
20. The Framework states that inappropriate development in areas at risk of flooding
    should be avoided by directing development away from areas at highest risk, but
    where development is necessary, ensuring that it is safe for its lifetime without
    increasing flood risk elsewhere. Policy F5 of the Framework adopts a strengthened
    sequential approach to flood risk and requires the Sequential Test to be applied
    unless specific exemptions apply.
21. The Sequential Test forms the first stage of the Framework's approach to
    managing flood risk and seeks to steer development to areas at the lowest risk of
    flooding. The Sequential Test is not intended to ensure that development can never
    be approved in areas at risk of flooding, and a degree of flexibility and pragmatism
    may be required in its application. Where development cannot reasonably be
    located in lower-risk areas, the assessment should consider alternative reasonably
    available sites.
22. I acknowledge the Appellant's assertion that the sequential test relates to the
    mobile home rather than the shed. However, the appeal proposal must be
    considered as a whole. The shed forms part of the development for which
    permission is sought.
23. The Environment Agency object to the proposal. Having reviewed the FRA2 they
    state that it has incorrectly classified the flood risk vulnerability as ‘More
    vulnerable’. Whilst the mobile home is described as ancillary accommodation, the
    proposal is not a householder development within an existing residential curtilage.
    Rather it comprises a material change of use of land to residential use together with
    the siting of a mobile home. Accordingly, as a mobile home intended for permeant
    residential use Annex F of the Framework classifies the development as ‘highly
    vulnerable’.
24. Furthermore, the EA advises that the submitted FRA does not include a specific
    assessment of residual risk to the site. The site is within an area which would be at
    risk if a breach was to occur. The nearest modelled breach point taken from the
    Fenland Strategic Flood Risk Assessment is located 470m from the site. The site
    falls within the 0.25m to 0.5m flood depth banding shown on the 2115 0.1% annual
    probability breach maps, which represent the most relevant data for single-storey

25. I have found above that it has not been demonstrated that there is an essential
    need for a worker's dwelling to be located on this site. In those circumstances, and
    having regard to the sequential approach required by Policy F5 of the Framework, I
    am not satisfied that the proposal passes the Sequential Test. Whilst I appreciate
    that substantial parts of the district lie within Flood Zone 3, there is evidence before
    me that sites with extant permissions exist within Flood Zones 1 and 2. Moreover,
    there is no compelling evidence that the development must be located on this
    particular site.
26. Whilst the Appellant asserts that there is no record of Redmoor Lane having
    flooded, this does not alter the site's designation within Flood Zone 3, which is
    based on national flood risk mapping rather than the occurrence of past flooding
    events. The Appellant also indicates an intention to seek a site-specific review of
    the flood zone designation. However, no such evidence has been submitted as part
    of this appeal.
27. Whilst the caravan may, in principle, be capable of being moved and occupants
    may subscribe to the Environment Agency's Floodline warning service, these
    measures would not overcome my concerns regarding flood risk. Reliance on
    advance warning and evacuation procedures does not avoid the exposure of future
    occupiers to flood risk, nor is there persuasive evidence before me that the caravan
    could be safely and effectively relocated in the event of flooding. I therefore afford
    these considerations limited weight.
28. In the absence of sufficient evidence demonstrating an essential need for the
    dwelling to be located on this site, I am not satisfied that the proposal meets the
    requirements of the Sequential Test. Consequently, the proposal conflicts with the
    Framework in this regard.
29. As the Sequential Test has not been satisfied, it is unnecessary for me to reach a
    definitive conclusion on the Exception Test, which is only engaged where the
    Sequential Test has first been passed. Nevertheless, even were that not the case,
    the concerns raised by the Environment Agency regarding the vulnerability
    classification of the development and the lack of a satisfactory assessment of
    residual flood risk would weigh against a finding that the development would be
    safe for its lifetime.
30. For the reasons given above, I cannot be satisfied that the proposal would accord
    with the sequential approach to flood risk set out in the Framework or that future
    occupiers would be adequately protected from flood risk. The proposal therefore
    conflicts with Policies LP12, LP14 and LP16 of the LP, as well as the flood risk
    policies of the Framework.

Planning Balance and Conclusion
34. The revised Framework introduced a ‘presumption in favour of sustainable
    development’ for specific types of proposals outside of settlement boundaries in
    National Decision Making Policies (NDMP) S3 to S5, which replaces the
    presumption as detailed in the previous Framework.
35. In this case, the appeal site lies outside a settlement, and the proposal therefore
    falls to be considered against NDMP S5. S5 stipulates that only certain forms of
    development fall into the group of schemes that should be approved outside
    settlements “unless the benefits of doing so would be substantially outweighed by
    any adverse effects”, including “development which would address an evidenced
    unmet need”.
36. The proposal does not comprise one of the forms of development listed in S5(1) as
    being acceptable in the countryside. Whilst S5(4) provides that development may
    be approved in exceptional circumstances where the benefits would substantially
    outweigh the adverse effects, S5(2) explains that the circumstances in which
    benefits are likely to be substantially outweighed by adverse effects include, but are
    not limited to, situations where a proposal would fail to comply with a national
    decision-making policy which states that development proposals should be refused
    in specific circumstances.
37. I have found that the proposal fails the Sequential Test and therefore conflicts with
    the Framework's flood risk policies. As those policies provide a strong reason for
    refusal, I conclude that the proposal does not satisfy NDMP S5. In these
    circumstances, there is no need for me to undertake any further assessment as to
    whether exceptional circumstances exist under S5(4).
38. Drawing the above together, the proposal would conflict with the development plan
    when read as a whole and there are no material considerations, including the
    Framework, which would indicate a decision other than in accordance with it.
39. For the reasons given above I conclude that the appeal should be dismissed.
