Appeal Ref: 6013651
69 Wellingborough Road, Rushden, North Northamptonshire NN10 9YG
•    The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 against a refusal
     to grant planning permission under section 73 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 for the
     development of land carried out without complying with conditions subject to which a previous
     planning permission was granted.
•    The appeal is made by Investment Street against the decision of North Northamptonshire Council.
•    The application Ref 26/00189/VAR, dated 22 January 2026, was refused by notice dated 19 March
     2026.
•    The application sought planning permission for Change of Use from (C3 Dwellinghouse) to (Sui
     Generis) HMO for 9 people, to include internal and external alterations, provision of bin storage, car
     parking and bicycle storage area without complying with a condition attached to planning permission
     Ref NE/24/01169/FUL, dated 15 August 2025.
•    The condition in dispute is No.2 which states that: The development hereby permitted shall not be
     carried out other than in accordance with the approved plans and details listed below: Location Plan
     (reference: AD-OS); received 07/01/25, Proposed Drawings (reference: AD-01-00), received
     07/01/25, Boundary Treatment Plan (reference: AD-01-30), received 17/06/25, Streetscene
     Elevations (reference: AD-01-31), received 17/06/25.
•    The reason given for the condition is: In the interest of securing an appropriate form of development
     in accordance with Policy 8 of the North Northamptonshire Joint Core Strategy.

Decision
1.     The appeal is allowed and planning permission is granted for the change of use
       from (C3 Dwellinghouse) to (Sui Generis) HMO for 9 people, to include internal
       and external alterations, provision of bin storage, car parking and bicycle storage
       area at 69 Wellingborough Road, Rushden, North Northamptonshire NN10 9YG, in
       accordance with the application ref 26/00189/VAR without compliance with
       conditions 2, 4, 6 and 7 previously imposed on planning permission ref
       NE/24/01169/FUL dated 15 August 2025 and subject to the conditions set out in
       the attached schedule.
Preliminary Matters
2.     There is competing evidence from the parties over whether the development
       approved by planning permission NE/24/01169/FUL (the original permission) has
       been implemented. I have no clear evidence to determine either way, however I
       note that the original permission remains extant and capable of implementation.
       Whilst noting the circumstances on site, including the differences in the boundary
       treatment erected relative to both the approved and proposed plans, I have
       determined the appeal on the basis of the plans before me. Any disputed matters
       in respect of the lawfulness of works undertaken are a matter for the parties to
       resolve.

3.     A new National Planning Policy Framework (the Framework) was published on 17
       August 2026. Both parties have had the opportunity to address the policies of the
       revised Framework in submissions.
Background and Main Issues
4.     Planning permission was granted by the council under reference
       NE/24/01169/FUL to change the use of the premises from a single dwellinghouse
       to a House in Multiple Occupation (HMO) for 9 residents. The approved plans
       show the frontage of the site to be enclosed by 1.2m high walls with railings, with
       no on-site parking spaces provided. The application before me seeks to vary the
       approved plans condition (no.2) on the original planning permission, to substitute
       alternative plans showing the provision of 2 parking spaces and an alternative form
       of boundary treatment.
5.     The main issues for this appeal are the effects that the provision of 2 parking
       spaces and revised boundary treatment would have on:
           •    highway safety and the promotion of sustainable means of travel; and,
           •    the character and appearance of the street scene.
Reasons
Highway safety and sustainable travel
6.     The appeal site fronts the A5001 Wellingborough Road close to a 4-leg
       roundabout and comprises a former church, more recently used as a single
       dwelling. The premises are set behind a wide pavement. Double yellow lines
       prevent on-street parking in the vicinity of the appeal site.
7.     The evidence shows that the site frontage has been used for the parking of
       vehicles for many years in association with the previous use of the property as a
       dwelling. The existing dropped kerb to Wellingborough Road, is, on the basis of
       the evidence before me, both longstanding and lawful.
8.     I have been provided with no evidence of any records of accidents in the vicinity of
       the site associated with the historic use of this existing access and parking area.
       Visibility of the access from both the roundabout and its associated spurs is clear
       and unobstructed and vehicles exiting the driveway would be visible to on-coming
       traffic for some distance. Despite its proximity to the roundabout, I have no
       evidence before me to indicate that the continued use of the access would be
       inherently unsafe.
9.     Whilst the council considers that the proposal would intensify the use of the
       access, I am not persuaded that this would be the case. Although the HMO would
       accommodate more occupants than the former single dwelling, the provision of
       only two parking spaces would limit vehicle movements associated with the site.
       Consequently, the use of the access and parking area would be likely to remain
       broadly comparable to that of the previous residential use.
10. Similarly, I am not persuaded that provision of the parking spaces would lead to
    other vehicles being parked on the pavement. In granting planning permission for
    the HMO, the council was satisfied that sufficient on-street parking capacity
    existed in the surrounding area to accommodate the parking demands of a 9-

       person HMO. The provision of 2 on-site parking spaces would reduce reliance on
       on-street parking and, consequently, lessen pressure on the local highway
       network. There is no evidence before me to suggest that in these circumstances
       pavement parking would be likely. In any event, separate regulatory powers exist
       to address obstructive or unsafe parking on the public highway.
11. The council suggests that the proposal would fail to promote sustainable travel.
    However, the approved HMO development includes a cycle storage area and its
    provision is secured by a planning condition. The proposed plans also provide for
    this facility. As such, opportunities for cycling as a sustainable mode of transport
    would remain available. The appeal site is also within walking distance of local
    services, facilities and bus services within Rushden. Consequently, I am satisfied
    that the proposal would not diminish opportunities for travel by sustainable modes.
12. Taking account of all of the above, the provision of 2 parking spaces for use in
    connection with the HMO would not give rise to unacceptable harm to highway
    safety nor remove opportunities for occupiers to choose alternative, more
    sustainable means of travel. The proposal accordingly complies with Policy 8 of
    the North Northamptonshire Joint Core Strategy 2011-2031 (2016) (the JCS) in
    encouraging sustainable travel, preventing unacceptable harm to highway safety
    and ensuring a satisfactory means of access is provided. Policy TR6 of the
    Framework confirms that development proposals should only be refused where,
    amongst other things, they would have an unacceptable impact on highway safety,
    taking into account measures to support sustainable patterns of movement. For
    the reasons given above, that is not the case here.
Character and appearance
13. The layout plan approved under the original permission shows the frontage
    enclosed by 1.2m high walling/railing. These plans included no indication of
    proposed surfacing treatment and, other than 2 planters immediately in front of the
    building, no landscaping was included nor required by condition. Accordingly, there
    was no certainty that the grassed lawn to the front of the property was to be
    retained as part of the change of use of the dwellinghouse to an HMO.
14. The proposed layout would retain the planters, provide 2 parking spaces, and the
    enclosure of the remaining frontage by a 1.2 metre-high wall and railings, with 1
    metre-high railings along the pedestrian access. The main distinction between the
    approved and proposed frontage arrangements is therefore the presence of a
    driveway to accommodate up to 2 parked vehicles.
15. Wellingborough Road is predominantly residential in character and contains a
    varied mix of single and two-storey dwellings. The majority of properties along the
    street, including those adjoining the appeal site, have hard-surfaced frontages
    used for vehicle parking, typically enclosed by low boundary treatments that allow
    parked cars to remain visible. I am also mindful that the evidence demonstrates
    that vehicles have been parked within the site frontage for many years.
    Consequently, whilst the proposed arrangement would depart from the scheme
    approved under the original permission, the resulting appearance would reflect the
    established character of the site and its surroundings.
16. Taking the above into account, I find that the proposal would not result in
    unacceptable harm to the character or appearance of the street scene. It would
    respond appropriately to the site's immediate surroundings and reflect the

Other Matters
17. I am satisfied that the proposed development would remain consistent with the
    operative part of the original planning permission. Specifically, I note that the
    original description of development included reference to the provision of car
    parking, despite none being proposed at that time.
18. The council’s delegated report raises the potential for two parking spaces serving
    a 9-person HMO to be used more intensively than a normal residential use,
    leading to additional comings and goings and vehicular noise, harming the living
    conditions of neighbouring residents. However, I am not persuaded that their use
    would be materially different to the use of 2 spaces serving a dwelling.
Conditions
19. The guidance in the Planning Practice Guidance makes clear that decisions for
    planning permission under section 73 should also restate the conditions imposed
    on earlier planning permissions that continue to have effect. As I have no definitive
    information before me about whether the original permission has been
    implemented or the status of the other conditions imposed on that planning
    permission, I shall impose all those that I consider remain relevant. In the event
    that the requirements of some have in fact been achieved through development
    already undertaken, that is a matter which can be addressed by the parties. The
    parties were invited to comment on the implications of this appeal on the other
    original conditions and I have taken account of the comments received in reaching
    my decision. I have also had regard to the 4 tests for planning conditions
    established in Policy DM6 of the Framework.
20. Taking account of the above, and for certainty, I have included a time limit
    condition requiring that the development commence within the date set by the
    original permission.
21. I have varied the wording of condition 2 to substitute the approved plans. I have
    reimposed condition 3 restricting the occupancy of the HMO and, with a minor
    amendment to correct a typographical error, condition 5 requiring the provision of
    cycle storage, in accordance with the terms of the original permission and to
    ensure a choice of means of transport is possible.
22. Condition 4 of the original permission required the substantial reinstatement of the
    dropped kerb forming the vehicular access from Wellingborough Road. Given that
    the appeal proposal relies on vehicular access being gained to the site frontage, I
    have removed this condition which no longer serves a purpose.
23. The reasons for original conditions 6 & 7 relate to highway safety and it is clear
    that the council’s intention in imposing these conditions was to prevent what was
    seen as unsafe vehicular manoeuvres onto the public highway. I have found that
    the provision of 2 parking spaces would not cause unacceptable harm to highway
    safety, but to ensure that additional parking spaces are not provided, I have
    removed conditions 6 & 7 and imposed a new condition [5] restricting the parking

Planning Balance and Conclusion
24. Policy S3 of the Framework applies a presumption in favour of sustainable
    development. For development within settlements, this means that Policy S4 of the
    Framework should be applied. Policy S4 sets out that development proposals
    should be approved unless the benefits of doing so would be substantially
    outweighed by any adverse effects when assessed against the national decision-
    making policies in the Framework.
25. I have found that the proposal complies with Policy 8 of the JCS. This policy is
    consistent with the Framework in that it seeks to deliver well-designed, well-
    connected places. The proposal would not conflict with any of the decision-making
    policies in the Framework. As such, Policy S4 points to permission being granted.
26. In the final balance, and for the reasons given above, the proposal complies with
    the policies of the development plan as a whole and there are no other
    considerations that indicate a decision should be made other than in accordance
    with it. As such, the appeal is allowed.
