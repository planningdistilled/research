 •   The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
     against a refusal to grant planning permission.
 •   The appeal is made by Karina Arden Maccabee against the decision of the Council of the London

 •   The application Ref is 2025/3444.
 •   The development proposed is described as alterations to existing front portico.

Decision
1. The appeal is dismissed.
Preliminary Matters
2. In the banner above I have taken the description of development from the Council’s
   decision notice, as it more concisely reflects the proposed development.
3. During the course of the appeal, a new National Planning Policy Framework (the
   Framework) was published on 17 August 2026. While the format has altered, the
   considerations material to this decision have not fundamentally changed. I am
   satisfied that no party has been prejudiced, and I have had regard to the latest
   version in reaching my decision.
Main Issue
4. The main issue is whether the development would preserve or enhance the
   character or appearance of the Westmead Conservation Area.
Reasons
5. The appeal site lies within the Westmead Conservation Area (the WCA). Under
   Section 72(1) of the Planning (Listed Buildings and Conservation Areas) Act 1990,
   special attention must be paid to the desirability of preserving or enhancing its
   character or appearance. The Westmead Conservation Area Appraisal and
   Management Strategy provides a clear understanding of the WCA’s special interest
   as a phased early-20th-century suburban estate. Insofar as relevant to this appeal,
   the significance of the WCA derives from its historic plan form, the architectural
   consistency and quality of its buildings, and its well-landscaped and verdant setting.
6. The appeal dwelling forms one of a group of four detached houses of similar scale
   and original design. Their largely unaltered front façades mean that, both
   individually and collectively, they make a positive contribution to the significance of
   the WCA. The existing porch at the appeal property is a modest projection whose

     siting, scale and design does not visually dilute the overall appearance of the host
     dwelling or disrupt the cohesive qualities of this part of the street scene.
7. Attached to this porch is an existing open-sided canopy with a shallow hipped roof.
   It is understood that this structure is unauthorised and was the subject of a recent
   appeal decision1. In dismissing that appeal, the Inspector found that the canopy’s
   overall scale, form and forward siting resulted in an incongruous and overly
   prominent modern feature that detracted from the planned nature of the dwelling
   and failed to harmonise with the relatively unaltered and consistent front elevations
   of neighbouring properties.
8. The current proposal seeks to replace the existing canopy with a smaller version.
   Its width and projection would be reduced, meaning it would sit further back from
   the street and appear less dominant against the existing porch. However, while the
   scale would be diminished, the essential form, design and relationship to the
   principal elevation would remain materially similar to the structure previously found
   harmful.
9. Notwithstanding the reduction in size, the proposed canopy would still introduce a
   modern, visually assertive feature that contrasts with the traditional character and
   appearance of the original dwelling. Its hipped roof and open-sided form would
   remain highly apparent in views from the street. When considered in the context of
   the neighbouring dwellings, the canopy would continue to appear as a prominent
   and incongruous addition that undermines the visual cohesiveness of this part of
   the WCA. This is an attribute that I have identified as contributing positively to its
   significance.
10. I have noted the appellant’s reference to other canopies within the WCA. However,
    I do not have full details of their planning status, and they do not appear to be
    wholly comparable in terms of siting or design. Moreover, they are not within the
    immediate visual context of the appeal site. In any event, I have considered the
    proposal on its own merits.
11. The Framework confirms that substantial weight should be given to the
    conservation of designated heritage assets (HE6(1)), and that any harm to such an
    asset is a matter of considerable importance and weight (HE6(3)). HE6(4) requires
    that harm to the significance of a designated heritage asset, and the effect on that
    asset and its significance, must be weighed against any public benefits resulting
    from the proposal. I have considered the benefits advanced. While the canopy
    would provide some weather protection for the appellant and visitors, such benefits
    are private in nature and carry limited weight. For the reasons outlined above, the
    proposal would not enhance the dwelling and any benefits would not outweigh the
    harm to the heritage asset that I have identified.
12. For the reasons above, the development would fail to preserve or enhance the
    character or appearance of the WCA. Therefore, the proposal conflicts with Policies
    LP1, LP3 and LP5 of the Wandsworth Local Plan 2023-2038. Together, amongst
    other things, these seek to ensure that development proposals are of a high-quality
    design, which conserve or enhance the historic environment.
