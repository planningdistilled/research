  •    The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
       against a refusal to grant planning permission.
  •    The appeal is made by Mr Asim Javed against the decision of Rochdale Metropolitan Borough
       Council.
  •    The application Ref is PL/2026/0088.
  •    The development is described on the planning application form as a ‘Proposed single-storey rear
       extension extending approximately 3 metres to align the rear elevation of the dwelling with the
       existing Victorian style hexagonal conservatory, including the infill of an existing approximately 2 m
  -
       × 2 m corner patio to bring it in line with the rear of the house’.

Decision
1. The appeal is dismissed.
Preliminary Matters
2. The National Planning Policy Framework (the Framework) has been revised since
   the parties made their original submissions. The main parties have been invited to
   make further representations in relation to the Framework. Any representations
   received have been taken into consideration.
3. The appellant has conceded in their appeal submission that the drawings submitted
   with the planning application are internally inconsistent and contain technical errors.
   The appellant has submitted an alternative set of drawings with this appeal
   purporting to overcome the inaccuracies identified by the Council.
4. The inaccuracy of the plans is the only reason for refusal in this appeal. These
   alternative drawings have not been seen by the Council or been the subject of
   public consultation. It is not the purpose of the appeal process to evolve a scheme.
   Therefore, in the interests of fairness and transparency to all parties, I have
   determined this appeal on the basis of the original plans considered by the Council.
Main Issue
5. The main issue is whether the submitted drawings are so inaccurate and internally
   inconsistent that the proposal cannot be properly assessed.
Reasons
6. The appeal property is a detached dwelling. The proposal would introduce a rear
   single storey extension and infill at first floor level.

7. The submitted drawings are internally inconsistent, to the extent that both the
   dimensions and design of the proposal are uncertain, including whether the
   conservatory is retained or demolished. The appellant has accepted this in their
   evidence.
8. Although the appellant considers it possible to assess the proposal based on the
   technically inaccurate plans I disagree, as the nature of the proposal is so unclear
   that the potential impacts cannot be identified. Moreover, any resultant planning
   permission would be uncertain and unenforceable.
9. Consequently, I find that the internal inconsistency and inaccurate measurements
   in the submitted drawings creates such a level of uncertainty that it is not possible
   for the proposal to be properly assessed.
Conclusion
10. For the reasons given, I conclude that the appeal should be dismissed.
