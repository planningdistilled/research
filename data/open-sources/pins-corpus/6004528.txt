8QA
•      The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
       against a refusal to grant planning permission under section 73 of the Town and Country Planning
       Act 1990 (as amended) for the development of land without complying with conditions subject to
       which a previous planning permission was granted.
•      The appeal is made by Mr Phillip Kelly of Malthouse Mews Residents against the decision of Hart
       District Council.
•      The application Ref is 25/01106/AMCON.
•      The application sought planning permission for variation of condition 2 attached to planning
       permission 17/02430/FUL dated 25/09/2019 to allow revisions to the approved plans without
       complying with a condition attached to planning permission Ref 21/02082/AMCON, dated 11 October
       2021.
•      The condition in dispute is No 8 which states that: The development hereby approved shall be fully
       implemented in accordance with the private refuse collection strategy in perpetuity as approved
       under discharge of condition approval ref: 20/01760/CON dated 08 October 2020.
•      The reasons given for the condition is: To ensure that the development is provided with adequate
       servicing arrangements in accordance with policy INF3 of the adopted Hart Local Plan- Strategy and
       Sites 2016-2032, saved local policy GEN1 of the Hart District Local Plan 1996- 2006 and the NPPF
       2021.

Decision
1. The appeal is dismissed.
Background and Main Issue
2. In 2019 planning permission was granted for the demolition of an existing building
   and the erection of 8 no dwellings together with associated access and
   landscaping1. Condition 9 of this consent read as follows:
         Details of a private refuse collection strategy, which means proposed
         arrangements for future private management and maintenance of bin collection
         services to the site, shall be submitted to and approved in writing by the Local
         Planning Authority prior to commencement of development, excepting site
         clearance and demolition. The private refuse collection strategy shall be
         implemented since first occupation of the dwellings hereby approved and
         thereafter maintained in accordance with the approved management and
         maintenance details. A copy of a completed agreement of the private management
         and maintenance company confirming funding, management and maintenance
         regimes shall be submitted to the local planning authority to discharge this
         condition.

        The reason for the condition was:
        To ensure that the development is provided with adequate servicing arrangements
        to satisfy Local Policies GEN1 and T14.
       Condition 9 was subsequently partially discharged2.
3. In 2021 planning permission was granted for:
        Variation of Condition 2 attached to Planning Permission 17/02430/FUL dated
        25/09/2019 to allow revisions to the approved plans.
        This was approved3 subject to conditions including condition 8, which is the
        subject of this appeal.
4. The main issue is whether the condition is necessary having regard to the provision
   of adequate servicing arrangements in respect of the collection of waste and
   recycling.
Reasons
5. The approved dwellings are now occupied, and the approved refuse collection
   strategy is operational. This involves a private company entering the appeal site to
   empty the shared bins which are stored adjacent to the undercroft parking areas.
6. It is proposed to remove the condition in question and to utilise public waste
   collection services, using standard issue waste receptacles. Malthouse Mews is
   accessed via Brewhouse Lane which runs between Oakland Terrace and High
   Street (A30). I am told that Brewhouse Lane is not on the public waste collection
   route. In order for public kerbside collection services to be used, containers would
   need to be taken to a point near the public highway.
7.      There are limited pavement areas near to the appeal site and the area was heavily
        parked at the time of my site visit. As such there is limited space near the highway
        where bins could be positioned ready for collection. It is proposed to use part of
        the lane adjacent to the property which is situated at the corner of Brewhouse
        Lane and Oakland Terrace to position the bins.
8. However, there is a parking space at the north end of the section of lane between
   the fire station and Oakland Terrace which, if occupied, would mean residents
   having to push wheelie bins either over, or around the area of green space to reach
   this area.
9.      Furthermore, this area is not within the appellant’s control and there would be no
        guarantee that there would be space for the receptacles of 8 separate households
        in this area on collection day if a car were parked on this space.
10. Consequently, residents may be put to considerable inconvenience, finding an
    appropriate place to leave their waste for collection. Even if a direct route to the
    proposed collection area were possible, this would still involve residents wheeling
    and/or carrying waste receptacles a significant distance from their homes and this
    would be increased if the proposed space were not available.

11. There are no specific requirements within local planning policy with respect to
    appropriate ‘trundle distances’ and I note that the Waste Collection and Highway
    Authorities do not object to the approach from a technical perspective. However, in
    my judgement, given the nature of the surrounding highway, the proposal would
    result in an unsatisfactory arrangement which would be inconvenient and
    impractical for occupiers of Malthouse Mews. Whilst current residents may be
    content with this, it would not be suitable or convenient for all future occupiers,
    particularly those with mobility issues.
12. I therefore conclude that the condition is necessary having regard to the provision
    of adequate servicing arrangements in respect of the collection of waste and
    recycling. The effect of removing the condition would be an arrangement which
    would fail to provide appropriate and accessible collection points for refuse vehicles
    as required by Policies INF3 and NBE9 of the Hart Local Plan (Strategy and Sites)
    2032 and Policy 2 of the Hartley Wintney Neighbourhood Plan 2017 – 2032
    (Referendum Plan August 2019).
Other Matters
13. I acknowledge the desire of residents to reduce costs and make use of public
    services, however this does not lead me to change my conclusion that removing
    the condition in question would result in an unsatisfactory arrangement.
Conclusion
14. For the reasons given above the appeal should be dismissed.
