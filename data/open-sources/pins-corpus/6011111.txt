Decision
1.       The appeal is allowed and planning permission is granted for alterations, ground
         floor extensions and formation of new second floor level with an art deco style
         remodel of the existing dwelling, first floor rear balcony, roof garden, formation of
         swimming pool, altered garage and new parking spaces off Sandbourne Road at
         18 Sandbourne Road, Bournemouth BH4 8JH in accordance with the application
         Ref P/25/04679/CONDR, without compliance with condition numbers 2, 8, 9 and
         10 previously imposed on planning permission Ref P/25/02311/HOU dated
         17 September 2025 and subject to the conditions in the attached schedule.
Background and Main Issue
2. Planning permission was granted for alterations, ground floor extensions and
   formation of new second floor level with an art deco style remodel of the existing
   dwelling, first floor rear balcony, roof garden, formation of swimming pool, altered
   garage and new parking spaces off Sandbourne Road.
3. The appellant sought to secure changes to this permission by varying condition 2 to
   allow material amendments to the approved plans to improve the design and
   functionality of the garage outbuilding by creating an additional floor and amend the
   boundary treatment with a new rendered boundary wall and front sliding gate. In
   addition, the proposal would remove condition 8 which restricted the installation of
   gates to the new parking area fronting Sandbourne Road. Conditions 9 and 10 are
   proposed to be reworded to a compliance-based trigger on matters relating to
   landscaping and a management plan, following submission of a landscape plan1
   and a landscape management plan2.
4. Apart from the alterations to the design of the garage, the Council raised no
   objection to the proposals. Consequently, the main issue is the effect that varying
   condition 2 to amend the plans in relation to the garage would have on the
   character and appearance of the area.
Reasons
5. The site is occupied by a detached two-storey dwelling on the corner of
   Sandbourne Road and Alumhurst Road. These are residential streets,
   characterised by two and three-storey properties, including traditional and
   contemporary designs and a mix of detached, semi-detached and terraced
   buildings. The rear garden to the appeal property extends along Alumhurst Road,
   with a single-storey detached garage at its far end, adjacent to the boundary with
   134 Alumhurst Road, and set back from the road.

6. The proposed amendments to the garage would include partial demolition of the
   ground floor and upwards extension with a pitched roof and dormers to the side
   facing the rear garden. The height of the building would increase by approximately
   a third.
7. The garage would be visible both from Alumhurst Road and on the approach from
   Crosby Road opposite. Due to its position, it would be primarily viewed in the
   context of No 134 rather than No 18 from which it is separated by the garden. The
   ridge height of the garage would be broadly level with the eaves to No 134, and in
   the context of this building it would not appear dominant. It would also be much
   lower than No 18, particularly once all approved enlargements to that building have
   been carried out. In this respect it would appear subservient to surrounding
   development. Moreover, the building would continue to provide garage space,
   served by a garage door, which would ensure it maintained its appearance as an
   ancillary building. As such, it would not compete with neighbouring development. It
   would also not undermine the established character of ancillary buildings in the
   locality as I observed, these are not especially common features and where they do
   exist, they are varied in form with some being more prominent than others.
8. The existing garage is relatively wide with a front facing pitched roof which makes
   the building prominent in views towards the site, particularly from Crosby Road and
   looking south along Alumhurst Road. Whilst the additional height would be
   apparent, with part of the existing garage demolished and its prominent pitched roof
   being replace with a gable fronted, narrower building, there would be some
   reduction in the bulk of the building. The existing building is wider and bulky and the
   proposed would be narrower and taller. On balance, I find the proposal would result
   in a limited increase in prominence of this building within the streetscene.
9. For these reasons, the proposed garage would not harm the character and
   appearance of the area and would therefore comply with Policy CS41 of the
   Bournemouth Local Plan Core Strategy 2012 which seeks to ensure that
   development respects the site and its surroundings and enhances local character.
   It would also accord with the guidance set out in the Residential extensions – A
   design guide for householders (2008)
Conditions
10. The Planning Practice Guidance3 makes clear that decision notices for the grant of
    planning permission under section 73 should set out all of the conditions imposed
    on the new permission, and restate the conditions imposed on earlier permissions
    that continue to have effect. As the development has not yet commenced, I have
    re-imposed all of the conditions that I consider remain relevant. In the event that
    some of these have in fact already been discharged, that is a matter which can be
    addressed by the parties. I have amended these in the interests of clarity and
    precision.
11. As section 73 should not be used to extend the time limit in which development
    shall be commenced, I have imposed the original time limit date at condition 1.
    Condition 2 has been varied to state the revised drawing numbers that the
    development should be carried out in accordance with, albeit the original
    permission remains in force should the appellant choose to revert to that scheme.

12. Conditions relating to external materials and landscaping remain necessary in the
    interests of the appearance of the development. I have however amended the
    wording and combined conditions (9) and (10) to which this appeal related so that
    the requirement is to ensure compliance with approved landscaping and
    management plans. Compliance with biodiversity recommendations is necessary in
    the interests of achieving a biodiversity gain.
13. Conditions requiring the provision of a visibility splay and suitable surfacing to the
    parking area remain necessary in the interests of highway safety. I have removed
    condition 8 to allow the formation of a gate from the new parking area but added a
    condition to control the manner by which the gate would open adjacent to the
    highway, in the interests of highway safety.
14. In the interests of protecting the living conditions and privacy of neighbouring
    occupiers, a condition restricting use of flat roofs remains necessary.
15. I have not imposed a condition restricting the use of the garage building on the
    basis that if it were to be used as a separate unit of living accommodation to the
    host property this would require planning permission. However, I have not been
    directed to any evidence or policy within the development plan covering the
    Bournemouth area that justifies restricting the use of the garage building from being
    let separately to another party, either permanently, or for any seasonal or holiday
    period.
Conclusion
16. For the reasons given above the appeal should be allowed.
