Appeal Ref: 6005855
10 Elsenham Street, Wandsworth, London SW18 5NS
•    The appeal is made under Section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
     against a refusal to grant planning permission.
•    The appeal is made by Messing against the decision of the Council of the London Borough of
     Wandsworth.
•    The application Ref is 2025/2919.
•    The development proposed is change of use from C3 (Residential) to a 8 bed HMO (Sui Generis).

Decision
1.    The appeal is dismissed.
Preliminary Matter
2.    The appeal concerns a proposal to use the property as a large eight-person house
      in multiple occupation (HMO). The Council determined the application on the basis
      that the existing use was a Class C3 dwelling, and its assessment of the proposal,
      including its conclusions on the loss of a family-sized home and the introduction of
      a Sui Generis HMO, proceeded from that understanding.
3.    However, the appellant has provided evidence, including tenancy agreements,
      demonstrating that the property is now operating as a six-person HMO pursuant to
      a previously granted lawful development certificate, and my site visit confirmed
      features consistent with multi-occupancy, such as multiple doorbells at the
      property. While I acknowledge the Council’s position at the point of determination,
      the appeal should be decided on the basis of the circumstances that exist at the
      time of my decision, and I have therefore proceeded on the basis that the existing
      use of the building is an HMO.
Main Issues
4.    The main issues are:
           •    whether the proposed development would provide occupiers with
                acceptable access to public transport, having particular regard to relevant
                development plan policies;
           •    the effect of the proposal on the availability of homes suitable for families,
                having particular regard to relevant development plan policies; and
           •    the effect of the proposal on the living conditions of future occupiers of the
                proposed development, having particular regard to daylight to, and outlook
                from, the second-floor front bedroom.

Reasons
Access to public transport
5.    Policy LP29 of the Wandsworth Local Plan 2023 – 2038 (LP) requires that new
      HMOs be located in areas with a public transport accessibility level (PTAL) of 4 or
      higher. The appeal site has a PTAL of 3. The Council’s case is that the proposal
      would intensify HMO occupation in a location that does not meet the accessibility
      requirement for HMOs, and that it therefore conflicts with Policy LP29.
6.    The site benefits from access to public transport and local facilities. Southfields
      Underground Station is within short walking distance, and the surrounding area
      contains bus stops and other services that would be readily available to residents.
      However, Policy LP29 adopts PTAL as the measure of accessibility and sets a
      clear minimum threshold of 4 for HMOs. The appeal site’s PTAL of 3 indicates that
      it does not benefit from the highest levels of public transport accessibility. The
      appellant’s argument that the distinction between PTAL 3 and 4 is overly rigid does
      not overcome the fact that the development plan requires HMOs to be located in
      areas with a PTAL of 4 or higher.
7.    I acknowledge the appellant’s point that Part A of Policy LP29 applies to new large
      HMOs and that the existing use is already an HMO. However, the proposal would
      increase occupation beyond the established six residents and therefore represents
      a further expansion of HMO use that falls to be considered as a new large HMO
      under Part A, as directed by paragraph 17.41 of the LP. The existing use does not
      alter the site’s PTAL rating, nor does it exempt additional occupation from the
      accessibility requirement. In a location with a PTAL of 3, further intensification
      would result in more residents living in an area that does not meet the threshold for
      HMOs, and the existing use does not diminish that policy conflict.
8.    The Council has not disputed that residents would have access to shops and
      services appropriate to their needs. However, the development plan requires more
      than general urban convenience. It requires that HMOs be located in areas with a
      PTAL of 4. For the reasons given above, the proposed development would not
      provide residents with an acceptable level of accessibility to public transport links.
      In these respects, it would not accord with Policy LP29 of the LP.
Effect on existing family accommodation
9.    Policy LP26 of the LP restricts the conversion of dwellings with an original
      floorspace of 130 square metres or less unless they are unsuitable for families.
      Policy LP29 seeks to protect the borough’s stock of family housing from loss to
      HMO use. The Council’s case is that the building was originally constructed and
      occupied as a single-family dwelling of less than 130 square metres and that any
      move to a Sui Generis HMO represents the loss of a family-sized unit. The Council
      emphasises the historic configuration and scale of the building and argues that, on
      that basis, the proposal falls squarely within the scope of both policies.
10. I acknowledge the Council’s position. The original floorspace was below 130
    square metres and, in its original form, the building was capable of occupation by a
    single household. When considered solely against those historic characteristics,
    the Council’s interpretation of Policies LP26 and LP29 is a legitimate one.

11. However, the appeal must be determined in the circumstances that exist at the
    time of my decision. Earlier in this decision I explained, based on the evidence
    before me, that the property is now operating as a six-person HMO pursuant to a
    previously granted lawful development certificate. In these circumstances, the
    building is no longer functioning as a single-family dwelling and its existing use is
    now an HMO in active occupation.
12. In light of the implemented HMO use, the proposal would intensify occupation
    within an established HMO rather than convert a family dwelling to HMO use. The
    property can no longer be realistically regarded as part of the borough’s existing
    stock of family housing. The material harm associated with the loss of a
    family-sized dwelling therefore does not arise. Accordingly, the proposal does not
    result in the loss of a family-sized dwelling for the purposes of Policies LP26 and
    LP29 of the LP, and there is no material conflict with those policies in that regard.
Living conditions
13. The second-floor front bedroom is served only by rooflights set within a sloping
    ceiling, which restrict views to the sky and roofscape and leaves the room without
    any visual connection to its surroundings. As a result, the room does not provide a
    meaningful outlook. On its face, this falls short of the standard sought by Policy
    LP27 of the LP, which expects habitable rooms within new or intensified residential
    accommodation to provide adequate outlook.
14. However, the room already exists in this form and is occupied within the existing
    six-person HMO. Even if permission were refused, the fallback position is that it
    would continue to be used as a bedroom. The proposal involves no physical
    alteration to the room and would not change the nature or quality of the
    accommodation experienced by its occupant. The increase from six to eight
    residents would not, in itself, worsen the living conditions of this particular room,
    and no evidence suggests that additional residents would place any greater
    reliance on it. The absence of meaningful outlook is a feature of the existing
    situation rather than a consequence of the proposed intensification, and the
    proposal would not alter the room’s size, openings or relationship to neighbouring
    buildings. Although the room does not meet the standard sought by Policy LP27
    when assessed in isolation, the proposal would not make the situation worse.
15. I have also considered the adequacy of daylight. Although no technical
    assessment has been provided, the size and positioning of the rooflights indicate
    that the room receives a reasonable level of natural light. Their placement allows
    daylight to penetrate the space effectively, and there is nothing before me to
    suggest that the room would be unduly dim or reliant on artificial lighting for normal
    daytime use.
16. Taking these matters together, I find a technical conflict with Policy LP27 of the LP
    because the room does not provide a meaningful outlook. However, having regard
    to the fallback position and the fact that the proposal would not worsen the existing
    living conditions, I am satisfied that the intensification from six to eight residents
    would not result in unacceptable living conditions for future occupiers. In these
    circumstances, and notwithstanding the identified policy conflict, the proposal is
    acceptable in terms of living conditions, having particular regard to daylight to, and
    outlook from, the second-floor front bedroom.

Planning Balance
17. The proposal would make a more efficient use of an existing lawful HMO by
    allowing two additional occupiers within the same envelope, thereby optimising an
    established shared house without any loss of family housing. It would also provide
    two further units of relatively affordable shared accommodation, contributing to the
    borough’s supply of flexible housing in line with the recognised role of HMOs in
    supporting labour-market mobility and meeting specific housing need. These
    benefits weigh in favour of the proposal.
18. Against these benefits is my finding that the proposed development would not
    provide residents with an acceptable level of accessibility to public transport links
    and, as a result, conflicts with Policy LP29 of the LP. The appellant argues that the
    PTAL element of the policy operates mechanistically and fails to reflect the site’s
    proximity to Southfields Underground Station. However, the underlying objective of
    Policy LP29, ensuring that shared accommodation is located in genuinely
    accessible and well-connected urban locations, is consistent with the National
    Planning Policy Framework, which expects housing for specific groups to be
    directed to sustainable, well-connected places. In this context, Policy LP29 carries
    significant weight, and the harm arising from the proposal’s failure to meet its
    accessibility requirement is harm that outweighs the benefits of the scheme.
Conclusion
19. For the reasons given above, the proposal conflicts with the development plan and
    the material considerations do not indicate that the appeal should be decided other
    than in accordance with it. Accordingly, the appeal should be dismissed.
