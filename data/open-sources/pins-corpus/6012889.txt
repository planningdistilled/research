Appeal Ref: 6012889
8 Flat Cliffs, Primrose Valley, Filey YO14 9RD
•    The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
     against a refusal to grant planning permission.
•    The appeal is made by Sarah and Andrew Shepherd against the decision of North Yorkshire Council.
•    The application Ref is PP-14332912.
•    The development proposed is conservatory, external works and all associated works.

Decision
1.     The appeal is dismissed.
Preliminary Matters
2.     Since the Council's decision was issued, a revised National Planning Policy
       Framework (the Framework) has been published. I have determined the appeal
       having regard to the current version. As the relevant policy requirements relating to
       development in areas susceptible to coastal change remain materially unchanged,
       the revised Framework does not affect my assessment, and no further comments
       have been sought.
3.     The application was submitted retrospectively and some of the works have already
       been carried out. However, this does not alter the assessment of the proposal,
       which I have considered on its planning merits and on the basis of the
       submitted plans.
4.     The appellant raises concerns regarding the description of development used by
       the Council. However, I am satisfied that the appeal relates to the replacement
       conservatory and the associated excavation works, retaining structures and patio
       area shown on the submitted plans. The nature and extent of the development are
       therefore clear and no party has been prejudiced by the description used by the
       Council. I have determined the appeal on the basis of the development before me.
Main Issue
5.     The main issue is whether the development would be acceptable having regard to
       coastal change and cliff stability considerations.
Reasons
6.     The appeal site forms part of the Flat Cliffs settlement at Primrose Valley. The site
       lies within an area identified in the Shoreline Management Plan 2 (River Tyne to
       Flamborough Head, February 2007) and subsequent coastal studies as being
       susceptible to coastal erosion and land instability. The evidence indicates that

       Flat Cliffs is located upon an extensive landslide complex formed in glacial
       deposits and that both coastal erosion and groundwater conditions influence
       slope stability. The established management approach for the frontage is one of
       no active intervention.
7.     The proposal comprises more than just the erection of a replacement
       conservatory. Extensive excavations have been undertaken within the rear garden,
       involving altered levels, retaining walls and the formation of a substantial patio
       area. The sectional drawings indicate significant differences between existing and
       proposed ground levels and therefore the engineering and excavation works form
       an important component of the overall development.
8.     The appellant submits that the works were carefully undertaken and have been in
       place for several years without apparent adverse consequences for cliff stability. In
       addition, reliance is placed upon recent coastal monitoring reports which indicate
       that no significant ground movements were recorded during the latest monitoring
       period. I acknowledge that evidence. I also recognise that recent monitoring
       suggests rates of coastal recession have generally been lower than some
       historical forecasts and that no significant movement was recorded at Flat Cliffs
       during the most recent monitoring period.
9.     However, the absence of recorded movement over a relatively short period does
       not, in itself, demonstrate that the development would be safe over its lifetime or
       would not adversely affect coastal stability. The submitted monitoring reports are
       directed towards wider slope behaviour and risk management across the frontage,
       rather than the effects of the specific development before me.
10. The Council's specialist consultees expressed concern regarding the extent of the
    excavation works and sought further technical evidence regarding stability. I have
    noted the appellant's comments on the consultation process. However,
    irrespective of those concerns, no site-specific engineering report is before me to
    demonstrate the effects of the development on coastal or slope stability.
11. The appellant refers to an email from a consulting engineer which suggests, as an
    initial impression, that some of the works may have reduced surcharge loading on
    the slope. However, that correspondence expressly recognises the need for further
    investigations before any definitive conclusions can be reached. I have also
    considered the appellant's contention that removal of the retaining structures could
    itself have adverse consequences. However, neither assertion is supported by
    substantive technical evidence. In the absence of appropriate site-specific
    analysis, I am unable to conclude whether the development would have a neutral,
    beneficial or harmful effect on cliff stability.
12. The evidence before me consistently identifies Flat Cliffs as a sensitive coastal
    location where groundwater conditions, coastal erosion and landslips influence
    stability. Policy ENV3 of the Scarborough Borough Local Plan (2017) (the SLP)
    seeks to ensure that development does not contribute to or exacerbate coastal
    erosion and landslip and is not exposed to those risks. Furthermore, the
    supporting text to the policy explains that development in areas at risk from coastal
    erosion should demonstrate, through appropriate evidence, that it would be safe
    over its planned lifetime and would not have an unacceptable impact on coastal
    change. This is consistent with Policy F9 of the Framework. Having regard to the
    scale and nature of the excavation and engineering works undertaken, robust

       site-specific technical evidence is required to demonstrate compliance with those
       requirements. The information before me does not satisfactorily do so.
13. Therefore, based on the evidence before me, it has not been demonstrated that
    the development would be safe over its lifetime or that it would not contribute to or
    exacerbate coastal change and cliff instability. As such, the proposal conflicts with
    Policy ENV3 of the SLP regarding development in vulnerable coastal areas.
Planning Balance
14. The development would restore additional living accommodation previously
    available to the dwelling and thereby improve the living conditions of its occupants.
    It would also be likely to deliver a limited improvement in energy efficiency through
    enhanced weather protection. Whilst these are benefits of the proposal, they would
    primarily benefit the occupants of the appeal property and would not result in
    significant wider public benefits. Consequently, I attach limited weight to them.
15. Against those benefits, it has not been demonstrated that the development would
    not adversely affect cliff stability or expose people and property to unacceptable
    risk within a recognised area of coastal instability and change, contrary to
    Policy ENV3 of the SLP and Policy F9 of the Framework.
16. The Framework requires planning decisions to apply a presumption in favour of
    sustainable development. Whilst I have identified some social and environmental
    benefits, they are limited in scale and largely confined to the appeal property. By
    contrast, the proposal has not been demonstrated to be safe in an area affected by
    coastal change and instability. This conflict with the development plan and national
    policy attracts considerable weight.
17. Having regard to the Framework as a whole, I find that the adverse effects arising
    from the unresolved concerns regarding coastal change and land stability
    substantially outweigh the benefits of the development. Accordingly, the proposal
    would not represent sustainable development.
Conclusion
18. For the reasons given above, and having regard to all other matters raised, I
    conclude that the proposal conflicts with the development plan when read as a
    whole. The material considerations, including the Framework, do not outweigh that
    conflict or indicate that a decision should be made other than in accordance with
    the development plan. Accordingly, the appeal should be dismissed.
