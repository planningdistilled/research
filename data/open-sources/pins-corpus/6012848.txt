Appeal Ref: 6012848
813 Manchester Road, Bradford BD5 8LT
•   The appeal is made under Regulation 17 of the Town and Country Planning (Control of
    Advertisements) (England) Regulations 2007 (as amended) against a refusal to grant express consent.
•   The appeal is made by Wildstone Estates Limited against the decision of City of Bradford Metropolitan
    District Council.
•   The application reference is 26/01172/ADV.
•   The advertisement proposed is described as the installation of a digital advertisement display.

Decision
1. The appeal is dismissed.
Preliminary Matters
2. Regulation 3 of the Town and Country Planning (Control of Advertisements)
   (England) Regulations 2007 (as amended) requires that local planning authorities
   control the display of advertisements in the interests of amenity and public safety,
   taking into account the provisions of the development plan, in so far as they are
   material, and any other relevant factors.
3. The Council’s reason for refusal relates specifically to matters of amenity. There was
   no objection raised on the grounds of public safety, and I find no reason to take a
   different view on this matter.
Main Issue
4. The main issue is the effect of the proposed advertisement on the visual amenity of
   the surrounding area.
Reasons
5. The appeal site comprises part of an existing car sales premises occupying a
   prominent position on Manchester Road, a principal route out of Bradford. The
   surrounding area is characterised by a mixture of commercial premises and
   residential development and is therefore not exclusively commercial in nature. The
   street scene includes business signage, street lighting, traffic signals and other
   highway infrastructure, reflecting its busy urban character.
6. The proposed advertisement would comprise a freestanding digital display measuring
   approximately 6 metres in width and 3 metres in height, elevated above ground level
   and positioned at right angles to Manchester Road so as to maximise its visibility to
   users of the dual carriageway.
7. I acknowledge the appellant's contention that advertisements and commercial
   signage are commonplace along Manchester Road and that the proposal would be
   viewed within an established urban environment. However, the presence of existing

     signage does not indicate that additional advertisements can be accommodated

8. By virtue of its scale, elevated siting and illuminated digital format, the proposed
   display would appear as a conspicuous feature within the street scene. Positioned
   adjacent to a principal route through the city, it would attract significant attention from
   a wide range of public viewpoints and would read as a dominant feature in its own
   right rather than as a subsidiary element of the existing premises. As a result, it would
   add materially to the concentration of visual features within the street scene and
   contribute to visual clutter, harming the visual amenity of the locality.
9. Drawing these matters together, I find that the proposal would appear as a visually
   dominant and intrusive feature that would fail to respond positively to its
   surroundings. Consequently, it would harm the visual amenity of the locality and, in
   so far as they are material conflict with Policies DS1 and DS3 of the Bradford Core
   Strategy Development Plan Document 2017, which seek to achieve high quality
   design and ensure that development responds appropriately to local character and
   context.
Other Matters
10. The appellant has drawn my attention to the digital advertisement display at 873-875
    Manchester Road and to other advertisements within the area. Whilst I have had
    regard to those examples, each proposal must be considered on its own merits
    having regard to its particular surroundings. Furthermore, the display at 873-875
    Manchester Road is attached to a building, whereas the appeal proposal would
    comprise a freestanding display positioned alongside the road. Consequently, the
    existence of these examples does not alter my conclusion.
11. I have also considered the wider comparison sites provided in Appendix One of the
    Appellant’s Grounds of Appeal. These appear to be in different streets which have a
    different character to the appeal proposal before me. They are also attached to
    buildings, whereas the appeal proposal would comprise a freestanding display
    positioned alongside the road. This, therefore, has limited bearing on my overall
    conclusions
12. The appellant has referred to a range of economic, social, environmental and
    operational benefits associated with digital advertising. Whilst I have had regard to
    those considerations, they do not outweigh the harm that I have identified to the
    visual amenity of the area.
13. The appellant has also suggested that any concerns could be addressed through
    conditions controlling luminance levels and display intervals. Whilst such conditions
    could regulate the operation of the display, they would not overcome the harm arising
    from the scale, prominence and visual impact of the advertisement itself.
14. I note the appellant's submission that the site is not within a conservation area and is
    not associated with any designated heritage assets. Whilst I have had regard to those
    matters, the absence of heritage designations does not indicate that visual amenity is
    unaffected by development. Advertisement control applies more broadly to the
    character and appearance of the area, and for the reasons given above I find that the
    proposal would result in harm to visual amenity irrespective of the site's heritage
    status.
