Appeal Decision
Site visit made on 2 September 2026
by H Marriott MPlan MRTPI
an Inspector appointed by the Secretary of State
Decision date: 14th September 2026


Appeal Ref: 6011256
Barn at Poplar Farm, Harps Hall Road, Walton Highway PE14 7DL
    The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
     against a refusal to grant planning permission.
    The appeal is made by Mr and Mrs B Wicks against the decision of King's Lynn and West Norfolk
     Borough Council.
    The application Ref is 25/01774/CU.
    The development proposed is for the change of use of an agricultural barn to a commercial use for
     the repair, sale and storage of camper vans and caravans.

Decision
1. The appeal is dismissed.
Preliminary Matter
2. On 17 August 2026, the Government published a revised National Planning Policy
   Framework (the Framework). This latest version of the Framework has introduced
   plan-making and national decision-making policies. It is effective from the day of
   publication. The main parties were given the opportunity to comment on the revised
   Framework, and any comments received have been taken into consideration.
Main Issue
3. The main issue is the effect of the proposed development on highway safety, with
   particular reference to the suitability of the surrounding road network.
Reasons
4.    Harps Hall Road is a narrow rural route linking St Pauls Road South and Station
      Road, around 1km east of the A47 and 2km east of Wisbech. It mainly serves
      sporadic agricultural and residential properties and is subject to the national speed
      limit. The road is generally straight and bound by grass verges, with bends
      towards its eastern end near the junction with Station Road. It lacks footways and
      street lighting reflecting its rural character.
5.    There is no dispute between the main parties that the carriageway varies in width
      and is not always capable of accommodating two-way traffic freely, particularly
      where larger vehicles are involved. Consequently, drivers must exercise care
      when passing oncoming vehicles, including avoiding the carriageway edge where
      possible, particularly where the grass verges are soft and adjoin ditches. During
      my site visit, traffic volumes appeared to be generally low and the carriageway
      surface appeared to be in reasonable condition overall. However, the Local
      Highways Authority (LHA) advises that parts of Harps Hall Road and the
      surrounding highway network are affected by peat-based soil conditions, resulting

       in deformed crowning, longitudinal cracking and undulating surfacing. I observed
       some of these defects during my site visit, and note the specific examples
       provided within the LHA evidence. I also observed restricted visibility at the
       junction with St Pauls Road. Overall, while Harps Hall Road functions adequately
       for its existing rural role, its physical constraints and those of the wider highway
       network, indicate a limited ability to accommodate additional traffic, particularly
       larger vehicles.
6. The appeal site comprises a portal-framed agricultural building (the Barn). It adjoins
   a similar sized grain store and forms part of a group of agricultural buildings. The
   proposed development would facilitate the change of use of the Barn for the
   purchase, repair and refurbishment of second-hand camper vans/caravans for
   subsequent resale. Sales would be arranged online and visits to the site would be
   by appointment only. No vehicle display area or sales forecourt is proposed.
7. Access would be taken from an existing farm access onto Harps Hall Road, which
   leads to a partly unbound and partly concrete-surfaced track, with gates setback
   from the carriageway. The appellant states that this long-established farmyard
   entrance has historically served a range of agricultural vehicles, including tractors,
   trailers, heavy goods vehicles (HGVs) and agricultural machinery. Agricultural
   traffic would continue to use the access, but the internal track would be divided,
   with vehicles associated with the proposed use turning shortly after entering the
   site. Parking and turning space would be provided adjacent to the Barn.
8. While the appellant’s Transport Statement (TS) indicates that continued agricultural
   use of the Barn could generate notable vehicle movements during harvesting and
   haulage periods, the Barn has largely been unused for around 15 years and the
   surrounding farmland is now in separate ownership. Although farming activities
   continue in the area, they are undertaken from elsewhere and are not reliant on the
   appeal site. Consequently, I am not persuaded that the agricultural fallback
   identified in the TS represents a realistic prospect, or that historic agricultural traffic
   provides a meaningful comparison with the proposed development. Instead, the
   proposal would introduce a new use and additional traffic unrelated to the operation
   of the surrounding farmland.
9. It is acknowledged that the proposed use would be modest in scale, with one
   part-time employee, around five camper vans/caravans on site at any one time and
   approximately 25 sales annually. Nevertheless, vehicle movements would arise
   from the delivery and collection of camper vans/caravans, potentially involving
   vehicles towing trailers or low-loaders, together with deliveries of parts and
   materials, employee travel and visits by prospective purchasers most likely by
   means of a private vehicle. Given the rural context and existing low traffic volumes,
   the proposed development has the potential to generate a significant amount of
   additional movements relative to the existing situation.
10. While the limitations of the local highway network are pre-existing, I am not
    satisfied that this additional traffic could be acceptably accommodated. The nature
    of the vehicles associated with the proposed use would be likely to increase
    occasions where two-way traffic flow is impeded, requiring vehicles to wait or
    reverse to allow others to pass. Such manoeuvres would be more difficult where
    larger vehicles, camper vans, caravans or trailers are involved. This would increase
    the potential for conflict with other highway users, including cyclists and horse
    riders. Furthermore, the constrained forward visibility on bends would make

        reversing manoeuvres more difficult and increase the risk of collisions. Whilst I
        have taken into account the absence of traffic accident data on Harps Hill Road,
        such data is only one indicator of highway safety. The LHA with expertise in
        assessing highways matters, objects to the proposal, and in the absence of
        substantive evidence to the contrary, I attach significant weight to their finding that
        the local highway network is unsuited to the additional nature of traffic likely to be
        generated by the proposed use.
11. While a condition restricting the number of camper vans/caravans on the site at any
    one time could be imposed, I am not persuaded that such a restriction would
    overcome my concerns. The identified harm arises principally from the introduction
    of a new commercial use in the countryside and associated nature and movements
    of vehicles generated by the use, rather than solely from the number of vehicles
    stored or being repaired/refurbished on the site. Such a condition would not directly
    control the frequency or nature of those movements, nor would it prevent towed
    vehicles or low-loaders associated with the business from using the local highway
    network.
12. Notwithstanding the appellant’s comments regarding whether or not the conditions
    suggested by the LHA meet the relevant tests, there is no dispute between the
    main parties that the visibility spays at the site entrance could be acceptable and
    that adequate parking and turning would take place within the site, ensuring
    vehicles can enter and exit in a forward gear. However, these matters do not
    overcome my concerns regarding the suitability of the highway network to
    accommodate the additional traffic likely to be generated by the proposed use.
13. I conclude that the proposed development would result in an unacceptable effect
    on highway safety, with particular reference to the suitability of the surrounding
    road network. In this regard, it would conflict with Policies LP13 and LP18 of the
    King’s Lynn and West Norfolk Local Plan 2021-2040 (2025) which require
    development proposals to be accessible and to demonstrate they provide for safe
    and convenient access for all modes. For the same reasons, there would be conflict
    with the promoting sustainable transport policies of the Framework, including
    Policies TR3 and TR6.
Other Matters
14. The appellant has drawn my attention to two developments approved on other sites
    along Harps Hall Road close to the appeal site, including a solar farm1 which has
    not yet been constructed, and a floristry business2.
15. In the case of the solar farm, despite the significant traffic anticipated during the
    construction phase, the LHA raised no objection and the Inspector noted that such
    impacts could be mitigated, including through the provision of passing places and
    other traffic management measures. By contrast, although the volume of traffic
    associated with the proposed use would be less, it would not be temporary, would
    persist for its lifetime and no comparable mitigation is proposed. Even if it was, I
    have insufficient evidence before me to conclude that similar mitigation would be
    effective in this case. Moreover, the solar farm was assessed on its own particular
    facts and circumstances which are unlikely to all be applicable to the appeal
    proposal.

16. In relation to the floristry business, I have limited information before me regarding
    the nature or frequency of vehicle movements associated with that use.
    Nevertheless, it is evident that the characteristics of that proposal differ from those
    of the appeal proposal before me. For example, the floristry business does not
    appear to rely on customer visits to the site or the delivery by trailered vehicles or
    low-loaders. Furthermore, the LHA considered that the fallback position associated
    with the previous use of the floristry building, made an objection difficult to
    substantiate. Consequently, neither development provides a direct comparison with
    the proposal before me and neither provides justification for a different outcome in
    this appeal.
17. A prior approval application for the change of use of the Barn from agricultural to
    light industrial use under Class R3 was refused by the Council in 20184. Although
    the refusal related to the conditions, limitations and restrictions of that permitted
    development right, the Council stated on its decision notice that, had those matters
    not arisen, prior approval would have been refused on grounds relating to the
    transport and highways impacts of that proposal. The appellant also advises that
    the site benefits from an HGV operator’s license for up to five units. However, the
    LHA explains that such licenses relate to the suitability of the site itself and do not
    assess the suitability of the surrounding highway network. I have also had regard to
    the letters of support submitted in respect of the proposal. Nevertheless, these
    matters do not lead me to a different conclusion on the appeal before me.
Planning Balance and Conclusion
18. Policy S3 of the Framework requires a presumption in favour of sustainable
    development in line with Policy S5 which relates to development outside
    settlements. This policy states that only certain forms of development should be
    approved outside settlements, unless the benefits of doing so would be
    substantially outweighed by any adverse effects, when assess against the
    national-decision making policies in the Framework.
19. In this case, the proposed development does comprise one of the forms of
    development listed as being acceptable in the countryside, namely the reuse of an
    existing building. The proposed development would bring an underused agricultural
    building back into use and would support the diversification of an existing business
    based in Wisbech, providing a modest level of employment of benefit to the rural
    economy. Policy E2 of the Framework states that substantial weight should be
    given to the economic benefits of proposals for commercial development which
    allow businesses to invest, expand and/or adapt.
20. Nevertheless, I have found that the proposed development would result in an
    unacceptable effect on highway safety. This is a matter that results in conflict with
    the development plan as whole. Moreover, the circumstances in which the benefits
    of approving development proposals are likely to be outweighed by adverse effects
    include situations where the development proposals would fail to comply with Policy
    TR6 of the Framework, a matter which attracts considerable weight. There is also
    conflict with the Framework’s promoting sustainable transport policies.
21. Taking into account the above conclusions, I find that the benefits of approving the
    proposed development would be substantially outweighed by the adverse effects,
