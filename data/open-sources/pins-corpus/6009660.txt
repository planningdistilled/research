Appeal Decision
Site visit made on 7 August 2026
by Gary Deane BSc (Hons) DipTP MRTPI
an Inspector appointed by the Secretary of State
Decision date: 24 August 2026


Appeal Ref: 6009660
6 Rooth Street, Wednesbury WS10 9QP
•   The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
    against a refusal to grant planning permission.
•   The appeal is made by Mr C Homer against the decision of Sandwell Metropolitan Borough Council.
•   The application reference is DC/25/71294.
•   The development proposed is internal alterations to form a change of use from a 6-person HMO
    (Class C4) to a 7-bed HMO (Sui Generis).

Decision
1. The appeal is allowed, and planning permission is granted for internal alterations to
   form a change of use from a 6-person House in Multiple Occupation (Class C4) to a
   7-bedroom House in Multiple Occupation (Sui Generis) at 6 Rooth Street,
   Wednesbury WS10 9QP in accordance with the terms of the application reference
   DC/25/71294, subject to the conditions set out in the schedule to this decision.
Procedural and preliminary matters
2. For clarity, the description of development in my decision refers to a ‘House in
   Multiple Occupation’ and to ‘bedroom’ rather than their abbreviated versions, HMO
   and bed as stated on the application forms lodged with the Council.
3. Planning law requires that applications for planning permission be determined in
   accordance with the development plan, unless material considerations indicate
   otherwise. The Council's reason for refusal does not allege conflict with a specific
   development plan policy. It does, however, refer to the National Planning Policy
   Framework, which is a relevant consideration.
4. A new National Planning Policy Framework (the Framework) was issued on
   17 August 2026. The Framework replaces the previous version published in
   December 2024 that was in force when the Council determined the application. I
   have had regard to the current Framework in reaching my decision. It does not
   raise any new matters that are determinative of the outcome of this appeal.
Main issue
5. The main issue is the effect of the proposal on highway safety and operation of the
   local road network with particular regard to on-street vehicle parking.
Reasons
6. The appeal property is a mid-terrace building in lawful use as a 6-person House in
   Multiple Occupation (HMO) with no off-street parking. The proposal is to provide

     one additional bedroom within the existing building. As a result, the maximum
     number of residents within the HMO would increase from 6 to 7. The Council
     considers that the proposed increase would generate a requirement for 1 additional
     on-street parking space.
7. The appellant's parking surveys conclude that, while roads within 200 metres of the
   site are well used, there is sufficient overall capacity for vehicle parking associated
   with the proposal. Nevertheless, the survey findings demonstrate that parking
   demand is not evenly spread across the surveyed area. Therefore, it is appropriate
   to consider each road within the study area in turn. It is along these roads that the
   Council and others are concerned that the proposal would exacerbate on-street
   parking problems and add to inconvenience experienced by residents.
8. Rooth Street is closest to the site and therefore most likely to be used by residents
   of the HMO. The appellant’s parking surveys found this road to be consistently
   heavily parked with examples of vehicles parked on double yellow lines and across
   crossovers. Brunswick Park Road and James Watt Drive were also identified as
   being close to capacity, with only marginal spare provision. On that basis, these
   roads do not provide a reliable source of capacity to cater for the additional demand
   arising from the proposal.
9. I share the Council's concern that Wood Green Road would be inappropriate to rely
   upon for parking associated with the HMO, given its function as a classified A road
   and the parking restrictions in place along the highway in the vicinity of Rooth
   Street. Hobs Road was also found to be over capacity when the surveys were
   carried out. Consequently, it, too, offers no realistic spare parking provision.
10. Jockey Lane is further away from the site although it still lies within the survey area.
    The appellant’s parking surveys consistently showed spare capacity, with no
    examples recorded of vehicles parked illegally or inconsiderately. During my
    afternoon site visit, I walked the route between the appeal property and Jockey
    Lane several times and found it to be reasonably direct, well-lit and served by a
    signal-controlled crossing of Wood Green Road. That journey would not present
    any obvious difficulty for a person walking between the appeal property and a
    parked vehicle. On that basis, I see no reason that the spare parking capacity on
    Jockey Lane should be ruled out simply because it lies on the opposite side of
    Wood Green Road to the site, as the Council suggests.
11. I recognise that on-street parking conditions in the vicinity of the site are already
    constrained, and that this is a source of considerable concern to residents.
    However, the test before me is the effect of the increase from 6 to 7 residents
    within the HMO, not existing parking conditions generally. Given the spare capacity
    available on Jockey Lane in particular, which I consider is within reasonable
    walking distance of the site, I am satisfied that the additional demand arising from
    the proposal would not result in an unacceptable impact on highway safety or a
    severe residual cumulative impact on the road network.
12. On the main issue, I therefore conclude that the proposed development would
    accord with Policy TR6 of the current Framework. Insofar as Policy ENV3 of the
    Black Country Core Strategy and Policy SAD EOS9 of the Site Allocations and
    Delivery Development Plan Document are relevant to the proposal, I find no conflict
    with them.

13. The Officer’s report records that refuse storage can be accommodated within the
    rear garden of No 6. It also notes that Private Sector Housing had granted a
    licence for the 6-person HMO and that the addition of the 7th room raised no
    concerns with regard to the living environment or provision of shared facilities. I
    see no reason to reach a different conclusion on these matters.
14. Interested parties have also raised concerns regarding property maintenance,
    landscaping, the ongoing management of refuse, visitor parking, and the general
    management and behaviour of occupiers. None of these matters formed part of the
    Council's reason for refusal. I have seen nothing in the submitted evidence to
    persuade me that the addition of 1 bedroom to the HMO would result in
    demonstrable harm on any of these grounds. They do not, therefore, provide a
    basis on which to withhold planning permission in this instance.
Conditions
15. It is necessary to impose the standard time limit condition, together with a condition
    requiring that the development be carried out in accordance with the approved
    plans in the interests of certainty. For the avoidance of doubt, a further condition
    limits the number of people residing within the HMO to 7 at any one time because it
    reflects the development sought and my assessment of its effects.
16. The Council has also suggested conditions requiring the permanent retention of the
    communal kitchen and a restriction to prevent the use of the appeal property as
    self-contained accommodation. However, I am satisfied that, having regard to the
    description of development, the approved plans, the HMO licensing regime, and the
    conditions imposed, these additional conditions are not necessary. They have not,
    therefore, been imposed.
Conclusion
17. I have found no conflict with the development plan insofar as its policies bear on
    the matters before me. I have also had regard to the other material considerations,
    including the current Framework, and find that none indicates that planning
    permission should be withheld. For the reasons given above, I therefore conclude
    that the appeal should be allowed.
