Arndale Centre, Market Street, Manchester, M4 3AQ
•   The appeal is made under Regulation 17 of the Town and Country Planning (Control of
    Advertisements) (England) Regulations 2007 (as amended) against a refusal to grant express
    consent.
•   The appeal is made by Xrafit 2 Limited against the decision of Manchester City Council.
•   The application Ref is 144365/AO/2025.
•   The advertisement proposed is Units 19A Market Street, Level 42 (Mezzanine) & Unit ABCD -
    Replacement external entrance finishes, including replacement entrance doors, to Market Street &
    Corporation Street entrances. New external facade signage, new internal facade signage (behind
    existing glazing systems), along with new internal facade translucent LED screens (behind existing
    glazing systems). New internal facade lighting installations (behind existing glazing).

Decision
1. The appeal is dismissed.
Appeal Procedure
2. The site visit was undertaken by a representative of the Inspector whose
   recommendation is set out below and to which the Inspector has had regard before
   deciding the appeal.
Preliminary Matters
3. This application was determined as a split decision by Manchester City Council.
   Advertisement consent was granted for the fascia and projecting signs, and these
   elements are not in dispute. This appeal relates solely to the refusal of the
   proposed high-level internally illuminated sign on the rotunda. Accordingly, this
   report concerns that element only.
4. Regulation 3(1) of the Town and Country Planning (Control of Advertisements)
   (England) Regulations 2007 (as amended) (the Advert Regulations) makes clear
   that advertisements should be subject to control only in the interests of amenity and
   public safety. Regard does not need to be had to the development plan. Therefore,
   while I have taken relevant development plan policies into account as material
   considerations, they have not been determinative.
5. The appeal site is located within the St Ann’s Conservation Area (the CA).
   Accordingly, in my consideration of the appeals I have been mindful of my statutory
   duty under Section 72(1) of the Planning (Listed Buildings and Conservation Areas)
   Act 1990 (the Act) which requires special regard be paid to the desirability of
   preserving or enhancing the character or appearance of the CA. For advertisement
   appeals this is only relevant in so far as it relates to visual amenity.

6. The site is also located close to the Royal Exchange, a Grade II listed building.
   Section 66(1) of the Act does not apply to applications for advertisement consent.
   Accordingly, in respect of the proximity of the site to listed buildings is again only
   relevant insofar as it relates to visual amenity.
Main Issue
7. The main issue is the effect of the proposal on the visual amenity of the area, with
   particular regards to the CA and Grade II listed Royal Exchange.
Reasons for the Recommendation
8. The appeal site lies within the setting of the St Ann’s Square Conservation Area, a
   designated heritage asset of special architectural and historic interest. The
   conservation area derives its significance from the presence of landmark civic and
   commercial buildings. While the conservation area contains a mix of building types
   and periods, its character is defined by the legible relationship between ground-
   floor commercial activity, upper facades and key heritage assets, rather than by
   uniformity of style or signage.
9. The appeal site is located opposite the Grade II listed Royal Exchange, one of the
   most architecturally and historically significant buildings in the area. Its importance
   lies in its classical design, fine stone detailing, and its prominence in views across
   the city centre, including from Market Street, Corporation Street, and St Ann’s
   Square itself. The significance of the Royal Exchange is appreciated not only
   though its fabric, but through its setting, which allows the building to be read as a
   coherent and dominant civic structure within the surrounding townscape.
10. The appeal site forms part of the Arndale shopping centre, a modern building
    finished with large glazed panels and red brick. It includes prominent cylindrical
    rotunda features, one of which forms the appeal site.
11. The proposed advertisement would be illuminated in a bright green colour and
    would occupy a substantial portion of the glazed elevation. Owing to its elevated
    siting, curvature and illumination, the sign would be visible from multiple
    approaches, including longer views through Market Street, Corporation Street,
    Cross Street, and from within St Ann’s Square.
12. While the area accommodates modern retail activity and contemporary
    advertisements, in this instance, the proposed advertisement would introduce a
    new, dominant layer of illuminated advertising above the established line of ground-
    floor shopfronts, where commercial signage is principally located. The elevated
    positioning, scale and brightness of the sign would disrupt the established visual
    hierarchy of the street and draw undue attention upward, increasing visual noise in
    an already busy environment.
13. Despite examples being given of other similar signage, the examples largely relate
    to fascia or projecting signs associated with entrances and shopfronts, and do not
    establish a precedent for prominent, wrap-around, first-floor signage at this highly
    visible location. Nor does the existence of varied colours at street level justify the
    introduction of a highly prominent illuminated feature at upper level within the CA’s
    setting.

14. When read together with the adjacent LED screen, the proposal would contribute to
    a cluster of high-level illuminated advertisements, resulting in perceptible visual
    clutter. This would erode the cohesiveness of the townscape and detract from the
    visual amenity of the area, including the CA and particularly in views experienced
    by pedestrians moving through St Ann’s Square.
15. The proposed sign would form part of the setting of the Royal Exchange and would
    be visible in views where the Royal Exchange is presently appreciated against a
    background of commercial buildings that do not visually compete with its
    architectural presence. The introduction of a bright, internally illuminated green sign
    at first-floor level would introduce a visually assertive and incongruous element into
    these views. Its colour, illumination and scale would contrast sharply with the Royal
    Exchange’s muted sandstone façade and refined classical detailing, drawing
    attention away from the listed building rather than allowing it to remain the focal
    point.
16. Taken together, the proposed advertisement would have an unacceptable harmful
    effect on visual amenity. Though not determinative, I have taken account of Places
    for Everyone Policies JP-Strat2, JP-P1 and JP-P2; Core Strategy Policies SP1,
    CC2, CC9, EN1, EN3, and DM1; saved UDP Policies DC14, DC18.1a(iv) and
    DC19, which seek to preserve the character of the area.
Conclusion and Recommendation
17. For the reasons given above and having had regard to all other matters raised, I
    recommend that the appeal should be dismissed.
