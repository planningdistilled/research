Appeal Ref: 6009126
6 Grenadier Drive, Liverpool L12 4ZN
•    The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
     against a refusal to grant planning permission.
•    The appeal is made by Mr James Edwards against the decision of Liverpool City Council.
•    The application Ref is 26F/0005.
•    The development proposed is the change of use from Class C3 (dwellinghouse) to Class C2
     (children’s residential care home) to accommodate up to three children.

Decision
1.     The appeal is dismissed.
Preliminary Matters
2.     The description of development above has been amended to omit superfluous
       wording relating to works that do not form part of the proposal.
3.     During the appeal proceedings the appellant has provided additional information,
       including clarification of the proposed staffing arrangements, a management
       statement, a parking management policy and a parking arrangement plan.
       Updated comments have also been received from Merseyside Police (the Police).
       As interested parties have had the opportunity to comment on this additional
       information, I am satisfied that no party would be prejudiced by its consideration.
Main Issues
4.     The main issues are:
           •    whether the proposed development would be a suitable location for future
                occupiers, with particular regard to safeguarding and community safety; and
           •    the effect of the proposed development on the living conditions of existing
                neighbouring occupiers, with particular regard to noise and disturbance.
Reasons
Suitable Location
5.     The proposed change of use of the dwelling to a children’s care home would
       accommodate 3 children aged between 7 and 16. The property lies within an
       established residential estate, known as The Point. The surrounding area is
       therefore predominantly residential in character.
6.     The appellant has referred to publicly available crime data to suggest that
       recorded incidents in the local West Derby Deysbrook area are lower than
       Liverpool City overall. However, the matter before me is not whether crime levels

       are high, but whether the appeal site is in a location that would provide a safe and
       suitable environment for a vulnerable group of future occupants.
7.     In this regard, I attach significant weight to the Police objection, which identifies
       multiple safeguarding concerns specific to the location that bear directly on the
       safety and suitability of the environment for future occupants. These include
       evidence of criminal exploitation of children, inadequate youth provision
       contributing to anti-social behaviour, the immediate area’s disproportionate crime
       levels, accounting for around one fifth of all local crimes, and several serious
       offences.
8.     The appellant submits that any risks to future occupiers could be mitigated through
       pre-placement assessments, staff supervision, training, risk assessment
       procedures, contact with safeguarding partners, along with measures to address
       missing-from-home incidents and the risks of child criminal and sexual exploitation.
       Reference is also made to management protocols intended to minimise the risk of
       children using public transport unsupervised. It is argued that these measures
       could be secured by conditions.
9.     I note that the Council's Children's Services team raised no objection to the
       proposal and confirm that the proposal accords with Liverpool’s sufficiency needs
       within children’s social care. I also acknowledge the appellant's evidence that a
       number of children's homes are operating within the city without Ofsted registration
       which, it is advanced, points to wider pressures on placement provision and the
       need for appropriate, regulated accommodation.
10. The appellant further refers to the presence of other children's homes within the
    general L12 area. However, limited information has been provided regarding their
    particular circumstances and locations. Consequently, I cannot be satisfied that
    they are directly comparable to the appeal proposal.
11. Nevertheless, the matter before me is whether the appeal site is, in principle, in a
    location that would provide a safe and suitable environment for vulnerable future
    occupants. In this respect, I have had regard to paragraphs 96 and 135 of the
    National Planning Policy Framework (the Framework) which together seek to
    ensure that development provides healthy, inclusive and safe places where crime
    and disorder, and the fear of crime do not undermine quality of life. I am also
    mindful of the duty under Section 17 of the Crime and Disorder Act 1998, which
    requires local authorities, and by extension decision-makers, to do all that they
    reasonably can to prevent crime and disorder.
12. Given all of the above, whilst I fully appreciate the appellant’s commitment to
    providing a safe, supportive and well-managed environment, I am not convinced
    that the significant concerns highlighted by the Council and the Police would be
    satisfactorily addressed by means of planning conditions. Consequently, I find that
    the proposed development would not be a suitable location for future occupiers,
    with particular regard to safeguarding and community safety. It would therefore
    conflict with Policy STP2 of the Liverpool Local Plan 2013-2033 (January 2022)
    (Local Plan), which seeks to support development that contributes towards the
    creation, improvement or maintenance of healthy, safe, sociable and inclusive
    communities, when read in the context of the Framework and relevant
    safeguarding duties.

Living Conditions
13. The appeal site comprises a relatively modern detached 3-4 bedroom dwelling on
    an estate where neighbouring properties are similar in scale and design. A single
    parking space is provided to the front, with the remainder of the frontage
    landscaped and open in character. The proposal demonstrates that 3 bedrooms
    on the first floor would each accommodate a child and a room on the ground floor
    would be used as a staff room and office. The appellant contends that the day-to-
    day living patterns would remain domestic in character and no external alterations
    are proposed.
14. However, the appellant has clarified that alongside the proposed 3 children
    residing at the property, a minimum of 2 and a maximum of 4 staff members would
    be present on site at any one time, with additional support and visitors possible, as
    and when required. Staffing would comprise a daytime shift from 7am to 10pm and
    a night shift from 10pm to 7am. Notwithstanding this, the evidence regarding staff
    changeovers lacks clarity, with submissions indicating that they would typically
    occur at around 10am and only 2-3 times per week.
15. The appeal property benefits from a driveway and an open landscaped frontage,
    with the principal entrance positioned on the side elevation of a projecting single
    storey element, facing the common boundary with 8 Grenadier Drive (No 8). As a
    result, access to the property is in close proximity to the ground floor lounge
    window and frontage of No 8.
16. Despite the lack of objection from the Council’s Environmental Protection Unit, I
    find that the proposal would result in a greater level of activity than that typically
    associated with a dwelling of this size. The presence of staff, shift changes,
    residents and visiting professionals would increase movements to and from the
    site which, given the close relationship with No 8, would be more noticeable and
    intrusive. In addition, the indicated possibility of extending the driveway across the
    full width of the frontage, to provide an additional parking space, would bring
    associated vehicular movements even closer to No 8.
17. The appellant contends that noise and disturbance could be minimised through the
    implementation of a management and parking plan. However, I am not satisfied
    that such measures would sufficiently mitigate the impacts arising from the
    intensified use of the dwelling to an acceptable level.
18. Consequently, I find that the proposal would materially harm the living conditions
    of neighbouring occupiers, with particular regard to noise and disturbance. It would
    therefore conflict with Policy H7 of the Local Plan which seeks to protect the
    amenity of existing residents.
Other Matters
19. I have had due regard to the Public Sector Equality Duty contained in section 149
    of the Equality Act 2010, which sets out the need to eliminate unlawful
    discrimination and to advance equality of opportunity. The intended occupants
    would be children, and I recognise the importance of safeguarding their welfare
    and advancing equality of opportunity.
20. However, this duty is not absolute and must be balanced alongside other material
    considerations. In this case, significant weight is attributed to the harms arising

       from the unsuitability of the location and the adverse impacts on the living
       conditions of neighbouring occupiers. My conclusions are based on the evidence
       before me, including the Police objection, and are consistent with the aims of the
       Equality Act.
21. The Council has not raised concerns regarding highway safety. However, while I
    have no reason to conclude otherwise, this is a neutral factor which does not
    weigh in favour of allowing the appeal.
22. Representations by third parties raise an assortment of other matters including
    concerns in respect of parking and highway safety, fear of crime, increase in anti-
    social behaviour, and loss of family housing. However, these matters did not form
    the basis on which the Council refused the planning application nor, therefore, do
    they comprise the key areas of dispute between the Council and the appellant. In
    light of this, and since I am dismissing the appeal, it is not necessary for me to
    address these matters in detail.
Conclusion
23. The proposal conflicts with the development plan and the material considerations
    do not indicate that the appeal should be decided other than in accordance with it.
    The appeal should therefore be dismissed.
