Appeal Ref: 6010348
Barn on West Lane, Galphay, Ripon HG4 3NU
•    The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
     against a refusal to grant planning permission.
•    The appeal is made by A Burningham against the decision of North Yorkshire Council.
•    The application Ref is 26/00533/FUL.
•    The development proposed is erect 1no dwelling including associated works to form new site access
     and driveway and demolition of existing agricultural building.

Decision
1.    The appeal is dismissed.
Preliminary Matters
2.    A revised National Planning Policy Framework (the Framework) was published on
      17 August 2026. The main parties were given the opportunity to comment on the
      changes introduced by the revised Framework, and I have taken their responses
      into account in determining the appeal.
3.    It is common ground that the appeal site benefits from an extant planning
      permission, reference 25/03836/FUL, for the conversion of the existing agricultural
      building to a dwelling. The permission remains extant and capable of
      implementation, and the appellant indicates that they would pursue the approved
      development should this appeal fail. Consequently, I am satisfied that the
      approved scheme represents a realistic fallback position and I have had regard to
      it as a consideration in determining the appeal.
Main Issues
4.    The main issues are:
           •    Whether the appeal site is a suitable location for the proposal, having
                regard to development plan policies concerning housing development in the
                countryside; and
           •    Whether the proposal would conserve and enhance the character and
                appearance of the area and the special qualities of the Nidderdale
                National Landscape.
Reasons
Location of new housing
5.    The appeal site comprises a traditional agricultural barn and associated structures
      located in open countryside to the south of the village of Galphay.

6.    Policies GS2 and GS3 of the Harrogate District Local Plan (2020) (the HLP) seek
      to direct development to sustainable locations and generally resist new dwellings
      in the countryside unless supported by other development plan policies or
      national policy. These policies remain broadly consistent with the Framework’s
      objectives to promote sustainable patterns of development.
7.    The site lies outside any defined development limit and, owing to its physical
      separation from the village, is not well related to the existing built form of Galphay.
      The surrounding roads are narrow, unlit country lanes with limited opportunities for
      safe pedestrian movement. Although Galphay contains a modest range of
      community facilities, access to services and facilities by means other than private
      car is limited. As a result, future occupiers would be likely to rely heavily on private
      vehicle travel to meet many of their day-to-day needs. Consequently, the site
      would not strongly support sustainable patterns of movement.
8.    The appellant places considerable reliance on the extant permission for the
      conversion of the barn to a dwelling. I have had regard to the officer’s assessment,
      which acknowledged that the extent of repair and rebuilding required would be
      substantial and that, in practical terms, the scheme would amount to a significant
      rebuilding exercise.
9.    Nevertheless, the approved scheme remains one for the retention, repair and
      adaptation of the existing building. It does not authorise the complete demolition of
      the traditional barn and its replacement with a new dwelling. Furthermore, the
      acceptability of the approved scheme was assessed against policies that
      specifically support the re-use and conversion of rural buildings. By contrast, the
      appeal proposal is for a new dwelling in the countryside, and I have identified no
      development plan or national policy that would support such development in
      this location.
10. I have carefully considered the structural evidence submitted by the appellant.
    Whilst that evidence identifies a number of defects within the building, it does not
    demonstrate that demolition is unavoidable or that the extant permission could not
    reasonably be implemented. On the evidence before me, I am not persuaded that
    the approved conversion is no longer a realistic prospect.
11. I attach significant weight to the extant permission, which demonstrates that a
    dwelling could lawfully be provided on the site. However, the appeal scheme is
    materially different from the fallback position. Rather than re-using the existing
    building, it would involve its complete demolition and the erection of a replacement
    dwelling in a different position. Consequently, whilst the fallback position reduces
    the weight to be attached to the conflict arising from the proposal’s countryside
    location, it does not remove that conflict.
12. For these reasons, I conclude that the appeal site would not represent a suitable
    location for a new dwelling, having particular regard to its countryside location, its
    separation from the existing built form of Galphay and the reliance that future
    occupiers would place on private car travel to access services and facilities. The
    proposal therefore conflicts with Policies GS2 and GS3 of the HLP.

Character and appearance and the National Landscape
13. The appeal site lies within the Nidderdale National Landscape. Section 245 of the
    Levelling-up and Regeneration Act 2023 requires decision-makers to seek to
    further the purpose of conserving and enhancing the natural beauty of the
    designated landscape.
14. Policy GS6 of the HLP seeks to conserve and enhance the natural beauty and
    special qualities of the Nidderdale National Landscape. Policy HP3 requires
    development to protect, enhance or reinforce those characteristics, qualities and
    features that contribute to local distinctiveness. Policy NE4 seeks to protect,
    enhance and restore landscape character, including the aesthetic qualities of both
    natural and man-made features. Taken together, these policies are consistent with
    the statutory purpose of the designation and with national policy relating to
    protected landscapes.
15. The existing stone barn is a traditional agricultural building that forms part of the
    area’s historic farmed landscape. It contributes positively to local distinctiveness
    and is a characteristic feature of the rural landscape that underpins the recognised
    special qualities of the National Landscape.
16. I acknowledge that the proposed dwelling would be similar in scale and
    appearance to that capable of being delivered under the extant conversion
    permission. Consequently, the completed development would not appear
    materially more prominent or intrusive within the landscape than the
    fallback scheme.
17. However, the principal matter in dispute is not the appearance of the completed
    dwelling but the loss of the existing traditional agricultural building. Whilst the
    structural evidence before me identifies some defects and deterioration affecting
    the barn, it does not demonstrate that demolition is unavoidable. Nor does it
    establish that the extant conversion permission could not reasonably be
    implemented. Rather, the evidence identifies repair, stabilisation and remedial
    works capable of addressing the defects identified.
18. In those circumstances, I am not persuaded that the complete demolition of the
    existing barn has been justified. The proposal would therefore result in the
    unnecessary loss of a traditional rural building that contributes positively to local
    distinctiveness and the agricultural character of the landscape. Whilst the fallback
    position is a material consideration, it is a permission that would repair and re-use
    the existing building. Consequently, the fallback position does not negate the harm
    arising from the loss of the traditional building.
19. I therefore conclude that the proposal would result in the unjustified loss of a
    traditional agricultural building that contributes positively to local distinctiveness,
    rural character and the special qualities of the Nidderdale National Landscape. As
    such, the proposal would not conserve or enhance an element of the landscape
    that makes a positive contribution to the area’s character and special qualities. It
    therefore conflicts with Policies GS6, HP3 and NE4 of the HLP. Having regard to
    the statutory purpose of the designation and the need to give substantial weight to
    the conservation and enhancement of the natural beauty of the designated area, I
    find that this harm weighs materially against the proposal.

Other Matters
20. The Highway Authority advises that the existing barn currently provides support to
    the adjacent highway due to the difference in ground levels between the site and
    the carriageway. The proposal would replace this arrangement with a retaining
    wall, which would provide a more robust long-term engineering solution. In
    addition, the vehicular access would be repositioned to a location that the
    Highway Authority considers to be safer than the existing point of access.
21. The relocation of the access would provide a clear improvement over the extant
    permission and would represent a benefit of the proposal. However, the evidence
    before me does not demonstrate that the highway support works could only be
    achieved through implementation of the appeal scheme. Nor is there substantive
    evidence that comparable engineering measures could not be delivered in
    conjunction with the extant conversion scheme. Consequently, whilst I attach
    moderate weight to the improved vehicular access, I attach more limited weight to
    the highway support works.
22. The appellant also contends that a replacement dwelling would achieve better
    environmental performance than the approved conversion scheme. However, the
    evidence before me contains no detailed assessment of the comparative
    measurable sustainability credentials of the two schemes. Consequently, I am
    unable to conclude that the environmental benefits claimed would be significant
    and I therefore attach little weight to this matter.
23. Reference has also been made to the contribution that the proposal would make
    towards housing delivery, particularly in light of the Council's inability to
    demonstrate a five-year supply of deliverable housing sites. However, an extant
    planning permission already exists for the creation of a dwelling on the site.
    Consequently, the appeal proposal would not materially increase housing supply
    beyond that which could already lawfully occur. In these circumstances, I attach
    little weight to this consideration.
Planning Balance
24. The proposal conflicts with Policies GS2 and GS3 of the HLP because it would
    introduce a new dwelling in a countryside location that is not well related to the
    existing built form of Galphay and would not support sustainable patterns of
    movement due to the reliance that future occupiers would place on private car
    travel to access services and facilities. It would also result in the unjustified loss of
    a traditional agricultural building that contributes positively to local distinctiveness,
    landscape character and the special qualities of the Nidderdale National
    Landscape, contrary to Policies GS6, HP3 and NE4 of the HLP.
25. In reaching that conclusion, I have had full regard to the national decision-making
    policies of the Framework. As the proposal would not strongly support sustainable
    patterns of movement it would be contrary to the aims of Policy TR3. Furthermore,
    the unjustified failure to conserve and enhance an element that contributes
    positively to the special qualities of the Nidderdale National Landscape is contrary
    to the objectives of Policy N4. In accordance with Policy N4 and the statutory
    purpose of the designation, I attach substantial weight to the conservation and
    enhancement of the National Landscape.

26. Set against those adverse effects, I attach significant weight to the fallback
    position afforded by the extant permission, which would allow a dwelling to be
    provided through the retention and conversion of the existing barn. Whilst this
    materially reduces the weight that I attach to the locational conflict arising from the
    provision of a dwelling in this countryside location, it does not diminish the harm
    arising from the loss of the traditional building. I also attach moderate weight to the
    improved vehicular access, more limited weight to the highway support works, and
    little weight to the claimed environmental and housing benefits.
27. The Framework requires a presumption in favour of sustainable development to be
    applied in decision making. As the site lies outside a settlement, I have considered
    the proposal against Policy S5. Although the proposal would replace an existing
    building, Policy S5(1)(c) does not apply because the replacement building would
    not be for the same use. Nor am I persuaded that the proposal falls within any
    other category of development identified by Policy S5. Whilst the Council cannot
    demonstrate a five-year supply of deliverable housing sites, the proposal would not
    materially increase housing delivery beyond that already capable of occurring
    under the extant permission and would not be physically well-related to an
    existing settlement.
28. Consequently, Policy S5(4) applies, which states that development proposals
    outside settlements which do not fall within one of the identified categories should
    only be approved in exceptional circumstances, where the benefits of the
    proposal would substantially outweigh the adverse effects, including those
    relating to the character of the countryside and the promotion of sustainable
    patterns of movement.
29. Having applied the presumption in favour of sustainable development and
    assessed the proposal against the national decision-making policies of the
    Framework as a whole, I find that the benefits of the proposal, together with the
    significant weight attached to the fallback position, would not substantially
    outweigh the adverse effects arising from the conflict with policies relating to
    development in the countryside, the promotion of sustainable patterns of
    movement, and the conservation and enhancement of the Nidderdale National
    Landscape. The exceptional circumstances required by Policy S5(4) have
    therefore not been demonstrated.
30. Accordingly, the proposal would not represent sustainable development when
    assessed against the national decision-making policies of the Framework as
    a whole.
Conclusion
31. For the reasons given above, and having regard to all other matters raised, I
    conclude that the proposal conflicts with the development plan when read as a
    whole. There are no material considerations that indicate a decision should be
    made other than in accordance with the development plan. Accordingly, the appeal
    should be dismissed.
