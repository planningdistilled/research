Appeal Decision
Site visit made on 23 June 2026
by Chris Mayes CMLI
an Inspector appointed by the Secretary of State
Decision date: 18th September 2026


Appeal Ref: 6006846
1 Kingswood Close, Boldon Colliery, South Tyneside NE35 9LG
•      The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
       against a refusal to grant planning permission.
•      The appeal is made by Mr & Mrs Stephen and Sharon Wilson against the decision of South
       Tyneside Council.
•      The application reference is 250347.
•      The development proposed is ‘erection of a single, detached use class C3 dwellinghouse across
       two storeys, with 3 bedrooms and the formation of vehicular access onto Cotswold Lane, car
       parking and garden/curtilage areas. Resubmission with altered car parking arrangements’.

Decision
1. The appeal is dismissed.
Preliminary Matters
2. Since the appeal was submitted the Council has adopted the South Tyneside Local
   Plan 2023-2040 (July 2026) (the LP). In light of this change in the development
   plan, the parties were invited to provide comments on the relevance of the newly
   adopted policies and any associated matters. I have taken those comments into
   account and have assessed the proposal against the policies of the adopted LP
   that are relevant to the determination of this appeal.
3. Furthermore, during the course of the appeal, a revised National Planning Policy
   Framework (the Framework) was published. As the revised Framework forms part
   of the policy context against which this appeal must be determined, the parties
   were invited to comment on any implications arising from the changes. I have had
   regard to the comments received in reaching my decision.
Main Issue
4. The main issue is the effect of the proposed development on highway safety, with
   particular regard to the introduction of a new vehicular access onto Cotswold Lane.
Reasons
5. This proposal follows an earlier application and appeal relating to the same site.
   The current scheme includes revisions to the parking and turning arrangements
   within the site, including the provision of two parking spaces and sufficient space
   for vehicles associated with the dwelling to manoeuvre and leave the site in a
   forward gear. These changes are intended to reduce the likelihood of vehicles
   reversing onto the highway and to accommodate visitor parking within the site. As
   these matters formed part of the Council’s concerns in relation to highway safety,
   they are relevant to my assessment and are considered below.

6. The proposed access would be taken directly from Cotswold Lane, which functions
   as a local distributor road. Its primary role is to accommodate through-movements
   between different parts of the wider area. The road is subject to a 30mph speed
   limit and includes traffic calming measures, including speed humps. While these
   features influence vehicle speeds, they do not change the underlying function of the
   road as a local distributor road. This is also reflected in the general absence of
   direct residential frontages and the expectation of relatively uninterrupted traffic
   flow along its length.
7. In the vicinity of the appeal site, there is a concentration of highway features
   requiring drivers’ attention. These include the nearby junction with Cinderford
   Close, a zebra crossing, and bus stops on both sides of the carriageway. In
   addition, hatched road markings within the carriageway act to separate opposing
   traffic flows in this section, reflecting the need to manage vehicle movements
   through what is already a sensitive part of the road.
8. Although visibility at the proposed access is shown to be acceptable, the suitability
   of an access in this location is not determined by visibility alone. The introduction of
   a private residential access would add a further point at which vehicles would enter,
   leave, slow or turn across or into the flow of traffic. This would occur in a location
   where drivers are already required to respond to several potential sources of
   conflict within a short stretch of road.
9. Taken together, the proposed access, even acknowledging likely trip numbers and
   reduced car usage as outlined in the Transport Advisory Note, would increase the
   overall level of interaction between vehicles, and between vehicles and
   pedestrians, in a part of the highway where movements are already more complex
   than along the remainder of Cotswold Lane. This would increase the potential for
   conflict and is a matter to which I attach significant concern.
10. I have considered the appellant’s reliance on the principles set out in Manual for
    Streets, including its support for more permeable layouts and direct access onto
    urban roads. However, those principles are context-dependent and are most
    appropriately applied where the street environment is characterised by active
    frontages, lower traffic dominance and a strong sense of place. In this case,
    notwithstanding the presence of traffic calming measures, Cotswold Lane retains
    the function and characteristics of a local distributor road, with limited frontage
    development and a primary role in accommodating through-movements.
11. In that context, the introduction of a new private access would not reflect a
    transition to a lower-speed, street-based environment but would instead add a
    further point of vehicular interaction within an already sensitive section of highway.
    Moreover, the presence of a zebra crossing and nearby bus stops indicates that
    pedestrian movements are already concentrated in this location. The addition of a
    vehicle access at this point would increase the potential for conflict affecting those
    users, rather than supporting the type of environment envisaged by Manual for
    Streets. In these circumstances, the proposal would not provide a separate and
    independent vehicular access that would avoid prejudicing the safe operation of the
    highway.
12. The informal parking arrangement at the former Gladstone Terrace junction is not
    directly comparable to the proposed development. Vehicles in that location are
    already visible within the carriageway environment, rather than emerging from a

     private residential curtilage, and the road layout and range of highway features
     differ materially. It does not therefore demonstrate that the introduction of a new
     access in this location would be safe.
13. Similarly, the absence of recorded accidents in the vicinity does not, in itself,
    demonstrate that the proposed arrangement would be safe. It reflects existing
    conditions, rather than the effects of introducing a new access into an already
    complex part of the highway and does not demonstrate that the development would
    achieve a safe and suitable access for all highway users.
14. I also do not accept that any potential reduction in vehicle speeds arising from
    increased driver awareness would amount to a highway safety benefit. Any such
    reduction in speed would be a response to the additional vehicle movements,
    features and potential sources of conflict associated with the proposed access. It
    would not therefore represent a highway safety benefit and, at best, would leave
    the overall effect neutral.
15. I acknowledge the amended parking and turning arrangements would reduce the
    likelihood of vehicles reversing onto the highway and would provide space for
    visitor parking within the site. However, while the provision of on-site parking would
    reduce the likelihood of parking associated with the dwelling taking place on the
    highway, it would not preclude it entirely. The proposal would nevertheless
    introduce a new dwelling with direct frontage and access onto Cotswold Lane,
    where none presently exists. Consequently, vehicle movements associated with
    occupants, visitors and deliveries would occur at this location. Having regard to the
    function of the road and the concentration of highway features in its immediate
    vicinity, the introduction of a new vehicular access would create an additional point
    of vehicular interaction and potential conflict in a location that is already sensitive in
    highway terms.
16. For the reasons given above, I conclude that the proposed development would
    have a harmful effect on highway safety, with particular regard to the introduction of
    a new vehicular access onto Cotswold Lane. As such, it would conflict with Policy
    47 of the LP, which requires development to incorporate safe layouts that minimise
    conflict between highway users and provide suitable access arrangements. For the
    same reasons, it would conflict with national decision-making policies TR4(1)(c)(i)
    and TR6(4) in the Framework, which states that development should minimise the
    scope for conflict between pedestrians, cyclists and vehicles, and that development
    proposals should be refused where they would have an unacceptable impact on
    highway safety.
Other Matters
17. I have had regard to the absence of harm to character and appearance, living
    conditions, drainage and other technical matters. However, the absence of harm
    does not, in itself, attract positive weight and these matters are therefore neutral in
    my assessment.
18. The appeal site is located within the zone of influence of the Durham Coast Special
    Area of Conservation and Northumbria Coast Special Protection Area/Ramsar site,
    afforded protection under the Conservation of Habitats and Species Regulations
    2017 (the Regulations) in recognition of the significance of their ecological features.
    However, as I have found the proposal unacceptable for other reasons, it is not

Planning Balance
20. The proposal would provide one additional dwelling on a windfall site within the
    urban area. Policy 13 of the South Tyneside Local Plan provides support for
    windfall and backland housing development where its criteria are satisfied and this
    weighs in favour of the proposal.
21. There is no dispute between the parties that the Council's most recently published
    Strategic Housing Land Availability Assessment identifies a 4.1-year supply of
    deliverable housing sites and that the Council’s latest Housing Delivery Test figure
    is 53%. While the Council states that the recent adoption of the LP means that it
    can now demonstrate a 5-year supply of housing land, the appellants highlight the
    Council's historic housing delivery record.
22. Acknowledging this, given that there is no upper limit on the provision of housing,
    and that Policy H07 of the Framework states that substantial weight should be
    given to homes contributing to evidenced accommodation needs, I give the
    provision of an additional dwelling substantial weight in favour of the proposal.
    However, given that the proposal would deliver only one dwelling any benefits
    would be modest in scale.
23. I attach limited positive weight to the modest economic benefits associated with
    construction of the dwelling. The site lies within an accessible urban location, which
    supports the principle of making effective use of land. However, that is an ordinary
    policy objective rather than a benefit of such significance as to attract additional
    weight beyond the contribution of the dwelling itself. Moreover, while the appeal site
    is capable of accommodating an additional dwelling, the existing garden is not
    inherently vacant or under-utilised simply because further development could
    occur. Accordingly, I do not attach additional weight to the proposal on that basis
    alone.
24. I have also had regard to Policy S4 of the Framework that states that proposals
    within settlements should be approved unless the benefits of doing so would be
    substantially outweighed by any adverse effects, when assessed against the
    national decision-making policies in the Framework. Policy S4 goes on to identify
    circumstances in which the benefits of approving development are likely to be
    substantially outweighed by adverse effects. These include situations where the
    development would fail to comply with one of the national decision-making policies
    that state development should be refused in specific circumstances.
25. Policy TR6(4) of the Framework is one such national decision-making policy. It
    states that development proposal should be refused if they would have a severe
    adverse impact on the transport network or an unacceptable impact on highway
    safety, taking into account any mitigation measures. For the reasons set out in my
    main issue, I have found that the proposal would have an unacceptable impact on
    highway safety. Consequently, the proposal would fail to comply with Policy
    TR6(4), and this is a circumstance to which Policy S4(2)(c) applies.

26. Drawing these matters together, while the proposal would make a modest
    contribution towards housing supply via a windfall site and provide limited economic
    benefits, I have found that it would result in unacceptable harm to highway safety.
    In light of the conflict with policy TR6(4) of the Framework, Policy S4(2)(c) applies.
    Consequently, I find that the benefits of the proposal would be substantially
    outweighed by its adverse effects. Consequently, the proposal conflicts with Policy
    S4 of the Framework and the development plan when read as a whole.
Conclusion
27. The proposal conflicts with the development plan when read as a whole. The
    material considerations, including the Framework, do not indicate that the appeal
    should be determined other than in accordance with it. I therefore conclude that the
    appeal should be dismissed.
