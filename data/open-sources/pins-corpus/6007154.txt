Appeal Decision
Site visit made on 26 June 2026
by Timothy Parton BA(Hons) MPLAN MRTPI
an Inspector appointed by the Secretary of State
Decision date: 17 August 2026


Appeal Ref: 6007154
7 Betjeman Close, Harpenden AL5 4XH
•    The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
     against a refusal to grant planning permission.
•    The appeal is made by Amy Featherby against the decision of St Albans City Council.
•    The application Ref is 5/2025/1838.
•    The development is described on the application form as the ‘demolition of the existing dwelling
     construction of enlarged replacement dwelling’.

Decision
1.     The appeal is dismissed.
Preliminary Matters
2.     Since the determination of the application, consultation on the Main Modifications
       to the emerging Local Plan was undertaken in April 2026. While preparation of the
       Plan has progressed, it can be afforded only limited weight as it may be subject to
       further change prior to adoption. Furthermore, the Council has confirmed that the
       emerging policies do not give rise to any material considerations that would alter
       the assessment of the proposal. Accordingly, this appeal has been determined
       having regard to the policies of the St Albans District Local Plan Review (1994)
       (the Plan).
Main Issues
3.     The main issues are:
          i.    the effect of the proposal on character and appearance of the area; and
         ii.    the effect on the living conditions of nearby properties, with particular regard
                to daylight.
Reasons
Character of the area
4.     The appeal site is located within a small cul-de-sac predominantly comprising of
       two rows of 1990s semi-detached dwellings positioned at right angles from each
       other. The appeal site occupies the end of its row, with the entire side boundary
       adjacent to the other row of dwellings, separated by a footpath and modest front
       gardens.
5.     Given the proximity of the two rows of dwellings, the setback position of the
       existing dwelling on the appeal site makes an important contribution to maintaining
       a visual connection between 2, 3, 4 and 5 Betjeman Close and the street.

       Furthermore, being setback from the side site boundary, the position of the
       existing dwelling on the appeal site contributes to providing the front gardens of
       the adjacent row with an open character and appearance. While the staggered
       arrangement of dwellings within the two rows is not a prevalent characteristic of
       the wider area, it adds visual interest within the street scene and helps to avoid an
       otherwise cramped and enclosed appearance.
6.     By extending both the front and side elevations, the proposal would diminish the
       visual connection between the adjacent row of dwellings and the street, resulting in
       a more enclosed frontage. In addition, the proposal would remove the existing
       staggered arrangement, leading to the loss of a beneficial design feature that
       contributes positively to the character and appearance of the existing
       development.
7.     While the proposed crown roof would have limited visibility from the public realm
       and would not, in isolation, cause harm, the development as a whole would
       adversely affect the character and appearance of the area. Accordingly, it would
       conflict with Policies 69 and 70 of the Plan and Policy ESD1 of the Harpenden
       Neighbourhood Plan (2018) which, taken together and amongst other things,
       require new development to have regard to the setting and context of its
       surroundings and to maintain or enhance the character of the area.
8.     In failing to function well, add to the quality of the area, and be sympathetic to local
       character, the proposed development would also conflict with paragraph 135 of the
       National Planning Policy Framework (2024) (the Framework).
Living conditions of nearby properties
9.     The proposal would bring built development on the appeal site closer to Nos 2, 3,
       4, 5, and 6. The reduced separation distances, together with the height and
       massing of the proposed development along the site boundary, have the potential
       to reduce the amount of daylight received by ground-floor windows serving those
       properties.
10. The submitted Daylight and Sunlight Impact Assessment Report (March 2026)
    considers separation distances, height, bulk, and massing of the proposal to
    determine the likely impacts upon daylight and sunlight to surrounding properties.
    The report concludes that the development is unlikely to result in a significant
    reduction in daylight to neighbouring habitable room windows, and any reduction
    would fall within recommended standards set out in BRE (British Research
    Establishment) daylight and sunlight guidance (Site Layout Planning for Daylight
    and Sunlight: A Guide to Good Practice, BR 209). The report also states that the
    proposal is unlikely to result in unacceptable impacts on neighbouring properties in
    terms of daylight, sunlight, or overshadowing, and any effects are expected to
    remain broadly consistent with the existing baseline environment.
11. However, no details are provided regarding the authorship or professional
    qualifications of those who prepared the report, nor the sources relied upon. It is
    unclear from the assessment which specific nearby properties were assessed, and
    the methodology used to reach the conclusions. Furthermore, no evidence is
    included to demonstrate the potential effects of the proposal based on the BRE
    guidance.

12. Consequently, the report does not provide sufficient evidence to demonstrate that
    the proposal would not result in an unacceptable reduction in daylight to the
    ground-floor windows of nearby properties, including Nos 2, 3, 4, 5, and 6. While I
    note that the tree located to the front of the adjoining row of dwellings may already
    limit daylight to some degree, this does not alter my assessment of the effects of
    the proposal before me.
13. Accordingly, the proposal would conflict with Policy 70 of the Plan which, amongst
    other things, seeks to ensure that new housing development has regard to its
    setting and meets sunlight and daylight requirements set out in the BRE guidance.
14. Furthermore, the proposal would also conflict with paragraph 135 of the
    Framework, which amongst other things, states that developments should create
    places that provide a high standard of amenity for existing users and do not
    undermine quality of life.
Other Matters
15. No objections have been received from properties within Betjeman Close or from
    the Town Council. However, the absence of objections does not in itself render a
    proposal acceptable, as representations are only one factor in the determination of
    planning applications and appeals.
       I have considered comments that the site occupies a sustainable location, that the
       proposed internal layout would provide satisfactory living conditions for future
       occupiers, and that there would be no harmful effects on the privacy of
       neighbouring properties. I also note comments that the parking provision would
       comply with policy requirements, that there would be no adverse highway impacts,
       and that the proposed materials and certain design features reflect those of the
       surrounding area. A lack of harm in these respects is neutral and thus weighs
       neither for nor against the proposal.
Conclusion
16. The proposal would conflict with the development plan when taken as a whole, as
    well as policies within the Framework, and there are no material considerations
    that indicate the decision should be made other than in accordance with the
    development plan.
17. For the above reasons, the appeal should be dismissed.
