Appeal Ref: 6012329
1 Bedford Road, Chiswick, London W4 1HU
•      The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
       against a refusal to grant planning permission.
•      The appeal is made by Mr Karayi against the decision of the Council of the London Borough of
       Ealing.
•      The application reference is 261252HH.
•      The development proposed is erection of a small summerhouse/ store.

Decision
1. The appeal is dismissed.
Preliminary Matters
2. Subsequent to the Council’s decision on the application to which this appeal
   relates, the Government published a new National Planning Policy Framework (the
   Framework) on 17 August 2026, which replaces the previous Framework published
   in December 2024, and all earlier iterations. Both the appellant and the Council
   were given the opportunity to comment on any changes relevant to the main issue
   in this appeal. No further comments were received.
3. In determining this appeal, I have a statutory duty 1 to have special regard to the
   desirability of preserving the listed building, No 1A Bedford Road W4 (Grade II
   listed), and its setting, and to pay special attention to the desirability of preserving
   or enhancing the character or appearance of the Bedford Park Conservation Area.
4. The decision notice cites Policy DAA of the Ealing Draft Local Plan 2024. The
   emerging plan is a material consideration; however, as its policies may change
   through the examination process, I attach limited weight to it.
5. Whilst the Council has raised concerns regarding the accuracy of the submitted
   plans, I note that the planning application was validated by the Council. On the
   basis of the evidence before me, I am satisfied that the submitted plans provide
   sufficient information for me to make an informed decision on the main issue of the
   appeal.
Main Issue
6. The main issue is the effect of the proposed development on the character and
   appearance of the host property and the surrounding area, with particular regard to
   the setting of the Grade II listed building at No 1A Bedford Road, and to whether

Reasons
7. The appeal site is situated towards the south-eastern part of the Bedford Park
   Conservation Area (CA). The significance of the CA derives from the historic and
   social interest, and the architectural quality, of its buildings. Bedford Park is a
   residential estate associated with the Aesthetic Movement, characterised by a
   distinctive and consistent architectural style, complemented by attractive
   streetscape detailing and verdant public spaces.
8. The Bedford Park Conservation Area Character Appraisal (Draft for Consultation)
   (2007) (BPCA) describes the properties in Bedford Road as occupying wide plots
   that create a generous sense of space, whilst the shallow front gardens contribute
   to the area’s distinctive character. Buildings are generally tall in scale, reaching
   three storeys, with pitched roofs and prominent dormers. No 1 Bedford Road is
   identified in the BPCA as occupying a prominent corner position and comprises a
   large Godwin house facing The Avenue. It was evident at my site visit that the
   property makes a positive contribution to the significance of the CA.
9. Furthermore, the property located within the appeal site, No. 1A Bedford Road
   (LB), is a Grade II listed building. Its significance derives, in part, from its
   architectural interest as an attractive building dating from around 1880. The
   property is characterised by its red-brick construction, low-profile form, steeply
   pitched tiled roof, gabled dormer above the entrance bay, and distinctive brick
   chimneys set within the roof slope, all of which contribute to its prominence within
   the street scene.
10. The proposed summer house would be located close to the boundary with Bedford
    Road, adjacent to a mature tree that makes a positive contribution to the character
    and appearance of the area. The boundary treatment comprises a close boarded
    wooden fence with a hedgerow situated behind. The structure would be
    constructed from materials that are appropriate to the conservation area, including
    timber and a suitable colour palette, and would incorporate a hipped roof form
    which would somewhat reduce its visual presence. However, the submitted details
    indicate that it would project significantly above the height of the boundary fence.
    Whilst the appellant has commented that the existing play apparatus occupies a
    similar location and height to the proposed summerhouse and that the hedgerow is
    evergreen, I noted at my site visit that the play apparatus was readily visible
    through gaps in the boundary hedgerow when looking towards the LB from Bedford
    Road. Similar views were also available from the junction of Bedford Road and The
    Avenue. Consequently, the proposed summerhouse would be readily perceptible
    within the street scene.
11. By reason of its height, scale and siting, the eye would be drawn unfavourably to
    the resultant built form. Positioned in close proximity to the LB, the structure would
    appear as a discordant and visually intrusive feature that would detract from the
    prominence and appreciation of the LB within its setting, to the detriment of the
    surrounding area. The proposal would also harm the significance of the CA, which
    derives in part from the spacious and verdant character of the area and the
    prominence of its historic buildings.

12. Policy HE5 of the Framework requires proposals affecting heritage assets to be
    accompanied by an assessment of the significance of the assets affected and the
    likely effect of the development upon that significance. In this regard, it is the
    impact on significance, rather than the scale of development itself, that is of primary
    importance. Whilst Policy HE9 of the Framework recognises that not all elements of
    a conservation area necessarily contribute to its significance, the proposed
    structure would detract from the character and appearance of the area and diminish
    the contribution made by the adjacent LB, which is identified within the BPCA as
    making a positive contribution to the CA. Consequently, the proposal would harm
    the setting of the LB and the significance of the CA.
13. Whilst the proposal is relatively modest in scale, the proposed development would,
    under the terms of the Framework, result in a low level of harm to the significance
    of the heritage assets. It is noteworthy that even a low level of harm to the
    significance of a designated heritage asset carries considerable importance and
    weight. Policy HE6 of the Framework establishes that when considering the
    potential effect of a development proposal on the significance of a designated
    heritage asset, substantial weight should be given to the asset’s conservation.
    Where a development proposal would harm the significance of a designated
    heritage asset, the effect on the asset and its significance should be weighed
    against any public benefits resulting from the proposal.
14. I have had regard to the benefits identified by the appellant, including the provision
    of an outbuilding to store equipment required for the maintenance of the garden.
    The appellant also states that the proposed location is the furthest point from the
    LB and that the structure would represent an improvement over other outbuildings
    within the gardens of nearby properties. Whilst I have given careful consideration to
    the public benefits advanced by the appellant, it is nevertheless my judgment that
    they would not outweigh the low level of harm that I have identified to the
    significance of the heritage assets. Conflict therefore arises with the historic
    environment conservation and enhancement policies contained within the
    Framework.
15. I have also had regard to the Bedford Park Conservation Area Management Plan
    (2007) (BPCAMP) insofar as it is a material consideration that provides guidance
    on the significance and special character of the CA. I note that the proposed
    summer house exceeds the dimensions of outbuildings that are usually considered
    acceptable in the locality, and that such proposals require an assessment of their
    relationship with the main house, trees and other relevant features. These
    considerations reinforce my findings.
16. For the above reasons, I therefore conclude that the proposed development would
    have a harmful effect on the character and appearance of the host property and the
    surrounding area, with particular regard to the setting of the LB and would fail to
    preserve or enhance the character or appearance of the CA. This is contrary to
    Policy 7C of the Ealing Development Management, Development Plan Document
    (2013) (DPD) which seeks development to enhance or better reveal the
    significance of heritage assets and their settings. I also find conflict with DPD
    Policies 7B and 7.4 which together seek high-quality design. The proposal is also
    contrary to Policy HC1 of The London Plan (2021) (LP) which requires
    development proposals affecting heritage assets and their settings to conserve
    their significance. I further find conflict with LP Policies D3 and D4 which
    collectively seek high-quality design which responds to local distinctiveness.

Other Matters
17. The Council has raised concerns regarding the siting of the proposed development
    adjacent to a mature tree within the appeal site, which makes a positive
    contribution to the CA. In this respect, the Council has commented that the Generic
    Management Plan for Conservation Areas (2022) advises against locating
    outbuildings in close proximity to trees. Other than the appellant’s statement that
    no-dig foundations would be used, no arboricultural evidence has been provided.
    Consequently, I have insufficient information before me to conclude that the
    proposal would not adversely affect the tree. Nevertheless, as I am dismissing the
    appeal for other reasons, this matter is not determinative of my decision and would
    be a matter to be addressed between the parties in the event of any future planning
    application.
Planning Balance and Conclusion
18. The proposed development would provide a summerhouse and the provision of
    storage for garden maintenance. However, the proposal would have a harmful
    effect on the character and appearance of the host property and the surrounding
    area, with particular regard to the setting of the LB, and would fail to preserve or
    enhance the character or appearance of the CA. The proposal therefore conflicts
    with the development plan, taken as a whole.
19. The proposed summerhouse would constitute development within a settlement and
    therefore Policy S4 of the Framework is engaged. The policy states that
    development proposals within settlements should be approved unless the benefits
    of doing so would be substantially outweighed by any adverse effects, when
    assessed against the national decision-making policies in the Framework.
20. As such, the proposal must also be assessed against the Framework's policies
    relating to design and the historic environment. For the reasons given above, I have
    found that the proposal would harm the setting of the LB and the character and
    appearance of the CA. Furthermore, the public benefits advanced by the appellant
    would not outweigh the low level of harm identified to the significance of the
    heritage assets.
21. Taken together, I find the benefits of the proposal would be substantially
    outweighed by the adverse effects when assessed against the national decision-
    making policies of the Framework. As such, Policy S4 of the Framework does not
    point to permission being granted in this instance.
22. In the final balance, material considerations in the proposal’s favour are not of
    sufficient weight to suggest that the decision should be made other than in
    accordance with the development plan. Therefore, for the reasons given, and
    having regard to all other matters raised, the appeal should be dismissed.
