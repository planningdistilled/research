Appeal Ref: 6009517
Fairfield Cottage, Cross in Hand Road, Heathfield, East Sussex, TN21 0SN
•    The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
     against a refusal to grant outline planning permission.
•    The appeal is made by Millen (Millen Land Group) against the decision of Wealden District Council.
•    The application Ref is WD/2025/0828/O.
•    The development proposed is the development of 2 self-build/custom build dwellings.

Decision
1.     The appeal is dismissed.
Preliminary Matters
2.     The application was submitted in outline form with all matters reserved except for
       access and layout which are matters for determination at this stage. I have
       therefore assessed the proposal on that basis, having regard to the submitted
       indicative plans and supporting material where relevant.
3.     Since the appeal was submitted, a new National Planning Policy Framework (‘the
       Framework’) was published in August 2026. As the Framework is a material
       consideration in the determination of planning appeals, both main parties were
       given the opportunity to comment on the implications of the updated national
       policy. I have taken their responses into account in reaching my decision.
4.     Wealden District Council is currently preparing a new Local Plan. The Regulation
       19 Proposed Submission version of the emerging Local Plan is currently in the
       representation period until November 2026. As it is not yet been examined and
       formerly adopted, I have only attached some limited weight to the draft policies
       contained within it.
Main Issues
5.     The main issues are:
           •    the effect of the proposed development on highway safety, having particular
                regard to access; and
           •    whether the proposed development would adequately address flood risk,
                having particular regard to layout.

Reasons
Highway safety
6.     The appeal site comprises the side garden of Fairfield Cottage, a two-storey semi-
       detached dwelling located on the northern side of the A267 between Cross-in-
       Hand and Heathfield. The proposal would utilise an existing access from Herring
       Lane, which joins the A267. The access would be used by the occupiers of the two
       new dwellings as well as the occupants of the host dwelling.
7.     The latest highway plan submitted with the appeal (drawing no. FFC 005 Rev 2)
       demonstrates that visibility in the eastbound direction can be increased. However,
       the plan continues to indicate a visibility splay of only around 80m in the
       westbound direction. This remains considerably below the 116m visibility distance
       identified as necessary having regard to the recorded vehicle speeds on the A267.
       The appellant's evidence therefore does not demonstrate that the visibility
       deficiency has been overcome but rather that it has been reduced.
8.     I have given careful consideration to the contention that the proposal would
       improve the existing situation by increasing visibility from below 50m to
       approximately 80m. However, the fact that a proposal would improve an existing
       arrangement does not necessarily demonstrate that the resulting arrangement
       would be safe and suitable. I accept that two dwellings would result in only a
       modest increase in traffic movements and that there would be no severe residual
       cumulative impact on the wider road network. However, even a relatively small
       increase in traffic may be harmful where it would rely upon access arrangements
       that have not been demonstrated to be safe. Accordingly, the limited traffic
       generation does not, in itself, resolve the highway safety concerns arising from the
       access arrangements.
9.     I have also considered the reliance placed upon the Manual for Streets 2 (MfS 2)
       guidance and the research referred to within the submitted highway evidence. I
       accept that the guidance recognises that visibility distances derived from design
       standards should not be applied mechanistically and that a failure to meet those
       standards does not automatically indicate an unsafe junction. Nevertheless, the
       guidance does not remove the requirement to undertake a site-specific
       assessment of highway safety nor does it establish that departures from
       recommended visibility distances should generally be accepted. Rather, it requires
       consideration of the particular circumstances of the site.
10. In this case, those circumstances include recorded 85th percentile traffic speeds of
    approximately 43mph in both directions and traffic flows exceeding 21,000 vehicle
    movements per day on the A267. The road is therefore neither lightly trafficked nor
    characterised by low vehicle speeds. Therefore, I attach significant weight to the
    fact that a considerable visibility shortfall would remain in the westbound direction.
    The absence of evidence of recorded collisions does not demonstrate that the
    remaining deficiency is acceptable, particularly where the proposal would intensify
    use of an access that is already awkward and which operates under constrained
    visibility conditions.
11. Furthermore, recorded traffic speeds are not irrelevant once the required visibility
    distance has been derived. The speed survey is part of the site-specific evidence
    describing the characteristics of the road environment in which the proposed
    access would operate. It is therefore a factor that may legitimately be considered

       when assessing whether the residual visibility deficiency would have unacceptable
       consequences for highway safety.
12. I do not support the view that the evidence satisfactorily resolves concerns relating
    to the relationship between the site access and vehicles turning into Herring Lane
    from the A267. The appellant's case is that drivers would necessarily be travelling
    at relatively low speeds because of the geometry of the junction and the narrow
    nature of Herring Lane. However, no measured speed data has been provided for
    Herring Lane itself. Consequently, the conclusion that the proposed arrangement
    would be adequate rests largely upon assumptions regarding vehicle speeds and
    driver behaviour rather than objective evidence.
13. Nor am I persuaded by the contention that the Highway Authority's concerns arise
    solely because the junction has been assessed as though it were a wholly new
    access. Regardless of whether the access is existing, the proposal would
    introduce additional residential traffic onto an arrangement which the evidence
    indicates would continue to operate with materially substandard westbound
    visibility. The appellant's evidence demonstrates an improvement but does not
    satisfactorily demonstrate that the resulting arrangement would be safe and
    suitable when assessed against the actual traffic conditions experienced at this
    location.
14. I also note that the latest highway plan before me, drawing no FFC 005 Rev 2, was
    not considered by the Highway Authority. The parties agree that the Highway
    Authority was not consulted on, and did not provide comments in respect of, the
    revised appeal submissions. I have therefore undertaken my own assessment of
    the latest evidence. However, in circumstances where the revised plan continues
    to indicate a substantial shortfall in westbound visibility and where the Highway
    Authority has not had the opportunity to assess whether the revised arrangement
    satisfactorily addresses its concerns, I am cautious about concluding that the
    remaining deficiencies are acceptable. This is particularly so given that the
    Highway Authority had previously maintained an objection following consideration
    of earlier revisions to the scheme.
15. Drawing these matters together, the submitted evidence does not demonstrate
    that safe and suitable access would be achieved for all users. A significant shortfall
    in visibility would remain on a busy A-road carrying substantial traffic volumes, and
    the concerns regarding visibility of the site access from vehicles turning into
    Herring Lane have not been satisfactorily resolved.
16. I therefore conclude that the proposal would give rise to an unacceptable impact
    on highway safety. Consequently, it would conflict with Spatial Planning Objectives
    SPO12 and SPO13 of the Wealden Core Strategy Local Plan 2013, saved Policy
    TR3 of the Wealden Local Plan 1998 insofar as they seek to ensure development
    is well-designed with a satisfactory means of access which does not create or
    perpetuate unacceptable traffic conditions.
Flood risk
17. The appeal site lies within Flood Zone 1 and it is common ground between the
    parties that the flood risk issue relates solely to surface water flooding rather than
    fluvial flooding. National policy and guidance make clear that development in Flood
    Zone 1 is not automatically free from flood risk considerations where other sources
    of flooding have been identified.

18. The Council's concern is that, as layout is a matter for determination at this outline
    stage, it must be demonstrated that the proposed built form and access
    arrangements can be accommodated without placing future occupiers at risk from
    surface water flooding. It also contends that a site-specific flood risk assessment is
    required and that insufficient information has been provided to demonstrate that
    flood risk elsewhere would not increase.
19. The surface water mapping indicates that areas of medium and high risk are
    present within part of the site. However, the affected area is limited and does not
    encompass the site as a whole. Importantly, the evidence before me does not
    demonstrate that the proposed dwellings or access would be located within those
    areas of greatest risk. The indicative layout places the proposed plots outside the
    mapped surface water flood extent, and I have not been directed to any evidence
    indicating that the development could not be accommodated in that manner.
    Indeed, the Council's own assessment recognises that it may be possible for the
    development to be laid out so that built form occurs outside the areas of medium
    and high surface water flood risk.
20. Whilst I acknowledge the absence of a detailed flood risk assessment, I am not
    persuaded that the information before me reveals a flood risk constraint that would
    render the development unacceptable in principle. Rather, the Council's concern is
    primarily that insufficient detail has been provided at this stage. In the
    circumstances of this case, where the site lies within Flood Zone 1, where the
    identified surface water risk is confined to a small part of the site, and where the
    proposed layout demonstrates that development can reasonably be located
    outside the affected areas, I am satisfied that the principle of the development has
    adequately addressed the surface water flood risk identified on the site.
21. Furthermore, there is no substantive evidence before me demonstrating that the
    proposal would increase flood risk elsewhere. Detailed matters relating to drainage
    design, surface water management, finished floor levels and the implementation of
    appropriate sustainable drainage measures could be addressed through suitably
    worded planning conditions.
22. Accordingly, the proposal would adequately address flood risk. It does not,
    therefore, conflict with Spatial Planning Objectives SPO10 and SPO13 of the
    Wealden Core Strategy Local Plan 2013 insofar as they seek to ensure
    developments are designed to provide safe environments for residents with
    regards to flooding and the predicted impacts of climate change.
Planning Balance
23. The appeal site lies outside a defined settlement boundary but is physically well-
    related to the existing linear development built along the A267 connecting the
    small settlement of Cross-In-Hand with Heathfield. Consequently, Policy S5 of the
    Framework is engaged. The proposal would comprise a form of residential
    development that would address an evidenced unmet housing need in a district
    that is unable to demonstrate a five-year supply of deliverable housing sites.
    Indeed, both parties agree that the Council is only able to demonstrate a supply of
    3.44 years. Therefore, the proposal falls within the category of development
    identified in Policy S5(1)(j) of the Framework. In such circumstances, the proposal
    should be approved unless its benefits would be substantially outweighed by any

       adverse effects when assessed against the national decision-making policies in
       the Framework.
24. The Regulation 19 Proposed Submission version of the emerging Local Plan is
    currently in the representation period until November 2026. The appellant
    highlights that the emerging plan shows the appeal site as within the settlement
    boundary of Cross-in-Hand and, therefore, Policy S4 of the Framework should
    apply in this case. I have attached some weight to this consideration although this
    is tempered by the fact that the emerging Local Plan is still in draft form and is yet
    to be examined by the Secretary of State and formerly adopted. In any case, both
    Policies S4 and S5 state that development should be approved unless the benefits
    of doing so would be substantially outweighed by any adverse effects, when
    assessed against the national decision-making policies in the Framework.
25. The proposal would deliver two additional dwellings and would therefore make a
    modest contribution towards meeting the district's identified housing needs. The
    Framework requires substantial weight to be given to the benefits of providing
    homes which contribute towards meeting evidenced accommodation needs (Policy
    HO7). Although the proposal is small in scale, I nevertheless attach substantial
    weight to this benefit in light of the Council's acknowledged housing land supply
    shortfall.
26. The proposal would also provide two self-build/custom-build plots. The Framework
    identifies people wishing to commission or build their own homes as a group
    whose accommodation needs should be addressed (Policy HO1(2)(i)), and the
    evidence before me indicates that there is unmet demand for such plots within the
    district. I therefore attach moderate weight to this benefit.
27. In addition, the site occupies a reasonably sustainable location. It is physically
    related to existing development and there is no substantive dispute that future
    occupiers would have access to services and facilities. Furthermore, given that
    appearance, landscaping, scale and other detailed design matters are reserved for
    future consideration, there is no substantive evidence before me that the site could
    not accommodate two dwellings in a manner that would respect the character and
    appearance of the area, safeguard the National Landscape, provide satisfactory
    living conditions or appropriately address ecological interests. These matters are
    therefore neutral considerations in the determination of this appeal.
28. The appellant argues that the previous Framework's requirement that safe and
    suitable access be achieved has been removed and that refusal must now be
    considered solely against Policy TR6(4). I agree that the structure of national
    policy has changed. However, I do not agree that this materially alters the
    assessment of the appeal. Policy TR3 requires significant adverse impacts on
    highway safety to be mitigated to an acceptable degree and Policy TR6(4)
    provides that development proposals should be refused where they would result in
    an unacceptable impact on highway safety.
29. Against the identified benefits, I attach substantial weight to the highway safety
    harm and the conflict with Policies TR3 and TR6. The proposal would introduce
    additional residential traffic onto an access arrangement which has not been
    demonstrated would operate safely.
30. Having regard to the Framework as a whole, I find that the benefits arising from
    the provision of two dwellings, including the delivery of self-build housing, together

       with the site's reasonably sustainable location and the absence of harm in other
       respects, are significant considerations weighing in favour of the proposal.
       However, the substantial positive weight is overcome by the even more substantial
       weight I attach to the identified highway safety harm. For the reasons set out
       above, I conclude that the adverse impacts of the proposal would substantially
       outweigh its benefits when assessed against the national decision-making policies
       in the Framework as a whole. Accordingly, the proposal conflicts with Policy S5 of
       the Framework.
Conclusion
31. The proposal conflicts with the development plan and the material considerations
    do not indicate that the appeal should be decided other than in accordance with it.
32. For the reasons given above the appeal should be dismissed.
