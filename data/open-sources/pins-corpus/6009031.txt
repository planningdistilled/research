Preliminary Matters
13. The appeals are made by the same appellant for similar developments. I have
    determined all appeals on their merits however to avoid duplication and in the
    interests of brevity I have issued a single decision letter.
14. Since the determination of the application the National Planning Policy Framework
    (2024) has been superseded by the National Planning Policy Framework (2026)
    (the Framework). No party would be prejudiced or caused any injustice by me
    proceeding with the appeal in light of this change in policy.
15. Appeal C, Appeal E, Appeal F, Appeal G, Appeal H, Appeal J and Appeal L are
    located within conservation areas and some are within the setting of other heritage
    assets, in accordance with the statutory duty imposed by section 72(1) of the
    Planning (Listed Building and Conservation Areas) Act 1990; I am required to have
    special regard to the effect of the proposed development on the character or
    appearance of the area. Policy HE6 of the Framework states that substantial
    weight should be given to the asset’s conservation when considering the potential
    effect of a proposal on the significance of a designated heritage asset. This is
    irrespective of the level of harm. The Council has not raised issue sufficient to
    warrant refusal on amenity grounds with regard to the effect of the proposed
    advertisements on any heritage asset. From the information before me and my
    observations during my site visits I agree.
16. The description of development for Appeal H and Appeal I refers to replacement of
    existing double sided internally illuminated 6 sheet advertisements. However,
    during my site visit I observed that the bus stops are identified by free standing
    signs. Whilst Google Maps show a bus shelter at both locations, these are historic
    and do not reflect the current situation, as such I have revised the descriptions of
    development accordingly.
Main Issue
17. The main issue in all the appeals is the effect of the advertisement on public safety.

18. The National Planning Practice Guidance1 confirms that all advertisements are
    intended to attract attention but proposed advertisements at points where drivers
    need to take more care are more likely to affect public safety. For example, at
    junctions, roundabouts, pedestrian crossings, on the approach to a low bridge or
    level crossing or other places where local conditions present traffic hazards.
19. The proposed double-sided digital advertisement is located on a bus shelter
    fronting Boroughbridge Road in Appeal A, within a particularly complex section of
    the highway network where drivers, cyclists and other road users are already
    required to process a significant amount of information. The site lies in close
    proximity to the access and egress of a petrol filling station, the junctions with
    Cranbrook Avenue, Princess Drive and the access to the Church of the Holy
    Redeemer, together with a signalised bus gate, bus lane, pedestrian crossing
    facilities and cycle lanes on both sides of the carriageway. The advertisement
    would be visible to traffic travelling in both directions and, unlike the existing static
    display, the proposed digital screen would change every 10 seconds, introducing a
    dynamic visual element into an already demanding driving environment. In these
    circumstances, the digital display has the potential to divert the attention of highway
    users at a location where their focus should remain on negotiating the competing
    highway movements, thereby creating an unacceptable distraction and giving rise
    to concerns regarding highway safety.
20. Appeal B relates to the installation of a double-sided digital advertisement on a bus
    shelter located on Hull Road. Whilst the site is situated close to the signalised
    junction with Tang Hall Lane, the surrounding highway environment is
    comparatively open, with good forward visibility for approaching drivers. Unlike
    more constrained urban locations, there are no particularly complex traffic
    movements or significant lane changing manoeuvres required in the immediate
    vicinity of the advertisement. In addition, although a cycle route passes through the
    area, it is accommodated on a segregated route separate from the carriageway
    rather than within the running lanes of traffic, reducing the number of competing
    movements that drivers are required to monitor simultaneously. The openness of
    the site and the relatively straightforward highway layout mean that it would be less
    likely to distract highway users to the extent that it would prejudice public safety.
21. Although the bus shelter in Appeal C is located on Lawrence Street between two
    signalised junctions and immediately before the approach widens to provide two
    lanes, the highway layout is generally straightforward, with good forward visibility
    and clearly defined traffic movements. The proposal is a single east-facing digital
    screen positioned on the inside of the bus shelter, thereby substantially reducing its
    prominence and the duration for which it would be visible to passing motorists.
    Whilst drivers are required to remain aware of buses, pedestrians and cyclists
    using the corridor, these are typical characteristics of an urban route and do not, in
    themselves, create an unusually high level of cognitive demand. Accordingly, the
    proposal is not considered to result in harm to public safety.
22. Whilst Poppleton Road is a relatively busy route and the carriageway is narrower
    than at some of the other appeal sites, these characteristics do not, in themselves,
    indicate that the proposed advertisement would give rise to an unacceptable impact
    on highway safety. The bus shelter is located close to a courtesy crossing and

23. Appeals E and F relate to bus shelters positioned on The Stonebow within York city
    centre, a location that experiences high levels of activity from buses, pedestrians
    and general traffic. During my site visit, traffic was observed to move slowly,
    reflecting the constrained nature of the city centre highway network rather than
    functioning as a through-route for higher-speed traffic. Whilst this observation
    represents only a snapshot in time, it is consistent with the character of the area,
    where vehicle speeds are naturally moderated by the urban environment, the
    frequency of bus movements and the presence of controlled junctions. In these
    circumstances, although the proposed digital advertisements would introduce a
    changing display, I am not persuaded that they would materially increase the risk of
    distraction to the extent that public safety would be compromised. The surrounding
    highway environment is one in which drivers are already expected to travel
    cautiously and anticipate regular interaction with buses, pedestrians and other road
    users. Consequently, I find insufficient evidence that the proposals would have an
    unacceptable effect on public safety.
24. Tadcaster Road is a busy highway, the appeal site in Appeal G is located in
    proximity to a signalised pedestrian and cycle crossing and cycle lanes. However,
    the presence of these features does not, in itself, demonstrate that the location is
    one where highway users would be subject to such increased cognitive demand
    that the proposed advertisement would give rise to an unacceptable risk to public
    safety. The bus shelter is situated within an open stretch of Tadcaster Road, with
    the surrounding land comprising allotment gardens and the open grounds of York
    Racecourse, and there are no nearby residential properties or other built
    development that would add to the complexity of the immediate environment. The
    proposed screen would be single-sided and inward-facing, with advertisements
    visible primarily to users of the bus shelter rather than directed towards
    approaching drivers. While the changing nature of the display has the potential to
    attract attention, there is insufficient evidence that its presence would materially
    distract highway users or compromise their ability to respond safely to the existing
    highway conditions. Accordingly, I do not consider that the proposal would result in
    harm to public safety.

    features results in such a level of highway complexity that the proposed digital

27. Piccadilly is a principal city-centre route, the appeal site, Appeal J, is located within

28. In Appeal K, the site is on Tadcaster Road which is a busy route located close to a
    major signalised junction, a signalised pedestrian crossing and a range of
    pedestrian and cycling facilities. Drivers approaching the junction may be required
    to make lane choices and that vehicles, including buses, turn into the York College
    access. The screens would be positioned perpendicular to the road, with York
    College and Dringhouses Cemetery providing the principal context on either side,
    and the existing street lighting means that the proposed illumination would not
    introduce an unexpected or incongruous source of light. The screens would also
    replace existing poster advertisements of a similar scale and would display static
    advertisements rather than moving or animated imagery. Whilst I accept that the
    advertisements would change at 10-second intervals and may attract some
    attention, due to the long open road I am not persuaded that the proposed adverts
    would materially distract drivers or impair their ability to respond appropriately to
    the existing highway conditions. I conclude that the proposal would not result in
    harm to public safety.

Other Matters
32. The appellant has drawn my attention to appeal decisions relating to digital bus
    shelter advertisements. I been provided with limited details to compare this to the
    proposal before me. Notwithstanding this each development must be considered
    on its individual merits, and I have reached my conclusion based on the individual
    merit of the appeal proposal.
33. The appellant has provided highway technical notes, correspondence with the
    Council’s highway team, journal papers, collision data which I have taken into
    account with regard to each appeal.
34. The appellant contends that the proposed advertisements would deliver combined
    social, environmental and economic public benefits for the City of York, its
    residents and visitors. Included in these benefits are fully recyclable bus shelters,
    some of which with living roof features, smaller advertisements, delivery of
    public/emergency messaging, social value fund and advertising support for SME
    businesses and local charitable causes.
35. Notwithstanding the appellants claims relating to environmental, social and
    economic improvements that the proposed advertisements would create, the
    Regulations2 and Planning Practice Guidance3 are clear that the appeal shall be
    considered with regard to amenity and public safety. Even if these improvements
    were demonstrated they would not outweigh the harm I have found in relation to
    public safety in relation to Appeal A.
Conclusion
36. Appeal A is dismissed and express consent is refused for the advertisements as
    applied for.

Schedule of Conditions
1) No individual advertisement display on the screen shall at any time contain moving
   images, animation, video or full motion images displayed.
2) In the hours of darkness, the advertisement display luminance shall be no greater
   than 300cd/m2.
3) In daylight hours, the advertisement display luminance shall be controlled in order
   to reflect ambient light conditions (to ensure it is neither too bright or too dull), and
   shall at all times be no greater than the recommended maximum daytime
   luminance values set out in Table 10.5 within the Institution of Lighting
   Professionals - Professional Lighting Guide (PLG 05) 'Brightness of Illuminated
   Advertisements including Digital Displays' (or its equivalent in a replacement guide)
   in cd/m2.
4) The minimum display time for each advertisement shall be 10 seconds.
5) The approved digital screen shall contain a feature that will turn the screen off
   (display a black/blank screen) in the event that the display experiences a
   malfunction or error.
