Appeal Ref: 6009002
Land at Windsor Lane, Little Kingshill, Buckinghamshire HP16 0EF
•    The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
     against a refusal to grant planning permission.
•    The appeal is made by Windsor Lane Equestrian against the decision of Buckinghamshire Council.
•    The application Ref is PL/25/2215/FA.
•    The development proposed is Change of use of existing agricultural land to equestrian for private
     and personal use.

Decision
1.     The appeal is allowed and planning permission is granted for the change of use of
       existing agricultural land to equestrian for private and personal use at land at
       Windsor Lane, Little Kingshill, Buckinghamshire HP16 0EF in accordance with the
       terms of the application, Ref PL/25/2215/FA, subject to the conditions in the
       attached schedule.
Application for costs
2.     An application for the award of costs has been made by Windsor Lane Equestrian
       against Buckinghamshire Council. This is subject to a separate decision.
Preliminary Matters
3.     At the outset of the appeal, the appellant submitted revised site plans, a
       Preliminary Ecological Appraisal, a BNG Assessment and the completed statutory
       metric. These documents were available to the Council and interested parties to
       comment upon. I have taken all responses into account. The revised plans clarify
       the retention of the Scout Hut, woodland, trees and hedgerows and the
       relationship of the proposed paddocks to those features. They do not alter the
       description of development, introduce a materially different use or increase the
       physical extent of the works. I am therefore satisfied that considering them would
       not deprive any party of a fair opportunity to address the scheme and would not
       result in determination of a materially different proposal.
4.     For clarity, I have determined the appeal on the basis that the existing steel-
       framed barn would be retained and altered to provide stabling. Permission is not
       granted for its demolition and replacement.
5.     A revised National Planning Policy Framework was published on 17 August 2026
       and replaced the December 2024 Framework. I have determined the appeal by
       reference to the policies now in force, including those concerning the natural
       environment, protected landscapes, the Green Belt, heritage assets and
       concerning key community facilities. Those policies do not raise a new

Main Issues
6.     The main issues are: i) whether the proposal would result in the loss of a
       community facility; ii) the effect of the proposal on biodiversity; and iii) the effect of
       the proposal on the character and appearance of the area, including the Chilterns
       National Landscape.
Reasons
       Loss of Community Facility
7.     The existing Scout Hut has its own access and occupies a clearly defined plot.
       Although it lies within the appeal site’s red line boundary, that boundary does not,
       of itself, merge separate uses into a single planning unit. From the evidence and
       my observations, the Scout Hut is within a community use and is physically and
       functionally distinct from the agricultural land subject to the proposed change of
       use.
8.     Furthermore, the description of development relates solely to the change of use of
       agricultural land to private equestrian use. The submitted plans show the Scout
       Hut retained, with no alteration to its use or access arrangements. Consequently,
       no material change of use would occur in relation to that part of the site for the
       purposes of section 55 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990.
9.     Given the limited stable accommodation and non-commercial nature of the
       proposal, I do not consider that activities associated with the equestrian use of the
       land would threaten the continued operation of the community facility.
10. Bringing these matters together, I find that the proposal would not result in the loss
    of a community facility. It would therefore not conflict with Policy GB24 of the
    Chiltern District Local Plan 1997, as amended (the Local Plan) insofar as that
    policy seeks to protect community facilities.
       Biodiversity
11. A Preliminary Ecological Appraisal (PEA) and Biodiversity Net Gain (BNG)
    Assessment have been submitted by the appellant. The PEA identifies modified
    grassland, woodland, mixed scrub, native hedgerows, mature trees and buildings
    within the site. Although these features provide suitable habitat for a range of
    species, it concludes that, subject to the retention of the trees, hedgerows,
    woodland and larger scrub parcels, the effects on protected species would be
    negligible and no further surveys are required. No substantive evidence leads me
    to a different conclusion.
12. No loss of trees, hedgerows or woodland is proposed, and the site’s principal
    habitats would be retained. A limited area of scattered scrub would be removed to
    form part of a paddock, but the submitted evidence indicates that this loss could be
    suitably addressed through habitat creation and enhancement.
13. The appellant contends that the proposal is exempt from mandatory BNG because
    the physical works would be limited. However, the proposal involves a change of
    use across an extensive area, and the use of the land as paddocks may affect

       habitats through changes in management, grazing, enclosure and associated
       activity. On the evidence before me, I am therefore not satisfied that the ‘de
       minimis’ exemption applies.
14. Nevertheless, the appellant’s BNG Assessment demonstrates that the statutory
    biodiversity gain objective could be achieved on site through habitat retention, the
    creation of replacement modified grassland and the enhancement of hedgerows,
    scrub and woodland. It identifies proposed increases of more than 10% in habitat
    units and more than 30% in hedgerow units. These gains depend on the delivery
    and long-term management of the identified enhancement measures.
15. The development is therefore subject to the statutory biodiversity gain condition,
    requiring approval of a Biodiversity Gain Plan before commencement. As the
    calculated gain relies materially on significant on-site habitat enhancements, their
    establishment, management and monitoring must be secured for at least 30 years
    through an approved Habitat Management and Monitoring Plan. Subject to those
    requirements, I find that the proposal would conserve and enhance biodiversity
    and would be capable of securing the mandatory biodiversity net gain.
16. In respect of this main issue, the proposal therefore would not cause harm to
    biodiversity. It would therefore accord with Core Strategy Policy CS24 that seeks
    to conserve and enhance biodiversity, along with Local Plan Policy TW6 that
    seeks to avoid the loss of woodland.
       Character and Appearance
17. The appeal site comprises approximately 20 hectares of gently sloping land within
    Little Kingshill. It is predominantly open grassland and agricultural land, enclosed
    in places by managed hedgerows, mature trees and woodland, and adjoins
    Priestfield Arboretum. Although residential development borders much of the site,
    the land retains a spacious, verdant and pastoral character. It therefore forms part
    of the village’s green setting and reflects the wider pattern of fields, woodland and
    settlement within the surrounding Chilterns landscape.
18. The appeal site lies within the Chilterns National Landscape. The Levelling-up and
    Regeneration Act 2023 strengthened the statutory duty on relevant authorities,
    requiring them to seek to further the purpose of conserving and enhancing the
    natural beauty of protected landscapes.
19. The special qualities of the Chilterns National Landscape include its chalk
    landscape, wooded hills and valleys, ancient hedgerows and trees, common land,
    historic routes, tranquillity and settled rural character. Around Little Kingshill, those
    qualities are expressed through the close relationship between the village, open
    pasture, mature boundary vegetation, woodland and local landscape features. The
    appeal site contributes positively to that character as a sizeable area of
    undeveloped grassland, framed by established vegetation and adjoining woodland.
20. The proposal would change the use of the land to private equestrian use. Having
    regard to the scale and nature of the proposed private use, and the extent of open
    managed grassland available, there is no substantive basis to find that it would
    require, or be likely to result in, the removal of trees, woodland or hedgerows. The
    appellant’s evidence indicates that no such loss is proposed, and no robust
    justification has been provided for me to reach an alternative view. The principal
    landscape structure of the site would therefore be retained.

21. No means of enclosure are proposed, though enclose of the paddocks would likely
    be required. In principle, paddock fencing would be a functional and familiar rural
    feature rather than an urbanising subdivision. Given the scale of the site and
    retention of its principal landscape features, suitably designed fencing would be
    visually recessive and would not disrupt its open pastoral character. A condition
    requiring approval of the enclosure details would secure that outcome. The
    proposal would also adapt an existing building for stabling rather than introduce
    substantial new built form. Subject to control over external materials, its scale and
    rural function would sit comfortably within the landscape setting.
22. The proposal would retain the site’s open pastoral character, mature trees,
    woodland and hedgerow network. In addition, the proposed enhancement and
    long-term management of hedgerows, scrub and woodland would strengthen the
    landscape structure and ecological connectivity that contribute to the natural
    beauty of this part of the Chilterns National Landscape. Those measures would be
    secured through the biodiversity gain and habitat management requirements.
23. I am therefore satisfied that the proposal would conserve and enhance the
    relevant special qualities of the Chilterns National Landscape and would further
    the statutory purpose of conserving and enhancing its natural beauty. The
    proposal therefore accords with Policy CS22 of the Core Strategy for Chiltern
    District (adopted 2011) (the Core Strategy) and Local Plan Policy LSQ1. Together
    these policies seek to conserve and enhance the natural beauty, special
    landscape character and scenic quality of the Chilterns National Landscape.
24. Although not stated in relation to the Council’s second reason for refusal, there
    would also be no policy conflict with Local Plan Policy TW6, which seeks to resist
    the loss of woodland including for landscape and amenity grounds.
Other Matters
25. Little Kingshill Grange and its associated listed outbuildings are Grade II listed.
    Their significance derives principally from their architectural and historic interest as
    a rural house and associated buildings, together with the understanding of their
    historic function provided by their open and verdant rural surroundings. The appeal
    site forms part of that rural setting. The proposed private equestrian use would
    retain the land in a predominantly open form, and the established trees,
    hedgerows and woodland that define the setting would remain. The limited
    alterations to the existing barn would not compete with the listed buildings or erode
    the ability to appreciate their architectural or historic interest. Giving special regard
    to the desirability of preserving their settings, I find that the proposal would
    preserve those settings and would cause no harm to the significance of the listed
    buildings.
26. Although the public may access the site for recreation or potentially use part of the
    site as a right of way, the land is privately owned. The ongoing rights of way
    proceedings fall under a separate statutory regime and do not affect my
    determination of the appeal on its planning merits. Equally, this decision does not
    determine the existence, status or extent of any public right of way.
27. The proposal may represent a step towards the future wider development of the
    appeal site. However, no such proposal is before me. The appeal must be
    determined on the merits of the development proposed, and any future scheme

       would be subject to separate consideration. Accordingly, speculation regarding
       future development carries little weight in my assessment.
28. The appeal site lies within the Green Belt, and the Council has identified that the
    proposal would not amount to inappropriate development. Indeed, the proposed
    material change of use for private outdoor recreation, together with the alteration
    of the existing building to provide an appropriate facility for that use, falls within
    Policy GB7 of the Framework, provided its impact on openness is minimised and it
    does not conflict significantly with Green Belt purposes. The land would remain
    predominantly open and the works would not materially increase the amount of
    built form. I therefore find that the proposal would not constitute inappropriate
    development and would preserve the openness of the Green Belt.
29. Although the site could accommodate more horses than the proposed stabling
    would serve, the size of the landholding does not indicate that the use would
    operate differently from that applied for or otherwise give rise to harm. I have
    determined the appeal on the basis of the proposal before me. Additionally, a
    condition could be attached to the permission to ensure the proposal is not used
    on a commercial basis.
30. Interested parties raise concerns about Hare Lane Holloway. However, the
    proposal would not alter the form, alignment or character of the historic lane. Its
    mature vegetation and distinctive sunken character would be retained. I therefore
    find no harm to its appearance or historic interest.
31. Parts of the site are proposed for designation in the emerging Little Missenden
    Neighbourhood Plan, and interested parties also refer to the emerging
    Buckinghamshire Local Plan. However, the emerging plans remain at an early
    stage and have not been examined or found sound. They therefore carry limited
    weight. In any event, the proposed equestrian use has not been shown to
    prejudice their future implementation.
32. No substantive evidence has been provided to find that the proposal would
    increase flood risk.
33. Given the limited stable accommodation and non-commercial nature of the use,
    the proposal would be unlikely to generate a significant increase in vehicle
    movements. The site already benefits from an agricultural use capable of
    generating movements by large vehicles and machinery, and no change to the
    existing access is proposed. On the available evidence, I find there would be no
    adverse effect on highway safety.
34. Other equestrian facilities may exist nearby, but no policy requirement has been
    identified for the appellant to demonstrate a need for the proposal.
Conditions
35. In addition to a standard time limit for commencement of development, a condition
    specifying the approved plans and drawings is reasonable and necessary for
    clarity.
36. The development is subject to the statutory biodiversity gain condition, which
    requires a Biodiversity Gain Plan to be approved before commencement. The
    submitted metric relies materially on the enhancement of hedgerows, scrub and
    woodland to deliver the required net gain. A condition requiring approval and

       implementation of a Habitat Management and Monitoring Plan is therefore
       necessary to secure the establishment, management and monitoring of those
       significant on-site enhancements for at least 30 years.
37. Conditions requiring approval of the external materials for the stable building and
    the means of enclosure to the paddocks are necessary to safeguard the character
    and appearance of the area.
38. A condition restricting the use to the private keeping of horses is necessary to
    accord with the terms of the proposal and to protect local amenity and highway
    safety.
Conclusion
39. For the reasons outlined above, the appeal scheme complies with the
    development plan, and there are no material considerations, including the
    Framework, that indicate a decision should be taken other than in accordance with
    it. The appeal is therefore allowed.

                                           Schedule of Conditions

    1) The development hereby permitted shall begin not later than 3 years from the
       date of this decision.

    2) The development hereby permitted shall be carried out in accordance with the following
         approved plans: Proposed Site Plan DWG. NO. J0057580_013_V3; Proposed Stable
         Block Elevations Drawing No. 015 V1.

    3) Prior to commencement of development, a Habitat Management and Monitoring
       Plan (HMMP), relating to the significant on-site habitat enhancements identified
       in the Biodiversity Gain Plan, shall be submitted to and approved in writing by
       the local planning authority. The HMMP shall include:
         (a) the persons or organisations responsible for implementation;
         (b) details of habitat creation and enhancement measures necessary to secure
         the biodiversity net gain identified in the approved Biodiversity Gain Plan;
         (c) management prescriptions for all created and enhanced habitats;
         (d) monitoring arrangements and reporting requirements;
         (e) remedial measures to be implemented where monitoring demonstrates that
         habitat targets are not being achieved; and
         (f) a timetable for implementation and management for a minimum period of 30
         years.
         The development shall thereafter be implemented, managed and monitored in
         accordance with the approved HMMP.

    4) Prior to their use in the development hereby permitted, details/samples of the
       materials to be used in the construction of the external surfaces of the stable
       building shall be submitted to and approved in writing by the local planning
       authority. The development shall be carried out in accordance with the approved
       details/samples.

    5) No fencing or other means of enclosure to the paddocks shall be installed unless
       details of its location, height, design, materials and colour/finish have first been
       submitted to and approved in writing by the local planning authority. The fencing
       or other means of enclosure shall thereafter be installed in accordance with the
       approved details and retained as such.

    6) The stable building and equestrian land shall be used solely for the private
       keeping of horses incidental to the enjoyment of Little Kingshill Grange, Windsor
       Lane, Little Kingshill, Great Missenden HP16 0DZ, and shall not be used for
       livery, riding instruction, breeding, sales, commercial events or any other
       commercial equestrian purpose.
