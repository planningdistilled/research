•   The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
    against a refusal to grant planning permission.
•   The appeal is made by Baxter Tailford against the decision of the Council of the London Borough of
    Hounslow.
•   The application Ref is P/2025/3712.
•   The development proposed is described as “The proposed works involves the replacement of a
    garage door with a traditionally proportioned timber window that replicates the detailing, glazing bars,
    and frame depth seen elsewhere within the mews.”.

Decision
1. The appeal is allowed and planning permission is granted for conversion of the
   garage into habitable room and replacement of the garage door with a timber
   window to the front of the house at 6 Caxton Mews, Brentford, TW8 8BN in
   accordance with the terms of the application, Ref P/2025/3712, subject to the
   following conditions:
     1)       The development hereby permitted shall begin not later than three years from
              the date of this decision.
     2)       The development hereby permitted shall be carried out in accordance with
              drawing no 6 Caxton Mews Planning Proposal – Dimensions.
Appeal Procedure
2. The site visit was undertaken by a representative of the Inspector whose
   recommendation is set out below and to which the Inspector has had regard before
   deciding the appeal.
Main Issue
3. The main issue is the effect of the proposed development on the character or
   appearance of the host dwelling and the Butts Conservation area (CA).
Reasons for the Recommendation
4. The appeal site lies within The Butts Conservation Area (CA), an area which
   derives its significance from its strong Georgian character. The CA appraisal
   identifies the predominant use of brown brick, traditional sash windows, panelled
   doors and dormers as key features that contribute to its special interest. Caxton
   Mews, where the appeal property is located, forms part of a 1970s infill
   development. The development was designed to sit sympathetically within the
   historic context. The CA appraisal recognises the buildings within Caxton Mews as

5. The host dwelling is one of a small group of three-storey townhouses arranged
   around a compact square. While these dwellings generally share a consistent
   design, including internal ground floor garages, no 9 Caxton Mews exhibits a
   different arrangement, with a ground floor window in place of a garage door and a
   garage attached to the side of the property. Furthermore, nos 1 and 2 Caxton
   Mews also differ from the prevailing pattern, as they feature ground floor windows
   and no garages, although they are set apart from the main square by an archway.
6. The proposed development involves the conversion of the existing garage into a
   habitable room, including the replacement of the garage door with a front-facing
   window. The proposed window would reflect the established architectural language
   of both Caxton Mews and the wider conservation area. The proportions, materials
   and detailing would be consistent with the existing fenestration found on the host
   property and neighbouring dwellings. The change would therefore read as a
   modest and coherent alteration. Although the mews is broadly symmetrical in its
   frontage design, there is already some variation, including at no 9, and the
   proposed development would sit comfortably within this context, reflecting the
   design approach seen there and thereby preserving the established character of
   the mews.
7. Given the sympathetic design approach and the limited functional role of the
   existing garage, the proposal would not materially erode the character or
   appearance of the CA. I therefore find that the proposed development would
   preserve the character and appearance of the CA and would not result in harm to
   the significance of the CA.
8. I therefore find no conflict with Policies CC1, CC2, CC4 and SC7 of the Hounslow
   Local Plan (2015) and the relevant policies of the London Plan and the national
   Planning Policy Framework. Amongst other things, these specifically aim to ensure
   that development incorporates high-quality design that is appropriately styled for
   both the building and its surroundings. Furthermore, they seek to promote a
   positive contribution to local character and distinctiveness, enhance the local
   context, demonstrate a clear understanding of the area’s historic environment and
   heritage values, and preserve the character and appearance of conservation areas.
Other Matters
9. During my site visit, it was evident that parking demand in the area is high. Many of
   the garages were obstructed by parked vehicles on the forecourt, strongly
   suggesting that they are not routinely used for parking. In addition, refuse and
   recycling bins were stored externally at several properties, indicating that the
   functional use of the ground-floor frontage already varies from the original design
   intention.
10. While the loss of a garage could theoretically intensify parking pressures, on-site
    observations indicate that the garage is not currently used for parking and that this
    is a common pattern across the mews. This is further supported by representations
    from neighbouring occupiers, several of whom raised no objection to the
    conversion of the space to habitable accommodation, with one specifically noting
    that the garages are generally too small to accommodate modern vehicles. As
    such, any additional impact on parking demand would be limited.
