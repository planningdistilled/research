Decision
1. The appeal is allowed and planning permission is granted for a two-storey side
   extension and rear single storey extension at 20 Irene Avenue, Sunderland SR2
   9SZ in accordance with the terms of the application reference 25/00633/FUL and
   the following conditions:
      1) The development hereby permitted shall begin not later than three years from
         the date of this decision.
      2) The development hereby permitted shall be carried out strictly in accordance
         with the following approved plans: Existing and Proposed Elevations and Plans,
         Proposed Site Plan, Location Plan.
      3) The external materials of the extension including render hereby permitted shall
         match those used in the existing dwelling.
Preliminary Matters
2. The National Planning Policy Framework (the Framework) has been revised since
   the parties made their original submissions. The main parties have been invited to
   make further representations in relation to the Framework. Any representations
   received have been taken into consideration.
Main Issue
3. The main issue is the effect of the proposal on the character and appearance of the
   area.
Reasons
4. The appeal property is one half of a semi-detached dwelling situated on a corner
   plot within a housing estate. On site, I observed that on the same side of the street,
   the majority of the semi-detached dwellings on Irene Avenue and behind on Joan
   Avenue have been extended to the side. The streetscene that would have originally

     featured semi-detached dwellings with rhythmic gaps along Irene Avenue and Joan
     Avenue, has been significantly eroded by a variety of side extensions. No 22 Irene
     Avenue (No 22) on the opposite corner to the appeal property at the junction with
     Joan Avenue also has a large two storey side extension that is not subservient to
     the host dwelling.
5. The proposal would introduce a two-storey side extension and rear single storey
   extension. The ridge height, lack of set back from the main elevation and extent of
   footprint do not comply with the guidance set out in the Council’s Development
   Management Supplementary Planning Document (2021) (SPD). The extension to
   the side would not appear subservient to the host dwelling.
6. The SPD at paragraph 4.21 states that in some instances greater flexibility can be
   afforded to two-storey side extensions. With this in mind, I find that within the
   context of the surrounding streetscene, having regard to the prevalent number of
   extensions in the immediate area that are not subservient, including No 22, the
   introduction of an extension on this corner plot would be consistent with the present
   character of the streetscene.
7. The materials to be used in the proposal would be acceptable and there would be
   no detrimental impact on the amenity of neighbouring occupiers. The roof design at
   the corner would mirror that of No 22 and would thus not appear incongruous in the
   immediate streetscene.
8. Overall, I find that notwithstanding the identified conflict with the Council’s SPD,
   within its context, the proposal would not cause harm to the character and
   appearance of the area.
9. The proposal would provide additional floorspace within the curtilage. As I find the
   proposal to be consistent with the overall streetscene, I have afforded substantial
   weight in the planning balance to the benefit of additional floorspace in line with
   Policy L2 of the Framework.
10. Consequently, I find that the proposal complies with policy BH1 of the Sunderland
    City Council Local Plan (2020), that, amongst other things, seeks development that
    contributes positively to the character and appearance of the area.
Conditions and Conclusion.
11. I have had regard to the planning conditions suggested by the Council. I have
    considered them against the tests in Policy DM6 of the Framework and the advice
    in the Planning Practice Guidance. I have imposed a condition specifying the
    approved plans to provide certainty and a condition relating to materials in the
    interests of safeguarding the character and appearance of the area.
12. The proposed development would comply with the development plan when read as
    a whole. Therefore, for the reasons given, I conclude that the appeal should be
    allowed.
