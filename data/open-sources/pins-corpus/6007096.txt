•      The appeal is made under section 78 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended)
       against a failure to give notice within the prescribed period of a decision on an application for
       planning permission under section 73 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended) for
       the development of land without complying with conditions subject to which a previous planning
       permission was granted.
•      The appeal is made by L&W Wilson (Higham) Ltd against Westmorland and Furness Council.
•      The application Ref is 2025/0643/FPA.
•      The application sought planning permission for a residential development for up to 40 dwellings
       without complying with a condition attached to planning permission Ref SL/2023/0007, dated 22
       February 2024.
•      The condition in dispute is No 3 which states that:
       The development hereby permitted shall be carried out in accordance with the following approved
       plans:
       Location Plan, 2148-003 Rev A, received 4 January 2023
       Proposed Site Layout, 2148-10 Rev A, received 27 February 2023.
•      The reason given for the condition is:
       For the avoidance of doubt and in the interests of proper planning.


Decision
1.       The appeal is allowed and planning permission is granted for a residential
         development for up to 40 dwellings at Barn Hey Farm, Flookburgh Road,
         Allithwaite LA11 7RJ in accordance with the application Ref 2025/0643/FPA,
         without compliance with condition number 3 previously imposed on planning
         permission Ref SL/2023/0007 dated 22 February 2024 and subject to the
         conditions in the attached schedule.
Applications for costs
2.       An application for costs has been made by L&W Wilson (Higham) Ltd against
         Westmorland and Furness Council and by the Council against the appellant.
         These are the subject of separate decisions.
Preliminary Matters
3.       A new National Planning Policy Framework (the Framework)1 was published on 17
         August 2026 during the appeal process. However, as the policy content of the
         Framework, insofar as it relates to this case, has not materially changed, there is
         no requirement for me to seek further submissions from the main parties. I am
         satisfied no party would be prejudiced by determining the appeal on that basis.

4.       The description of development in the banner heading and decision above are
         taken from the application form. I have, however, omitted the words ‘application for
         outline application (some matters reserved)’ and ‘(major application)’ as they do
         not describe an act of development.
5.       The appeal arises from the Council’s failure to determine an application under
         section 73 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (the Act) to vary condition 3
         of an outline planning permission granted in 2024 for residential development of up
         to 40 dwellings. All matters other than access were reserved for subsequent
         approval.
Reasons
6.       Condition 3 requires the development to be carried out in accordance with listed
         approved plans. The proposed variation would replace the plan title ‘Proposed Site
         Layout’ with ‘Outline App Access’, which I note is the correct title of the approved
         plan.
7.       The appellant argues that the reference to an incorrect plan title in condition 3
         creates an inconsistency with the subsequent reserved matters approval, under
         which a different revision of the site layout plan was approved. It contends that this
         prevents the lawful implementation of the development.
8.       In connection with the appeal, reconsideration is also sought of the affordable
         housing obligations contained within the planning obligation entered into pursuant
         to section 106 of the Act2 (the S106), which is associated with the outline planning
         permission. A Financial Viability Assessment (FVA) has been submitted in support.
9.       During the appeal process, the Council has confirmed that the proposed change to
         the name of the approved plan in condition 3 is acceptable. In addition, the Council
         has confirmed that, following an independent review of the FVA, it accepts that the
         development would not be viable if the affordable housing required by the S106
         were provided. Nevertheless, it maintains that the amendment to condition 3
         should be pursued through the non-material amendment procedure under section
         96A of the Act. It further contends that any variation or discharge of the S106
         ought to have been sought through a separate process rather than in conjunction
         with the proposed amendment to condition 3.
10. The Planning Practice Guidance (PPG) states3 that there is no statutory limit on
    the degree of change permissible to conditions under section 73 of the Act, but the
    change must only relate to conditions and not the operative part of the permission.
    Accordingly, section 73 may be used to secure both significant and minimal
    modifications. Therefore, whilst the appellant could have sought the amendment to
    condition 3 under section 96A given its non-material nature, that did not preclude it
    from pursuing the change by way of a section 73 application.
11. Irrespective of the merits of the appellant’s stated concerns arising from the
    reference to the incorrect plan title in condition 3, the proposed amendment would
    correct the obvious error. In these circumstances, I find that the condition should
    be varied as proposed.

12. The PPG further explains4 that a permission granted under section 73 results in a
    new independent permission, subject to new or amended conditions, which sits
    alongside the original permission, which remains intact and unamended.
    Accordingly, if planning permission is granted in this case, it is necessary to
    consider whether planning obligations should also be secured, as the obligations
    contained within the existing S106 would not automatically apply to the new
    permission.
13. A section 73 application therefore provides an opportunity for the terms of the
    S106 to be reassessed and, where justified, modified or discharged, without the
    need for a separate process. There is no compelling evidence before me that a
    reassessment of planning obligations is limited solely to matters arising directly
    from the proposed variation of condition 3. Consequently, even if the principal
    purpose of the application was to facilitate reconsideration of the S106, as
    suggested by the Council, this would not undermine the appropriateness of the
    appellant’s approach.
14. As set out above, the Council accepts that the FVA demonstrates that the
    development could not support any affordable housing without rendering the
    scheme unviable. While I acknowledge the concerns expressed by interested
    parties regarding the absence of affordable housing, there is no substantive
    evidence before me that calls into question the FVA’s methodology or conclusions.
    The viability evidence indicates that requiring affordable housing would prevent the
    development coming forward. Even in the absence of affordable housing, the
    development would make a meaningful contribution to housing delivery and the
    Government’s policy objective of significantly boosting housing supply. I therefore
    find no basis to conclude that the absence of affordable housing renders the
    development unacceptable in this case. In these circumstances, any affordable
    housing obligations would not be necessary to make the development acceptable
    in planning terms and would therefore fail the test of necessity set out in
    Regulation 122 of the Community Infrastructure Levy 2010.
15. The only obligations set out within the S106 relate to the provision of affordable
    housing, including First Homes. No case has been advanced to justify their
    replacement with alternative obligations. Consequently, notwithstanding the
    objectives of development plan policy, there is no justification for requiring any
    planning permission granted pursuant to this appeal to remain subject to the S106.
16. I acknowledge the parties’ intention to execute a Deed of Discharge to provide
    administrative clarity and to avoid potential complications during future land
    registry and conveyancing process. However, in the absence of evidence to the
    contrary, that is a matter of administrative convenience rather than planning
    necessity. As I have found no justification for retaining the obligations, the absence
    of a Deed of Discharge at this stage does not weigh against the proposal.
Other Matters
17. I note that the Council’s concerns as to whether the development was lawfully
    commenced. However, it is not within my remit in determining a section 78 appeal
    to make a formal finding on the lawfulness of works undertaken pursuant to the
    permission. A section 73 cannot be used where the period for commencing the
    development has expired, as there would be no extant permission capable of

         appeal. Furthermore, my determination of this appeal does not prejudice the

18. Interested parties have raised other concerns regarding the development which do
    not relate to the variation of condition 3. The PPG, however, states that under
    section 73 consideration should only be given to the disputed conditions and that it
    is not a complete reconsideration of the application5. I have therefore determined
    the appeal on that basis.
Conditions
19. I have modified condition 3 by reference to the plan title ‘Outline App Access’ in
    place of ‘Proposed Site Layout’.
20. The PPG advises that where an application under section 73 of the Act is granted,
    the planning permission should include the relevant conditions from the original
    planning permission unless they have already been discharged.
21. Section 73(5) of the Act prevents the grant of planning permission under section
    73 where this would have the effect of extending the time limit for commencement.
    I have therefore amended the time limit condition to ensure that it is consistent with
    the original permission and reflects that the reserved matters have been approved.
22. I do not consider it necessary to impose a condition requiring the development to
    be carried out in accordance with the approved reserved matters, as this will be
    addressed within the relevant decision notices. However, in the interests of clarity,
    I have imposed a condition specifying the matters that remain reserved following
    the granting of outline planning permission.
23. The evidence before me indicates that the existing hedgerow has been removed
    rather than transplanted as required by condition 6 of the outline planning
    permission. Accordingly, compliance with that condition is no longer possible.
    However, I have amended the condition securing soft landscaping works to secure
    replacement hedgerow planting in the interest of ecology.
24. Conditions 4 and 5 impose a strict timetable for compliance because construction
    works have already commenced onsite and the opportunity to secure the
    outstanding details through pre-commencement or negatively worded conditions
    has therefore passed. The conditions provide an effective mechanism to secure
    submission, approval and implementation of those details, while enabling
    enforcement action should the requirements not be met.
25. In relation to the remaining conditions of the outline planning permission, the
    evidence before me is that they have not been discharged. I shall therefore impose
    those conditions that remain relevant, amending them where necessary to reflect
    the reserved matters approval. If any have in fact been discharged, or the required
    matters approved under the reserved matters application, that is a matter for the
    parties to address.
