**National Planning Policy Framework 2026**

### Introduction

1. A new National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF) was issued on 17 August 2026, which replaces the previous NPPF published in December 2024, and all other iterations. Substantially amended, it has been designed to support a new system (led by spatial development strategies and new-style local plans, supported by national decision-making policies), the new NPPF sets out which policies are for decision-making (DM) and which are for plan-making (PM). The Development Plan retains its primacy, and the opening paragraphs confirm that the NPPF represents the government’s policies for plan-making and for making decisions on development proposals in England and that it is a material consideration of critical importance in both contexts. Planning Practice Guidance (PPG) will still play “an important but supporting role to national planning policy and its status should be regarded in that light”.

2. The Framework 2026 and the PPG are material considerations which should be taken into account from the day of publication. However, there are some transitional arrangements affecting decision-making (appeals / applications) and plan-making (local plans) set out at “Annex A: Implementation” of the Framework 2026 and summarised later in Appendix A of this Note.

3. Alongside the new NPPF, updated Housing Delivery Test results for 2024 and 2025 have been released. Alongside this, the Housing Delivery Test

<!-- PINS Note p.2 -->

measurement rule book guidance has been updated as the way housing need is calculated has been updated to fit the Government's wider planning reforms. The biggest policy change is that a HDT result below 75% is now framed as evidence of unmet housing need under the new Framework. The Government has also introduced transition arrangements for the revised standard method used to calculate housing need, as the old method continues to be used for HDT monitoring years up to and including 2026/27, with the revised method applying from 2027/28 onwards – see para 27 below.

4. The Government’s policy paper “Creating a clear, rules-based planning system” is to be read in conjunction with these new publications. This sets out the Government’s move towards a more nationally consistent planning system. Key measures include stronger support for development around transport hubs, higher densities where appropriate, recognition of strategic sites, and streamlined decision-making. The package is supported by reforms to statutory consultees, updated Housing Delivery Test results, and proposals to standardise section 106 agreements to reduce delay and uncertainty.

### Overall Changes

5. The first point of difference is that this NPPF introduces national decision-making policies alongside plan making policies, with a shift in emphasis from discretionary decision-making guided by the NPPF to a rules-based approach. It also revises the “presumption in favour of sustainable development” to be “a permanent presumption in favour of suitably located development” and applies for all proposals within and outside of settlements.

6. The second point of difference is the NPPF’s structure. The chronological paragraph numbering is replaced with policy numbering structured on a chapter by chapter basis, but with a much clearer distinction between plan making and decision-making policies. Section 1 of the NPPF explains how to use it, noting:

- National decision-making policies (NDMPs) should be read as a whole with their footnotes and annexes;

- Some policies advise weight to be applied (see below) and where permission should be refused;

- Plan-making policies should not be used when determining development proposals; and

- Boxed text provides context and is not national policy.

7. Despite the change in structure, the ‘golden thread’ of the previous NPPFs (economic, social, environmental objectives) remains intact.

8. A notable feature of this NPPF is not the variety of weight descriptors but the opposite: the Framework overwhelmingly relies on "substantial weight" as its principal prescribed weighting term and there are very few alternative prescribed planning balance descriptors such as great weight, moderate weight, significant weight or limited weight. For decision makers, this means many aspects of the

<!-- PINS Note p.3 -->

planning balance will still require the exercise of planning judgement and explanation of the weight attached to competing considerations, unless the Framework expressly directs that substantial weight should be given.

9. Annex A of the NPPF also contains details of Written Ministerial Statements and other planning policy documents whose policy has been fully or partially incorporated within the Framework – these are set out separately in Appendix B below for ease of reference. The Knowledge Team will work through and update the ITM, with any PINS Note adjustments, required as a result of this NPPF and Annex A notations.

### Action for Inspectors or other decision makers

Planning & Environmental Appeals Service and Appeals Casework

10. Despite the change in approach, it is not anticipated that you will need to revert to parties for comment on every case. This should not, therefore, be done as a matter of course. You should, however, review your live cases as soon as possible and, where the policy emphasis has changed, consider whether, in the interests of natural justice, you should seek further comments. In order to facilitate the efficient and timely delivery of appeal decisions, you will need to consider a reasonable timeframe for the submission of responses (and any final comments) while being mindful of ministerial targets. Depending on the complexity of the case, a response in 5 working days is reasonable.

11. If comments are deemed necessary, in most cases it is expected that they should be required only from the principal parties. This should be arranged through the Case Officer. Some suggested standard wording is set out in Appendix C.

12. For cases being dealt with under the new Expedited Written Reps (Part 1) Appeals process, it is not anticipated that cases will need to be transferred to Part 2 because of the publication. Cases can be kept under Part 1 and if it is considered that it is necessary to seek the views of the parties, consultation under Regulation 8 of the 2009 regulations should be undertaken. Further guidance can be found in the Written Representations Part 1 Appeals chapter of the ITM.

13. Cases should only be moved to Part 2 in exceptional circumstances. If you do consider that a change to Part 2 is necessary you must discuss this with your Manager in the first instance before a transfer decision is made.

14. For cases which are following the previous rules (for applications dated on or before 31 March 2026) the principles set out in paragraphs 10 & 11 above apply.

15. For all appeals casework procedures, Inspectors should review cases allocated to them early to check whether consultation is needed in order to avoid delays.

16. If parties submit unsolicited (late) representations on the basis that the content of the documents has a bearing on their case, Inspectors are advised to follow the

<!-- PINS Note p.4 -->

advice on late submissions in the Approach to Decision Making chapter of the ITM.

17. For Hearing and Inquiry cases, it can be helpful to clarify at the event the updated policy references from the parties as set out in the proofs of evidence/statements. Ahead of the event, you may wish to consider asking parties for an updated statement/proof dealing with any specific matters arising from the publication, or an updated Statement of Common Ground.

Recovered Appeals and Call-ins

18. Where the case is still with the Inspector, PINS will be responsible for seeking any further comments from the parties (where necessary). Once the Inspector’s Report has been sent to the National Planning Casework Unit in MHCLG, they will be responsible for seeking any further comments deemed necessary.

Planning & Environmental Applications Service Casework

19. For s.62A and Crown Development applications the Inspector should consider whether it is necessary to seek comments on the documents, and from whom, taking into account the stage the application has reached, the procedure and the issues involved. The wording in the template letters in the linked Appendix C can be adapted as necessary.

Rights of Way and Commons Casework

20. These changes are unlikely to directly impact on rights of way or commons casework.

21. However, Inspectors may wish to note that the new Framework recognises that the network of public rights of way can be enhanced through suitable diversions agreed with the highway authority. It also encourages development proposals to take opportunities to extend, link or improve the quality of existing routes where they enable improved access to green spaces and the countryside, improve connections between local and long-distance routes, provide accessible, safe and convenient links to services and facilities and improve connections with public transport (TR8).

Local Plan Examinations

22. Annex A: Implementation sets out transitional arrangements. Paragraphs 4 to 8 deal with those that relate to plan-making. New-style local plans prepared under the new plan-making system should be produced in accordance with this new Framework. This applies to any local plans, minerals and waste plans (and supplementary plans) being prepared under Part 2 of the Planning and Compulsory Purchase Act 2004 (as amended by the Levelling up and Regeneration Act 2023) and the Town and Country Planning (Local Planning) (England) Regulations 2026.

<!-- PINS Note p.5 -->

23. Local plans being prepared under the current/legacy system legislation should be produced in accordance with the version of the Framework published in December 2024, unless any of the transitional arrangements in Annex A to that Framework apply (in which case those transitional arrangements continue to apply).

24. In summary, for the purpose of plan-making, this new Framework only applies to plans being prepared under the new, reformed plan-making system and not to any current/legacy system plans.

25. Moreover, paragraph 11 of Annex A is clear that the transitional arrangements also apply to the list of superseded Written Ministerial Statements and other planning policy set out in paragraph 10 (listed in Appendix B below). Consequently, planning policies from that list, including the Planning Policy for Traveller Sites, will be relevant to examinations of current/legacy system plans.

26. It is likely that some local planning authorities preparing current/legacy system plans will wish to ‘future proof’ their plans by ensuring that (at least) some policies are consistent with the policies in this new framework. That is understandable. However, the transitional arrangements are clear, and current/legacy system plans should not be examined for consistency with the policies in this new Framework.

27. The publication of the Housing Delivery Test results for 2024 and 2025 will need to be taken into account by Inspectors examining local plans. Paragraph 80 of the 2024 Framework is clear that the Housing Delivery Test consequences set out in paragraph 79 of the Framework will apply the day following publication of the Housing Delivery Test results. The versions of the Framework published in September and December 2023 include similar provisions. Consequently, Inspectors will need to ascertain the implications of the 2025 Housing Delivery Test Results for the buffer to be incorporated into the five-year housing land supply calculation. Where necessary, the local planning authority should be asked for its view on this and to prepare revised figures.

Infrastructure Decision and Applications Service

28. This NPPF does not contain specific policies for nationally significant infrastructure projects. These are determined in accordance with the Planning Act 2008 (as amended) and relevant National Policy Statements (NPSs) for major infrastructure, as well as any other matters that are relevant (which may include this Framework). Local Development Plans remain important and relevant under s104 including via the Local Impact Report and under s105.

29. NPSs may, where relevant, be a material consideration in preparing plans and making decisions on planning applications.

30. The NPPF is capable of being important and relevant in National Infrastructure work, but where there is a designated NPS, this will take precedence unless the NPS itself refers to the Framework. Unless any specific changes within the NPPF 2026 are a significant matter in the current examination, then it will not be necessary to revisit matters in the examination.

<!-- PINS Note p.6 -->

31. Where Hearings are planned or written questions are being issued, you should include a reference to the NPPF 2026 in general terms as to whether it affects any matters in the examination.

32. The NPPF retains references to PPGs and future updates to these (including where process guidance is relied on in NPSs such as flood risk and heritage assessments) will be relevant.

33. For Transport and Work Act and Highways Act orders the NPPF is capable of being a material consideration. The overall emphasis of the updated framework on sustainability and place making linked to transport infrastructure is an important context. Similarly, considerations on heritage, biodiversity, active and sustainable transport and Green Belt designations may all be relevant. Downstream LDPs, allocations and local transport strategies will be affected.

34. The specific matters will be dependent on the need case and existing local frameworks relevant to the Order. If specific matters are not identified, then a generic question on any impacts from the NPPF 2026 should be raised in live cases.

### Appendix A - Implementation

For the purposes of decision-making

1. The policies in this Framework are material considerations which must be taken into account in decision-making from the day of its publication

2. Development plan policies (or parts of those policies) which are materially inconsistent with national decision-making policies in this Framework should be given very limited weight. The only exception to this is where they have been examined and adopted1 or made against this Framework. Other development plan policies2 should not be given reduced weight simply because they were adopted prior to the publication of this Framework.

3. Where a local planning authority can demonstrate a five year supply of deliverable housing sites (with the appropriate buffer) and where the Housing Delivery Test indicates that the delivery of housing is more than 75% of the housing requirement over the previous three years, for the purposes of policy S5(1)(j), if the most up-to-date local housing need figure (calculated using the standard method set out in Annex D) is greater than the housing requirement set out in adopted development plan policies, this should not be considered as evidence of unmet need, for a period of five years from the date of the plan’s adoption.

For the purposes of plan-making

4. Any local plans, minerals and waste plans, and supplementary plans being prepared under Part 2 of the Planning and Compulsory Purchase Act 2004 (as amended by the Levelling up and Regeneration Act 2023) and the Town and Country Planning (Local Planning) (England) Regulations 2026 should be produced in accordance with this Framework.

5. Spatial development strategies should be produced in accordance with this Framework.

6. Neighbourhood plans that have been submitted to the local planning authority under Regulation 153, on or before, the date of publication of this Framework should be prepared in accordance with the Framework published in December 2024. Any neighbourhood plans that have not been submitted must comply with this Framework. For neighbourhood plans that have already been submitted, the qualifying body may choose to withdraw the plan and update it to take account of this Framework.

[^1]: Reference to the ‘adoption’ of a plan in annex A also applies to where a plan has been ‘approved’ by the Secretary of State.

[^2]: Including policies in made neighbourhood plans.

[^3]: Neighbourhood Planning (General) Regulations 2012.

<!-- PINS Note p.8 -->

7. For all plans that should be produced in accordance with this Framework, this means the policies in this version of the Framework will apply from 17 August 2026.

8. Any other plans being prepared should be produced in accordance with the version of this Framework published in December 2024, unless any of the transitional arrangements in Annex A to that Framework apply (in which case those transitional arrangements continue to apply).

Planning freedoms

9. The government will continue to explore with individual areas the potential for planning freedoms and flexibilities, for instance where this would facilitate an increase in the amount of housing that can be delivered.
