# 52 Tree Casework: Introduction

<!-- Part4 p.75 -->

## Introduction

This chapter is spilt in to 3 parts which cover tree casework. Areas that are applicable to all are included in this first section (Conditions and Costs for example) so as to avoid duplication throughout.

Trees - covers matters that may crop up in s78 casework

Tree Preservation Orders – deals explicitly with Sections 198 – 210 of The Town and Country Planning Act 1990 and Town and Country Planning (Tree Preservation)(England) Regulations 2012 which, together, form the basis for the implementation of policy for the legal protection of trees

Felling Licences – deals with The Forestry Act 1967 which requires that a licence to fell growing trees must be obtained from the Forestry Commission.

### Conditions – all tree casework

The Conditions PPG contains the six tests that conditions must satisfy. Specifically, in relation to trees, remember to include on-going maintenance clauses where necessary. As a starting point, PINS suite of suggested model conditions contains conditions relating to trees and landscaping which you can adapt to meet the specific circumstances of the case.

The TP 2012 Regs make provision for conditions to be applied to consents, with regulation 17(2) limiting their scope to:

- Conditions relating to the planting of replacement trees (such as requiring trees to be planted; requiring how, when and where planting is to be done; requiring things to be done or installed to ensure the protection of any replacement trees);

- Conditions requiring approvals to be obtained from the person giving consent;

- Conditions specifying the standard to which the works must be carried out; and

- Conditions specifying that the works may be carried out on multiple occasions or within a specified time period only.

In the absence of a bespoke condition, Regulation 17(4) specifies the following defaults:

- any consent is valid for a two year period, starting from the date of the consent; and

- the works granted may only be carried out once.

Where you intend to grant consent unconditionally, the defaults set out in TP 2012 Regs 17(4) should be cited in the decision letter, as a matter of good practice.

### Costs – all tree casework

Costs may be awarded for cases dealt with by any appeal procedure. In the case of appeals proceeding by the Written Representation procedure, the costs application must be made in writing when the appeal is submitted, if the application is made by the appellant, or within 14 days of the date of the ‘start date’ letter if the application is made by the local authority. Comments will be exchanged between the parties. The decision on the costs application will normally be given at the same time as the appeal decision.

In the case of Hearings and Inquiries, all costs applications must be formally made to you before the event is closed. Any such application must be brought to your attention at the event, and can be added to or amended as necessary in oral submissions.

If the application relates to behaviour at a Hearing or Inquiry, the applicant should advise you that they are going to make a costs application before the event is adjourned to the site, or before the inquiry is closed. You will then hear the application, the response by the other party, with the applicant having the final word. The decision on the award of costs will be made after the Hearing or Inquiry.

Inspectors may exercise their powers to make an award of costs where they have found unreasonable behaviour, including in cases where no application has been made by another party.

See the Costs Awards ITM chapter for further information.
