# 34 Landscape and Visual Impact Assessment: Green Infrastructure

<!-- Part2 p.542 -->

## Green Infrastructure

120. Green Infrastructure (GI) is a network of interconnected spaces and features and can include parks, open space, playing fields, woodland, street trees, allotments, private gardens, green roofs and walls, and SuDS systems. It can also include rivers and canals and other water bodies, although this is sometimes called blue infrastructure. It promotes multi-functionality.

121. This is a term often misused; it is not an alternative means of describing a parcel of open space even though this is increasingly terminology used in applications where developers overstate the landscape/visual and ecological benefits of features such as highway verges and play areas.

[^14]: Guidance Note 01/21 The Reduction of Obtrusive Light.

<!-- Part2 p.543 -->

122. NE has published guidance which sets out a comprehensive overview of the concept and benefits of GI15.

123. The NPPF defines Nature Recovery Networks which are networks supporting multiple benefits with co-ordinated action for species and habitats.

124. NPS EN-1 says, “Applicants should look for a holistic approach to delivering wider environmental gains and benefits through the use of nature-based solutions and Green Infrastructure.” And defines GI as follows: “Green infrastructure is a network of multi-functional green and blue spaces and other natural features, both rural and urban, which is capable of delivering a wide range of environmental, economic, health and wellbeing benefits for nature, climate, local and wider communities and prosperity”

[^15]: Natural England Green Infrastructure Guidance.
