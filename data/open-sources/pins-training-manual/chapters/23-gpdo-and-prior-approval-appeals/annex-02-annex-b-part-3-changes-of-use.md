# 23 GPDO and Prior Approval Appeals: Annex B: Part 3 – Changes of Use

<!-- Part2 p.47 -->

## Annex B: Part 3 – Changes of Use

1. Part 3 of the GPDO 2015 sets out Classes of [material] changes of use and, in some cases, associated operational development which are granted PP. Part 3 has been amended through various Orders since 2015, with the result being the revocation of some Classes and changes to others. The revoked Classes are B, C, D, E, J, JA, K, O and PA. Queries relating to amendments and transitional arrangements should be sent to Knowledge Centre.

2. Some PD rights set out under other Parts and Classes are disapplied to uses permitted under Part 3. For example, the PP granted under Part 1, Class A to enlarge a dwellinghouse does not apply, under A.1(a), if permission to use the dwellinghouse as such was granted by Part 3, Class G, M, MA, N, P, PA or Q.

### Prior Approval Requirements

3. All of the permitted changes in Part 3 are subject to limitations and conditions, but only some are subject to a pre-commencement condition requiring a prior approval procedure.

- Development is permitted under Part 3, Classes G, M, MA, N, P, Q, S and T subject to conditions G.1(d), M.2(1), MA.2(1), N.2(1), P.2(b), Q2.(1), S.2(1)(b) and T.2(1)(b) that, before beginning the development, the developer must apply to the LPA for a determination as to whether their prior approval will be required in relation to specified matters.

- The same applies to development permitted under Part 3, Class R above a floorspace threshold discussed below; R.3(1)(b). Below that, it is only necessary for the developer to provide information to the LPA; R.3(1)(a).

- The PD rights set out under Classes O, P and PA expired on 31 July 2021,10 June 2019 and 1 October 2020, via O.2(2)(b), P.1(c) and PA.1(c) respectively, but were subject to a prior approval procedure.

- Development is permitted under Part 3, Class A subject to condition A.1 that, before beginning the development, the developer provides written notification to the LPA of the date on which the use will change. No other class under Part 3 is subject to a notification or prior approval condition.

4. Part 3, paragraph W sets out the procedure for applications for prior approval under Part 3. Paragraph W.10(b) requires the decision-maker to ‘have regard to the National Planning Policy Framework…so far as relevant to the subject matter of the prior approval, as if the application were a planning application’. Reference should not be made to Framework policies that are not relevant to the prior approval matters.

5. Various terms are defined within the Classes themselves or under paragraph X of Part 3, if not within Article 2.

Limitations

6. Some Classes in Part 3 provide that development is not permitted where the site is on Article 2(3) land, in a SSSI, in a safety hazard area, a military

explosives storage area, or if the building is a listed building or the building / site is or contains a scheduled monument31.

7. Various classes of PD in Part 3 are subject to limitations that the previous use of the building must have taken place for a specified period or on a specified date. In such cases, it must be established as a matter of fact on the evidence that the previous use was both subsisting and lawful. Consideration may need to be given as to whether the building had become divided into separate planning units, bearing in mind that – where relevant to the Part and Class – the term ‘building’ may mean ‘part of a building’, as defined in Article 1(2).

8. Using Class MA as an example, paragraph MA.1(1)(b) requires that the last “use of the building” fell within one or more of the classes specified in MA.1(2) for a continuous period of at least two years before the date of the application for prior approval. Thus, development is only permitted under Class MA if:

- The use of the whole building fell within one or more of the specified classes and the use was lawful,

- The part of the building subject to the proposed change of use could be deemed a separate planning unit, as a matter of fact and degree, and its use fell within one or more of the specified classes and was lawful.

9. If there is insufficient evidence to adjudge the planning unit, the appeal can be refused on this basis with reference to paragraph W.(3))b). The Enforcement ITM chapter provides advice on the ‘planning unit’.

Statement on the ‘Net Increase in Dwellinghouses’

10. Paragraph W.(2)(ba) requires that prior approval applications relating to Classes G, M, MA, N and Q (and, previously, O, P and PA) are accompanied by a statement specifying the net increase in dwellinghouses proposed. This does not apply to applications made on or before 5th April 201632.

11. The ‘net increase in dwellinghouses’ is the number of dwellinghouses proposed that is additional to the number on the site immediately prior to the development. Paragraph W.(2)(bb) sets out specific details required in the statement for development proposed under Class Q33.

### Curtilage

12. Classes A, G, H, I, L and M permit a change of use of a building only. Classes AA, MA, N, Q, R, S and T permit a change of use of a building and land within its curtilage; the same was true of Classes O, P and PA.

and serving the purposes of the agricultural building, or (ii) an area of land immediately beside or around the agricultural building no larger than the land area occupied by the agricultural building, whichever is the lesser.’

14. Specific definitions of ‘curtilage’ were set out in paragraphs P.3 and PA.3 for the now expired Classes P and PA. Paragraph Q3.(1) sets out a specific definition for the purpose of Class Q from 21 May 2024. It should be clear on any relevant prior approval application as to which land is ‘curtilage’ and subject to the proposed change of use. If there is no such clear plan, the application should be treated as relating to the building only, noting that ‘any land’ can comprise ‘a building’ under s336(1) of the TCPA90.

15. The GPDO does not define curtilage for the purposes of Class AA, MA, N, O or T. In an Enforcement or prior approval appeal relating to such classes, and if the matter is in dispute, the curtilage should be considered on the basis of the common law definition as discussed in the Enforcement chapter.

16. If there is any dispute in relation to Class P, PA, Q, R or S, the extent of the curtilage should be considered in accordance with the statutory definition, that is, paragraph P.3, PA.3, Q3.(1) or X, as the case may be. The land must be ‘immediately beside or around the building’, and the effect of the text ‘whichever is the lesser’ is that development cannot be PD if it would involve the change of use of an area of land that is larger than the curtilage.

Curtilage: Change of Use of the Building only

17. It is open to an applicant to propose a change of use of the building, along with any associated conversion works, without requesting any change of use of the land within the curtilage of the building at all. The effect of this would be that the use of the building would change, but the surrounding land would remain in the previous use.

18. Any subsequent application for prior approval of the change of use of land to create a garden around the dwelling must relate to land that meets the relevant GPDO definition of curtilage. If the developer obtains prior approval for a change of use of a building (and land) to a dwellinghouse and then wishes to provide a larger garden, they would need to apply for express PP for a material change of use of land and such an application would be considered on its merits, that is, on a s38(6) basis.

19. There is no requirement for a development proposed under Part 3 to include a curtilage and so an appeal should not be dismissed simply because one is not shown within the red line of the site. An appeal can be determined within the terms of the Order and without the need for reference to a curtilage.

Curtilage: Building or Part of a Building

20. In cases where the prior approval application relates only to part(s) of a building, with other parts being demolished or not subject to the proposed change of use, the area to be considered as within the curtilage of the building for the purposes of paragraphs P, PA, Q3.(1) and X (where appropriate) should be strictly related to – that is, no larger than – the part of the building which is subject to the proposed change of use.

Curtilage: Excluded from the Site Edged Red and/or Unclear

<!-- Part2 p.50 -->

21. It may be apparent from the representations that the proposed development encompasses land within the curtilage of the building, although that is not shown within the site edged red. In such cases, prior approval can be granted if the extent of the curtilage and its compliance with the relevant definition can be identified from the papers overall.

22. If the curtilage would not comply with the relevant definition, or the developer has provided insufficient information to enable the Inspector to establish such compliance, the Inspector may:

- Dismiss the appeal with reference to paragraph W.(3) or

- Conclude that there is no curtilage and grant prior approval strictly for the change of use of the building or

- Define the curtilage and grant prior approval simply in respect of an area which meets the definition.

23. The key point is that prior approval can be granted in respect of land within the curtilage of the building only where the extent of the curtilage is or can be made clear and that curtilage would meet the relevant definition. It must be apparent which land is subject to the permitted change of use.

Curtilage: Different to Site Edged Red and/or Too Large

24. Whether or not it is clearly identified on a plan, the curtilage may differ from the site edged red. The correct approach remains to assess the area identified as curtilage against the relevant definition. The Inspector should state for the avoidance of doubt that prior approval is only granted, and the change of use is only permitted, in respect the curtilage shown on the plan.

25. If the proposed change of use would relate to an area that exceeds the relevant definition of curtilage, it is again open to the Inspector to consider whether prior approval could be granted in respect of a smaller curtilage or, if that cannot be reasonably established, to dismiss the appeal.

Curtilage: Proposed Access

26. Where it is proposed to create a new access to the building subject to the change of use, the point where the access would not be ‘immediately beside or around the building’ should be assessed on a fact and degree basis. It may be noted that:

- Any operational development required to construct or upgrade an access beyond the curtilage may be PD under Part 2, Class B, which permits the formation, laying out and construction of a means of access to a highway that is not a trunk road or classified road. The access would have to be ‘required’ to serve the dwelling but not restricted in length.

- However, the use of any land outside of the curtilage where a proposed access would be formed, or where an existing access which serves the existing lawful use would be utilised by the proposed dwelling, will remain in its existing lawful use. Any material change of use of the land to use for residential purposes would be liable to require a separate grant of PP.

### Classes M, N and Q – Building Works

<!-- Part2 p.51 -->

Types of Application

27. Classes M and N permit development consisting of (a) a change of use of a building or (b) a change of use together with building operations reasonably necessary to ‘convert’ the building. The same applies to Class Q prior to 21 May 2024 (and subject to transitional arrangements discussed below).

28. As amended from 21 May 2024, Class Q permits (a) a change of use of a building, (b) a change of use together with the extension of a building or (c) a change of use together with building operations reasonably necessary to convert the building or to extend that building. Notwithstanding the repetitions of "or", the PD right is likely to encompass building operations reasonably necessary to convert and/or extend the buildings.

29. Applications may still be made for prior approval under Classes M, N or Q in relation to a change of use only. Where prior approval is sought under these or any other Class in Part 3 for a change of use only, but building or other operations are nonetheless shown on the submitted plans, the Inspector should be clear that those works are outside of their remit. This applies even if it appears likely that the operations would be required to facilitate the change of use or meet any requirements of the GPDO such as the provision of natural light in all habitable rooms. The Inspector should state in Preliminary Matters that they cannot speculate either as to whether the works would require a separate grant of planning permission, or on the merits of the works.

30. W.(2)(a) states ‘the application must be accompanied by… a written description of the proposed development, which, in relation to development proposed under Class M, N or Q of this Part, must [in the same application] include any building or other operations’; emphasis added. No such information is required if no building or other operations are proposed.

31. Where applications are made for a change of use and associated operations as described under Class M(b), N(b), Q(b) or Q(c), the works should take place with the change of use. If an applicant applies for prior approval for a change of use only under Classes M(a), N(a) and Q(a), and later finds that works are required, they must submit a new prior approval application for the change of use plus operations before the development is commenced.

32. An Inspector can make a split decision on an application for a change of use plus works, so as to grant prior approval for the change of use alone. This applies even if some operations would be necessary, since they could be subject to a later application for express PP.

33. Taking an appeal made in respect of Class Q(c) (from 21 May 2024) as an example: if the Inspector is satisfied in respect of the change of use and the matters referred to in Q2.(1)(a) to (e) and (g), but they are not satisfied with the design or external appearance of the building (Q2.(1)(f)) as shown on the proposed plans, they can grant prior approval for the change of use only.

34. However, the appeal should be dismissed if the Inspector is not satisfied – or needs more information – in respect of the change of use and any of the associated prior approval matters referred to in Q2.(1)(a) to (e) and (g). In that situation, the Inspector would not need to consider the proposed works.

<!-- Part2 p.52 -->

35. If the LPA refuses a Class M, N or Q application on grounds relating to building operations when none had been proposed, but the appellant then submits information on works during the appeal process, the Inspector should still determine the appeal as though it relates to a change of use only. In the interests of openness and fairness, the appellant should be advised to make a further prior approval application for a change of use plus works.

36. Multiple prior approval applications can be made for the same building over time, subject to the relevant limitations for Classes M, N and Q.

Internal and Structural Alterations

37. Under s55(2)(a) of the TCPA90, development that requires PP does not include works of ‘maintenance, improvement or other alteration which affect only the interior…or do not materially affect the external appearance of the building’. The nature and extent of proposed building operations should be assessed – on a fact and degree basis – to inform a conclusion as to whether they would amount to development and require PP via the GPDO at all.

38. The nature and extent of works should also be assessed to inform a conclusion as to whether they would fall within the PD rights set out in Class M, N or Q, as the case may be. The operations should be ‘reasonably necessary’ to facilitate the permitted change of use and comply with the relevant limitations and conditions.

39. There is a difference in law between ‘maintenance’ and rebuilding34. If the operations would amount to rebuilding, PP will be required but not granted under Class M, N or Q. It was held in Hibbitt v SSCLG [2016] EWHC 2853 that the building must be capable of conversion to residential use without operations amounting to complete or substantial re-building of the pre-existing structure or, in effect, the creation of a new building; see also PPG paragraph ref ID: 13-105-20180615 for discussion on this issue in relation to Class Q.

40. In considering whether operations would be permitted under Class M, N or Q, the GPDO 2015 makes no distinction between structural and non-structural works. There is no restriction on whether works are structural or not, and the operations do not need to be demonstrably essential in order to be found 'reasonably necessary'. Even if other options would be open to the developer, the question for the decision maker is whether the course proposed is one that a reasonable person would choose on the balance of probabilities.

41. Once a permitted change of use has occurred under Classes M, N and Q – or indeed any other Class – the building may be altered internally without reliance on PD rights or other permission.

Floorspace

42. Given the definition of floorspace set out in Article 2(1) of the GPDO 2015, the term would include any existing mezzanines or additional storeys. However, an additional internal floor may be added to the building following an initial

change of use under any other Class of Part 3 since the works would be unlikely to constitute ‘development’ under s55(2).

43. The amount of floorspace subject to the proposed change of use is limited under Class I, and limited on a cumulative basis under Class M, N or Q, as discussed in respect of each of those classes below. Prior approval may be refused for development under Class M, N or Q where the cumulative floorspace in the new use would exceed the relevant limitations.

44. However, the limitations are to the floorspace ‘of the existing building changing use’ and ‘not on the size of the building or buildings in which the change of use occurs’; Mansell v Tonbridge and Malling BC & others [2017] EWCA Civ 1314. This is because ‘the existing building’ in question must be read as potentially referring to ‘part of a building’ as set out in Article 2(1).

45. In other words, the change of use of part of the building is permitted under Class M, N and Q. It may be argued that the limitation on cumulative floorspace does not apply to a proposed development where a previous change of use under the same Class was previously granted prior approval in respect of a different part of the same building. The Inspector will need to look at the relationship between the previously approved and now proposed changes of use on a fact and degree basis.

46. There is a limitation to the gross floor space of the existing building subject to the PD right set out under Class PA; the same was true for the now expired PD right under Class P. However, Article 2(1) provides that the term ‘building’ does not include any part of a building for the purposes of Classes P or PA.

47. From 21 April 2021, paragraph W.(2)(bc) requires, in relation to Classes G, M, MA, N, O, PA or Q, the floor plan to indicate “the total floor space in square metres of each dwellinghouse” as well as the dimensions and proposed use of each room, the position and dimensions of windows, doors and walls, and the elevations of the dwellinghouses.
