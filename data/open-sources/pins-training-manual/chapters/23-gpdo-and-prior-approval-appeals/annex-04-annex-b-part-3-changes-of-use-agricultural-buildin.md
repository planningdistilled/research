# 23 GPDO and Prior Approval Appeals: Annex B: Part 3 – Changes of Use: agricultural building that was (but is no longer) part of an

### agricultural building that was (but is no longer) part of an

established agricultural unit and any land within that building's curtilage, to a use falling within Class C3 (dwellinghouses)…

(b) development referred to in sub-paragraph (a) together with the extension of the building…or

(c) development referred to in sub-paragraph (a) together with building operations reasonably necessary to convert the building to a use falling within Class C3 (dwellinghouses)…or to extend that building.

113. The most significant changes to Class Q relate to what is permitted and the temporal limitations. Prior approval may be granted under Q.(a)(i) for a

change of use of a building which is part of an established agricultural unit. It must be part of the established agricultural unit at the time of the prior approval application, and have been so on 24 July 2023 or for a period of at least ten years before the date that the Class Q development begins. However, it is not necessary in Q.(a)(i) cases for the building itself to be in agricultural use. Prior approval may be granted for the change of use of a building that was, for example, in non-agricultural use as part of a mixed use planning unit.

114. Prior approval may be granted under Q.(a)(ii) for the change of use of a former

agricultural building that was but is no longer part of an established agricultural unit. Limitation Q1.(b) provides that such development is not permitted if (i) the site was part of an established agricultural unit on 24 July 2023, Iii) if it ceased to be part of the established agricultural unit after that date and was not part of the established agricultural unit for a period of at least ten years before the date that the Class Q development begins or (iii) since ceasing the to be part of an established agricultural unit, the site has been used for any non-agricultural purposes. The upshot of Q1.(b)(iii) is that, while the building does not need to be in agricultural use or part of an established agricultural unit, it cannot have been put to any other use.

115. Another change from 21 May 2024 is that Class Q now permits a change of use under Qa(i) or (ii)…

(b) …together with the extension of the building…or

(c) …together with building operations reasonably necessary to convert the building to a use falling within Class C3 (dwellinghouses)…or to extend that building.

116. Class Q should be interpreted purposively and read as permitting building operations to both extend and convert the building. Prior approval appeals should not be determined on the basis that reasonably necessary conversion works would preclude the construction of an extension or vice versa.

117. Limitations to the size and design of any extension are set out under paragraphs Q1.(h)(i) and Q1.(i). Amongst other stipulations, an extension must be single storey, to the rear of the existing building and not sited on land that was previously covered by a hard surface.

118. Limitations to operations reasonably necessary to convert the building are set out under Q1.(h)(ii) and Q1.(j). Under Q1.(h)(ii), the developer may construct protrusions of up to 0.2m to accommodate works described in Q.1(j), which essentially replicate the old Class Q limitations discussed above. Such operations do not include excavating, installing or strengthening foundations. It is likely to be the case, however, that such works would be permitted for an extension or 20cm protrusion that meets the relevant limitations.

119. The limitations to Class Q have also been changed in respect of the number and size of dwellinghouses permitted, with the old references to ‘larger’ or ‘smaller’ dwellinghouses now removed. Development is not permitted if:

Q1.(c) the floorspace of any C3 dwellinghouse exceeds 150sqm.

Q1.(d): together with any previous development under Class Q within the original limits of an established agricultural unit [see paragraph Q3.(2] would

<!-- Part2 p.64 -->

result in the cumulative number of separate C3 dwellinghouses exceeding 10 or the cumulative floorspace of C3 dwellinghouses exceeding 1000sqm.

120. Thus, the new Class Q note permits – through one or a series of prior approval applications – more dwellinghouses with more cumulative floorspace than before via Q1.(d). None of the dwellinghouses, however, from 21 May 2024, may be larger than 150sqm under Q1.(c).

121. Under limitation Q1.(g), development is not permitted under the new Class Q if development under Class A(a) or B(a) of Part 6 has been carried out on the established agricultural unit during the period of ten years before the date development under Class Q begins.

122. There is no change to the condition imposed on Class Q, under Q2.(1) that, before beginning the development, the developer must apply to the LPA for a determination as to whether their prior approval will be required. There is no change to the prior approval matters relating to development permitted under Q(a) or Q(c). However, where the development proposed would include an extension permitted under Q(b), the developer must also apply to the LPA for a determination as to whether their PA will be required as to impact of the extension on the amenity of any adjoining premises.

123. However, a final change to note in relation to the new Class Q is that the term curtilage is now defined in paragraph Q3.(1) rather than Paragraph X, and ‘the original limits of an established agricultural unit’ are defined in paragraph Q3.(2). Within that phrase, the agricultural unit should be considered as above and still not confused with the planning unit.

Planning Policy and Guidance

124. PPG paragraphs ref ID: 13-104-20180615 to 13-109-20150305 relate to Class Q and advise in particular that: ‘Impractical reflects that the location and siting would “not be sensible or realistic”, and undesirable…that it would be “harmful or objectionable”…the location of the building…may be undesirable if it is adjacent to other uses such as intensive poultry farming buildings, silage storage or buildings with dangerous machines or chemicals’68.

125. Those PPG paragraphs are not yet updated in relation to the ‘new’ Class Q and therefore should be applied only where clearly still relevant. Where an extension is proposed, its ‘impact…on the amenity of any adjoining premises’ should be considered on a similar basis as in Part 1 or Part 20 appeals, with regard to matters such as overlooking, privacy and light; see, for example, PPG paragraph ref ID: 13-122-2021820.

126. Planning policy on green belts in the Framework is not relevant to Class Q, and nor are matters such as housing land supply, agricultural occupancy etc. Such issues should only be mentioned where it is necessary to say they are not relevant and given no weight; see the advice on the Framework above.

127. As per East Hertfordshire, judgments about prior approval matters should be framed by the context that this is not an application for express PP, but for the prior approval of development that is permitted by the GPDO for the purpose

### Class R: Agricultural Buildings to a Flexible Commercial Use

128. Class R was also amended from 21 May 2024 but not such that transitional arrangements are required69. Prior approval applications and appeals could be made before that date for development consisting of a change of use of a building and any land within its curtilage from a use as an agricultural building to a flexible use falling within one of the following: R(a) use class B8 (storage and distribution, (b) C1 (hotels) or (c) E (commercial, business or service.

129. From 21 May 2024, the permitted change of use is to a flexible use R(a) falling within one of (i) use class B2 (general industrial), B8, C1, E or F.2(c) (outdoor sport or recreation) or (b) for the provision of agricultural training.

130. While the change of use could be to a mix of uses all falling (say) within use class E, the PD right does not (either side of 21 May 2024) extend to a mix of uses between the use classes specified, or a mix of uses encompassing agricultural training.

131. The term ‘building’ may mean ‘part of a building’ for Class R, and the term ‘curtilage’ is defined in paragraph X of Part 3 for the purposes of this PD right.

132. Development is not permitted by Class R, under limitation R.1(a), either side of 21 May 2024, if the building was not used solely as an agricultural use as part of an established agricultural unit (i) on 3 July 2012, (ii) in the case of a building which was in use before that date, when it was last in use, or (iii) in the case of a building which was brought into use after 3 July 2012, for a period of at least 10 years before the development under Class R begins. In cases where (i) or (ii) applies, but agricultural use was resumed after 3 July 2012 after a period of cessation or other use, Inspectors should follow the approach set out in relation to lawfulness above.

133. Limitation R.1(b) limits the cumulative floorspace of buildings (or parts of buildings) changing use under Class R to 500sqm prior to 21 May 2014, and 1000sqm from that date. There are no restrictions within Class R relating to Article 1(5) land.

134. Condition R.2(a) provides that a site which has changed use under Class R may, subject to the prior approval procedure set out under R.3, subsequently change use to another use falling within one of the use classes comprising the flexible use. However, under R.2(b), once the use has been changed under Class R, the site is to be treated as having a ‘sui generis’ use rather one falling within a use class.

135. What that means is that – for example – if PD rights are exercised under Class R to change the use of the agricultural building to a use falling within use class E, the developer could subsequently use the Class R PD rights again to change the use of the building to one falling within use class C1.

However, the building would nonetheless be treated as having a sui generis use (rather than one within use class E or C1) so that:

- Any other change of use – even within (what would otherwise be) the same use class – would not be exempted from the definition of development set out under s55(2)(f), so that planning permission would be required and

- PP would not be granted via the GPDO because the PD rights set out under other classes of Part 3, such as Class MA (change of use of commercial, business and service uses falling within use class E to a use falling within use class C3) do not apply to the sui generis uses created under Class R.

136. Condition R.2(d) also restricts the uses that may take place where the change of use is to a use falling within use class B2 (from 21 May 2024).

137. Before changing the use of the site under Class R, and subsequently making any further change of use to one of the use classes comprising the flexible use, the developer must:

R.3(1)(a): provide specified information to the LPA where the cumulative floorspace of the building(s) changing use does not exceed 150sqm. The information required is unchanged from 21 May 2024.

R.3(1(b): apply to the LPA for a determination as to whether their prior approval will be required where the cumulative floorspace of the building(s) changing use does exceed 150sqm. The prior approval matters are unchanged from 21 May 224

138. Condition R.3(2) requires that, where the cumulative floorspace exceeds 150sqm and the prior approval procedure is required, the development is begun within a period of three years starting with the PA date.

139. PPG ref ID: 13-102-20211020 provides advice on Class R. It is not yet updated following the 21 May 2024 amendments.

### Class S: Agricultural Buildings to State-Funded Schools

140. A change of use is permitted under Class S in respect of a building (which may include part of a building) and any land within its curtilage (defined in paragraph X of Part 3 to use as a state-funded school falling within use class F.1(a) of Schedule 2 to the UCO. Class S also permitted a change of use to a nursery use but that provision was revoked from 1 August 202170.

141. Under paragraph S.1(a), development is not permitted by Class S if the site was not used solely for an agricultural use as part of an established agricultural unit (i) on 20 March 2013 or (ii) when last in use, or (iii) if brought into use after 20 March 2013, for a period of at least ten years before the date that development under Class S begins.
