## Annex C: Part 4 – Temporary Buildings and Uses

### Class A: Temporary buildings and structures

1. The size and means of construction of a building is highly relevant to Part 4, Class A PD rights; the larger and more permanent the building, the less likely it is to be genuinely ‘required temporarily’ in connection with the carrying out of development. It is for the appellant to show why the building is reasonably required. His or her intentions are relevant to that assessment but must be objectively assessed; R (oao Wilsdon) v FSS and Tewkesbury BC [2006] EWHC 2980 (Admin); [2007].

2. Where a building or structure is said to be ‘required temporarily’ in connection with operations, the operations themselves need to be lawful – as stated in paragraph A.1(b) – and to have commenced or be about to commence. It will be a matter of fact and degree as to whether the operations are continuing or can reasonably be held to have ceased at the time an enforcement notice was issued, such that the building or structure is in breach of condition A.2(a).

3. The tolerances for temporary uses in Part 4 do not apply when the intention is that the development should be permanent; Tidswell v SSE & Thurrock BC [1977] JPL 104. It will be for the appellant to show that the use was temporary, and the PD right was genuinely implemented.

4. Where an enforcement notice is upheld in respect of a caravan site, motocross, war games, market or other transitory use of land, on the basis that – on the facts – there is an intermittent permanent rather than a temporary use, and there is no Article 4(1) Direction in force, the developer can still implement PD rights. Again, it is for the developer to show that it is a genuine implementation of temporary use rights and not a recommencement of the prohibited permanent use71.

5. The developer could still utilise Part 4 rights even if there is no express saving in the requirements of the enforcement notice; Cord v SSE [1981] JPL 40. A notice cannot take away lawful use rights. Under s181(2), a notice can only require that an alleged use be discontinued permanently ‘to the extent that it is in contravention of Part III’. The implementation of a temporary use permitted under the Order (and thus in accordance with s60) is not in contravention of Part III of the Act.

6. In other words, while unlawful uses do not benefit from PD rights under Article 3(5)(b), this does not apply where the unlawful permanent use is carried out on a temporary basis in accordance with Part 4, Class B. The temporary use rights in Class B subsist alone and are not related to any other existing unlawful use.

7. The presence of permanent buildings and facilities, and changes to the character of the land may be relevant as to whether the proposed use is temporary within Part 4 or a permanent change of use – but only when the

### Class B: Temporary use of land

10. Class B provides that ‘the use of land for any purpose for not more than 28 days in total is PD, except in relation to the uses specified in Class B(a) and B(b), where the limit is 14 days. In considering whether either or both limits have been exceeded, it is appropriate to look at the planning unit and take into account the aggregate of the occurrence of different uses.

11. In a LDC appeal under s191, where uses undertaken were similar to B(b) uses but did not comply with the limitations in B.1(d), it was held that they could not be aggregated with permitted B(b) uses to claim activity in excess of 14 days in any one year over the necessary ten year period, such that the uses would be immune from enforcement action; Miles v NAW & Caerphilly CBC [2007] EWHC 10 (Admin).

### Class BA: Additional temporary use of the land during the relevant period

12. On 1 August 2020, given the Covid pandemic, Class BA was added to grant PD rights for temporary uses of land, in addition to the temporary PD rights set out in class B between 1 July and 31 December 202073. The “relevant period” set out in condition BA.2, following a subsequent amending order, is 1 January to 31 December 202174.

### Class BC: Temporary recreational campsites

16. Class BC was introduced on 26 July 202377 to permit the use of any land as a recreational campsite for not more than 60 days in any calendar year, with the provision of not more than 50 pitches and moveable structures reasonably necessary for the purposes of the permitted use. Such structures could include toilet and waste disposal facilities, which indeed the developer must provide on-site under condition BC.2(a).

17. Condition BC.2(b) requires the developer to notify the LPA in writing before the commencement of development in each calendar year, providing a copy of the site plan with particulars of the toilet and waste disposal facilities and the dates on which the site will be in use. Where the proposed development is in Flood Zone 2 or 3, however, the developer must apply for prior approval under condition BC.(d), following the procedure set out in BC.3.

### Class C: Use as a state-funded school for [2 academic years]

18. Class C was amended in 202178, in line with amendments to the UCO, to specify that the use would fall within use class F.1(a). The PD right is not subject to any prior approval by the LPA but, under condition C.2(a), the site must be approved for use as a state-funded school by the relevant Minister.

### Class CA: Provision of a temporary state-funded school on previously vacant commercial land

19. Whereas Class C relates to the use of an [existing] building and land, Class CA permits the provision of temporary buildings on vacant commercial land as well as their use as a state-funded school falling within use class F.1(a). This PD right applies for up to three years, rather than two as in Class C. The term ‘vacant commercial land’ is defined, with other terms, in paragraph CA.3

20. The conditions imposed via CA.2 include that the site must be approved for use as a state-funded school by the relevant Minister and the developer must apply to the LPA for a determination as to whether their prior approval will be required as to specified matters.

### Class CB: Temporary provision of buildings for a school with a RAAC-affected building

21. This PD right was introduced in 202379 with limitations to the location, siting and size of buildings permitted and, under CB.1(e), to the duration of the PD right itself, which will end at 11.59pm on 24 October 2026.

### Class D: Commercial, business and service etc to temporary flexible use

23. In short, Class D permits a change of use from use as a betting office, pay day loan shop, hot food takeaway or a use within use class E to a flexible use within use class E or F,1(b), (c), (d) or (e) for a single continuous period of up to three years80. The PD right is subject to the developer giving written notification to the LPA but not complying with any prior approval procedure.

Class DA: Restaurants and cafes, drinking establishments and drinking establishments with expanded food provision to temporarily provide takeaway food

24. This PD right from the Covid pandemic81 ended on 23 March 202282.

### Class DB: Use of qualifying high-street premises changing to a suitable high-street use

25. Class DB was introduced in December 202483. It permits the change of use of a building which is a qualifying high street premises within the meaning of s192(2) of the Levelling Up and Regeneration Act 2023 (LURA23) and any land within the curtilage to a suitable high street use as defined by s192(4) of LURA23, for the duration of a tenancy granted following a rental auction of the premises held under Part 10 of LURA23.

26. S192(2) of the LURA23 provides that premises are “qualifying high street premises” if they are (a) situated on a designated high street or in a designated town centre and (b) the local authority considers them suitable for a high street use.

27. S192(4) defines “suitable high street use” as a high street use for which the local authority considers the premises to be suitable with regard, under s192(5), to any works that the landlord would be required or the tenant would be permitted and likely to carry out.

28. Condition DB.2(a) requires the local authority responsible for the auction to notify the LPA of the suitable high street use for the premises and the dates on which that use will commence and cease. Under DB.2(b), the premises must revert to their former use at the end of the tenancy.
