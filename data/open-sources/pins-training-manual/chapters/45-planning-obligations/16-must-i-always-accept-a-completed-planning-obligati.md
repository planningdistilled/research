# 45 Planning Obligations: Must I always accept a completed planning Obligation?

<!-- Part3 p.634 -->

## Must I always accept a completed planning Obligation?

88. Regardless of any deadlines that have been set, there is no requirement that you must accept and consider a completed Obligation. A completed and correctly executed obligation may be binding upon the parties, even if you have not seen it. It will, therefore, bind the parties to do, or not do, whatever they have promised. Consequently, an existing planning obligation must still be assessed by you.

89. Most planning obligations are capable of being material planning considerations. However, following the Akhtar case18 it was found that a material planning consideration can be disregarded where procedural regulations and guidance have not been complied with. If you exercise your discretion and do not accept a late Obligation it is imperative that you address this in your decision. You must also carefully consider any blue pencil clauses.

90. In circumstances where you are seeking to allow an appeal, you have turned away an executed obligation and that Obligation does not include any blue pencil clauses you must consult with your IM/PfL before your decision is issued.

91. If an Obligation is submitted late, and you subsequently accept it but you determine that you will not take it in to account, or that it has no material bearing on your decision, then you must clearly explain this – this has recently been confirmed in the Courts in Tiwana Construction Ltd v SSHCLG & West Suffolk Council [2025] EWHC 1485 (Admin)
