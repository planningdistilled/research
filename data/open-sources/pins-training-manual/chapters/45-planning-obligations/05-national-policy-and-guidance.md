# 45 Planning Obligations: National policy and guidance

<!-- Part3 p.617 -->

## National policy and guidance

11. Consideration should be given to whether otherwise unacceptable development could be made acceptable through the use of conditions or planning obligations. In terms of obligations, Chapter 3, DM6(4) of the National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF) states that planning obligations should only be used where it is not possible to address potential unacceptable impacts through a planning condition. Further advice on the use of planning conditions can be found in the ITM chapter Conditions. Where national model planning

obligations are relevant to the development, they should be used unless there are strong reasons for using a different obligation.

12. Importantly, Regulation 122 of the Community Infrastructure Levy (CIL) Regulations 2010 also says a planning obligation may only constitute a reason for granting permission for a development if the obligation is:

- necessary to make the development acceptable in planning terms;

- directly related to the development; and

- fairly and reasonably related in scale and kind to the development.

13. Whilst these tests are no longer repeated in the NPPF, they continue to apply by virtue of the CIL Regulations (and are also set out in the Planning Practice Guidance). Further detail is provided in paragraphs 32-39 of this chapter under the heading "Community Infrastructure Levy – Statutory Tests”.

14. Further useful guidance is provided in the government’s Planning Practice Guidance chapter Planning Obligations, which you should study carefully.

15. The Procedural Guide – Planning appeals – England2 also provides detailed advice.
