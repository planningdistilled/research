# 45 Planning Obligations: What if the planning obligation is undated, flawed/incomplete or missing?

<!-- Part3 p.630 -->

## What if the planning obligation is undated, flawed/incomplete or missing?

76. An obligation that is undated, incomplete, flawed or missing cannot be given any weight.

77. However, the 2026 quashing (by consent) of a dismissed appeal—where a main issue was that the obligation had not been dated—shows that a deed is not automatically invalid simply because the date is missing. In practice, while there is no strict requirement for an Inspector to go back to the parties for clarification (especially in written reps case), any decision not to do so should be clearly explained in the Decision. This is particularly important where a straightforward administrative request for a date could have resolved the issue and avoided any risk of procedural unfairness.

Normally this is in the form of an up to date copy entry or entries from the Land Registry.

[^15]: The Procedural Guide – Planning appeals – England which explains the correct format options where execution is by an individual or a company.

78. If you intend to ask the parties to provide a date on an otherwise acceptable obligation, it is advisable to set a clear deadline and make it explicit that, once that deadline has passed, the decision will be issued without waiting further.

79. The Procedural Guide – Planning appeals – England16 indicates at what stage in the appeal process planning obligations should be provided. In summary:

### Written Representations:

The Procedural Guide states that the appellant must ensure that we receive an executed and certified copy of the planning obligation at the time of making their appeal.

### Hearings and inquiries:

Final draft, agreed by all parties to it – should be received by PINS no later than 10 working days before the Hearing or Inquiry opens.

A certified copy of the executed planning obligation – should normally be received before the Hearing or Inquiry closes, without the need for an adjournment. If that is not practicable then you can agree the details for the receipt of a certified copy of the executed planning obligation with the appellant/applicant and the local planning authority at the Hearing or Inquiry. Any extension given should be the minimum length of time necessary so as not to affect PINS targets for determination of casework.

80. There is no requirement for you to chase for an executed/corrected obligation which has been provided but is incomplete or flawed; nor do you need to chase even where there is a firm indication that one of the parties intends to provide an obligation but it is missing If, however, the scheme is acceptable in all other respects, and this is the only obstacle to allow the appeal, then you may consider taking the following action:

### Written Representations:

The Planning Obligations PPG advises “The Inspector will not delay the issue of a decision to wait for an obligation to be executed unless there are very exceptional circumstances”.

For appeals under Part 1: if there is evidence that, during the application process, an Obligation was being negotiated, or the need for one agreed, you could ask the parties, via the Case Officer, if they intend to provide a completed version of the planning obligation. Regulation 8(1) allows you do this, and it is best to set a deadline and remind them that you will not delay issuing the decision to wait for a completed one.

[^16]: The Procedural Guide – Called-in planning applications – England applies to all applications which are ‘called-in’.

If, however, you have not seen any evidence indicating that an Obligation was in progress or discussion, then you should not follow this up and the need for one would become part of your determination.

For appeals under Part 2: you can consider writing to the parties, via the Case Officer, and seeking their views on the need for one. you can also set any deadlines you think reasonable for the submission of an executed Obligation, but you should also remind the parties that you will not delay issuing the decision to wait for a completed one

### Hearing and Inquiries:

If you realise before opening the event that, although no planning obligation has been provided, it is reasonably likely that one will be submitted, ask the Case Officer to write to the parties stating that any draft should be provided no later than 10 working days before the event opens (or whatever date is now feasible) and that it should be completed by the close of the event.

It is helpful to raise this as part of your Case Management Conference (for Inquiries) at set out expectations early. During your opening – check what progress has been made and for any key changes from previous drafts. Remind the parties that it should be finalised by the close of the event.

If there is a procedural problem – for example, the obligation has not been signed or dated or it contains obvious inconsistencies – it is reasonable to point this out to the parties. However, you should avoid commenting on the planning merits of an obligation.

If the obligation has not been completed by the end of the event – establish why. There may be good reasons. For example, if the wording of a draft is only finalised during the event, it can sometimes be difficult to secure the signatures of all relevant parties. If so, you might allow a short period for the document to be completed (for example, one week). However, you should stress that if it has not been provided by this time you will proceed to make your decision. You should confirm the timetable in writing through the Case Officer. Do not leave the deadline open-ended. It is prudent to seek the views of the parties at the event about what bearing the absence of a completed obligation would have on the outcome of the appeal.

81. You will need to decide if, in the interests of natural justice, whether any of the parties need to be given an opportunity to comment on an obligation, or a final version of it, which they have not previously seen. This is particularly likely to be the case where the LPA has not seen a unilateral undertaking. Always check to see if there are any material differences between a draft obligation and the final version.

82. Article 40(3)(b), of The Town and Country Planning (Development Management Procedure) (England) Order 2015, requires the LPA to publish all Obligations on its planning register. This is so members of the public have the opportunity to comment on the planning obligation before any planning permission is granted. A recent (April 2025) Judgment from the Court of

Appeal17 found that the grant of planning permission, without following the legislative procedure, was unlawful.

83. Whilst the case referred to had an active interested party involved who was concerned about the level of contribution being offered and had regularly been checking the Council’s website tracking updates on the planning obligation, Inspectors must be vigilant to any detailed/specific concerns raised by IPs to heads of terms and/or draft planning obligations and if there is, ensure the planning obligation has been made available for a short period (perhaps five working days) for public inspection to ensure natural justice(there is unlikely to be any need for a formal re-consultation or for this to be done in every case routinely). This can be communicated via the Case Officer to the LPA.

84. Once the deadline has passed, and if a completed obligation has not been provided, you can proceed to make your decision. If you consider an obligation was necessary, it is likely that you would dismiss the appeal, unless exceptionally the matter could be dealt with by means of a negatively worded condition (see the following section for advice). If this option has not been considered by the parties you may need to give them the chance to comment.
