# 37g Local Plan Examinations (NPPF 2024): Housing: Assessing and meeting the need for affordable housing

<!-- Part3 p.59 -->

## Assessing and meeting the need for affordable housing

### Calculating affordable housing need

247. Affordable housing is defined in the NPPF Glossary. The calculation of affordable housing need is explained in PPG “Housing and economic needs assessment” Paragraph 022 and PPG “Housing needs of different groups” Paragraph 007. The total net figure for affordable housing need is arrived at by subtracting total affordable housing stock from total gross need. The net figure is then annualised.

248. As part of this calculation, gross affordable housing need is estimated by assessing past trends and current estimates of household numbers within the categories of need listed in PPG “Housing needs of different groups”, Paragraph 006. Projections of affordable housing need should take in the range of socio-economic factors referred to in PPG “Housing and economic needs assessment”, Paragraph 21.

### Policies for affordable housing

249. Where a need for affordable housing is identified, planning policies should specify the type of affordable housing required. The mix should meet identified local needs across Social Rent, other affordable housing for rent and affordable home ownership tenures and policies should specify the minimum proportion of Social Rent homes required. Policies should expect affordable housing to be met on-site unless off-site provision or an appropriate financial contribution in lieu can be robustly justified and the agreed approach contributes to the objective of creating mixed and balanced communities. (NPPF 64 and 66).

250. To achieve this, plans normally include policies that require a percentage of any new major housing development to be provided in the form of affordable housing. Plans may also include affordable housing policies for specific site allocations. The percentage sought is often the same across the plan area, but in some plans certain site allocations or locations may be given a different affordable housing requirement to reflect characteristics, needs, aspirations and viability.

251. Other than in designated rural areas, the provision of affordable housing should not be sought for residential developments that are not major developments (see the NPPG Glossary definition). Planning authorities often try to include policies in their draft plans which seek contributions towards the provision of affordable housing from non-major developments on the grounds that their identified affordable housing need is high and the plan’s site allocations are mostly small. However, this is contrary to the NPPF’s stated policy. Other than for designated rural areas, the NPPF does not allow for any exceptions. (NPPF 65).

252. In designated rural areas (National Parks, National Landscapes and areas designated under s.157 of the Housing Act 1985) policies may set a lower threshold of 5 units or fewer. (NPPF 65).

253. Viability assessment is clearly critical to ensure that development is deliverable. Policy requirements, particularly for affordable housing, should be realistic and deliverable. The affordable housing requirement, including the percentages sought, should be supported by robust viability evidence. PPG ID: 10 provides detailed guidance on this subject. There are special policies for housing development on sites released from the Green Belt: see below.

### Affordable housing on sites released from the Green Belt by the plan

<!-- Part3 p.61 -->

254. Where a new or reviewed plan releases land from the Green Belt for major housing development, a specific affordable housing requirement or requirements should be set for the housing sites that have been released. Under the “Golden Rules”, these should be set higher than elsewhere, and a single rate or different rates can be used for different sites. At least 50% of the housing should be affordable unless it would make the development of the site unviable when tested against national planning practice guidance on viability. (NPPF 67 and 68).

255. Despite the latter statement, PPG “Viability” states that site specific viability assessment should not be undertaken or taken into account in respect of sites released from the Green Belt for the purpose of reducing developer contributions, including affordable housing. The government intends to review viability guidance to consider whether there are circumstances where site specific viability may be taken into account, but such a review has not yet been published. (PPG “Viability” Paragraph 029).

256. It follows that until the review has been completed, site specific viability assessment cannot be taken into account in setting the percentage of affordable housing on these sites. This may present a potential challenge for the authority in demonstrating deliverability, particularly in the case of strategic sites. Indeed, PPG “Viability” advises that it is important to consider the specific circumstances of strategic sites. (PPG “Viability” Paragraph 005).

257. However, the PPG also states that assessing the viability of plans does not require the individual testing of every site. A typology approach may be used. On this basis overall plan viability assessments, including typology approaches, could be used to establish the general viability context for the plan and this could inform the minimum level of affordable housing for sites released from the Green Belt. But this should still be set higher than for other sites. (PPG “Viability” Paragraphs 3 and 4).

258. The approach to addressing the particular housing needs of other groups will vary according to identified need. Plans should allocate sites for, or have positive policies towards, gypsies and travellers, student housing, housing for service families and sheltered housing for older or disabled people, accommodation for looked-after children and private rented housing. The needs of older people and families with children should be addressed by policies on housing mix. Policies could allocate sites for those wishing to build or commission their own homes, or require such provision on large allocated sites.

259. Plan-making authorities should consider how these needs can be addressed within the overall housing need that has been established. As with affordable housing (see below), it may be that the plan is unable meet all the identified needs in full, but all reasonable steps should be taken to meet them as far as is practicable. (PPG “Needs of different groups” Paragraph 1).

### Community-led development for housing

260. Plan policies should support proposals for community-led development for housing. These are developments taken forward by, or with, a not-for-profit organisation that is primarily for the purpose of meeting the needs of its members or the wider local community rather than being a primarily commercial enterprise. The NPPF Glossary contains further detail.

### First homes

261. First homes are a type of discounted market sale housing and meet the definition of affordable housing. They are discounted by a minimum of 30% against the market value and are subject to eligibility criteria. The discount is passed on in subsequent sales. (PPG “First Homes” Paragraph 001).

262. The former requirement to deliver a minimum of 25% of affordable housing as First Homes was removed in the December 2024 NPPF and no longer applies. However, delivery of First Homes can continue where local planning authorities judge that they meet local need.
