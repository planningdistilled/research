# 37g Local Plan Examinations (NPPF 2024): Housing: Housing in rural areas

<!-- Part3 p.68 -->

## Housing in rural areas

### Assessing rural housing need

303. Where a plan includes a rural area, the plan making authority will need to demonstrate an understanding of rural housing needs. This will help to inform policies that support housing developments reflecting those needs. (PPG “Housing needs of different groups” 67-009-20190722).

304. In rural areas, planning policies should be responsive to local circumstances and support housing developments that reflect local needs. (NPPF 82).

305. It follows that, in relevant areas, the authority should have clear evidence of the level and type of local housing need and should reflect this in the plan’s policies, including site allocations. (PPG “Housing needs of different groups” Paragraph 009).

<!-- Part3 p.69 -->

306. Housing should be located where it will enhance or maintain the vitality of rural communities. Planning policies should identify opportunities for villages to grow and thrive, especially where this will support local services. Where there are groups of smaller settlements, development in one village may support services in a village nearby. (NPPF 83).

307. A wide range of settlements can play a role in delivering sustainable development in rural areas, so blank policies restricting housing development in certain settlements will need to be supported by robust evidence. (PPG “Housing needs of different groups” Paragraph 009).

308. Neighbourhood plans can allocate housing sites, and where the local plan anticipates that some of the housing requirement is going to be met in this way, it should set targets for the number of homes that are expected to be delivered by each neighbourhood planning area, where relevant. (See paragraphs 177 to 179).

309. In designated rural areas, affordable housing may be sought on non-major sites.

### Rural exception sites

310. Policies should also support opportunities to bring forward rural exception sites that will provide affordable housing to meet identified local needs. These are small sites used for affordable housing in perpetuity where sites would not normally be used for housing. Rural exception sites seek to address the needs of the local community by accommodating households who are either current residents or have an existing family or employment connection. (NPPF 82 and NPPF Glossary).

311. A proportion of market homes may be allowed on the rural exception site at the local planning authority’s discretion, for example where essential to enable the delivery of affordable units without grant funding. This will need to be addressed in the policy.

312. The wording of any plan policy that concerns rural exception sites will need to make sure that it does not contain criteria that unnecessarily impede the ability to bring such sites forward. Location criteria should be avoided that would have the effect of restricting the rural exception sites to land that could be brought forward under other planning policies, rendering the policy ineffective.

<!-- Part3 p.70 -->

### Isolated homes in the countryside

313. Planning policies should seek to avoid the development of isolated homes in the countryside other than in the circumstances listed in NPPF 84. Plans should normally adhere to the wording of NPPF 84 and any proposed variation will need to be supported by clear and convincing local evidence and will be tested at the examination.

314. NPPF “Housing needs of different groups”, paragraph 010, provides a list of considerations that might be taken into account in assessing specific local needs for such housing.

315. The judgement of what is isolated is a matter for the decision maker, but the local plan may assist by providing a definition.
