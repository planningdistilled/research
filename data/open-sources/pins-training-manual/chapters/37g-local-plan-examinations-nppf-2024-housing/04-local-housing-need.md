# 37g Local Plan Examinations (NPPF 2024): Housing: Local housing need

<!-- Part3 p.20 -->

## Local housing need

19. NPPF 11 indicates that plans should promote a sustainable pattern of development with aligned growth and infrastructure. Strategic policies should as a minimum provide for objectively assessed needs for housing and other uses, as well as any needs that cannot be met within neighbouring areas (as established through statements of common ground), unless the exceptions listed in NPPF 11b apply.

### Calculating local housing need

<!-- Part3 p.21 -->

20. Local housing need is defined in the NPPF Glossary as the number of homes identified as being needed through the application of the standard method set out in national planning practice guidance. It is an unconstrained assessment of the minimum number of homes needed in an area. It is assessed separately from land availability, the housing requirement and the housing needs of different groups of people. (PPG “Housing and economic needs assessment”, Paragraph 001).

21. Local housing need should be calculated by means of a local housing need assessment using the standard method in PPG. This should be carried out by strategic plan-making authorities at the start of the plan-making process. (PPG “Housing and economic needs assessment” Paragraph 008).

22. The standard method uses as a baseline 0.8% of the authority’s housing stock, taken from the most recent published data, and applies an affordability adjustment factor based on median workplace-based affordability ratios. (NPPF 61, PPG “Housing and economic needs assessment” Paragraph 004 et seq).

23. The method identifies a minimum annual average housing need figure which can be applied to the whole plan period. It does not itself produce a housing requirement figure. (PPG “Housing and economic needs assessment” Paragraphs 2 and 12).

24. Previous NPPF content which referred to the standard method as an advisory starting point has been deleted from the December 2024 NPPF.

### The affordability adjustment, and past under and over delivery prior to the plan period

25. The affordability adjustment embedded within the calculation directs more homes to where they are most needed (PPG “Housing and economic needs assessment” Paragraph 6) and therefore automatically compensates for under-delivery against previous housing requirements prior to the date to which the affordability ratio relates.

26. It follows that there is no requirement to address under-delivery prior to the date to which the affordability ratio relates separately in the calculations of housing need or the housing requirement. (PPG “Housing and economic needs assessment” Paragraph 11). For the same reason it should not be necessary to take account of over-delivery against previous housing requirements prior to that date. The affordability adjustment should account for both under delivery and over delivery.

<!-- Part3 p.22 -->

27. The under-delivery or over-delivery of homes after the date to which the affordability ratio relates is a different matter and is addressed later: see paragraphs 86 to 92.

### Changes to, and the duration of, the housing need figure

28. New estimates of housing stock and updated affordability ratios are published annually, so the housing need figure calculated by the standard method may change between the beginning of the plan-making process and the submission of the plan for examination. The examination should normally be based on the most recent pre-examination standard-method LHN figure, but there may be exceptions: see below.

29. Once the plan is submitted for examination, the housing need figure calculated through the standard method can be relied on for plan making for 2 years from the date of submission. (PPG “Housing and economic needs assessment” Paragraph 008). This provides a stable 2 year basis for the examination of strategic-level local plans and for the production of any subsequent non-strategic plans, such as Part 2 site allocation plans. However, sometimes in strategic-level local plan examinations there might be justification for using a more recent post-submission figure, for example where there have been significant delays in the process combined with changes in the housing need figure since submission; this will depend on individual circumstances. Thus the figure may need to be re-based after the 2 year period, but the new figure should not be retrospectively applied to the plan period before the re-basing date.

30. It should be emphasised that it is not legally within the remit of Part 2 site allocation plans to set or revise the housing requirement, even if there have been substantial changes in housing need from the figures on which the Part 1 Plan is based – unless the plan is specifically charged to do so by an adopted strategic-level local plan. (See also paragraphs 64 to 74).

### Local need assessments covering more than one area

31. Where local needs assessments cover more than one area, such as where strategic policies are being prepared jointly, or where spatial development strategies are being prepared by elected mayors of combined authorities with strategic policy making powers, the housing need figure that is established for the defined area should be at least the sum of the local housing needs for each planning authority within the area. It is up to the strategic planning authority to distribute the total housing requirement. (PPG “Housing and economic needs assessment” Paragraph 13).

### Alternatives to the standard method

<!-- Part3 p.23 -->

32. The NPPF no longer refers to the use of alternative approaches to the standard method, but the PPG continues to recognise the possibility of locally determined methods for identifying housing need figures. There are certain circumstances where an alternative approach may have to be used, such as where strategic policy-making authorities do not align with local authority boundaries. Examples might be the National Park Authorities and the Broads Authority, and local authorities whose boundaries have changed in the last five years. Authorities proposing to use locally determined methods should provide evidence that their approach is consistent with other authorities in similar situations.

33. If an alternative approach is used, it will be tested at examination, giving consideration to whether the assessment provides the basis for a plan that is positively prepared, taking into account available information on existing levels of housing stock and housing affordability. (PPG “Housing and economic needs assessment” Paragraphs 14 and 15)

34. It is important to appreciate that the local housing need figure stands separately from other factors. Please note the definitions in paragraphs 12 to

18. It is not the same as the housing requirement, which is dealt with below.
