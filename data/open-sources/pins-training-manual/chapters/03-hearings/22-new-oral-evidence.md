# 03 Hearings: New oral evidence

<!-- Part1 p.158 -->

### New oral evidence

79. In addition to new written evidence, you may also be offered new oral evidence at a hearing. This may be uninvited or in response to a question they have asked. For example, a proposal for an agricultural worker’s dwelling where the farmer has not provided much information in writing but then proceeds to explain in detail how the farm works, what animals they have and why they need to live on site. Other examples might be when an appellant introduces personal circumstances at an event for the first time or the Council a new concern.

80. If the new information is material and of substance, then you will likely need to treat it as late evidence to ensure natural justice. You should therefore follow the same process as with written evidence. In this respect, you may need to intervene to explain that what you are hearing is new evidence and ask whether that party wishes to persist in delivering it. If they do, you should ascertain what they intend to say, why it is material and why it is not covered in the written submissions. You should get the other party’s view on whether you should hear the new oral evidence as they may have concerns with you doing so and/or may need an adjournment to reflect on what they have heard. You may need to warn that the late oral submission could result in an application for an award of costs. Sometimes the point may be minor and can be addressed by the parties present without much fuss. If the submission is likely to be lengthy you may need to ask for it to be made in writing for clarity.

81. As part of your inquisitorial burden, you may wish to ask the parties what weight you should give late verbal evidence, especially if it has not been independently verified and/or supported by documentary evidence and where the opposing party has not been able to test it.

82. Interested parties may also raise new points and you should ensure they are dealt with in accordance with natural justice.

83. If you accept late oral evidence you should advise about the possibility of a costs application being made – whether that is by one of the parties to the appeal or the Inspector. See the Costs ITM for more guidance.
