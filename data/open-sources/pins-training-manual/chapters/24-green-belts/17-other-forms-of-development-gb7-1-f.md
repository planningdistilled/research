# 24 Green Belts: Other forms of development - GB7(1)(f)

<!-- Part2 p.100 -->

## Other forms of development - GB7(1)(f)

94. Other forms of development are not inappropriate provided the impact on the openness of the Green Belt is minimised and there would not be a significant conflict with the Green Belt purposes. These are:

<!-- Part2 p.101 -->

i. Mineral extraction and processing14, engineering operations15, and transport, electricity network, water and telecommunications infrastructure required in a Green Belt location;

ii. Development brought forward under a Community Right to Build Order or Neighbourhood Development Order

iii. Material changes in the use of land (such as changes of use for outdoor sport or recreation, or for cemeteries and burial grounds); and

iv. The provision of appropriate facilities (in connection with the existing use of land or a change of use), including buildings, for outdoor sport, outdoor recreation, cemeteries and burial grounds and allotments.

95. Exception iv relates to facilities including buildings. It will be a matter of judgement as to whether a proposal comprises “facilities” but this might reasonably be expected to include artificial sports pitches and all-weather outdoor equestrian exercise areas. If not considered to be a facility, proposals for vehicular access, car parking areas and embankments may be engineering operations which would be considered under Exception i.

96. For Exception iv, the NPPF sets up 5 tests which must be satisfied before such a new facility can be regarded as not inappropriate. The facility must:

- be for outdoor sport, outdoor recreation, cemeteries, burial grounds or allotments

- be appropriate – this might be taken to mean appropriate for its intended purpose

- be provided in connection with the existing use of land or a change of use

- the impact on the openness of the Green Belt is minimised

- there would not be a significant conflict with the Green Belt purposes

97. In RB of Kingston upon Thames v SSLUHC [2023] EWHC 2055 (Admin) the judge confirmed that residential uses do not fall within exception iii relating to material change of use. Furthermore, that this is not an open-ended category but rather, because of the words “such as”, the uses that will be within it should take their flavour or extent from the examples given.
