# 07 High Court Challenges: Handling challenges within PINS

<!-- Part1 p.298 -->

## Handling challenges within PINS

18. GLD will send a copy of the claim form to PINS High Court team. They will notify the Inspector’s IM who will contact the Inspector to ask for their initial comments on the grounds of challenge. Inspectors will normally be given 5 working days to respond, due to the tight timescales of the challenge process. These are passed to GLD who will then provide advice on the merits of the challenge and appoint Counsel to advise further if required. On the basis of the GLD advice, plus any advice from Counsel and any further comments from the Inspector and their IM, we will then decide whether the challenge can be successfully resisted. We will instruct GLD accordingly and they will brief Counsel either to prepare the Summary Grounds of Defence (to be filed within 21 days of service of s288 claims) or to attend the oral permission hearing (in enforcement appeals). We will always resist a challenge unless there has clearly been an error in law that has substantially prejudiced one of the parties to the appeal.

19. A brief overview of the process is at Annex 1.

### The Inspector’s comments

20. You will be asked for your comments about a challenge as early as possible in the process. Whilst you may be occupied with other work, and the deadline will be tight (usually about 4 or 5 days), please do make time to fully consider the matter – your comments can play a very important role in how the challenge proceeds.

Some tips of good practice

21. When providing your initial comments try to be as prompt as possible, as litigation is costly, and the deadlines are also tight. Focus on the essence of the case that is being made by the party, however you must respond to all the grounds unless they are matters beyond your remit e.g. admin matters. If that is the case, make clear in your comments the points to which you are responding.

22. Particularly in cases where the challenge involves procedural matters, claims of unfairness, factual accuracy, or allegations of perceived or actual bias against the Inspector, your comments can be very helpful to GLD / Counsel in understanding the case. This is because in these circumstances the appeal decision letter may not tell the full story. You should point out any important areas where you disagree with the claimant and provide your version of events. Even if you find the claimant’s version to be broadly correct, it is still helpful to inform GLD / Counsel of that, so they understand the position clearly.

<!-- Part1 p.299 -->

23. It is also helpful to point out any key documents which are missing from the claimant’s analysis, or any other guidance which you consider applies (including PINS’ internal guidance).

24. If you have made a mistake, e.g. used a wrong measurement or direction, or policy, put your hands up, acknowledge the mistake and move on. Learn from it and try to make sure that you do not make the same mistake again. Where it is plain that an Inspector has gone seriously wrong, e.g., relied on an old policy, we will instruct GLD not to resist the challenge and submit to the judgment of the court, on the papers. This normally avoids the need for a formal court hearing and can save considerable costs. The decision will be quashed and the appeal returned to the Secretary of State for re-determination.

25. Try to approach the matter objectively. Your input at this stage is key to assisting GLD / Counsel to understand the case, and you can do this by being candid about matters of accuracy and fact. If we decide to defend the challenge, it is Counsel’s job to construct the defence not yours.

26. Avoid excessively long comments which go into great detail on the minutiae of the challenge. Your response should aim to clearly and concisely address the grounds of challenge, and any specific questions asked of you by GLD / Counsel.
