## Legislation and Guidance

- The Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended) – ‘the 1990 Act’

- The Town and Country Planning (Appeals) (Written Representations Procedure) (England) (Amendment and Saving Provisions) Regulations 2026 – ‘the 2026 Regs’

- Town and Country Planning (Appeals) (Written Representations Procedure) (England) Regulations 2009 (SI 2009/452), as amended – ‘the 2009 Regs’

- Town and Country Planning (Development Management Procedure) (England) Order 2015 (SI 2015/595), as amended – ‘the 2015 Order’

- Procedural Guide – Planning Appeals – England - Post 1st April 20261.

## Introduction

1. The Town and Country Planning (Appeals) (Written Representations Procedure) (England) Regulations 2009 (Statutory Instrument 2009/452) (as amended) provides for two different written representations procedures for appeals made under section 78(1) and 78(2) of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 (as amended) against a decision by a local planning authority to refuse a planning application, a failure to determine the application within the timeframe or against any conditions imposed with a grant of planning permission. These fall under Part 1 and Part 2.

2. The changes introduced by the 2026 Regulations will apply to appeals submitted under section 78(1) of the 1990 Act in relation to planning applications made on or after 1 April 2026. They also apply to appeals submitted under section 78(1) of the 1990 Act as modified by regulations made under section 220 of the 1990 Act in relation to applications for advertisement consent made on or after 1 April 2026.

3. Appeals relating to applications made before this date will continue under existing procedures. The Procedural Guide has been updated to reflect this.

4. All householder appeals, minor commercial appeals and applications for advertisement consent appeals submitted after the coming into force date will follow same Part 1 process as other written representations appeals. The only exception being the timescales for submission of these appeals which remain unchanged.

[^1]: The Procedural Guide – Planning appeals – England has been developed in two parts to account for the transition period. The original Guide (pre 31st March 2026) applies to planning appeals, householder development appeals, minor commercial appeals, listed building appeals, advertisement appeals and discontinuance notice appeals.

<!-- Part1 p.273 -->

5. This chapter will only cover appeals submitted under Part 1 for the time being, until a new chapter is developed.

6. It is expected that most written representation appeals will proceed under Part 1, which is an expedited procedure. The statutory instrument, the 2026 Regulations, amends the existing Part 1 process by expanding the scope of Part 1 to include all written representation appeals made under section 78(1) of the 1990 Act. However, the legislation also gives PINS the discretion to progress s78 appeals under Part 2, Hearing or Inquiry, where we deem it appropriate to do so.

7. The amended statutory instrument also limits what information can be submitted at the point of making the appeal via amendments to Article 37 (3) (a) of the 2015 Order.

8. Appeals under Part 1 are determined on the same basis as the original planning application. The appellant can submit limited reasons for appealing, but this should not add any new material or evidence, and the LPA will have the option to raise factual inaccuracies with those. The LPA will also provide the plans upon which it made its decision, along with all representations it received during the application process.

9. There is no opportunity for any interested parties to submit any representations during the appeal, but all representations submitted during the application phase will form part of the appeal, unless they are withdrawn. The LPA will cover this in their Notification Letter.

## The scope of Part 1 Appeals

10. The cases which fall within the scope of Part 1 are set out in the 2026 Regulations, the 2015 Order and in the Procedural Guide.

11. With few exceptions (see para 12/13 below) all s78(1) appeals will follow the Part 1 process in the first instance. These are appeals against:

- Refusal of planning permission;

- Grant of planning permission subject to conditions to which the applicant objects;

- Refusal of prior approval;

- Refusal of advertisement consent;

- Refusal of an application to approve a reserved matter;

- Refusal of an application to modify or remove a condition under section 73 of the 1990 Act;

<!-- Part1 p.274 -->

- Refusal of an application for planning permission for development already carried out under section 73A of the 1990 Act; and

- Refusal of Permission in Principle or Technical Details Consent.

12. The following section 78(1) appeals will not usually follow the Part 1 process after initial screening:

- Appeals proceeding as Hearings or Inquiries;

- Appeals against Biodiversity Net Gain Plans because of the requirements of article 37E of the 2015 Order; and

- Redetermination appeals. These may follow a different process so the relevant PfL will decide which process it will follow. It may be possible for the appeal to remain in Part 1.

13. Appeals that will not follow the Part 1 process because they are not made under section 78(1) of the 1990 act include:

- Appeals against non-determination (failure cases);

- Appeals made under directions given under section 77 of the 1990 Act (call-in cases);

- Appeals made under section 20 of the Planning (Listed Buildings and Conservation Areas) Act 1990 for listed building consent (Y appeals);

- Tree Preservation Order appeals, Hedgerow removal and High Hedge appeals;

- Appeals following the service of an enforcement notice (C appeals);

- Appeals relating to certificates of lawful development or use (X appeals); and

- Modification of section 106 agreements (Q appeals).

## The Part 1 appeal process

14. The appeal process is set out in the 2026 Regulations and, in summary, is as follows:

### Process | Timescale

Appeal made | Advertisement – within 8 weeks of the LPA decision Householder – within 12 weeks of LPA decisions Minor commercial – within 12 weeks of the LPA decision And all other appeal types within 6 months of the LPA decision

<!-- Part1 p.275 -->

Appellant’s Reasons for Appeal ‘Reasons for Appeal’ are not required but PINS has the power to ask for them within the appeal form.

PINS confirm appeal suitable As soon as practicable upon receipt of a valid appeal for Part 1 (the start date).

LPA provides questionnaire | Within 5 working days of the start date of the appeal and all documents and plans the LPA considered in their determination LPA tells interested people | Within 5 working days of the start date of the appeal about the appeal

Inspector visits the site | Between 2 and 6 weeks after the start date

Inspector makes decision | Decision issued as soon as practicable after the SV is complete.

### Appeal documents and evidence

15. All the appeal documents should be available on the Manage Appeals system but if you think a document is missing contact the Case Officer.

16. The Part 1 process is based on the assumption that a decision can reasonably be made on the basis of:

- The plans which were before the LPA when it made its decision – this includes any revisions made during the application process. If there is any doubt about the correct plans – ask the Case Officer to seek clarification from the parties;

- The LPA’s case as set out in the reasons for refusal and in any officer/committee report/minutes;

- The appellant’s reasons for appealing;

- Interested party representations made in connection with the application; and

- Any other relevant documents provided with the LPA questionnaire.

17. The Part 1 process does not allow any opportunity for:

- the LPA to comment on the appellant’s reasons for appealing, other than to set out factual errors therein, the assumption being that the LPA’s case should be clearly and adequately documented at the time their decision was made – even if the decision has been made contrary to the officer’s

<!-- Part1 p.276 -->

recommendation, whether through the Officer’s Report or a member overturn/elected members/planning committee;

- Interested parties to make any additional comments during the appeal process; or

- Amended plans or documents to be submitted.

18. However, Regulation 8 of the 2009 Regs does allow you to request further information relevant to the appeal. Regulation 8 allows that:

- ‘The Secretary of State may in writing require the appellant, local planning authority and other interested persons, to provide such further information relevant to the appeal as may be specified.’

19. The LPA will notify interested parties of the appeal and offer them the option, by writing to PINS, of withdrawing any representations made in response to the planning application.

### What must the appellant submit with the appeal?

20. A copy of the original application form, the appeal form (containing their reasons for appealing), and the LPA decision notice

21. The appellant’s reasons for appealing should provide a concise summary of why they believe the LPA’s decision should be overturned. It should not introduce new evidence, extra material, technical evidence or arguments not previously put to the LPA at the application stage.

22. Amended drawings and/ or documents will not be accepted and the appeal form does not allow for their submission. The appellant would need to reapply to the LPA.

### What must the LPA submit?

23. The LPA will be responsible for submitting all the documentation considered at application stage and a copy of all the relevant plans and reports it based its decision on Additionally, LPAs will be encouraged to set this information out within the decision notice so as to minimise disputes as to the relevant documentation.

24. If the decision is a member/committee overturn then the LPA should ensure that committee minutes are sufficiently detailed. You should rely on the committee minutes and the reasons for refusal as the LPA’s case.

25. The LPA can only respond to the Appellant’s reasons for appealing by identifying any factual errors in that documentation. It may also explain any change in circumstances that have occurred since the decision was made such as a new local plan, national policy changes, changes to the housing land supply position.

## Transferring cases out of Part 1 into Part 2

26. Regulation 9 of the 2009 Regs allows the Secretary of State, thus PINS, to determine that an appeal is not suitable for Part 1 and should be dealt with under Part 2. Cases will only be moved to Part 2 in exceptional circumstances and you must discuss this with your Manager in the first instance before a transfer decision is made.

27. Examples of cases that will not be suitable for Part 1 include where:

- The case falls outside the scope of Part 1 (see here);

- The case raises more complex issues that require the parties to make extensive representations or would require an oral event to enable a full exploration of the issues at hand; or

- The appeal should be linked with another related appeal, though not another s78; for example, an enforcement or listed building appeal.

28. A change in circumstances does not automatically justify a move to Part 2. In many cases, changes can be addressed by inviting comments under Regulation 8 of the 2009 Regs which allows that:

- ‘The Secretary of State may in writing require the appellant, local planning authority and other interested persons, to provide such further information relevant to the appeal as may be specified.’

29. Consequently, it may be appropriate to seek the views of the parties if the issue is a straightforward one and it would be reasonable to require comments to be made within a restricted time period (for example, 5 days) - this might include cases where it is necessary to seek clarification about a newly adopted development plan policy or to seek comments about the potential imposition of a non-standard condition. A template for the wording you must use is at Annex 1.

30. Case Officers will look to see if the parties have provided any evidence which might mean the case should be taken out of Part 1 or invalidated. However, responsibility rests with you, so you need to make sure the principles of natural justice are adhered to. You should not take into account evidence which any party (such as the appellant, LPA and/or neighbours) would not have been aware of and ought to have been given the opportunity to comment upon. This is most likely to involve technical evidence such as a biodiversity survey, traffic or flood report for example.

31. Ultimately, whether you are initially reviewing the case or preparing for the site visit, you should consider whether there might be any reasons that require the appeal to be transferred out of Part 1.

32. If it is agreed with your Manager that, exceptionally, a case should be taken out of Part 1 you should contact the Case Officer and Case Team Leader as soon as

<!-- Part1 p.278 -->

possible and provide some explanation for the letter to the parties. The case will need to be transferred from Part 1 to Part 2 and it will be necessary to restart the appeal as the appeal timetable will need to be revised to accommodate the appellant’s full statement of case, comments from interested parties, and the local planning authority’s statement.

### What happens if I need to undertake a HRA and need more information?

33. Part 1 does not remove any requirements or process that are required under the Habitats Regulations (2017) (as amended). Further guidance can be found in the ITM Natural Environment chapter and if you are unsure on seeking more information, this should be discussed with your Manager.

34. Under the 2009 Regs, Regulation 8 allows for an Inspector to require the appellant, LPA and other statutory or interested persons, to provide further information relevant to the appeal. This information must be provided in writing, and you should ensure you set clear deadlines to avoid the decision being delayed. A template for the wording you must use is at Annex 1.

35. When submitting a request under Regulation 8, Inspectors should also complete the Regulation 8 Feedback Form to support monitoring of consistency, effectiveness of the Part 1 process, and potential areas for external engagement to help reduce delays.

36. If you require further information under Regulation 8, you must consider whether the nature of the information sought interferes with the principles of natural justice and would, therefore, require it to be shared with interested parties for comment, and opportunities given to the appellant/LPA to comment on any responses. If this is the case, it may need to be transferred to Part 2.

### What happens if an Environmental Statement is required?

37. If an ES was submitted at application stage, and has been appropriately consulted upon already, the appeal can stay in Part 1.

38. The appeal would only need to move to Part 2, if it is subsequently deemed that an ES is required.

### What if I need further information to discharge the PSED or where Article 8 or other Human Rights may be engaged?

39. The appeal can stay in Part 1, if nature of the information is straight forward. It could move to Part 2 if it requires additional consultation and representations to be made or where the evidence needs to be tested or explored further at a Hearing or Inquiry

40. Regulation 8 allows for an Inspector to require the appellant, LPA and other statutory or interested persons, to provide further information relevant to the appeal. This information must be provided in writing, and you should ensure you

<!-- Part1 p.279 -->

set clear deadlines to avoid the decision being delayed. A template for the wording you must use is at Annex 1.

## Safeguarding

41. Are there any safeguarding issues? If you have any safeguarding concerns, advice is contained in the Approach to Decision Making chapter

## Site visits

42. There are two types of site visit in Part 1 casework and you should read the ITM chapter on Site Visits for full information but as a summary:

Unaccompanied site visits (USV)

43. A USV will be arranged where you can see everything you need to from a public area such as a road and so have no need to go on to the appeal site. None of the parties to the appeal will attend.

Access required site visits (ARSV)

44. An ARSV will be arranged where you need to go onto the appeal site. It is important that you respond quickly when you are asked for site visit times.

Viewing the appeal site from a neighbouring property

45. The questionnaire asks the LPA “Will the Inspector need to enter a neighbour's land or property?” and has a space for the details if they say yes. Whilst the LPA may consider this essential, it is your decision whether to visit or not. If you do need to visit a neighbouring property, it will be undertaken as ARSV and the Case Officer will notify the neighbour of the date and time of your site visit.

## Conditions

46. If the LPA suggests any non-standard conditions you should consider whether the appellant should be given the opportunity to comment on them. However, some conditions would be unlikely to come as a surprise and so you would not need to seek comments - for example, obscure glazing a bathroom window or the ‘plans condition’. Consultation with the appellant will be required for pre-commencement conditions in the usual way. See the Conditions ITM chapter.

## Legal Agreements and Late Evidence

47. The Procedural Guide requires, for written reps appeals, the submission of a completed and executed agreement at the point the appeal is submitted. The appeal form asks the appellant if an agreement is to be submitted and asks for a copy to be downloaded.

<!-- Part1 p.280 -->

48. However, if a s106 is submitted after this time, you have the discretion, via Regulation 8(3) to disregard it. Indeed, caselaw (Akhtar, 2017) confirmed that even a material planning consideration can be disregarded where procedural regulations and guidance have not been complied with. These considerations will also apply where late evidence has been submitted. The default will be that it is not accepted and must be returned.

49. There may be exceptions which you need to consider. For example, Heads of Terms, ongoing negotiations about an Obligation, or other such information is evidenced to have been before the LPA during application stage but the Obligation was simply not signed and executed until after the appeal submission. If this is the case, and it is not clear from the paperwork, then you should clarify the situation with the Council, via your Case Officer, and you may accept the completed s106.

50. The parties may ask for a draft to be accepted, and whilst this is standard practise in oral events, it is your decision whether to accept it or not. In general, for written reps appeals, if an Obligation is submitted with an appeal in order to address a reason for refusal, (therefore it was not before the Council at any point in the application process) it should be turned away.

51. Regardless of whether you do or not, you must make it clear that you will not delay the determination of the appeal in order to wait for a fully executed one. You will need to carefully consider if the appeal can continue to be dealt with under Part 1, utilising Regulation 8(1) or whether it should be transferred to Part 2 so that appropriate consultation or publicity can be given to it.

52. If you have decided to disregard an executed Obligation, you will need to give reasons for this as a procedural matter. Remember that executed Section 106 agreements, whether unilateral or bilateral, may be binding on the parties whether or not you accept it as part of the appeal. You should consider carefully how to approach this situation if you are allowing the appeal, and there is an executed Obligation before you. See the Planning Obligations ITM chapter for further details.

## Costs applications

53. The 2008 Planning Act permits costs applications to be sought and awarded in written representations cases.

54. The appellant’s claim for costs should be made at the same time as the appeal. The LPA has 14 days from the start date of the appeal to make a claim. The party against whom the costs application has been made will be given an opportunity to comment within a set timescale. National guidance on the award of costs is provided in the Appeals section of the government’s ‘Planning Practice Guidance’.

<!-- Part1 p.281 -->

55. It is usual practice, where possible, to issue the appeal and costs decisions at the same time. However, it can be acceptable to issue the appeal decision first, to be followed by the Costs one. See the Costs Awards ITM chapter.

## Submitting the decision

56. Your completed decision should be sent to the case team for the Case Officer in the normal way. However, it is helpful to identify that it is a ‘Part 1 Decision’ in the subject bar of your e-mail. This helps the team identify and prioritise such cases.

57. The LPA will notify interested parties that they have a right to withdraw any application representations made within 4 weeks of the date the LPA letter is sent. Consequently, when sending your decision to despatch, it is helpful to note when this 4 week period will have passed (if it has not already) as the reasoning in a decision should not rely on a representation that has been withdrawn.
