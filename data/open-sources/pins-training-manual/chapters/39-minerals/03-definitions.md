# 39 Minerals: Definitions

<!-- Part3 p.320 -->

## Definitions

5. In planning appeals relating to minerals and in the examination of local plans that contain policies on mineral development reference is likely to be made to a number of phrases or acronyms, the most common of which are set out below:

- Aggregate Minerals Survey - this is undertaken by the Government approximately every four years and covers regional and national sales, inter–regional flows of materials, transportation, consumption and permitted reserves of primary aggregates, to inform review and update of guidance. The latest Survey took place in 2020.

- Local Aggregate Assessments (LAAs) - these are assessments by the Mineral Planning Authority (MPA) of demand and supply for aggregates in its area.

<!-- Part3 p.321 -->

- Regional Aggregate Working Party (RAWP) - a group comprising local and national Government officers and industry stakeholders with the role to assess the amounts of aggregates each of its component MPAs should aim to provide in their local plans and to provide sales figures to inform their annual LAAs.

- Marine Dredged Aggregates – Usually sand and gravel that is dredged from licensed areas in coastal waters using purpose-built dredgers.

- Mineral Safeguarding Area (MSA) - An area designated by minerals planning authorities which covers known deposits of minerals which are desired to be kept safeguarded from unnecessary sterilisation by non-mineral development.

- Mineral Consultation Area (MCA) - a geographical area based on a Mineral Safeguarding Area, where the district or borough council should consult the Mineral Planning Authority for any proposals for non-minerals development.

- Mineral Resources are defined as the natural concentrations of minerals or bodies of rock in sufficient quantities to be of potential economic interest, subject to evaluation by drilling and testing.

- Mineral Reserves are defined as those parts of the resource which have been evaluated and deemed commercially viable.

- Permitted Mineral Reserves, in land-use planning terms, are evaluated mineral reserves which have legal access and valid planning permission for extraction.

- Primary Aggregates are formed of crushed rock or sand and gravel deposits extracted from locations where they naturally occur.

- Recycled aggregates are aggregates resulting from the processing of inorganic materials previously used in construction, for example construction and demolition waste.

- Secondary aggregates are aggregates from industrial wastes such as glass (cullet), incinerator bottom ash, coal-derived fly ash, railway ballast, fine ceramic waste (pitcher), and scrap tyres; and industrial and minerals by-products, notably waste from china clay, coal and slate extraction and spent foundry sand. They can also include hydraulically bound materials.

## Minerals Overview and Economic Importance

6. Minerals planning is essentially concerned with the winning, working and use of economic minerals for construction, manufacturing, agriculture and energy supply, as defined by the British Geological Survey (BGS) to include metals, rocks and solid and liquid hydrocarbons that are extracted from the earth by mining, quarrying or pumping.

<!-- Part3 p.322 -->

7. Energy minerals are used to produce electricity, fuel for transportation, heating for homes and offices and in the manufacture of plastics. Energy minerals include coal, oil, natural gas and uranium.

8. Metals have a wide variety of uses. For example, iron (as steel) is used in cars or for frames of buildings, copper is used in electrical wiring, and aluminium is used in aircraft and to make drink cans. Precious metals are used in jewellery and mobile phones.

9. Construction minerals include sand and gravel, brick clay and crushed rock aggregates. They are used in the manufacture of concrete, bricks and pipes and in building houses and roads.

10. Industrial minerals are non–metallic minerals used in a range of industrial applications including the manufacture of chemicals, glass, fertilisers and fillers in pharmaceuticals, plastics and paper. Industrial minerals include salt, clays, limestone, silica sand, phosphate rock, talc and mica.

11. Annual UK production of economic minerals is of the order of:

- 200 million tonnes of primary aggregates

- 50 million tonnes of crude oil

- 40 million tonnes of natural gas

As a measure of its importance, the UK minerals industry and the manufacture of mineral products accounts for some £7-800 billion annually. Aggregates account for approximately 85 per cent of the non–energy minerals extracted in the UK.
