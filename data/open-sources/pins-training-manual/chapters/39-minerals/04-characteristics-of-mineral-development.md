# 39 Minerals: Characteristics of Mineral Development

<!-- Part3 p.322 -->

## Characteristics of Mineral Development

12. Minerals can only be worked where they naturally occur, meaning that they need to be protected from non-mineral development in defined safeguarding areas. In order to supply minerals in areas where they are needed, there are high volumes of interregional flows of primary aggregates. This can have significant sustainability implications as transport of land won minerals is usually by both road and rail. Marine dredged aggregates are usually ‘off-loaded’ in dedicated wharves, particularly in the south-east.

13. Mineral working is a temporary use of land, although it takes place over a long period.

14. Mineral working may have both adverse and positive environmental effects, but some adverse effects can be effectively mitigated.

15. Extraction is usually a continuous process and therefore there is a requirement for routine monitoring of the secure compliance with conditions that are necessary to mitigate the impacts of operations.

<!-- Part3 p.323 -->

16. Following working, land should be restored to make it suitable for beneficial after-use.

17. The Mineral Planning Authorities (MPAs) are the main regulators of this activity, which is often controversial due to conflicts between economic, environmental and safety interests.
