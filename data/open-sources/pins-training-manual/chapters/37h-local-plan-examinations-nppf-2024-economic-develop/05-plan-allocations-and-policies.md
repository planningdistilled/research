# 37h Local Plan Examinations (NPPF 2024): Economic Development: Plan allocations and policies

<!-- Part3 p.91 -->

## Plan allocations and policies

### The spatial strategy

49. Planning for business and economic development should be an integral part of the plan’s spatial strategy and should take account of the location of housing growth and infrastructure provision. (See also “Local Plan Examinations: Housing”.)

50. Strategic policy-making authorities will need to consider the most appropriate locations for meeting identified needs, whether through the expansion of existing sites or development of new ones. (PPG “Housing and economic needs assessment”, paragraph 031) This is likely to require co-operation and co-ordination with other authorities.

51. Planning policies should recognise and address the specific locational requirements of different sectors, including (but not limited to) clusters of knowledge and data-driven industry, creative or high technology companies and supporting infrastructure; storage and distribution operations at a variety of scales in suitably accessible locations and the expansion or modernisation of other industries of local, regional or national importance. (NPPF 87) The strategy should seek to counter economic weaknesses and build on strengths, in accordance with national policy.

### Site allocations and development management policies

52. Site allocations will need to recognise that there are overlapping boundaries and functional links between different uses. Business activities may not fall neatly

<!-- Part3 p.92 -->

into specific classes and may include a range of activities. Thus although site allocation policies should be clearly written and unambiguous (NPPF 16) it may be necessary to use broad descriptions to allow for flexibility of operation. Unnecessary prescription and policy restrictions may hinder beneficial development and the attainment of national policy objectives. Such policies are likely to be ineffective.

53. The need for reasonable flexibility applies as much to development management policies as to site allocation policies. Plans should positively and proactively encourage sustainable economic growth and set criteria for local and inward investment to match the strategy. Overprescription in development management policies may act to obstruct the objectives of economic growth. This is the case for both inward investment and for existing businesses wishing to expand in the area, either on or beyond their existing sites.

54. Use Class E was created to allow for flexibility and encourage economic development. Some of Use Class E falls within the definition of main town centre uses but there are some activities within Use Class E that can help to sustain the viability and vitality of business areas, such as creches, gyms, cafes, takeaways and local shops. Care should be taken to ensure that the plan’s policies do not seek to frustrate the intentions behind the creation of Use Class E.

55. The NPPF no longer refers to live/work units, and the PPG does not mention them. Permitted development rights allow certain changes from business to residential use. In this context any proposed development management policies on live/work accommodation will be critically examined to see whether they are necessary.

### Offices and laboratories

56. Offices fall within Class E but come under the NPPF glossary definition of main town centre uses so, where there is an identified need for town centre type offices, suitable sites (if available) should be allocated in the town centre to meet the scale and type of development likely to be needed. If they are not available, then edge of centre sites may be allocated, and if sufficient edge of centre sites cannot be identified, policies should explain how identified needs can be met in other accessible locations (NPPF 90). This approach should be reflected in the plan’s policies and site allocations.

57. However, the plan should also recognise the many other circumstances that affect the location of office development. Office use may overlap with research and development facilities and laboratories, which are Use Class E but not normally main town centre uses. A non-town centre office location might be appropriate to make the best use of land, support a cluster of activities, provide employment space in an integrated mixed scheme; enable the growth of an established business, or develop or expand a science research facility.

58. The plan’s site allocation and development management policies will need to take these considerations into account. If any sites outside town centres are

<!-- Part3 p.93 -->

allocated for office development, policies should make it clear that the sequential and impact tests will not be applied to office development proposals there.

### Distribution facilities

59. Where appropriate the plan will need to contain policies for distribution, which plays a critical role in supporting other economic activity and in enhancing consumer choice. Strategic distribution facilities require significant amounts of land, good access to strategic transport networks, sufficient power capacity and access to appropriately skilled local labour. Where a need has been identified, plans should ensure that appropriate provision is made in the right places to meet identified need.
