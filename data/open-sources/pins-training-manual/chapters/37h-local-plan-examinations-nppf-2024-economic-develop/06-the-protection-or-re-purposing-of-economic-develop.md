# 37h Local Plan Examinations (NPPF 2024): Economic Development: The protection or re-purposing of economic development land and buildings

<!-- Part3 p.93 -->

## The protection or re-purposing of economic development land and buildings

60. The NPPF does not include a specific policy towards the protection of economic development land or buildings. It gives substantial weight to the value of using suitable brownfield land within settlements and it promotes and supports the development of under-utilised land and buildings, especially if this would help to meet identified needs for housing. (NPPF 125)

61. The NPPF also states that planning policies need to reflect changes in the demand for land. Where development does not come forward, planning authorities should reallocate the land for a more deliverable use that can help to address identified needs. (NPPF 127 and 128)

62. It follows that there is no overriding national policy requirement to protect employment and business land or buildings as a matter of principle, and local plans should not contain blanket policies of that sort without good reason.

63. It also follows that under-used industrial brownfield land and buildings, and employment allocations, that have not been brought forward for those purposes, should be re-evaluated by the local authority for allocation for a more deliverable use, especially for residential purposes.

64. On the other hand, in highly constrained areas such as those in densely developed areas or in areas with extensive environmental constraints, business and employment land might be in very limited supply and the evidence might justify a policy in which most existing employment and business land is protected from changes of use.

65. In other instances, the protection of some economic sectors might be justified if it is necessary to help achieve national or local goals to deliver a strong, responsive and competitive economy and support growth, innovation and improved productivity. For example, there may be particular sectors or clusters

<!-- Part3 p.94 -->

that are significant to the local or wider sub regional economy. If so, it might be appropriate for the plan to include policies designed to address the specific issues associated with those land uses.

66. The approach taken by each local plan should therefore depend on the circumstances and be justified by the evidence. Thus where a plan contains development management policies that apply to existing employment or business areas or buildings, they will be assessed to see whether they are justified and effective, having regard to national policy, their continued suitability for their existing uses, the overall need for floorspace and supply of new sites, and the need for other uses, especially residential accommodation.
