# 04 Inquiries: Controlling the pace of a long Inquiry

<!-- Part1 p.234 -->

### Controlling the pace of a long Inquiry

305. You need to set a reasonable pace, as Inquiries that go on for many weeks are tiring for all concerned. Especially for those who have to travel long distances.

306. You should ensure that you have adequate time for preparation; your programme immediately before the event should not include cases of significant size. All outstanding work should be completed before a long inquiry begins – otherwise timeliness will suffer and it will be hard to get back to an earlier case when you emerge from a long inquiry.

307. The Inquiry programme should be based on a programme running from Tuesday to Thursday and a half day on a Friday, or simply Tuesday to Thursday if your own or the parties’ travel distance is considerable.

308. Be realistic when estimating how long the various stages of the Inquiry will take and understand that there will be considerable amounts of work taking place ‘behind the scenes’ by the main parties, who may need time to address matters between themselves. You may also need time to catch up and/or prepare for subsequent sessions.

309. You may find that an adjournment of a week part way through a multi-week Inquiry is beneficial to allow you and the parties time to consolidate the outcomes of the previous weeks. This possibility should be flagged up at the CMC and incorporated into the programme - this is not wasted time and would

<!-- Part1 p.235 -->

be unlikely to delay the decision/report as you can be working on the first stage(s) of the inquiry in the gap.

310. Inspector Managers facing a long Inquiry should ask a fellow IM to deal with day-to-day queries from members of their groups.

### Joint Inquiries and non-planning cases

311. Some joint Inquiries are difficult to programme. An example is a joint Inquiry into an application for planning permission and a CPO relating to the same site, where the relevant procedural rules cannot be followed strictly.

312. When considering the programme of an Inquiry other than a s78 case, it should be remembered that the party that is asking the Secretary of State to do something should normally go first and end last. Thus, if the subject of the Inquiry is the confirmation of a CPO, the order making authority should go first.

313. If the Secretary of State is making a proposal, such as a modification or revocation order, their representative should make their statement first.

314. You should declare at the outset what variant of the Rules should be applied and ask the main parties to consent to it. The procedures customarily followed in Inquiries under the Highways Acts should not, however, be used in other types of case. If you are in doubt about the best and fairest procedure, first seek advice from your IM of IPL, and seek the parties agreement.
