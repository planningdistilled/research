# 04 Inquiries: Closing submissions

<!-- Part1 p.206 -->

### Closing submissions

101. Invite each party that called witnesses to make a closing submission. This will usually entail each advocate reading out a pre-prepared text, a copy of which will be provided to you and a copy sent electronically to the Case Officer.

102. Generally, it should not be necessary to interrupt a closing submission, but you should intervene if it appears that new evidence is being introduced, new points are being made or if anything is unclear.

103. You can ask the advocates at any point during the Inquiry to cover specific points in Closing. They may also state openly that they will leave certain points “to submissions” (code for Closings), usually if headway is not being made on something with a witness in cross-examination.

104. PINS have produced a guidance note for advocates as to the general form and contents of Closings for SoS cases. It is only directly relevant to those cases, but many of the principles are relevant to transferred cases SoS Casework.

<!-- Part1 p.207 -->

105. If the Inquiry lasts eight or more days, the closing submissions must be provided in writing (Rule 16(14)). That said, written submissions are nearly always provided in shorter Inquiries and are invariably helpful. You can request this at the start of the Inquiry, and/or at the CMC, and although you cannot strictly require them, it is now common practice.

106. Well-prepared closing statements can be very helpful when writing your decision as they will summarise the key points. Take careful notes if they are not submitted in writing, or if advocates depart from the script that they have given you. If a reference is made to a legal judgment, secure a full reference and get a copy of the judgment from the party that raised it.

107. It is important to arrange the programme so that the advocates have time, without having to work through the night, to prepare their closings in writing. It can also be helpful to hold closing submissions virtually (perhaps a few days after the final discussions), especially if the only reason to bring everyone back together after, say, a weekend adjournment is to hear the submissions. You can raise this during the CMC or during the event.

108. Sometimes, sequential closings in writing (with the LPA, then any R6 and finally the appellant) are suggested by the parties. Though not conventional practice this can save time – virtual closings can also help. Some Inspectors find that closings in writing are less helpful than in person or on Teams. Do not hesitate to say so if that is the case for you. There is also a risk that new points could be raised in the written versions, necessitating further exchanges between the parties.

109. If written closings are to be submitted, ensure that a submission timetable is agreed. You would then close the Inquiry in writing via the Case Officer, pending any applications for costs. You also need to ensure that the submissions are placed on the Inquiry website, for all interested parties, as soon as they become available to you.
