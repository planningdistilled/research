# 04 Inquiries: Call-in applications and recovered appeals

<!-- Part1 p.231 -->

### Call-in applications and recovered appeals

285. Under s77 of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 the Secretary of State may “call-in” planning applications for them to make the decision instead of the LPA. Inquiries into these applications are held under section 77. The Town and Country Planning (Inquiries Procedure) (England) Rules 2000 9SI 2000/1624) apply.

286. A call-in is enacted by a direction, which requires the planning application to be referred to the Secretary of State. The direction can only be given before the application is decided by the LPA, that is, before the decision notice has been issued. Call Ins normally relate to planning applications which raise issues of national significance.

287. Appeals are usually transferred to PINS for determination by an Inspector on behalf of the Secretary of State. However, the Secretary of State may also ‘recover’ the jurisdiction to decide an appeal (having regard to s79 of the Act). The appeal may be recovered at any stage before the decision is issued even after the Inquiry has taken place. The recovery letter will set out the reasons why the Secretary of State has recovered the decision.

288. In both cases, the Inspector will write a report to the Secretary of State which contains a recommendation on whether the application/appeal should be allowed or dismissed. The Secretary of State takes the report into account when making the decision. Further details are in the ITM chapter Secretary of State Casework

289. In both Call-In and Recovery cases the Secretary of State will have indicated those matters about which s/he particularly wishes to be informed. Especially in the former type of casework, this may be preliminary and will have been drafted before the submission of evidence. It is important that those aspects of the proposal which are not the subject of objection by the LPA are probed and reasoned for the benefit of the SoS – correspondence from interested parties may be a useful source of material for these matters. You should explain that you may be more inquisitorial than normal for this reason, but that this does not indicate bias. Additionally, you may allow more questioning from interested parties than would otherwise be the case. Each of the SoS’s matters, and other material considerations, need to be concluded upon.
