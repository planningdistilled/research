# 04 Inquiries: Rule 6 parties

<!-- Part1 p.214 -->

### Rule 6 parties

148. “Rule 6 status” allows interested parties to take an active part in the Inquiry, and they become main parties. However, it is not the case that for a group to participate in an Inquiry they have to seek Rule 6 status. It is unusual for Rule 6 status to be granted to individuals.

149. To have Rule 6 status, the third party must comply with the requirement to provide a Statement of Case within four weeks of being required to do so.

150. If it is a group/organisation making the request, they should provide information on whether or not they are incorporated (for example, a limited liability company), have a written constitution, officers, a membership list, hold regular meetings and have an address (for example, a registered address if a limited liability company). They will often be legally represented.

151. In most cases the Rule 6 party will prepare proofs of evidence and will take part in the same way as the Appellant and LPA. They will be invited to any Case Management Conference(s).

152. If there are multiple Rule 6 parties, it may be beneficial and more efficient to ask them to join forces, especially where they are presenting similar arguments. If they are unwilling to do so, you will need to manage the Rule 6 parties, before and during the Inquiry, to ensure that evidence is not repeated and time is not wasted.

153. Rule 6 parties have the same responsibilities as main parties and can be the subject of an award of costs for unreasonable behaviour.

154. You will need to adjust the running order to accommodate Rule 6 parties. If they are opposing the proposal any opening statement, witness evidence and closing submission from a Rule 6 party would usually follow those of the LPA.

155. Rule 6 parties can cross-examine the opposing party (usually the appellant), normally after the LPA advocate has done so. They may find that the LPA advocate has covered all their ground, and there is no need to repeat questions.

156. Further advice is provided in the ‘Guide to Rule 6 for interested parties involved in an Inquiry – planning appeals and called-in applications – England’.
