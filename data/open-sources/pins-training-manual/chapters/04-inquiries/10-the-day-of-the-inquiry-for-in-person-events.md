# 04 Inquiries: The day of the Inquiry for in-person events

<!-- Part1 p.196 -->

### The day of the Inquiry for in-person events

50. Aim to arrive at the venue around 45-60 minutes before the Inquiry opens. This will allow you to:

- ensure the room and venue are suitable in terms of accessibility, overall setup and health and safety (see Annex H). If the room is unsatisfactory or requires furniture to be moved, request changes. You should not make them yourself unless they are very minor in nature. If you consider the facilities to be wholly unacceptable you should report this to the Council at once.

- Public inquiries; Hearings and Examinations - venue and facilities requirements guidance provides advice on the location of the venue and the layout of the Inquiry room.

- Check that you have water and that it is available for other parties.

<!-- Part1 p.197 -->

- Familiarise yourself with any microphone and livestream system. Ensure that someone is monitoring the livestream so that you can be advised if it fails.

51. Once you have set out your belongings, it is best to leave the room, taking your notes with you. If anyone wants to engage you in conversation about the appeal, ask them to raise it once you have opened the Inquiry. You can, however, and may well need to, discuss matters relating to the Inquiry venue (such as the layout of the room) with those present – preferably someone responsible for the venue, not a witness.

52. Return to the Inquiry room a few minutes before the Inquiry starts. While you wait to open the Inquiry you can use the time to check that the main parties are present, that the room is still set up as you would like and whether there is anything upon which you need to act (e.g. placards or external noise).

53. You may wish to greet the representatives of the parties. There is no harm in being polite and friendly but do so equally.
