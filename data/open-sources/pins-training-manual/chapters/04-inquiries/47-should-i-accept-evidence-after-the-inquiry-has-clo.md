# 04 Inquiries: Should I accept evidence after the Inquiry has closed?

<!-- Part1 p.223 -->

### Should I accept evidence after the Inquiry has closed?

222. Rule 18(2) states that the Inspector may disregard any written representations, evidence or documents received after the close of the Inquiry. The intention of the Rule is to ensure that the parties provide evidence and documents, including s106 obligations, on time. There may be occasions, however, where you do need to accept late evidence.

223. For example, important matters may arise after the Inquiry has closed but before you have made your decision. This could include a change in national or adopted local planning policy or the issuing of a clearly relevant appeal decision. A failure to take these into account could leave a decision vulnerable to challenge in the High Court9.

224. Issues may be brought to your attention by one of the parties or they may be apparent to you for other reasons. In either case, if the issue is one which might reasonably have a material bearing on your decision, you should accept the evidence offered or proactively raise the issue.

225. Rule 18(3) states that if, after the close of an Inquiry, you propose to take new evidence into account which was not raised at the Inquiry you shall afford those entitled to appear at the Inquiry with an opportunity to make written representations or to ask for the re-opening of the Inquiry.

[^8]: Assuming that the action has caused unnecessary or wasted expense.

[^9]: In Wainhomes v SSCLG [2013] EWHC 597 the issue of five-year housing land supply was central. The Inspector declined to consider two recent appeal decisions. However, these decisions dealt with the same issues and might have caused the Inspector to reach a different conclusion. Consequently, the court found, they should have been considered.

<!-- Part1 p.224 -->

226. You can give your views on the most appropriate method of handling the matter, but the Inquiry must be re-opened if the LPA or Appellant ask for it (Rule 17(7)). Alternatively, you might (rarely) decide the Inquiry should be re-opened for your own reasons.
