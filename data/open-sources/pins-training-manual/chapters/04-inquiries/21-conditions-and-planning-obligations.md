# 04 Inquiries: Conditions and Planning Obligations

<!-- Part1 p.205 -->

### Conditions and Planning Obligations

92. Conditions are usually best dealt with after all the witnesses and any others present have been heard, and by means of an Inspector-led RTS involving the Appellant, the LPA, any Rule 6 party and any others (e.g. statutory consultees) who wish (or need) to be involved.

93. It is good practice to get the parties to submit a set of agreed (or partly agreed) conditions well before the inquiry. You can issue a Tracked Changed version of conditions, with your suggestions or comments, well before the RTS to aid the discussion.

94. You will need to consider whether the suggested conditions meet the tests in the Framework, even if they have been agreed by the main parties (N.B. you may have had conditions suggested during the Inquiry. Do not forget to cover them).

<!-- Part1 p.206 -->

95. See the Conditions section of the ITM for further advice.

96. Planning Obligations are also best considered by means of an Inspector-led RTS. You will need to assess whether each obligation complies with the three tests set out in the relevant section of the Framework (and CIL Regulation 122 if relevant) and whether it would be effective (see Section 18 of the planning appeals: procedural guide)

97. If an obligation (or lack of one) was central to a main issue, it may already have been discussed. Even so, you might have questions about its format, wording and effectiveness. Keep an eye out for ‘blue or grey pencil clauses’ which require you to certify or delete provisions in the s106. In the case of grey pencil clauses you may be asked to pick a figure dealing with, for example, education contributions. This sort of clause requires you to do the calculations and justify them and should be avoided unless strictly necessary.

98. Inspectors should be vigilant to ensure that interested parties who are concerned with the terms of any obligation have an opportunity to inspect the draft and make comments thereon, so as to ensure natural justice. There is unlikely to be a need for a formal reconsultation or for this to be done routinely.

99. You should request a CIL Compliance Statement at the CMC, to be prepared by the LPA. This should contain a detailed justification for each obligation sought, setting out how it complies with the CIL Regulations and the Framework, how any financial sums have been reached and should include reference to any policy support for the obligations. It should be submitted no less than two weeks before the inquiry opens.

100. See the Planning Obligations chapter of the ITM for more advice.
