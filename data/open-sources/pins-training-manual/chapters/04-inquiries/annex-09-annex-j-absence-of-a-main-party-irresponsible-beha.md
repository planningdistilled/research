# 04 Inquiries: Annex J - Absence of a main party (irresponsible behaviour)

<!-- Part1 p.264 -->

### Annex J - Absence of a main party (irresponsible behaviour)

1 Section 79(6A) of the Town and Country Planning Act 1990 gives the Secretary of State the authority, after issuing a warning notice, to dismiss an appeal if the appellant is responsible for undue delay in its progress. It is intended that this power should only be used where an appellant/agent refuses to co-operate in processing the appeal or obstructs that process. Consult your IM or a PfL is this is in your mind.

2 “Undue delay” is most likely to occur due to the appellant or their representative’s failure to attend arranged site visits or events without providing a satisfactory reason but it also applies when the appellant does not complete necessary procedural steps (such as clarifying which conditions are being appealed or undertaking any statutory notifications) which prevents the Inspector from progressing with the determination of the appeal

3 If you have reason to believe that the appellant is behaving irresponsibly and has caused/is causing an undue delay in the process, the case of the LPA and those of any other parties present at an Inquiry may still be heard, along with any applications for costs. Where it is possible to make an unaccompanied site visit the Inquiry should be closed. On such a visit particular care should be taken not to get into conversation with any person near or at the site or to trespass on private property.

4 If it is clear from your pre-Inquiry site visit that an accompanied site visit is necessary, and it has not been possible to carry one out during the course of the Inquiry, agree a time and date with the LPA and any other parties who wish to be present at the site visit. The date should be 2 to 3 weeks ahead to allow time for contact to be made with the absent party. The Inquiry should then be closed.

5 You should contact the Case Officer with the relevant site visit details and inform them that you wish to invoke the process under section 79(6A). The Case Officer will write to the parties telling them of the date and time that you will attend to visit the site. The letter will also draw the absent party’s attention to s79(6A) of the Town and County Planning Act 1990 as amended by s18 of the Planning and Compensation Act 1991. This applies to all appeals made under s78 (and s174), regardless of procedure or site visit type, and states:

“If at any time before or during the determination of such an appeal it appears to the Secretary of State that the appellant is responsible for undue delay in the progress of the appeal, he may

a) give the appellant notice that the appeal will be dismissed unless the appellant takes, within the period specified in the notice, steps as are specified in the notice for the expedition of the appeal; and

<!-- Part1 p.265 -->

b) if the appellant fails to take those steps within that period, dismiss the appeal accordingly.

6 If the Appellant fails to turn up to the subsequent site visit or fails to allow the Inspector onto the site, the visit should be aborted and the Case Officer notified so that the appropriate letters can be issued dismissing the appeal. In that event, any correspondence about costs that pre-dates the site visit should be considered by the Costs Team.

7 The Case Officer has access to the suite of letters needed to complete the process before the appeal can be finally dismissed. The Inspector may need to provide some wording for the letters on a case-by-case basis.

8 This also applies to enforcement appeal and similar cases. In such cases, you will first determine whether the Appellant has acted responsibly or irresponsibly. After closing the Inquiry, if it is necessary to enter the site, arrange to meet the parties at the site and see if the Appellant or anyone else with authority to allow entry is there and will let you in (although s324 of the1990 Act does provide for rights of entry).

9 If you and other parties are let in the site visit can take place. If not, abort the visit, inform the Case Officer, who will write to the Appellant inviting further representations on the issue and highlighting any costs application. You will then determine the appeal based on the evidence before you.

10 If the Appellant has not allowed entry to check vital measurements, they may have failed to satisfy you, on the balance of probability, that their own asserted measurements (if any) are correct and accordingly has failed to discharge the onus of proof which is on them to demonstrate that the development is lawful and the appeal may be dismissed with or without costs.

11 Where an application for costs is made you will prepare a report for Cost Team who will complete the process.
