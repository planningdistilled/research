# 04 Inquiries: Annex K: Managing Disruptive Parties

<!-- Part1 p.266 -->

### Annex K: Managing Disruptive Parties

As a responsible employer PINS has a duty of care to its staff. Our Customer Charter states that we expect all staff to be treated with courtesy and respect and warns that we will not tolerate rude or abusive behaviour. All staff are entitled to carry out their duties without fear of abuse or harassment.

The purpose of this note is to advise on the steps to follow when action is required.

### Powers

1. Rule 16 (9) of the Town and Country Planning (Inquiries Procedure) (England) Rules 200013 and Rule 11 (8) of the Town and Country Planning (Hearings Procedure) (England) Rules 200014 empower Inspectors to require participants at Hearings and Inquiries to leave if they are being disruptive15.

2. The Inspector may refuse to allow the person who has been asked to leave to return or permit a return only on such conditions that the Inspector may specify. Rule 15 (11) and Rule 11 (10) allow the Inspector to proceed in the absence of any person entitled to appear at the event.

### What is unreasonable/unacceptable behaviour?

3. Unreasonable/unacceptable behaviour is anything which disrupts the smooth running of the Inquiry, prevents you from focusing on the arguments or prevents any party from making their case. This could range from threats or shows of aggression to constant low-level interruptions. This may exhibit itself as bullying or belittling a witness or an opposing barrister.

4. Filming and recording of events should be allowed. If, however, you consider the way this is happening is intimidating you should ask that it stops. If the person recording refuses, this constitutes unreasonable behaviour.

### What to do about unreasonable/unacceptable behaviour?

5. Your training will have equipped you to deal with most situations. In short, if a party’s behaviour becomes disruptive, explain:

i. why their behaviour is unreasonable and that if they continue you will adjourn to give them time to calm down/reflect. If necessary/appropriate you could set conditions for their return (see Rules 15 and 11 above). Explain that if you are forced to adjourn because of their unreasonable behaviour you have the power to instigate an award of costs against them.

ii. that if they continue to behave unreasonably you will invoke your powers under Rule 15 (11) 16 or Rule 11 (10) and have them removed.

iii. that if they are removed, they may submit any evidence or other matter in writing before the close of the Inquiry.

6. All the above needs to be properly documented in order that any subsequent complaint or challenge may be addressed. If a party refuses to leave, adjourn and request that the Council’s security accompanies the disruptive person from the premises. If that is not possible, or in the event of serious disruptive behaviour or threat activate, your lone worker protection alarm (ORBIS) or call 99917.
