# 04 Inquiries: What if the LPA no longer intends to defend a reason for refusal or withdraws its entire case?

<!-- Part1 p.220 -->

### What if the LPA no longer intends to defend a reason for refusal or withdraws its entire case?

196. The LPA may announce at the start of the Inquiry, or before it, that they no longer intend to defend a particular reason for refusal or withdraw their opposition completely. This may often occur in cases where the refusal has been against officer advice and the withdrawal is on the basis of subsequent legal advice..

197. Even though this may have been made clear in writing, it is helpful to ask the LPA to explain the reasons for their position, particularly if other people are attending the Inquiry. This could be in the form of a short opening statement. It is important to ascertain if the Council considers that the proposal is still in conflict with the development plan but other considerations outweigh this, or if the proposal is now considered to comply with the development plan.

198. The LPA may state that it no longer intends to present any evidence on certain matters. Or at all. Even so, if you have questions or if there are interested persons who continue to oppose the proposal on these grounds, you may request that an LPA witness is made available to answer questions.

199. If the issue is a technical one (such as traffic or noise) it can be useful to hold a session where the expert witnesses for the LPA and Appellant share the witness table and answer any questions from you and other parties in turn. In any case the LPA would still be expected to take part in the discussions about conditions and obligations.

200. The Appellant may still want to present their evidence-in-chief on the subject, particularly if there are third-party objections. Similarly, they may wish to ask questions of interested persons opposing the proposal. You should allow this.

201. If the LPA declines to present evidence on a topic or as a whole they should not be allowed to cross-examine the Appellant. The evidence of the Appellant will be un-tested, therefore, except by any questions that you or interested persons raise.

202. It is becoming increasingly common for an LPA to withdraw its entire case before the Inquiry. This is often in cases where officers have recommended approval, but Members have refused permission. Close to the Inquiry they reverse their position.

<!-- Part1 p.221 -->

203. The question at that point is how best to proceed with the appeal. It is at the Inspector’s discretion whether to continue by way of written representations. (bearing in mind that a position statement explaining the LPA’s position would be needed and the public notified), proceed by way of a Hearing, or continue with a (truncated) Inquiry. Experience shows that the latter is often the most efficient if the change in the LPA stance is relatively close to the opening date.

204. This approach enables interested parties to put their case, for the Inspector to ask questions of witnesses to clarify matters, and for the LPA to set out its position. The detail of such a short Inquiry will vary in each case, but little more than half a day is usually needed to ventilate outstanding issues and get the answers to the Inspector’s questions. If this is an option you are considering seek advice from your IM or PfL on the running order and procedure.
