# 04 Inquiries: Opening the Inquiry

<!-- Part1 p.199 -->

### Opening the Inquiry

62. Open the Inquiry at the appointed time. Your opening should be delivered in a confident and purposeful manner. Look up and avoid undue reference to your notes. Your opening sets the scene for your control and running of the inquiry.

63. The essential items to cover in your opening remarks are set out below and an example opening script is set out in Annex I. This is not prescriptive and you can vary the order to suit your own style and the case, provided that you cover the essentials: The Annex also includes an optional section aimed at deflecting potential bad behaviour – use and adapt this if you feel there is potential for this to arise.

- Preliminary matters.

- Appearances: for the benefit of others present, ask the advocates in turn to give their names, who is instructing them, and the witnesses they intend to call. It is helpful to ask the parties for a list in Word.

- Then ask if anyone else who is present wishes to speak. If anyone is speaking for an organisation, ask them to give their position and their authority to speak/give evidence for the organisation.

Record the names, and any qualifications, of anyone who wishes to speak and remind them that their details will be listed in your decision (N.B. if someone asks to speak on a later day, or decides to speak at a later point, you should accommodate them if possible. But remember that you will need to record their details too at the time).

There should be no attendance list as this is no longer a requirement.

- Filming and recording (please see separate section below).

- Case management conference (content and purpose)

- Notification letters Check that there are two notifications – the first notifying of the appeal, the second giving details of the inquiry. If the second letter is missing, and there are no members of the public present, this could be a reason for adjournment.

- Representations. Stress that you will consider those made at application and appeal stages

- Statement of Common Ground and proofs of evidence. Refer to the documents received and check that all main parties have them.

<!-- Part1 p.200 -->

- Procedural matters and running order: explain the overall order of the Inquiry and its format (such as opening statements, the process(es) by which the main issues are being dealt with (formal presentation of evidence and/or round-table sessions), the without prejudice discussion on conditions and any planning obligations, closing submissions, costs and site visit (if required).

- It may be necessary to stress the formality of the occasion and the need for politeness from all parties. Annex F may assist.

- You should seek the assistance of the advocates to ensure that the programme runs smoothly. Set out when, roughly, you intend to break in the morning, for lunch and in the afternoon, and when you intend to start and finish each day. Make sure that you include a decent period (an hour ideally) for lunch – for your benefit and others.

- Make it clear that you will be intervening if the timetable is not adhered to.

- Include adjustments to arrangements to accommodate for Virtual/Blended/Hybrid Events as necessary.

- Plans: see The Approach to Decision Making ITM Chapter for more information on amended plans.

- Late evidence/documents (if there are any): see advice below. Further guidance can be found in ‘Approach to Decision-Making’ ITM Chapter.

- Main issues and any other matters: Rule 16(2) states that, at the start of the Inquiry, the Inspector shall identify what are, in their opinion, the main issues to be considered and any matters on which further explanation is required.

- The main issues will have been agreed at the CMC. However, matters may have changed since then – for example due to further discussions between the parties. You should read them out if there are other persons present. Note that Rule 16(3) also allows other people who wish to speak to refer to any issue they consider relevant.

- Use of AI. If you have any reason to suspect any party – main party or otherwise, has used AI in preparation of their evidence, you should seek confirmation and refer them to PINS Guidance AI external guidance – which requires a declaration.

- Commence: start with the opening statements.

<!-- Part1 p.201 -->

64. Many of these matters will have been covered in the CMC but you may need to explain them for the benefit of other persons who are present. If there is no-one present apart from those who took part in the CMC, your opening can be much briefer, just covering the preliminary matters and any outstanding matters that need to be resolved.
