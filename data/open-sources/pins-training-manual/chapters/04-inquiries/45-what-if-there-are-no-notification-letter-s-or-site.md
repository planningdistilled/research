# 04 Inquiries: What if there are no notification letter(s) or site notice?

<!-- Part1 p.221 -->

### What if there are no notification letter(s) or site notice?

205. There should be two notification letters (Rules 4(4)(b) and 10(5)). The first notifying relevant parties about the lodging of the appeal and the second about the Inquiry arrangements. A site notice should also have been posted or a press notice published.

206. Check that the copies of the letters and site notice you receive from the LPA are correctly dated, relate to the correct appeal and venue and have been sent to the right people. It is important that you request a copy of both letters, if they are not already on the file, at least two weeks before you open the Inquiry to ensure they have been sent. This could avoid abortive delays.

207. If the correct notification has not taken place you will need to decide whether to postpone/adjourn the Inquiry to address this deficiency. You will only need to do this if you consider that there is a significant risk that the interests of any person or party could be prejudiced. Were they, for example, deprived of the opportunity to attend or to respond to evidence? Seek the views of the parties, as necessary. Be pragmatic but try to keep the inquiry on course.
