# 04 Inquiries: Officers who disagree with their local authority

<!-- Part1 p.215 -->

### Officers who disagree with their local authority

162. Elected members do not always accept the advice of their professional officers and, consequently, some decisions are made against the officers’ recommendation. This is perfectly legitimate and reflects the democratic process, of which Planning Committee decisions are a part.

[^5]: The term statutory party is defined in Rule 2(1).

<!-- Part1 p.216 -->

163. In these circumstances, it is for the LPA to decide whether to call officers as witnesses. Section 15 of the Procedural Guide: Planning Appeals explains the duty of an expert to help the Inspector. Expert evidence is evidence that is given by a person who is professionally qualified to express an opinion on a particular subject. This duty overrides any duty the expert may have to the party that involved them in the appeal or that is paying them (this applies equally to appellant witnesses). The evidence should be accurate, concise, and complete and should represent the expert’s honest and objective opinion.

164. If the officer who gave the original advice does represent the LPA, therefore, it would be reasonable for the opposing advocate to ask the officer questions about their own professional views and any advice they provided to the LPA.

165. In most such cases the LPA will employ a consultant or use a different officer or an elected member to give evidence instead.
