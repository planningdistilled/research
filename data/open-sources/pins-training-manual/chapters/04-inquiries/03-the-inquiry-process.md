# 04 Inquiries: The Inquiry process

<!-- Part1 p.188 -->

### The Inquiry process

7. The Inquiry process is set out in the Planning appeals: procedural guide, which applies to most Inquiries determined by Inspectors in England. Slightly different processes may apply to appeals determined by the Secretary of State, enforcement appeals and other type of Inquiry – however the basic principles – focussed on the Franks Principles – will be the same.

8. Most Inquiries have been, conventionally, conducted in person. This has changed with advances in virtual meeting technology (such as Teams), meaning that in-person events, virtual or hybrid events may all be undertaken.

9. It is at the Inspector’s discretion how the event will be held, having taken views from the main parties as necessary. It could be held entirely virtually or as a hybrid event e.g. with parts taking place virtually (such as discussions on conditions or obligations, and closing submissions). The Inspector may also deal with some elements of an appeal which is proceeding by way of an Inquiry as written submissions.

10. When you are appointed to conduct an Inquiry, you will need to provide the Case Officer with the dates you want to sit, along with the Case Management Conference (CMC) date and time, in accordance with the Inquiry timetable.
