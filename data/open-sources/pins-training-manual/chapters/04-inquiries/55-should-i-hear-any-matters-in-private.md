# 04 Inquiries: Should I hear any matters in private?

<!-- Part1 p.226 -->

### Should I hear any matters in private?

246. Section 321 of the 1990 Act requires that Inquiries are held in public, subject to very limited exceptions related to national security. Oral evidence shall be heard in public and documentary evidence shall be open to public inspection.

247. In such cases the Secretary of State can direct that the hearing of specific evidence be in private. If this suggestion arises, seek guidance from your IM or a PfL.

248. Commercial confidentiality or the privacy of individuals is not a sufficient justification for an in-camera session. Such requests must be rejected.

249. However it sometimes occurs that a party indicates that there is a personal matter which they need to discuss in private, as it could have an effect on how the inquiry progresses.

250. Inspectors must not have conversations with a single party, but it is important that you respond sensitively to this sort of occurrence. It might be that a witness (or an advocate) is unwell or has a personal issue (such as a bereavement) which they naturally do not want discussed in an open forum.

251. Under these circumstances you should invite the advocates for both parties (and any Rule 6 party) - perhaps with the person concerned if appropriate – to

<!-- Part1 p.227 -->

discuss the matter in your retiring room. It is impossible to be proscriptive about how you might resolve the issue – you will have to balance the need to progress the inquiry with the need to be sensitive to the particular issue.

252. When you resume the inquiry you should announce that you have a conversation with representatives of the main parties about a particular personal issue which has arisen. You have not been hearing evidence. You should not give any further explanation but announce any effect on the inquiry (especially related to timetabling).

253. All the above applies equally if you have a personal issue which requires a discussion with the parties.
