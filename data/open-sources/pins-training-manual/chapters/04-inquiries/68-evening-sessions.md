# 04 Inquiries: Evening sessions

<!-- Part1 p.233 -->

### Evening sessions

300. Evening sessions may occasionally be necessary when there are a high number of interested parties who wish to speak, and/or if there are a high number of potential attendees who are not free during the day. Virtual participation may reduce the need for/length of evening sessions.

<!-- Part1 p.234 -->

301. You should seek the agreement of the main parties well in advance, to ensure that they are able to accommodate the evening session, given potential domestic commitments or logistical issues.

302. Evening sessions can be beneficial in facilitating public access to the event, but must not result in an excessively long day for anyone – including the Inspector. The main session should adjourn by early/mid afternoon, to enable everyone to take a decent break.

303. An evening session needs to be arranged and controlled carefully. You must make it clear that it is part of the Inquiry and not a public meeting. All speakers must observe the normal Inquiry rules, addressing you rather than the public at large and not repeating points already made (although be mindful that speakers may be inexperienced and be nervous). All attendees should be respectful, refraining from commenting, clapping or cheering.

304. If possible, the Programme Officer or an LPA representative should collect in advance a list of those wishing to speak, together with a brief outline of the points they wish to make. You should hear first from those who have requested to speak, before asking if any other attendees wish to speak.
