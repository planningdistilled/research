### Annex E: Assessors at Inquiries

### The status of an Assessor

1. An Assessor (as defined under Rule 2(1)) is a specialist adviser, usually scientific or technical, selected to assist you by hearing, testing and weighing evidence of a specialised nature that may be outside your normal experience, but which may have a bearing on the issues to be decided.

2. Assessors are appointed by the Secretary of State for a particular Inquiry and should hold a letter to that effect in case their status is challenged. In planning cases the Assessor's name and qualifications will be notified to the parties together with the matters on which the Inspector is to be advised. An Assessor may be a suitably qualified / experienced Planning Inspector or a third party.

3. It is important that Assessors should not have had any previous connection with the appeal proposal nor any professional association nor connection with the parties. Where the number of experts in the relevant field is so small that these conditions cannot be met it is essential that a statement of the position is made at the beginning of the Inquiry.

4. It is also important that the Assessor should not have taken a public stance on the policies or matters at issue in the Inquiry.

5. If Assessors realise, after accepting the appointment, that they have had some previous connection with the case or the parties, or if any other situation arises in which they might find their position gives rise to a perception of bias, they should immediately advise the Inspector.

6. Once an appointment as an Assessor has been offered there should be no private communication by them with the parties or with any interested person. If the Assessor considers that further information should be obtained from any of the parties before the Inquiry, they should, after discussion with the Inspector, ask the Case Officer to obtain it.

### Function of an Assessor

7. The Assessor's task is to evaluate the specialist evidence within their field that is presented at the Inquiry and to indicate the weight which it should, in their opinion, be given in your conclusions. It is the Assessor’s responsibility to ensure that, as far as possible, all relevant evidence within their specialised field is considered.

### Before the Inquiry

8. Assessors are sent copies of the Inquiry papers as soon as possible after accepting the appointment. They are also notified of the name of the

<!-- Part1 p.250 -->

Inspector. It is important that you and the Assessor discuss the case at an early stage. It will be very useful for the Assessor to attend the CMC.

9. Matters which might be discussed before the CMC or the Inquiry include:

- the precise boundaries of the Assessor's specialist interest in relation to the subject matter of the Inquiry and the extent to which they will be involved in asking questions at the event itself;

- the definition of issues and topics on which evidence will be needed; the adequacy of the specialist evidence coming forward; whether further information should be obtained from the parties; whether it appears that a witness does not intend to consider a key document (for example a published technical report) known to the Assessor; and whether there are any serious inconsistencies which the parties could be asked to clear up before the Inquiry. Such matters may form the basis for advice to the parties;

- points of procedure on which the Assessor requires clarification, including points arising from this advice;

- the programming of the Inquiry with reference to the specialist content and whether it is necessary for the Assessor to attend all sessions;

- how the Assessor sees the specialist issues standing at the beginning of the Inquiry and the aspects which need to be pursued.

10. The full statements of case, SOCGs and Proofs should be sent to the Assessor. The Assessor must arrange adequate preparation time before the Inquiry so that points of clarification and follow-up questioning can be identified. Inquiry

### Accompanied site visit (if necessary)

11. The assessor should normally attend the site visit. The Assessor and the Inspector should keep close together throughout the visit, because if something is pointed out to one, the other should also be aware of it.

### The report or decision

12. The Assessor will give advice to the Inspector on the specialist issues and will collaborate in the production of the report or decision. It is for the Inspector to ascertain the facts, however, and to reach their own conclusions.

<!-- Part1 p.251 -->

13. Where the specialist issues are complicated or difficult, the Assessor may assist you by preparing draft findings on those issues and any conclusions to be drawn from them which you may adopt. It must be clearly understood that they become your findings and conclusions, and you must accept full responsibility for them. Any draft conclusions of the Assessor's should derive from what has been seen and heard at the Inquiry.

14. Assessors' conclusions will be arrived at in the light of their specialist knowledge and experience and a background of generally accepted data on such matters can be assumed. The Assessor should not, however, take into account any new or controversial technical material which has not been considered at the Inquiry.

15. In many cases, all that will be necessary is for you to state at the end of your conclusions, "The Assessor, [Mr/Ms/Mx] .......... agrees with my conclusions in paragraphs ......" provided that is so.

16. Alternatively, if it is felt that the Assessor's contribution should be more clearly identified, it should be possible to frame the report in such a way that the specialist advice can be introduced in appropriate places by the phrase "I am advised by the Assessor that ....".

17. In cases where there has been a great deal of argument and where the decision turns on specialist issues, it may be appropriate for the Assessor to produce a written report to you. In a Secretary of State case, this is appended to your own report and you state how far it is accepted. In a standard case, it is not normally appended to the decision but a reference to its existence is made and it is made available for inspection.

18. If a report is produced, it must bear the Assessor's signature. It should carry the appropriate file reference and be headed by the appropriate brief title, such as "Compulsory Purchase Order ......" and the suffix "Assessor's Report".
