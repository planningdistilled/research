# 04 Inquiries: Should I allow filming and recording?

<!-- Part1 p.227 -->

### Should I allow filming and recording?

254. The presumption is that filming and recording will be allowed. You should ask in your opening if anyone intends to film or record the event. If so, check that everyone is comfortable with this (some may not, for example, wish to have their faces shown or voice recorded).

255. If there are concerns, you can ask that filming/recording is restricted to certain angles. It is never appropriate to film children or vulnerable adults even if no objections are raised. If filming/recording does take place, ask that it is carried out responsibly.

256. Inquiries may be live streamed (and therefore often automatically recorded). The LPA should normally arrange for the livestreaming, as they will usually be providing the venue (but there is no reason why the Appellant cannot do so). The livestreaming should generally be arranged either prior to or at the CMC, but increasing numbers of people or specific requests for livestreaming may change the position. If the Inquiry is to be livestreamed (and recorded), you should advise participants of this and ensure that the information is included in the Council’s notification letter to local residents if it is known at that stage

257. In circumstances where there are factors which outweigh the public interest in allowing the Inquiry to be filmed or recorded, it may be necessary for you to prevent or restrict it. If the venue has restrictions on filming in place, then confirmation will be needed before the Inquiry that those restrictions can be lifted.

258. If PINS receives a request to film or record beforehand, the Press Office or the Case Officer will inform you that this is being proposed.
