# 04 Inquiries: Dealing with issues on which the parties agree but you may not

<!-- Part1 p.224 -->

### Dealing with issues on which the parties agree but you may not

232. It is important to make sure that everyone has the chance to consider and comment on evidence upon which you intend to rely for your decision. Consequently, all potentially important issues should be identified and discussed at the Inquiry – even if the main parties have reached agreement.

233. This includes issues raised by the parties but also those raised by you. This is particularly important if you consider that your decision could turn upon matters that may already have been agreed by the main parties (for example, in a SoCG, about which you have concerns, or upon matters that they have not considered10.

234. If you have questions about matters in agreement between the parties or matters that neither has addressed in evidence, you should make this clear to them as early as possible (ideally at the CMC but before the Inquiry opens at
