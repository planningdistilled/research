# 33 Human Rights and Equality: Gender Reassignment

<!-- Part2 p.492 -->

### Gender Reassignment

225. In Goodwin v UK [2002] ECHR 588, the Strasbourg Court held that the claimant’s rights under Articles 8 and 12 (right to marriage) were breached by the UK Government’s failure to make provision for ‘transsexuals’ to obtain legal recognition of their gender reassignment.

226. That breach was remedied by the passing of the Gender Recognition Act 2004 (GRA04) which provides that a person of either gender who is aged at least 18 may make an application for a gender recognition certificate (GRC) on the basis of (a) living in the other gender, or (b) having changed gender under the law of a country or territory outside the UK. While the GRA04 remains in force and holding a GRC confers certain rights in law, a GRC is not required for a person to have the protected characteristic of gender reassignment for the purposes of the EA10 and PSED.

227. S7(1) of the EA10 provides that a person has the protected characteristic of gender reassignment if they are proposing to undergo, are undergoing or have undergone a process (or part of a process) for the purpose of reassigning their sex by changing physiological or other attributes of sex. The process need not involve surgery or medication; it may be limited – given the reference to ‘other attributes’ – to changing name, pronouns and/or appearance.

228. The Employment Tribunal held in Taylor v Jaguar Land Rover Ltd: 1304471/2018 that, on a proper interpretation of s7, 'gender reassignment need never be a medical process’. The claimant had the protected characteristic of gender reassignment although they identified as gender fluid and expressly had no plans for surgical transition. It follows that a trans person does not need to

undergo or have undergone any surgical or medical procedure in order to have the protected characteristic for the purposes of the PSED.

229. S7(2) describes persons with the protected characteristic of ‘gender reassignment’ as ‘transexual’. Where it is not necessary to quote the EA10, the respectful term is ‘transgender’ or ‘trans’ as set out in Annex A, although people may also identify as non-binary54 or gender questioning/queer/fluid.

230. Under s16(2), for the purposes of work, a trans person is discriminated against if they are absent because of gender reassignment and treated less favourably by their employer than if they were absent for sickness, injury or other reason where it would be unreasonable to treat the person less favourably. In this context, under s16(3), the person must be proposing to undergo, be undergoing or have undergone the process (or part of the process) described in s7(1). In other words, absence due to gender reassignment should not be interpreted as limited to absence for surgical or medical transition.

231. The Census 2021, for the first time, included a voluntary question: ‘is the gender you identify with the same as your sex registered at birth?’ 262,000 people, representing 0.5% of the population aged 16+ in England and Wales, answered ‘no’ to the question. 93.5% answered ‘yes’ and 6.0% did not answer. Of those who did not identify with their sex registered at birth, 118,000 gave no further information, 48,000 identified as a trans man, the same number identified as a trans woman, 30,000 identified as non-binary and 18,000 gave a different gender identity.
