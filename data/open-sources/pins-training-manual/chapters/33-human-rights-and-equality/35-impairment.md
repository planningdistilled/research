# 33 Human Rights and Equality: ‘Impairment’

<!-- Part2 p.489 -->

### ‘Impairment’

207. Government guidance (the Guidance on Matters) on matters to be taken into account by a Court or Tribunal, if a challenge to a decision or a discrimination claim raises questions relating to the definition of disability, states that:

- The term ‘impairment’ should be given its ordinary meaning49.

- It is not necessary for the cause of the impairment to be established or the impairment to be the result of illness.

- Impairments may be fluctuating, recurring or progressive.

- It may not be possible or necessary to categorise an impairment as strictly physical or mental.

- The impairment may not be readily identifiable at all50.

49 ‘Impairment’ means ‘the state or fact of being impaired, especially in a specified faculty’ – OED.

50 The colloquial term is ‘invisible disability’.

<!-- Part2 p.490 -->

208. It is not vital or necessarily helpful for a person to have a formal diagnosis when establishing whether they are disabled for the purposes of the PSED51. For some conditions, there are long waiting lists to get a diagnosis which may not even result in medical treatment. A diagnosis shows the cause of an impairment, but the key questions are how a person is impaired (what their symptoms are), how the impairment affects normal day-to-day activities, and whether the effects are substantial and long-term.

209. For example, if it is claimed that a house extension is needed for a relative with pulmonary fibrosis, the Inspector may gain little useful information from medical records. The crucial details would be that the person is impaired by severe ‘long-term’ breathlessness and fatigue so that they need a separate bedroom with a hospital bed and oxygen equipment, and there is no space for that in the dwelling as it stands. A person with a different diagnosis could have the same requirements, while a person with the same diagnosis might not.

210. Similarly, if a participant asks that a hearing is adjourned because of their anxiety, evidence of a diagnosis would not aid the decision as to accept or refuse the request. The questions to ask are why anxiety would prevent the person from attending the event on the scheduled date, whether things would realistically be different on another date, and whether steps less drastic than adjournment could overcome the problem. It might be that the person experiences panic when speaking in public but could prepare a written statement for a friend or the Inspector to read out instead.
