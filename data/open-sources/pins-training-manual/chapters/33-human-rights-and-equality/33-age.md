# 33 Human Rights and Equality: Age

<!-- Part2 p.488 -->

### Age

198. S5 of the EA10 provides that a reference to a person with the protected characteristic of age is a reference to a person of a particular ‘age group’; persons who share that protected characteristic are in the same age group. A reference to an age group is a reference to a group of persons defined by reference to age, whether that be a particular age or a range of ages.

199. As the Commission explains, age groups can be narrow or wide; for example, ‘people in their mid-30s’ and ‘people under 65’ are both age groups. The parties’ terminology may be quite informal, and references to ‘young people’ or ‘the elderly’, say, can be enough to show that the protected characteristic of age is engaged.

200. The results of the Census of England and Wales 2021 (Census 2021) revealed that ‘the trend of population ageing has continued’. One sixth or 18.6% of the population were aged 65+ years in 2021, up from 16.4 in 2011. The numbers of people aged less than 15 years, or being in the 15-64 age range, had risen absolutely but fallen as a proportion of the overall population.

201. S13(2) of the EA10 allows for age discrimination – positive or negative – as a proportionate means of achieving a legitimate aim. The Commission describes circumstances where being treated differently due to age is lawful, such as “a service provider…making age-related concessions and benefits”. The provision of housing for a particular age group would normally fit into that category.

202. The EA10 itself also describes circumstances where organisations may provide different treatments in employment or services based on age. S84 of the EA10 provides that the protected characteristic of age is not applied to s85-89 so that schools may discriminate on the basis of age in respect of matters such as the way they provide education for a pupil.
