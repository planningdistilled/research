# 33 Human Rights and Equality: Unlawfulness and Intentional Unauthorised Development

<!-- Part2 p.472 -->

### Unlawfulness and Intentional Unauthorised Development

103. In any casework concerning residential development that has taken place and is being lived in, the Inspector should address the likelihood of a refusal of permission resulting in the occupiers being made homeless, as may well occur if the home is unlawful.

104. It was held in Chapman v UK [2001] ECHR 43 that:

‘If the home was lawfully established, this factor would self-evidently be something which would weigh against the legitimacy of requiring the individual to move. Conversely, if the establishment of a home in a particular place was unlawful, the position of the individual…is less strong. The Court will be slow to grant protection to those who, in conscious defiance of the prohibitions of the law, establish a home on an environmentally protected site…to do otherwise would be to encourage illegal action to the detriment of the protection of the environmental rights of other people…’

105. Chapman should not be read as meaning that Article 8 rights are not engaged if the building or residential use is unlawful. Unlawfulness is not a point in favour of an appeal, but it equally need not count against development, given that s73A and s174 of the TCPA90 provide for grants of retrospective planning permission26. Article 8 rights may be engaged even in final injunction proceedings if a person continues to occupy an unlawful home after an enforcement appeal has been dismissed27. And children cannot be held to blame for any breach of planning control made by their parents or carers28.

106. In accordance with NPPF Policy DM8, if it is concluded based on evidence that unauthorised development was intentional, that fact should be given substantial weight in considering whether to grant planning permission. Even then, however, whatever weight the Inspector attaches to a finding of IUD should not influence the weight attached to any other considerations, including those such as an appellant’s need for a home which might weigh in favour of the appeal.
