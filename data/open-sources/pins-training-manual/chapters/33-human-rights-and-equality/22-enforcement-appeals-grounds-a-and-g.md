# 33 Human Rights and Equality: Enforcement Appeals: Grounds (a) and (g)

<!-- Part2 p.477 -->

### Enforcement Appeals: Grounds (a) and (g)

132. In Quarry Mews Ltd v SSHCLG & Oxford CC [2025] EWHC 1968 (Admin), an enforcement notice concerned with the erection of 6 x 1 bedroom dwellings required demolition of the same. One of the dwellings was occupied by a family with one child. The Inspector dismissed the appeal on ground (a) without giving express consideration to the effect of a refusal of planning permission on the occupiers with regard to the best interests of the child. However, the Inspector did take such account when deciding to extend the period for compliance with the notice pursuant to ground (g).

133. HH Judge Jarman KC, sitting as a judge of the High Court, held that the Inspector did not fail to have regard to the need to safeguard and promote the welfare of children or to comply with the PSED37. He stated that:

“In my judgment it is highly unlikely that the inspector had proper regard when deciding whether to extend time but did not do so when deciding whether or not to grant planning permission. The context in which he was making both parts of the decision includes those matters set out in DL70 but also that the dwellings were all one-bedroom dwellings and not family dwellings. In my judgment,

134. Nonetheless, Inspectors are advised that, where a decision to grant or refuse planning permission would engage Article 8 (or other human) rights, including the best interests of the child, given the effects of or need for the development and/or the consequences of upholding the notice, that issue should be addressed as part of the reasoning and conclusion on ground (a).

135. The principle that decision letters should be read fairly and as a whole does not alter the fact that grounds (a) and (g) raise different questions for the decision-maker. Whether it is reasonable to give a family more time to look for somewhere else to live does not answer whether planning permission should be granted so as to avoid them being made homeless at all.

136. Even if the parties’ evidence bearing on human rights is only set out in relation to ground (g), Inspectors should consider whether it is relevant to ground (a), and vice versa. It may be necessary to take account, even if briefly, of effects on private and family life and home in deciding either or both grounds.
