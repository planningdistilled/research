# 33 Human Rights and Equality: Other Policy and Guidance

<!-- Part2 p.461 -->

### Other Policy and Guidance

69. The National Networks National Policy Statement (DoT, 2024) reminds applicants that the Secretary of State must have [due] regard to the PSED when exercising their functions, and that, for example, all reasonable opportunities to deliver improvements to accessibility on and to the existing national road network should be taken, including for improvements for non-motorised users. Applicants should also seek to deliver improvements that reduce community severance while national network infrastructure should incorporate good design which includes delivering accessible infrastructure.

70. Other National Planning Policy Statements do not directly address human rights or the PSED but are clear that matters such as human health and quality of life may be relevant in National Infrastructure casework – and that applicants should set out information on the likely environmental, social and economic

71. Guidance on the Compulsory Purchase Process (MHCLG, 2026) states that ‘when making and confirming a compulsory purchase order, acquiring…and confirming authorities should be sure that the purposes for which the…order is made justify interfering with the human rights of those with an interest in the land affected. The Guidance also states that ‘all public sector acquiring authorities are bound by the’ PSED and ‘throughout the compulsory purchase process…must have due regard to’ the three aims.

72. The statement of reasons should include the acquiring authority’s justification for the compulsory purchase with regard to A1FP and Article 8 if appropriate, meaning in the case of a dwelling. It should also include a statement in relation to the PSED and how that has been taken into account in making the compulsory purchase order.

73. However, the Guidance also states that ‘the confirming authority…has to be able to take a balanced view between the intentions of the acquiring authority and the concerns of those with an interest in the land…and the wider public interest’. The confirming authority must, given the statement of reasons and all other evidence before them, reach their own conclusions as to whether the order should be confirmed, given their own assessment of any human rights and/or equality implications of the order.

74. Authorising structures (gaps, gates and stiles) on rights of way: Good practice guidance for local authorities on compliance with the Equality Act 2010 (Defra 2010) aims to ensure that authorities discharge the PSED when providing furniture such as gates and stiles on public rights of way. The British Standard 5709:2018 gives further advice on this topic but, as the Public Rights of Way ITM describes, public path order casework may give rise to wider accessibility and equality issues and/or engage the rights of residents under Article 8 or landowners under Article 1 of the First Protocol.
