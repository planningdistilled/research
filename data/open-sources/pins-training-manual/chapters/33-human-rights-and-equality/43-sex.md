# 33 Human Rights and Equality: Sex

<!-- Part2 p.496 -->

### Sex

252. ‘Sex’ has long been recognised as having a different meaning to ‘gender’, although there is interaction. The World Health Organisation explains that ‘gender refers to the characteristics of women, men, girls and boys that are socially constructed…sex relates to the different biological and physiological characteristics of females, males and intersex persons…gender identity relates to a person’s deeply felt, internal and individual experience of gender’.

253. S11(a) of the EA10 provides that a reference to a person with the protected characteristic of ‘sex’ is a reference to a man or woman. However, those terms are simply defined under s212 as a man of any age and a woman of any age. The Supreme Court considered the meaning of ‘sex’, ‘man’ and ‘woman’ for the purposes of the EA10 in For Women Scotland Ltd v The Scottish Ministers [2025] UKSC 16. It was held that the terms mean biological and not certificated sex. A person with a GRC in the female gender does not come within the definition of ‘woman’ for the purposes of sex discrimination in s11 of the EA10.

254. The Supreme Court took a person’s biology as simply their sex [recorded] at birth; the judgment does not address variation in the biological attributes that

<!-- Part2 p.497 -->

comprise sex or in how those attributes are expressed57. The Court recognised that the GRA04 ‘provides a framework for recognising a person's reassigned gender’ for Article 8 purposes, but the judgment left open the question of how to read s11 of the EA10 in a way which is compatible with the Convention rights, so far as possible, as required under s3 of the HRA98.

255. The Supreme Court stressed that their conclusion should not be taken as removing or diminishing the important protections available under the EA 2010 for trans people. The Commission’s Code of Practice for Services, Public functions and Associations states when providing a separate or single-sex service, a service provider (including a person providing a service in the exercise of public functions) must be able to demonstrate that doing so is a proportionate means of achieving a legitimate aim.

256. S13(6)(a) of the EA10 provides that less favourable treatment of a woman includes any because she is breast-feeding. Under s13(6)(b), no account is to be taken of special treatment afforded to a woman for pregnancy or childbirth when considering whether there is less favourable treatment of a man.

257. The 2021 Census showed that women and men respectively make up 51% and 49% of the population of England and Wales, the figures being similar to those from 201158.
