# 33 Human Rights and Equality: Overall Conclusion – Human Rights raised but no interference

<!-- Part2 p.512 -->

### Overall Conclusion – Human Rights raised but no interference

Representations were made to the effect that the appellant’s human rights under Article 1 of the First Protocol, as set out in the Human Rights Act 1998, would be violated if the appeal is dismissed. Since I have decided to allow the appeal and grant full planning permission for the proposed development, there will be no interference with the appellant’s right to peaceful enjoyment of their possessions.

Overall Conclusion – Human Rights raised but limited interference.

Representations were made to the effect that the rights of [the adjoining occupier] under Article 8 as set out in the Human Rights Act 1998 would be violated if the appeal were allowed. I have found that the proposed development would not result in the neighbouring property being overlooked so that [the occupier] would suffer unacceptable harm to their living conditions. The development would not conflict with Local Plan Policy Z or guidance in the SPD. I am satisfied that a grant of planning permission would not unacceptably interfere with [the occupier’s] right to a private and family life and home. It is proportionate in the circumstances to allow the appeal.
