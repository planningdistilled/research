# 33 Human Rights and Equality: The Data Protection Act 2018

<!-- Part2 p.457 -->

### The Data Protection Act 2018

42. The Data Protection Act 2018 (DPA18) enacts the UK General Data Protection Regulation (UK GDPR) and thereby prohibits, subject to exemptions, the processing of “special categories of personal data”, meaning personal data which reveals:

The racial or ethnic origin, political opinions, religious or philosophical beliefs or trade union membership [of an identifiable living individual]; genetic or biometric data for the purpose of uniquely identifying a natural person; or data concerning a natural person’s sex life or sexual orientation7.

43. Data relating to health should be treated as subject to Article 9(1) even if it is not strictly ‘genetic or biometric’. Some personal data is more sensitive by nature, such as a child’s name, address or school. Some persons with protected characteristics may suffer greater adverse impacts from their personal data being disseminated or published. An Inspector should do so only if and where they have identified a substantial public interest.

44. Human rights and/or the PSED may be engaged in cases (partly) because personal circumstances have been raised. Even then, however, it is rarely necessary to disseminate [unredacted] personal data or to describe it in any detail in the decision letter, as set out further in Annex 5 to the Approach to Decision-Making ITM.
