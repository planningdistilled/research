# 37i Local Plan Examinations (NPPF 2024): Retail and Main Town Centre Uses: Retail needs assessments

<!-- Part3 p.99 -->

### Retail needs assessments

12. Advice on gathering the evidence needed to plan for all kinds of business needs, including main town centre uses, appears in the PPG on Plan-making ,and advice on assessing the need for employment land (including land for offices) can be found in the PPG on Housing and economic needs assessment2. That advice, however, is fairly generic. In practice, most retail needs assessments have tended to follow the methodology in Appendix B to the cancelled Practice Guidance on Need, Impact and the Sequential Approach (December 2009).

13. Reference is made here to that document, which has been cancelled and does not represent Government policy, solely to enable Inspectors to understand the methodology underlying evidence that may be presented to them.

14. As with any assessment of future needs, the outputs from a retail needs assessment are sensitive to the assumptions and variables that the assessment contains. Inspectors should therefore sense-check the key inputs, which are likely to include the definition of the study area, the adequacy of the household surveys, “benchmark” turnovers, and productivity and market share

<!-- Part3 p.100 -->

assumptions. If any of these are disputed by other informed representors, it is likely they will need to be discussed at a hearing session.

15. The retail needs assessment should be up-to-date. If it is not, the Inspector should consider whether it is necessary for the LPA to commission an update. The Procedure Guide advises that assessments that are two or more years old when the plan is submitted are at risk of being overtaken by events. However, it may often be possible for the LPA to rectify this by means of an update report rather than a full review.
