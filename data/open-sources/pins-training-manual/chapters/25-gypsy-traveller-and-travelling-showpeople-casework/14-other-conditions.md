# 25 Gypsy, Traveller and Travelling Showpeople Casework: Other Conditions

<!-- Part2 p.166 -->

## Other Conditions

257. PPTS paragraph 28 expects LPAs – and, by extension, Inspectors – to consider how they could overcome planning objections to particular proposal using planning conditions or obligations. However, this chapter does not duplicate advice in the Conditions ITM.

258. The PINS suite of suggested conditions includes model conditions for Traveller sites but, as in any other decision, the starting point should be the conditions suggested by the parties, and reasons should be given for imposing (or not) any condition.

259. In most cases, permission is granted for Traveller sites based on the special accommodation needs of Travellers. A condition should thus limit occupancy to Gypsies and Travellers (or Travelling Showpeople) as defined in PPTS 2024 or any subsequent replacement guidance. This condition may be imposed alongside a temporary or personal condition, with care taken to not exclude any relevant occupiers, including dependents, who have never travelled.

260. Since the grant of permission will generally be for the use of the land as a residential caravan site, it is usually necessary to impose a condition specifying the maximum number of caravans overall or the maximum number of pitches together with maximum number of caravans per pitch. The condition may also need to specify the types of caravan allowed; typically there will be a minimum of one static caravan and one touring caravan per pitch.

261. Since caravans may be moved by definition, a condition should be imposed to control the position of caravans within the site if that is necessary, perhaps for visual reasons. The condition should either tie the permission to the approved plans or require that details of the site layout are submitted to the Council for approval and then implemented as approved. Site licensing regulations require minimum distances between caravans for reasons of fire safety.

262. In accordance with PPTS paragraph 28b), however, another condition could specify a number of days per year when the site can be occupied by more than the allowed number of caravans, to allow for visitors or attendance at family or community events.

263. Amenity or toilet blocks, and day or utility rooms may also be required for site licensing reasons, as well as to meet the appellant’s own needs. If these are needed but not shown on submitted plans, a condition may again need to be imposed which requires the submission and approval of details.

264. Other matters which often need to be controlled by condition for Traveller sites, including through the submission of further details include:

- Hard and/or soft landscaping

- Boundary treatments

<!-- Part2 p.167 -->

- External illumination

- The means of access into the site

- The layout and surfacing of parking and turning areas

- Foul and surface water drainage, including sustainable drainage.

265. Travellers are less likely than in the past to need space for business activities and, if planning permission is sought strictly for residential use, bear in mind that conditions restricting business activities would not normally be considered reasonable or necessary in respect of any other residential development. The Council has powers in any event to enforce against any future unauthorised material change of use to put the site into commercial or mixed residential and commercial use.

266. However, where it is proposed to create a mixed use site – such as for Travelling Showpeople – or it is made clear that occupiers might need space for incidental (and acceptable) work-related activities, PPTS paragraph 28a) advises that conditions can limit which parts of a site are used for any business operation in order to minimise the visual impact and limit the effect on noise. As with any commercial use, conditions can also be imposed to control, for example, the height and/or nature of outdoor storage, hours of operation and/or harmful effects such as odour.

267. Travellers often own vehicles larger than domestic scale, for towing a caravan, transporting horses or working away. It is customary to impose a condition which limits the weight of vehicles and the number of large vehicles that may be parked or stored on a Traveller site. The usual upper weight limit is 3.5 tonnes, but sometimes a higher upper limit of 7.5 tonnes is accepted depending on the occupiers’ needs and any concerns regarding character and appearance, living conditions and/or highway safety.

268. When planning permission is to be granted for a transit site or transit pitches on a permanent site, conditions must be imposed to specify the length of time any occupier may reside on the site (as set out in PPTS paragraph 28c)) and the interval before which they may be permitted to return, and how such occupation is to be monitored by the local planning authority.
