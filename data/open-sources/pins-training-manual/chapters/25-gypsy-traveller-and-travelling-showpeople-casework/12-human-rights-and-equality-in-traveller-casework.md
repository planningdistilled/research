# 25 Gypsy, Traveller and Travelling Showpeople Casework: Human Rights and Equality in Traveller Casework

<!-- Part2 p.160 -->

## Human Rights and Equality in Traveller Casework

235. Rights set out under the Human Rights Act 1998 (HRA98) and the Public Sector Equality Duty (PSED) set out under the Equality Act 2010 are frequently engaged in Traveller casework. Comprehensive advice on upholding human rights and complying with the PSED is set out in the Human Rights and Equality ITM Chapter and not duplicated here67. The only additional points to stress here are that:

- For the purposes of Article 8, the “private and family life” of a Traveller encompasses living in accordance with their Gypsy or Traveller heritage, including living in a caravan, and a Traveller pitch or plot is a “home”.

- The best interests of the child(ren) – being those under 18 years old, even if they have left education or started work – is relevant to Article 8.

- For the purposes of the PSED, Romani Gypsies68 and Irish Travellers69 are ethnic minorities and thus have the protected characteristic of race under s149(7) of the EA10, whether they have PPTS status or not.

- The appellant and/or occupiers may have other relevant protected characteristics, such as age, disability, pregnancy/maternity and/or sex.

- The Article 8 rights and/or protected characteristics of interested parties may also be engaged in a Traveller case.

- Where engaged, irrespective of whether the occupiers have PPTS status, human rights and the PSED must be dealt with as an integral part of the reasoning that leads to the final decision. The Inspector should address, in their Conclusion to the decision letter, whether their decision to grant or

- To carry out that assessment properly, Inspectors should identify and address the main issues with regard to where or how human rights or the PSED may be engaged.

- For the purposes of Article 8(1), issues such as flood risk, highway safety, living conditions and general need for Traveller sites can bear just as much as personal circumstances on someone’s right to a private and family life and home.

- For the purposes of Article 8(2), “public safety” can include highway safety and flood risk issues, while “the economic well-being of the country” and “rights and freedoms of others” encompass the protection of the environment and thus a huge range of planning interests.

- Any unlawful use of the site can be relevant to the final balance70, not least given the Government’s policy regarding intentional unauthorised development. However, the occupiers’ personal need for a site and personal circumstances should be assessed and weighed separately.

236. Inspectors should also be particularly mindful of Article 6 (the right to a fair trial or hearing) in Traveller casework. Again, this is discussed fully in in the Human Rights and Equality ITM Chapter but extra care may be needed in Traveller cases to avoid barriers to participation which are difficult or impossible for the parties or participants to surmount.

237. If, for example, the appellant lacks financial resources to make their case, an Inspector may take a positive step to adjourn the inquiry so that the appellant can apply for public funding for representation – so long as this would not lead to unreasonable delay75.

238. Likewise, if it appears that the appellant or others may not have the literacy skills needed to participate on a level playing field, the Inspector should seek to establish – sensitively – whether that is the case, and what assistance may be required at an early stage of the hearing or inquiry.

239. Any representative, friends or relatives of the appellant or occupiers may be able to help, but, even then, it may be necessary for the Inspector to read out documents and/or make other adjustments to proceedings. The Inspector should ascertain that the persons understand and agree the contents of any written statements submitted on their behalf.
