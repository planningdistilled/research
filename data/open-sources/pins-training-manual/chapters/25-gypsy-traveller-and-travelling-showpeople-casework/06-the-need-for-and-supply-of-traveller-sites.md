# 25 Gypsy, Traveller and Travelling Showpeople Casework: The Need for and Supply of Traveller Sites

<!-- Part2 p.139 -->

## The Need for and Supply of Traveller Sites

137. The need for and supply of Traveller sites is a main issue or consideration in almost all appeals concerning the change of use of the land to introduce new pitches or plots. It is necessary to distinguish between and deal separately with the ‘general’ need for sites by the authority, and the ‘personal’ need of the appellant(s) and/or site occupier(s)26.

138. With respect to general need, the key matters to test at hearing or inquiry and address in the appeal decision are:

[^26]: Hedges v SSE & East Cambridgeshire DC [1996] EWHC Admin 240

<!-- Part2 p.140 -->

- The Council’s ‘locally set targets’ for the provision of Gypsy and Traveller pitches or Travelling Showperson plots in the development plan or a Gypsy and Traveller Accommodation Assessment (GTAA).

- Any other evidence of a need for pitches or plots over the relevant period.

- The supply of land for pitches or plots against the locally set targets or other assessment of need.

- Whether there is a shortfall of sites to meet existing needs, and the broad extent of the shortfall. This is what is known as ‘unmet need’.

- Whether the authority has a five year supply of specific deliverable sites for pitches and plots against their locally set targets. Whether the authority has a five year supply is not the same question as to whether they have an unmet need and it should therefore be assessed separately.

- Any proposals from the authority to redress any shortage of sites or lack of five-year supply through the development plan process or other means.

139. The ‘relevant’ area in terms of need for a site may extend beyond than the local authority boundaries27. Some authorities co-operate when carrying out GTAAs as described below.

### Assessments of Need and Supply

140. Local authorities are required under s8 of the Housing Act 1985 (HA85) to undertake reviews of housing needs in their district. S225 of the Housing Act 2004 (HA04) required that such reviews address the accommodation needs of Gypsies and Travellers residing in or resorting to their district.

141. Prior to PPTS, local authorities were required to undertake GTAAs to inform core strategies and allocations in development plan documents; see Annex A. The duty on authorities now is to consider the needs of people residing in or resorting to their district with respect to the provision of sites on which caravans can be stationed; s124 of the Housing and Planning Act 2016 (HPA16) repealed s225 and amended s8 to this effect.

142. PPTS paragraph 7 expects local authorities to use a robust evidence base to establish accommodation needs to inform the preparation of local plans and planning decisions. The need for Traveller sites should be assessed (in a GTAA or otherwise) as a sub-set of needs for caravan sites generally, which in

[^27]: Linfoot v SSCLG & Chorley BC [2012] EWHC 3514 (Admin); see also Beaver v SSCLG & South Cambridgeshire DC [2015] EWHC 1774 (Admin) and Sykes v SSHCLG & Runnymede BC [2020] EWHC 112 (Admin) in Annex B.

<!-- Part2 p.141 -->

turn is a sub-set of overall housing needs subject to a Strategic Housing Market Area Assessment (SHMA). Since the SHMA and any GTAA form part of the evidence base for the development plan, advice on how they should be prepared and what they should include is given in the Local Plans ITM Chapter.

143. The Council’s assessment of need and supply is the usual starting point for appeal decisions; indeed, the lack of any reliable or up to date assessment may be a material consideration in favour of an appeal. Where there is a GTAA, however, Inspectors are not required to make any findings on its accuracy or quality, and they should rarely do so, particularly where it was tested through the examination of a recently adopted local plan. GTAAs for the most part contain the best evidence of need and supply in the local area.

144. It will be necessary to address any arguments that the actual need for pitches or plots is materially greater or qualitatively different than anticipated in the GTAA. Appellants may raise concerns on some or all of the following:

- Whether the GTAA relates to all Travellers in accordance with s124 and s828 and reflects the PPTS Annex 1 definition as of December 2024.

- The appropriateness of the methodology and/or reliability of the evidence informing the assessment.

- Whether any backlog of need that was known or likely to have existed on the base date has been factored in.

- How the assessment addresses need arising from overcrowding – perhaps from ‘doubling-up’29 or ‘hidden’ or ‘concealed’ households30.

- Whether the assessment misses any other known need, for example, if the appeal site was occupied but not counted on the base date.

- Whether future need that is likely to arise is properly factored in, for example, when temporary permissions are due to lapse.

- Whether the assessment addresses the range of needs, such as for private and public sites, small and large family groups, permanent and transit sites, and for different Traveller communities including showpeople.

[^28]: See also the draft ‘Guidance to Local Housing Authorities on the Periodical Review of Housing Needs: Caravans and Houseboats’– DCLG 2016

[^29]: Where caravans that accommodate two or more households are stationed on one pitch.

[^30]: Where one pitch or even caravan accommodates an extended family, including adult children who are still at home through lack of access to a pitch of their own.

- The reliability of assumptions made, for example, on migration of Travellers in and out of the area31 or vacancies on public sites – where turnover is usually low and waiting lists are usually long.

- Whether the GTAA anticipates the supply of sites increasing through Travellers moving into bricks and mortar housing, bearing in mind that PPTS seeks to facilitate the Traveller way of life. Persons who already live in a house but have ‘a cultural tradition of nomadism or of living in a caravan’ may in fact drive a need for sites.

- Whether the GTAA anticipates supply increasing through Travellers moving onto private sites that are unlikely to be made available in practice.

- Whether planning permissions are accurately factored in, excluding any granted on a temporary and/or personal basis.

- Whether the GTAA includes ‘tolerated sites’ in the supply when they are not immune from enforcement action or may not be ‘deliverable’.

- The likelihood of and timescale for delivery of new site provision.

- The reliability of estimates of new household formation.

145. While considerable evidence may be presented, it is usually unnecessary – if not inappropriate – to go into extensive detail on these issues in an appeal decision. The reasoning should simply suffice to support conclusions as to:

- Whether the Council’s assessment is broadly accurate or there is likely to be a greater or lesser need for pitches or plots.

- Prospects and timescales for the anticipated supply coming forward.

146. It may be more straightforward to assess the need for travelling showpeople’s sites because their membership of trade associations has long left little room for doubt about their status. It should be borne in mind, however, that any flaw in the GTAA methodology may apply equally to the assessment of need and supply for travelling showpeople’s plots as well as Gypsy and Traveller pitches.

147. Since there are relatively few travelling showpeople, and they are traditionally concentrated across scattered districts, assessments may not be useful unless carried out by authorities co-operating across sub-regions. There may be wide variations between numbers of showpeople in adjoining authorities, affecting local needs for additional, alternative or enlarged sites.

148. If the Inspector’s findings in respect of need and/or supply would differ from those set out in the GTAA, it may be prudent to state that the conclusions are

### Other Evidence and ‘Need on the Ground’

149. Other evidence pertaining to the need for or supply of Traveller sites may be given at appeal. The Traveller Caravan Count has been undertaken every year in January and July since 197932. It is carried out for MHCLG, usually by local authority Gypsy and Traveller Liaison, Housing or Environmental Health Officers. It provides a record of the number of caravans on authorised public and private sites and on unauthorised developments and encampments.

150. The accuracy and consistency of the count varies between local authorities, and it is in any event only a record of occupation; it is best regarded as a snapshot of the number of caravans present in that area on those dates. In that regard, however, the counts may indicate general (patterns of) need over time, and whether there is likely to be any ‘need on the ground’.

151. Information may be submitted with an appeal relating to changes in the area that have occurred since the GTAA base date:

- Any new planning permissions granted, and whether any such additional sites would be available to Travellers not known to the landowner.

- The progress of a site allocations development plan document, the prospects of draft allocations being permitted and the likely date(s), if known, at which such new sites may come forward.

- Evidence of need arising from unauthorised developments or encampments, or the loss of Traveller sites to redevelopment.

152. The appeal must be decided on the basis of current circumstances, which may not reflect the estimates of need and supply on the GTAA base date. Any evidence of changes in the area since the base date and/or of further ‘need on the ground’ may reduce the weight of even the most accurate GTAA for the particular appeal decision. That said, outside of the GTAA process, counts of travelling showpeople tend to be carried out on an irregular basis.

### Unmet Need

153. The Council’s assessment and/or other evidence may show that the local authority does not have sites available to meet the current needs of Travellers residing in or resorting to the district. This situation may be variously described as a ‘backlog of need’ or ‘unmet need’ or ‘shortfall of sites’.

[^32]: The counts typically show fewer caravans in July than in January, since Travellers are more likely to be on the road in the summer months.

<!-- Part2 p.144 -->

154. It is normally necessary to make a finding in an appeal decision as to whether there is an outstanding need for pitches/plots and, if so, the broad scale of the unmet need relative to the Traveller population.

155. Inspectors should be aware that unmet need can indicate an immediate and pressing need for Traveller sites – which in turn can be expected to result in unauthorised encampments, environmental damage, lack of access to health care, education and even fresh water, sanitation and washing facilities, and to community tensions. As with any material consideration, however, the weight attached to unmet need for the decision-maker.

156. Even if there is not an unmet need, it does not follow that there will be a five year supply of deliverable sites. That question must be assessed separately as set out in the next section.

### Five Year Land Supply Issues

157. PPTS paragraph 10a) requires local authorities, in producing their local plan, to identify and update annually a supply of specific deliverable sites sufficient to provide five years’ worth of sites [five years’ worth of pitches or plots] against their locally set targets. If the authority cannot demonstrate an up to date five year supply of Traveller sites, PPTS paragraph 28, states that the provisions of NPPF paragraph 11d) apply33.

158. Consideration should be given not only to whether the authority has a five year supply in quantitative terms, but also to matters such as:

- Whether the supply includes a mix of tenures given PPTS paragraph 4e) and a mix of site sizes (including the size proposed), and it would meet the identified needs for different Traveller groups.

- Whether the supply includes sites subject to a temporary or personal permission which will in fact expire.

- Whether the Council intends to allocate existing unauthorised sites or sites with temporary permissions – which would ensure deliverability but only address the needs of the existing occupiers.

- Evidence of the deliverability of new sites or sites which the authority rely on as part of its five-year supply. The reasoning should clearly resolve any arguments about deliverability for the purposes of the decision.

- The anticipated provider of, funding source for and delivery timescale for any proposed affordable pitches – and where pitches or plots would be provided within mixed allocations.

[^33]: Including NPPF footnote 7 relating Traveller sites within the Green Belt or otherwise subject to NPPF policies that protect areas or assets of particular importance.

<!-- Part2 p.145 -->

- The likely acceptability of any (prospective) allocated sites at planning application stage, with regard to the development plan and PPTS.

- Constraints to delivery, such as the cost of environmental mitigation work.

159. As with unmet need, it is not necessary to describe the Council’s supply of sites with arithmetical precision. It was held in Swale BC v SSHCLG [2018] EWHC 3402 (Admin) that an Inspector did not err in granting temporary permission for a Traveller site partly on the basis of a ‘substantial shortfall’ of pitches.

### Emerging Plans

160. If there is an emerging local plan and/or DPD, the Inspector should establish the stage(s) it is at, and whether it contains any policies and/or allocations in order to bring forward Traveller sites. Other questions to address may include:

- Whether there is or will be a new accommodation needs assessment.

- Whether the Council accepts that there is a need for more Traveller sites and, if so, for what Traveller groups and of what tenure(s) and sizes etc.

- The likelihood of and timescale for adoption of the plan in its current form.

- The prospects of and timescales for any proposed allocations being granted planning permission and made available for occupation.

161. Weight should be attached to emerging local plans and their policies or allocations with regard to the factors set out in NPPF paragraph 49. The Inspector should also consider the prospects of an emerging plan being adopted within the period of any temporary permission that may be granted.

### ‘Large Scale Unauthorised Site[s]’

162. PPTS paragraph 12 states that: ‘In exceptional cases, where a local planning authority is burdened by a large-scale unauthorised site that has significantly increased their need, and their area is subject to strict and special planning constraints, then there is no assumption that [they are] required to plan to meet their Traveller site needs in full.’

163. At the time of introduction in August 2015, the Government stated that ‘…there is only one local authority caught in this position (Basildon District Council in respect of Dale Farm)’34. Since Dale Farm was an exceptional site with some 80 unauthorised pitches, Inspectors should take it that there is a high threshold for a ‘large scale unauthorised site’ to be a material consideration.

164. Moreover, the implications of any large scale unauthorised site for meeting Traveller site needs should be addressed at the local plan examination. If that
