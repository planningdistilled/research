# 25 Gypsy, Traveller and Travelling Showpeople Casework: The Planning Balance

<!-- Part2 p.159 -->

## The Planning Balance

231. The overall conclusion in Traveller appeals will normally involve carrying out a balancing exercise in the usual way, starting with the planning balance before carrying out any human rights and/or equality assessments.

232. Set out the findings on each of the main issues, including the weight attached to each harm or benefit of the development with regard to conditions that could be imposed to mitigate the harm and/or ensure the benefits. If and where appropriate, the possibility of making a split decision should also be addressed.

233. As advised above, the decision should be made in accordance with s38(6) and with regard to material considerations including PPTS paragraph 28 and the application of NPPF paragraph 11d) where the local planning authority cannot demonstrate a five year supply of deliverable Traveller sites.

234. If the Inspector decides to not grant permanent permission, it will be necessary to undertake a second balancing exercise as to whether a grant of a temporary and/or personal permission would be justified given:

- Any harm to the Green Belt would still carry substantial weight although it should also be recorded that the duration of such harm would be reduced.

- Whether the limited period of the permission would overcome or result in reduced harm in respect of other matters, perhaps in respect of the character of the area or, given current rather than future risk, flooding.

- Whether conditions would be necessary to mitigate even temporary harm or to secure benefits still needed to justify the temporary permission and, if
