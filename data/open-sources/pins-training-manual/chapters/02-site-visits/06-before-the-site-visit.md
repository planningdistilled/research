# 02 Site Visits: Before the site visit

<!-- Part1 p.129 -->

## Before the site visit

37. Your forthcoming programmes of written representation cases will be available to view in your worklist on Manage Appeals.

38. When you first receive the case assignment – check the following and take up any problems with the Case Officer straightaway:

- Do you have any potential conflicts of interest? (see ‘'Conflicts of Interest’)

- Is the case suitable for the Written Representations procedure? (see ‘Role of the Inspector’)

- Are there any safeguarding issues? If you have any safeguarding concerns, advice is contained in the Approach to Decision Making chapter

39. Contact the Case Officer immediately if you are unable to carry out a site visit because of a conflict of interest or you feel a change in procedure is required. Email the Case Officer explaining the circumstances.

40. If the cases are suitable, you should email the Case Officer (via their Team email address) with the timings of your site visits as soon as possible and, preferably, not later than three weeks before the date of the site visit. For ARSVs you should

<!-- Part1 p.130 -->

provide a one or two hour time slot. For ASVs you should provide a precise time. For health and safety reasons, you should also note the dates you intend to carry out USVs. This will ensure the information is pulled through to your decision template.

41. When timing site visit programmes:

- Make sure you leave enough time to conduct the site visit without being rushed and to travel safely to the next site visit. As a rough guide, a straightforward site visit relating to a smaller case (for example, a house extension) might usually take around 15-20 minutes.

- If the site is very large, set out where you will meet the parties.

- Check for any information provided by the Case Officer which indicates that you might need to allow more time for any site visits (for example if the LPA has requested that you view from a large number of neighbouring properties)

- Tools such as Google Maps and Bing Maps can help you work out how long it will take to travel between sites, and where car parking might be available in the area. However, allow enough time to cope with potential traffic delays, your unfamiliarity with an area and finding somewhere to park.

- Allow for short winter days and longer journeys in rush hours and school traffic.

- Try to work out where you will park if the site is in a city/town centre or any area where parking is likely to be restricted. Public transport can be the best solution in such areas.

- Check to make sure that the programme of visits is practical.

- Think carefully about how many site visits you can reasonably do in one day. If you have a programme of several visits, do you need to consider an overnight stay either by travelling the day before or splitting the visits across two days? On health and safety grounds a working day of visits and travel of more than 10 hours should be avoided.

42. The parties may ask you to view the site at a particular time or day of the week but it is for you to decide if this is necessary. Is the case one where you could reasonably use your experience and judgement to assess the effects of a proposal even if you do not visit at the suggested time? If so, you should provide the Case Officer with a written explanation as to the reasons why. However, if the request can be easily accommodated into your programme, then it is good practice to do so.

43. The site visit is your opportunity to see the site and its surroundings and to assess the significance of what has been set out in the written representations.

<!-- Part1 p.131 -->

44. Before you carry out the site visit – have you:

- Checked that relevant parties have been informed of the date and time?

- Worked out where you will meet the parties and where you might park?

- Considered where toilet and other breaks might be taken?

- Made sure you understand the proposal and the main issues and have identified the relevant plans?

- Made a list of everything you want to see on the site visit, including in the surrounding area – and anything you want to check with the parties (for example, in relation to physical features)?

- Made a note of any interested parties who you have been asked to call upon?

- Identified any missing documents (policies, conservation area plans, interested party representations etc.) and asked the Case Officer to secure them?

- Got your note taking device, plans, a contact number for the Case Officer, ID card, calling cards, sat nav and maps?
