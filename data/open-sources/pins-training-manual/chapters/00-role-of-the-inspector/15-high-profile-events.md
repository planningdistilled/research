# 00 Role of the Inspector: High Profile Events

<!-- Part1 p.18 -->

## High Profile Events

57. Some cases will attract a high level of media attention either because the appellant is high profile or because the development, or enforcement case, has captured the attention of a wide audience.

58. This can generate a great deal of social media activity and press coverage. It is not unusual for this activity to include threats to the parties. Behind the scenes there may be many representations and potential witnesses.

59. It remains that the Inspector should follow the guidance in the Inquiry and Hearing Chapters of the ITM. Alongside that the following matters should be considered, with action taken where necessary.

### Inspector Safety

60. Inspectors should be aware that they may be under closer scrutiny than usual. If you have social media accounts, you should review your security and privacy settings.

61. Use a hire car if you are not travelling by public transport. If you do take your own vehicle, make sure that it is parked well away from the venue and/or site.

62. LPAs may well have security measures in place for the event. Review these measures and carry out your own risk assessment beforehand, as well as dynamically throughout the event. If needed, ask for a colleague/manager to observe proceedings in a supportive capacity.

### Press/media contact and interaction

63. PINS Press Office should contact you and will deal with all press/media queries.

64. Where many members of the press are expected to attend an event, the Inspector should discuss with the Communication Team the best way to handle this, including whether a Press Officer accompanies them to deal with media queries on the day (liaising with the Inspector during adjournments, as necessary).

<!-- Part1 p.19 -->

65. Consider whether you will allow filming at the event. You may be happy to have your opening filmed to give the media something. Conversely, it may be that you consider that any filming will be disruptive.

66. In addition, for example, you may be aware that threats have been made against witnesses and/or interested parties. If so, you may consider that filming is not appropriate (as it may dissuade some witnesses from giving evidence and/or embolden others).

67. Inform the parties and Press Office beforehand if no filming is to be carried out.

68. Journalists / reporters will be persistent. They will approach you outside the event venue, during adjournments and/or at the site visit. They are also clever with their questioning. Do not engage. Even a simple “Yes” or “No” answer to a seemingly innocent question can be reported as “The Inspector informed us that...”.

69. Journalists / reporters may also ask odd/leading questions and/or interview witnesses during adjournments. Deal with individuals fairly and, if necessary, firmly, reminding them that you want witnesses to be unhindered in giving evidence and that you will exclude the media if you think are their activity is unhelpful.

70. You may be filmed and/or photographed entering the venue and/or at the site visit. On one occasion a drone was used to follow an Inspector around a site. There is little you can do to prevent this and the landowner may or may not be happy to have the press on their land. Nevertheless, you should be prepared that you may be on film and act accordingly. Pay particular attention to protecting your note taking on site.

71. Familiarise yourself with the corporate Media Policy.

### General

72. Once a case is allocated, Inspectors should avoid reading newspaper reports, TV reporting and social media activity concerning the development until the decision is issued.

73. Be fully appraised of any security and/or event management measures the LPA may be proposing to, or has, put in place. If necessary, in addition to a Case Management Conference, consider a pre-event meeting (on the day if needed) to iron out any matters.

74. Remember that these are public events where everyone has the right to attend and to speak, so you may need to be flexible in your approach.

75. If large numbers of interested parties are involved, you may wish to consider asking the LPA to livestream the event, which might assist with reducing in-person attendance.

<!-- Part1 p.20 -->

76. Make sure that you have a retiring room and have planned for your lunch/refreshments. You may not wish to be followed to the shops!

77. You may want to consider staying a bit further away from the venue than normal.

### Further Advice

78. If you need further advice on handling a high-profile event, please speak to your Professional Lead, and/or the Communication team, who will be able to put you in touch with Inspectors who have had experience in this area.
