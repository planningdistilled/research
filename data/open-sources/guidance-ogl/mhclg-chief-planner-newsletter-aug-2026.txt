National Planning Policy Framework

The new Framework represents a significant restructuring of national planning policy, providing
a clearer distinction between policies for plan-making and those which apply to decision-
making. The new decision-making policies are intended to provide greater certainty and
consistency, standardise policies which apply across England and reduce unnecessary
duplication or unjustified local variation.

The new decision-making policies in the Framework took effect on 17 August 2026.

The Framework maintains many of the reforms proposed in the consultation published last
December, while making a number of changes in response to consultation feedback. These
include:

•   Well-connected stations – the new ‘default yes’ approach to development around well-
    connected stations has been expanded to apply within the top 80 Travel to Work Areas by
    Gross Value Added, rather than the top 60 proposed at consultation.
•   Density – policies have been refined to support more intensive use of land, including a
    wider range of development within the curtilage of residential properties and increases in
    floorspace as well as additional units. The approach to minimum densities around rail
    stations has also been adjusted to ensure that densities are maximised where they can
    appropriately be supported.
•   Strategic sites – the Framework introduces a consistent and distinct category for strategic
    sites of around 1,500 homes or more, with policies tailored to support their delivery where
    relevant.
•   Local standards – the Framework confirms that quantitative standards in development
    plans should generally be limited to matters where local variation is justified, while
    providing greater clarity on the circumstances in which local energy efficiency and internal
    space standards may be appropriate.
•   Retail and regeneration – greater flexibility is expected when applying parking standards
    to the redevelopment of large retail and similar sites, helping support their regeneration.
•   Telecommunications – clearer support is provided for telecommunications infrastructure
    in appropriate locations, including development which can improve connectivity for rail
    users.

•   Community facilities – policies protecting valued facilities have been strengthened,
    including ensuring that public houses are not lost where this can reasonably be avoided.
•   Protected Landscapes – the Framework makes clear that major development in Protected
    Landscapes should be refused other than in exceptional circumstances, alongside the
    existing statutory protection for their landscape and scenic beauty.

The Framework also provides greater clarity across a number of other areas of decision-
making, including the consideration of transport impacts, pollution, heritage, Protected
Landscapes and the safety of women and girls.

Following consultation feedback, the proposed specific reference to fast food outlets has not
been taken forward. The government will instead consider whether other land-use planning
approaches, including potential changes to the Use Classes Order, could help address the
issues raised.

The new National Planning Policy Framework and the government response to the
consultation are available to view here: National Planning Policy Framework - GOV.UK


Wider planning announcements

A number of related measures have also been published as part of the government’s wider
programme to create a clearer, rules-based planning system.

Reforms to the statutory consultee system

The government has published its response to the consultation on reform of the statutory
consultee system (available here: Reforms to the statutory consultee system - GOV.UK). This
confirms a package of changes intended to make consultation requirements more
proportionate and reduce delays, including reforms affecting Active Travel England, National
Highways, Historic England and the Mining Remediation Authority. Sport England will remain a
statutory consultee, with its remit focused more closely on applications where its input adds
most value. The statutory consultee status of The Gardens Trust and Theatres Trust will be
removed, alongside a new notification requirement. Regulations to implement these reforms
will follow in due course.

Housing Delivery Test

The 2024 and 2025 Housing Delivery Test results have been published, together with an
updated Housing Delivery Test Rule Book. The 2025 Housing Delivery Test results should be
used for the purposes of decision-making and are available to view here: Housing Delivery Test
- GOV.UK.

Permitted development rights for electricity network ground investigations

The Department for Energy Security and Net Zero is inviting local planning authorities to
engage with a consultation, published on 6 August, on proposed changes to permitted
development rights (PDRs) for ground investigations and surveys to support the delivery of
electricity network infrastructure projects in England.

The consultation is seeking views on the introduction of a new PDR for certain temporary, low-
impact ground investigations and surveys by electricity network developers (distribution and
transmission network operators that are licensed to undertake works relating to electricity
networks). The consultation is open until 4 September 2026 and is available to view here:
Permitted development rights for electricity network ground investigations - GOV.UK.

Extract – now live for all local planning authorities

Extract, an AI-powered tool developed by MHCLG’s Digital Planning programme in partnership
with the Incubator for AI (i.AI), is now live and free for every local planning authority to use. Visit
https://provide.planning.data.gov.uk/extract

The tool converts planning documents, including Conservation Areas, Article 4 Directions and
Tree Preservation Orders, into structured, standardised data, cutting a process that can take
up to 2 hours down to as little as 2 minutes for some document types. Users review and correct
all outputs before exporting them into standardised formats for their own systems and the
national Planning Data Platform.

To get started, visit the platform and log in with your GOV.UK email address. Guidance and an
introductory video are available to help you begin digitising your documents, and the team is
on hand at extract@communities.gov.uk for any questions. There are also regular online drop-
in sessions. For more information read our MHCLG Digital blog post.

Using digital tools to support site identification and assessment

New guidance on using digital tools to support site identification and assessment has been
published. The guidance, which includes nine case studies, draws on the experiences of local
planning authorities across the country, sharing practical advice on how digital tools can help
identify potential development sites, improve assessment processes and make better use of
planning data.

Tackling planning delays

A series of pilots have been selected to find digital solutions to some of the persistent
challenges facing the planning system including cross-boundary and cross-tier plan-making
and Section 106 negotiations. 11 consortia of technology companies working with local
planning and combined authorities and other partners have been selected to take part in
Round 6 of the Digital Planning programme’s PropTech Innovation Fund, sharing £2.4 million in
funding. MHCLG is co-delivering Round 6 with PUBLIC. For more information read our MHCLG
Digital blog post.

New Sustainable Drainage Systems (SuDS) Strategy templates published

The Environment Agency, working with CIRIA, the Association of SuDS Authorities, Arup and a
working group of SuDS practitioners, has produced new SuDS Strategy Templates to help
planning applicants submit the right information on SuDS first time. The templates should also
make it easier for LPAs and LLFAs to check whether drainage systems, proposed as part of new
development, have been designed in accordance with Defra’s National standards for
sustainable drainage systems.

To reflect the proportional approach advocated in the National Planning Policy Framework, two
templates have been produced:

   •   SuDS Strategy Template for Major Development

   •   SuDS Strategy Template for Non-major Development.

Users should select the template that best fits the scale of their proposed development. The
templates can be accessed from the Planning Portal or from the CIRIA website. Users should
always access these versions to ensure the latest iteration is used.

The Environment Agency encourages LPAs and LLFAs to adopt these national templates to
improve consistency across the country and negate the need for local versions to be produced.
If you would like to provide feedback on how the templates could be improved, please use
fratemplatefeedback@environment-agency.gov.uk.

New training for planners on SuDS

SuDS are an increasingly important aspect of the design of urban areas and new
developments. It is critical that planners, and flood risk practitioners working with the planning
system, understand policy and best practice to support the implementation of SuDS through
planning. The Environment Agency and the Town and Country Planning Association (TCPA)
have teamed up with leading experts from the Association of SuDS Authorities (ASA), to
develop an e-learning module covering key principles and practical advice for securing high-
quality SuDS through planning.

Further support and input has also been provided by CIRIA and CIWEM.

The course is interactive, free of charge, and can be completed at your own pace. Register now
to complete the course: https://learning.tcpa.org.uk/courses/intro-suds-for-planners

Closing remarks

Further work will follow over the coming months to support implementation of the planning
reforms set out above, including updates to relevant Planning Practice Guidance and
implementation of provisions in the Planning and Infrastructure Act 2025.

I recognise that publication of a substantially restructured Framework represents an important
change for planning authorities and practitioners. I would encourage authorities to familiarise
themselves with the new Framework, and in particular the decision-making policies which
have applied since 17 August 2026.
