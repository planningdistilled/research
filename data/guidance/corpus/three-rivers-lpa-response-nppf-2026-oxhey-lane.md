## Summary

The council's note to the Inspector on a called-in Green Belt housing scheme, written after the Inspector asked on 19 August 2026 for comments on the new Framework. It lists the relevant policies (DM4, S5, HO7 to HO9, L3, GB6 to GB8, DP3, TR3, TR4) and maps them to the old paragraphs: GB6 replaces paragraph 153, GB7 replaces 154, GB8 replaces 155 to 158, and DM4 replaces 49 to 51. It says the emerging Regulation 19 plan keeps the weight it had before. On GB7(1)(h), the new route for land near stations, the council says the site is over 800 m from Carpenders Park station. Given the topography and what local residents said at the inquiry, it is also not within a "reasonable walking distance". The council keeps its position that the site is not grey belt, and runs its accessibility case through TR3(1)(a) ("genuine choice" of transport modes) and DP3(2)(d) (movement). It argues that even if the Secretary of State finds the scheme not inappropriate, S5(5) together with S5(2) and DP3 should still lead to refusal.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Map each old NPPF 2024 paragraph to its new policy code and say whether the test has actually changed. The note says most have not changed: "The NPPF 2026 is not considered to fundamentally change the considerations of this particular case".
- For GB7(1)(h), test the Annex B "reasonable walking distance" against topography and route quality, not only the 800 m line.
- Under GB7(1)(g)(iii), test sustainable location through TR3(1)(a), meaning a genuine choice of modes for users who cannot walk the distances.
- Note that compliance with the Golden Rules (GB8) now carries "substantial" rather than "significant" weight.
- Treat S5(5) as a second-stage balance even where Green Belt development is found not inappropriate: refuse if the benefits are substantially outweighed, using S5(2) and conflict with DP3.

## Positions against our propositions

- **TR, agrees.** The council treats topography and route conditions as the reason the site is not within a reasonable walking distance, not distance alone: "given the topography of the site, taken alongside comments from local" residents "it is not considered that the site falls within" a reasonable walking distance (Green Belt section, on GB7(1h)). It also says "The scheme lacks any robust alternative to walking and" cycling for those who cannot walk the distances (TR3 discussion).
- **GB, agrees.** The note treats passing GB7 as not the same as approval. "If the Secretary of State concludes that the Scheme is not inappropriate development," then "the Council’s position is that the benefits of approving the Scheme would be substantially" outweighed by adverse effects (Tilted Balance section, applying S5(5)).

## Leads

- The other documents in the same inquiry library (ID series on cdn.threerivers.gov.uk), especially the appellant's NPPF 2026 response. Check the Secretary of State decision on APP/P1940/V/26/3378268 when it is issued.
- The note's reading that the Annex B 800 m / 10-minute definition applies to S5(1)(h), L3(2)(c) and GB7(1)(h) only for the part of a site within that distance.
