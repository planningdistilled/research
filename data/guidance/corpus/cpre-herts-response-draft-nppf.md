# Our response to the new draft National Planning Policy Framework

## Summary

A short news post announcing CPRE (Campaign to Protect Rural England) Hertfordshire's response to the December 2025 draft National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF) consultation and urging supporters to respond by 10 March 2026. It objects that the draft weakens protection for the Green Belt, National Landscapes, valued landscapes, Local Green Spaces and heritage assets, and that 'grey belt' is further promoted and extended. It predicts that the revised standard method will set unachievable housing targets, so councils will be unable to show a five-year housing land supply, triggering a flood of speculative applications and consents. It gives no guidance on how to apply the final August 2026 Framework; the full response is in a linked download not retrieved here.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Nothing addressed to officers; it urges the public to respond to the consultation (deadline 10 March 2026).
- Implicitly: expect five-year supply shortfalls under the new standard method and more speculative applications.

## Positions against our propositions

- **SUP: silent (checked; not a disagreement).** "This then triggers a continuous flood of speculative planning applications and consents but leaves local authorities without any means of enforcing delivery" (fourth paragraph of the article body, verbatim). This is a campaign prediction about the December 2025 draft and its effect on volume across the whole system: unachievable standard-method targets lead to five-year supply failure, which leads to more applications and more consents, which leads to land-banking. It says nothing about how much weight a shortfall carries in an individual decision, or whether it decides the case. More consents once the tilted balance is engaged is consistent with our finding that supply opens the gate. It offers no evidence and does not address the final August 2026 text. No change to our analysis.
