## Summary

A news analysis of Bath & North East Somerset (B&NES) Planning Committee's 5-4 resolution on 2 Sep 2026 to delegate outline permission for up to 200 homes on Green Belt land west of Charlton Road, Keynsham (25/04952/EOUT). Officers found the site was grey belt (not strongly contributing to purposes (a), (b) or (d)), that evidenced unmet need was met by a roughly 2.5-year housing land supply, and that the GB8 Golden Rules were met, raising affordable housing from the local 30% to 45%. The article stresses that grey belt did not remove the Green Belt or create automatic permission, and that officers still had to make each judgement. It also reports that the shortfall reduced the weight of "out-of-date" housing policies.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Assess grey-belt status on the application site against purposes (a), (b) and (d).
- Test each GB7(1)(g) limb in turn: grey belt, evidenced unmet need (supply or HDT), sustainable location, Golden Rules for major housing.
- Apply GB8: absent a Green Belt-specific local requirement, add 15 percentage points to the highest existing affordable-housing requirement (subject to the cap), plus infrastructure and accessible green space.
- Give substantial weight to Golden Rules compliance.
- Record a committee resolution as a resolution to grant subject to a Section 106 agreement, not as a permission.

## Positions against our propositions

- **GB (agrees):** "That did not make the Green Belt designation disappear. Nor did it create an automatic permission." (section "Grey belt changed the starting point"); and "So the better planning-law lesson is not that Green Belt housing is now automatically acceptable." (section "Why this is not simply a case of councillors being forced to approve") — a grey-belt pass at a town edge, but passing GB7 is not approval.
- **A2 (silent; reported council practice only):** "The shortfall also reduced the weight that could be given to out-of-date housing policies." (section "A 2.5-year housing land supply mattered directly") — a one-line news report of the B&NES officer's balance, not argued and not tied to Annex A para 2; it says nothing about heritage, design, access or spatial-preference policies keeping or losing weight. Not a disagreement with A2. At most a lead for DIV: a council still using supply-based "out-of-date" weight reduction, which patterns.md finding 9 already flags as 2024 reasoning that some decision-makers persist with.
