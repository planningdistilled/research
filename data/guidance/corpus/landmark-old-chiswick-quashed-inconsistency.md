## Summary

A case note on R (Old Chiswick Protection Society) v London Borough of Hounslow [2026] EWHC 2278 (Admin), where Andrew Parkinson acted for the successful claimant. The High Court quashed a permission for a high-rise scheme in the Chiswick House Conservation Area because the officer report described an earlier appeal-approved scheme as poor quality and poor design without telling the committee that the inspector had expressly rejected those criticisms. The court held the North Wiltshire consistency principle (like cases should be decided alike, and a decision-maker must give reasons for departing from an earlier decision) was engaged, and the error went to the heart of the heritage balance under paragraph 215 of the 2024 NPPF. It is not about the August 2026 text, but it is direct authority on how officers must treat inspectors' findings when briefing members.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- When an earlier appeal decision on the same or a comparable site exists, the officer report must tell the committee what the inspector found and why.
- Officers must give reasons if they depart from an inspector's conclusions; they cannot present as their own judgements that an inspector has rejected.
- Legal advice to members should not tell them to focus only on the outcome of an appeal rather than its reasoning.

## Positions against our propositions

- **METH - agrees.** "It benefits no one for officers to give advice or convey judgments, particularly as to material considerations, that they know to have been rejected by a planning inspector." (passage from the judgment quoted after "Overall, the Court observed:"). Supports treating inspectors' findings as weighty evidence that decision-makers must engage with, though it concerns individual previous decisions rather than counting outcomes.
- **DIV - silent (checked 2026-10-02).** The note (para 3: "The report failed to inform the Committee that the Inspector had expressly rejected those criticisms, or to explain the Inspector's reasoning, or to give reasons for departing from those conclusions.") is about the North Wiltshire consistency duty on one site, where an officer report contradicted an earlier appeal decision on the same land without saying so. It says nothing about whether councils, across cases, pass what inspectors fail, and the departure here ran the other way (the council criticised a scheme the inspector had found acceptable). It does not limit DIV; at most it is a legal lever for challenging an individual council decision that departs from an inspector's finding on the same or a closely comparable site without reasons.
