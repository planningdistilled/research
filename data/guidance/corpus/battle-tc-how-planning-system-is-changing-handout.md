## Summary

This is a 32-slide training handout for local councils, dated 24 February 2026, on the reform package and the December 2025 draft National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF). Curl returned 403. The PDF was obtained through WebFetch's saved binary and text-extracted locally. The handout presents "NPPF 2026" as a set of numbered "National Decision Making Policies" that "take priority over (replace)" overlapping local and neighbourhood plan policies, with inconsistent policies carrying "very little weight". On the presumption, it tells councils that development within settlement boundaries will be permitted in most circumstances. A five-year housing land supply still matters for speculative development outside settlement boundaries. It also records strong support for homes near well-connected stations "even in the Green Belt". It is Sussex-focused, with examples of appeal pressure from five-year supply shortfalls in Horsham, Mid Sussex and Chichester.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Parish councils must know the detailed content of NPPF 2026, because "most" decision policies will be found there.
- Assume national decision-making policies replace local and neighbourhood plan policies on the same issue.
- Expect development inside settlement boundaries to be permitted in most cases. Focus objections on development outside settlement boundaries, where supply is still significant.
- Note that, from 1 April 2026, written-representation appeals will mostly take into account only representations made on the original application.

## Positions against our propositions

- **A2 (qualifies; checked 2 Oct 2026, not a real disagreement):** "these will take priority over (replace) existing policies in a local plan or neighbourhood plan which cover the same issues" and "‘inconsistent’ existing policies will carry ‘very little weight’" (slide 18, "NPPF and decision making redefined"). Both quotes are verbatim. The slide is a bullet-point summary of the December 2025 draft, given to parish councils before the final Framework existed (it guesses it "will apply from ... May/June 2026?"). It restates the draft's own precedence rule and does not address whether a whole policy or only the inconsistent part loses weight. It also says nothing about heritage, design, access or spatial-preference policies. So it is not a considered reading against our clause-only view. It does show that local councils were trained on a broad "replace" message, which may colour how parish councils read Annex A. The final Annex A paragraph 2 ("or parts of those policies", "materially inconsistent") supports our narrower reading. No change to A2 is needed.
- **SUP (agrees):** "the significance of a five-year housing land supply is retained in relation to speculative development outside of settlement boundaries" (slide 21, "What else will the new NPPF do?"). This supports the "opens the gate" half of SUP. It does not say that supply decides cases.
