## Summary

The council's full answers to the December 2025 draft National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF) consultation, submitted on 10 March 2026. It "strongly agrees" with splitting plan-making policies from decision-making policies. It "partly disagrees" with the glossary definition of settlements: it wants settlements defined as functioning communities with services, wants allocated or permitted land left out until built, and supports excluding hamlets and scattered groups. It "strongly disagrees" with the presumption in favour, with S4 (development within settlements, because it ignores settlement size or sustainability), with development around stations, with GB6 and GB7, and with GB7(1)(h). It "partly disagrees" with S5: it predicts the list of categories is so broad that S5(4) would rarely apply, and S5(1)(j) "well related" is unclear. On grey belt it cites Wrotham Parish Council v SSHCLG [2026] EWHC 165 and says that grey belt now works through case-by-case appeal decisions rather than plans. The positions are officers' views on the draft, not guidance on the final text.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

(A consultation response rather than instructions, but it shows how the council's officers read the draft.)
- Read "settlement" as a place that works as a community with services, not just a built-up area. Hamlets and scattered groups are not settlements.
- Treat S5(1)(j) "well related to an existing settlement" with caution, because it is unclear and may let in speculative development that conflicts with the spatial strategy.
- Do not assume a rural railway station makes a sustainable location: check services and safe walking routes.
- Expect grey belt to be decided proposal by proposal, after Wrotham.

## Positions against our propositions

- **SH1, agrees.** The council complains that the national settlement policy ignores local settlement tiers, which is consistent with tiers carrying no standalone NPPF weight: "The policy provides no consideration of the size or sustainability of settlements, being too broad in its" application (Q37, on S4). It also asks how criterion (h) should relate to "settlement hierarchy" (Q39).
- **S5, silent (compatible, not a qualification).** On the December 2025 draft, the council predicts that the S5(1) categories are so broad that "list of acceptable development is very broad, so there wouldn’t be many applications left that point" S5(4) would apply to (Q38, p.14). This is a policy-design objection about breadth (landscape impact, plan-led growth), not a view on how S5(4) cases are decided. It does not question that falling outside every category is fatal, and it fits our point that the S5(1) category is the whole case.
- **S5, agrees** (competing readings of (j)). "‘well related to an existing settlement’ is insufficiently clear and may enable" speculative development outside settlements (Q39, criterion j).
- **TR, agrees.** Rural stations may lack "safe walking routes", so a nearby station does not make a location acceptable: "area will have no other supporting infrastructure - like schools, bus stops, shops or even safe walking" routes (Q40).
- **METH, agrees.** It sees grey belt (and so Green Belt outcomes) as being decided in individual appeals, which supports treating appeal decisions as the main evidence of how the policy is applied: the Wrotham approach "undermines the primacy of plan‑making and shifts control to case‑by‑case appeal decisions" (Q136).
