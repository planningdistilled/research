## Summary

This is a short consultancy briefing for clients, mostly developers. It reads the August 2026 National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF) as keeping the pro-growth direction of the December 2025 draft. On decisions, it expects local planning authorities (LPAs) to come under more scrutiny if they put procedure ahead of "constructive engagement". It says the settlement-based approach "has been largely retained", with "tighter controls" in less sustainable countryside. It also says it is getting harder to resist schemes "solely on the basis of local character or settlement-edge locations". It reads HO7 as giving substantial weight to housing benefits whether or not there is a supply shortfall. On transport, it says TR3's Connectivity Tool does not replace a site-specific assessment. It also covers the removal of the footnote-7 exclusion from the grey belt definition and the station route into the Green Belt (about 800 metres). On design it covers DP1-DP4. Its Green Belt section mostly restates the Framework.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Take a pragmatic, proportionate approach: front-load engagement, accept amendments during determination, and avoid delaying decisions while waiting for consultee replies.
- Give substantial weight under HO7 to homes that meet evidenced needs, "not only where there is an identified shortfall".
- Use the Connectivity Tool under TR3 alongside other quantitative and qualitative evidence, plus a site-specific assessment of local access.
- Assess protected areas and assets separately once land is classed as grey belt.
- Treat housing within about 800 metres of a qualifying station as not inappropriate in the Green Belt if it is physically well-related, of a scale that existing or proposed infrastructure can take, and does not prejudice longer-term comprehensive development.

## Positions against our propositions

- **TR, agrees.** "although it does not replace the need for a site-specific assessment of local accessibility and transport conditions" (page 5, "Protecting Green Belt Land", on TR3 and the Connectivity Tool). It supports judging access on the actual local conditions rather than on a tool or a distance alone. It says nothing about the walking route itself.
- **S5, agrees.** "whilst maintaining tighter controls on development in less sustainable countryside locations" (page 2, "Achieving Sustainable Development"). This is consistent with reading S5 as a restrictive, category-led test outside settlements.
- **A2, silent.** "making it increasingly difficult to resist development proposals solely on the basis of local character or settlement-edge locations where broader sustainability objectives are met" (page 2, "Achieving Sustainable Development"). On checking, this is general commentary on the planning balance: it says more weight now goes to meeting needs in and around settlements, so character or edge-of-settlement objections are less likely to win on their own. It says nothing about Annex A para 2, about plan policies being out of date or inconsistent with the Framework, or about how much weight those policies keep. A policy can keep full weight and still be outweighed, so this does not cut against A2. It is a developer-side expectation, not tested against decisions.
- **SUP, silent (restates policy).** "strengthening the weight to be afforded to housing delivery not only where there is an identified shortfall, but wherever proposals would help meet the needs of the community" (page 3, "Delivering a sufficient supply of homes", on HO7). This paraphrases the HO7 text: substantial weight for homes that meet evidenced needs. It describes the weight given to housing benefits, not what a supply shortfall does in the balance. It says nothing on whether a shortfall decides cases, so it neither supports nor qualifies SUP. If anything, a source of substantial housing weight that does not depend on supply makes the shortfall less decisive, which fits SUP.

## Leads

- Its comparison with the December 2025 draft, especially on data centres and town centres. It has nothing on rural settlements.
- Newsteer's statement that LPAs often refuse amendments or delay decisions is an untested claim. Our DIV material on process could test it.
