## Summary

A planning barrister's day-one "top 10" reflections on the August 2026 National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF). The 79 national decision-making policies (NDMPs) apply immediately as material considerations, and local plan policy that is materially inconsistent "in whole or part" gets "very limited weight". The concept of "out of date" plans and the "basket of most important policies" is gone: whether the S4 and S5 presumptions apply turns on place, need and stations, not on the age of the plan. S4 is a "default yes" within settlements; S5 gives a strong tilt for listed categories outside settlements and refusal "absent exceptional circumstances" for everything else. Five-year housing land supply matters less, because under S5 it is only one way to show unmet need.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Apply the NDMPs now, as material considerations the Government expects to be given "critical" importance.
- Give very limited weight to local plan policy that is materially inconsistent with the new Framework, in whole or part; expect these arguments case by case before officers, committees and inspectors.
- Stop running "out of date" and "basket" arguments; ask instead whether the site is within a settlement (S4) or falls in an S5 category.
- Outside settlements, identify the S5 category first; outside every category, refuse unless exceptional circumstances apply.
- Treat the station policy (reasonable walking distance of a well-connected station) as applying in S4, S5(1)(h) and GB7(1)(h).
- Note that unmet need for S5 can be shown in ways other than a five-year supply shortfall (affordable, older persons' housing, etc.).

## Positions against our propositions

- **A2 (silent; restates policy):** "if existing local plan policy is - in whole or part - materially inconsistent with the new NPPF, it gets “very limited weight”." (section "The slow death of local development management policy"). Checked 2 Oct 2026: this paraphrases Annex A para 2, which reads "Development plan policies (or parts of those policies) which are materially inconsistent ... should be given very limited weight". The "it" is loose shorthand, not a claim that a whole policy loses weight when only part conflicts; the Framework wording supports our clause-only reading. He also notes each "materially inconsistent" argument will be had "case by case", which is consistent with our coding. No conflict with A2.
- **S5 (agrees):** "The flip-side: if you’re outside those particular categories, you get refused permission absent exceptional circumstances where your benefits substantially outweigh your adverse effects." (section "Outside settlements (S5)")
- **SUP (agrees):** "First, outside the green belt anyhow, 5 year supply isn’t as important as it was. That’s because under the new S5 it’s only one way - just one - that unmet need can be demonstrated." (section 8 "Housing land supply"). Consistent with SUP: a five-year shortfall is one of several routes to "evidenced unmet need" under S5 (largely restating the S5 text), so supply matters less than before. It says nothing about supply deciding a case, and SUP never claimed supply was the only gate, so this is not a qualification. Adjudicated 2026-10-02: originally coded "qualifies", recoded "agrees" (weak; mostly policy restatement).
