## Summary

The news page of the Community Planning Alliance (CPA), an alliance of local campaign groups. It carries campaign items (the April 2026 Day of Action, a letter to the Housing Secretary, an Observer advert to the new Prime Minister) and a 12 January 2026 item on how to respond to the draft NPPF consultation. It gives no guidance on applying the final August 2026 Framework. Its policy content is opposition: reversing "grey belt" rules, ending high rural housing targets, and a "greenfield last" approach. It reads draft S5(1)(j) as meaning councils without a five-year housing land supply lose the power to refuse speculative housing outside settlements.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Nothing addressed to officers. It asks campaigners to email MPs and the NPPF team, and asks government to reverse grey belt and adopt "brownfield first" sequential testing.
- Proposes a "Sustainable Development" definition that all plans and decisions should pass (carbon, biodiversity, natural capital, flood risk, social equity).

## Positions against our propositions

- **SUP - no genuine disagreement (checked 2 Oct 2026).** The 12 January 2026 item ("How to respond to the National Planning Policy Framework") says: "Councils unable to demonstrate a five year housing land supply or meet the 75% Housing Delivery Test would lose the ability to refuse speculative housing outside settlements" (quote checked word for word against the local text). It appears in a template lobbying email to MPs about the December 2025 consultation draft. It describes in campaign terms what draft S5(1)(j) would do. It does not say how the final August 2026 Framework is applied, and it does not argue against appeal evidence. It leaves out the other S5(1)(j) limbs, such as being physically well-related to a settlement. Treat it as a campaign description of the draft, not a stance against SUP. Appeal evidence (e.g. Tandridge, Newchapel) still shows a supply shortfall does not decide the case on its own.
