# Opinion: Rural Roar (The Guildford Dragon, Cllr Paula Moss)

## Summary

A campaign opinion piece from the vice-chair of Effingham Parish Council, who founded "Rural Roar" to seek reform of the grey belt test. It says Guildford's AECOM Green Belt assessment puts 551 of 575 areas (almost 96%) in grey belt, and that the test "excludes villages", so land around villages falls into grey belt by default (similar figures are cited for Sevenoaks, Tonbridge and Malling, North Somerset and Buckinghamshire). It uses appeal-outcome statistics as its main evidence: a GapSense review of 710 appeal decisions (79% success where grey belt was decisive), HM Treasury's Budget 2025 claim that 80% of major residential grey-belt appeals were allowed since the December 2024 National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF), and CPRE (Campaign to Protect Rural England) / Good Plan Consulting case research. It calls for the grey belt test to be suspended for sites around villages. It gives no procedural guidance on applying the August 2026 Framework; note that the statistics it cites mostly pre-date that Framework.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- No direct instruction to officers. It asks residents to lobby MPs and calls for an immediate suspension of the grey belt test for sites around villages until it is reformed.
- It reports that Guildford's planning team will not assess whether individual developments "fundamentally undermine" the remaining Green Belt until its spatial strategy is settled, so grey-belt applications are being decided without that check.

## Positions against our propositions

- **GB (agrees)**: grey belt is won at the village edge because the purposes test does not credit villages. "Regardless of its role in meeting the purposes of the green belt, most land around villages now falls by default into grey belt." and "It’s labelled grey belt regardless, because the test excludes villages from its assessments — entirely." (section "Guildford’s “grey belt” according to AECOM Study")
- **GB (silent on "passing GB7 is not approval")**: it says grey-belt status usually wins on outcome rates, but it accepts that "other factors [are] weighed in the balance". That fits our point that passing GB7 is not approval. It does not dispute the point; it reports a high approval rate. The rates it cites (79-80%) come from the December 2024 NPPF, not the August 2026 one, and they also mean about 1 in 5 grey-belt appeals still failed. "Of course, there are other factors weighed in the balance. However, the evidence now suggests that designation as grey belt appears to win out in the end." (before the heading "Grey belt status secures development status 80 per cent of the time")
- **METH (agrees)**: it relies on counts of appeal outcomes as evidence of how the policy works. "A large-scale review by GapSense of 710 substantive appeal decisions found 79 per cent succeeded where grey belt status was the deciding factor." (section "Grey belt status secures development status 80 per cent of the time"). It also cites HM Treasury: "an unprecedented 80 per cent of major residential appeals located on grey belt land have been approved". Note these figures are under the December 2024 NPPF, not the August 2026 one.
