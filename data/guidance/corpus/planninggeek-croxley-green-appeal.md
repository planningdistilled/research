# Croxley Green appeal: 600 homes, disputed grey belt and the new NPPF

## Summary

Previews the undecided Three Rivers inquiry (appeal 6004972) for up to 600 homes on 40 hectares of Metropolitan Green Belt at Croxley Green. Officers recommended approval on 22 January 2026, treating the land as grey belt under the December 2024 NPPF. Councillors refused, finding a strong contribution to checking sprawl. The council later withdrew its Green Belt and woodland refusal reasons. The article sets out the GB7 grey-belt tests and the GB8 Golden Rules, and notes that inappropriate development needs very special circumstances under GB6. It says the grey-belt label alone cannot decide the case, and that the eventual decision will be a practical example rather than a legal precedent.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Decide the appeal against the development plan and the August 2026 NPPF in force at the date of decision, not the Framework at committee.
- Remember grey belt is still Green Belt; check the GB7 tests (not fundamentally undermining purposes, evidenced unmet need, sustainable location) and the GB8 Golden Rules for major housing.
- Where development is inappropriate, apply GB6: very special circumstances require Green Belt harm and any other harm to be clearly outweighed.
- Note that the Inspector is not bound by the council's withdrawal of refusal reasons.

## Positions against our propositions

- **GB (grey belt / Green Belt): agrees.** "That is why the label alone cannot decide Croxley." (section "Grey belt does not make the other planning issues vanish"). Grey belt and passing GB7 are not approval.
- **DIV (council vs inspector divergence): silent.** The article records an officer-vs-councillor split at committee (22 Jan 2026) on whether the land strongly contributes to checking sprawl: "So the disagreement was not simply officers wanting homes and councillors opposing them. It was a genuine disagreement about how Green Belt policy applied to this land and how the benefits and harms should be weighed." (section "Why officers and councillors originally disagreed"). This is a single-case description, not a position on council-vs-Inspector divergence: the officer view was formed under the December 2024 NPPF, the council later withdrew its Green Belt reason, and no Inspector decision exists yet. Useful context (divergence can arise inside a council on the grey-belt contribution judgement) but not a stance on DIV.
- **METH (our method): agrees (weak, passing remark).** "The eventual decision will not be a legal precedent in the way a court judgment can be. But it could become a useful practical example of how Inspectors apply the August 2026 Green Belt and grey belt policies." (section "Why the eventual decision will be worth reading"). A one-line aside about a single pending appeal, not a critique of counting outcomes or of empirical analysis. It supports treating Inspector decisions as practical evidence of how the Framework is applied; the precedent caveat is one our method already observes (we never claim appeal decisions bind). Adversarial check (2 Oct 2026): quote verbatim; downgraded from "qualifies".
