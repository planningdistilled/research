## Summary

A law firm overview (authors Joanne McIvor and Alex Duncliffe-Vines) of the August 2026 National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF). It explains the move from numbered paragraphs to policy codes and warns that older appeal decisions and reports citing paragraph numbers may be of limited value. It explains that the paragraph 11(d) "tilted balance" has gone, replaced by S4 (inside settlements), S5 (categories outside settlements) and S6 (neighbourhood plans). A five-year housing land supply (5YHLS) shortfall or a Housing Delivery Test (HDT) result below 75% now counts only as evidence of unmet need under S5, and Annex A gives very limited weight to materially inconsistent plan policies. It also covers station-area density, the medium-site category, the national settlement and strategic-site definitions, grey belt and the DM1 planning statement.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Check old paragraph references in appeal decisions, committee reports and legal agreements against the new policy codes.
- Run a different planning balance from the old tilted balance: supply shortfall feeds S5 as evidence of unmet need, not an automatic trigger.
- Give very limited weight to materially inconsistent plan policies (unless the plan was examined or made under the 2026 Framework); ask whether a policy is aligned, not how old it is.
- Require robust evidence before treating land as grey belt.

## Positions against our propositions

- **SUP (agrees)**: "Instead, under the new framework, those factors become evidence of unmet housing need that may be relevant when applying policy S5." (section on the presumption). Shortfall opens a route (S5) rather than deciding the case.
- **A2 (silent)**: "development plan policies which are materially inconsistent with the new national decision-making policies should generally be given very limited weight" and "Policies that are inconsistent with the new Framework could now carry substantially less weight in decision-making processes." (Annex A section and conclusion). This restates Annex A in summary form only; it does not address whether the weight reduction applies to a whole policy or only the inconsistent clause, nor any heritage, design or access policies, so it neither supports nor contradicts A2. (Adversarial check 2026-10-02: downgraded from "qualifies".)
- **METH (agrees)**: "Those references may now be of limited value unless they are checked against the new policy framework." (section on restructuring). This supports confining the evidence base to decisions taken under the August 2026 Framework, as our 971-decision set does.
