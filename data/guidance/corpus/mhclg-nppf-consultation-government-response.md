## Summary

The government's 155-page response to the December 2025 to March 2026 consultation explains, question by question, why the final August 2026 National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF) differs from the draft. It is the main official statement of policy intent. For our work the key passages are: the settlement definition amended "to address how the definition applies to villages within the Green Belt" (Q35, p.30) and GB4 changed so Green Belt villages are not identified as settlements in spatial strategies (Q132, p.90); S5(1)(j)(i) clarified to mean "physically" well-related (Q39, p.33); the new glossary "reasonable walking distance" of 800 metres, qualified by local barriers (Q133/137/138, p.93) and 400 metres for HC5 qualified by "route availability and quality" (Q161, p.109); Annex A para 2 changed to apply to parts of plan policies and only to material inconsistency (Q192, p.132); HO7 substantial weight to the benefits of additional homes (Q62); and the heritage "great" to "substantial" weight change said not to reduce protection (Q189). It also commits to Planning Advisory Service (PAS) training on viability, revised transport planning practice guidance (PPG) and possible guidance on identifying settlements.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Treat villages lying in the Green Belt as not identified settlements for the spatial strategy (GB4), and read the Annex B settlement definition accordingly.
- Read S5(1)(j)(i) "well-related" as "physically" well-related.
- Use the glossary "reasonable walking distance" (800 metres, about 10 minutes) for S5(1)(h), L3 and GB7, while allowing for physical barriers or constraints that discourage walking.
- Use quantitative and qualitative evidence alongside the Connectivity Tool when judging location sustainability under TR3.
- Give very limited weight only to the materially inconsistent parts of plan policies (Annex A para 2), not whole policies.
- Give substantial weight to the benefits of additional homes, including for particular groups (HO7); treat housing as an important public benefit in heritage balances even though it is not listed in HE6(4).
- Treat any harm to a designated heritage asset as of considerable importance and weight; following the NPPF discharges the statutory duties.

## Positions against our propositions

- **SH2 - agrees.** "address how the definition applies to villages within the Green Belt" (Q35 response, p.30) and "changes to policy GB4 to recognise that villages lying in the Green Belt should not be identified as settlements for the purpose of the spatial strategy in the development plan" (Q132 response, p.90). Direct support for treating a washed-over Green Belt village as outside the settlement route.
- **SH1 - silent.** "Changes have been made to clarify the relationship with development plans where settlements have yet to be defined" (Q35 response, p.30) and "We will consider whether additional planning guidance would be helpful to assist the identification of settlements in plans." (Q35 response, p.31). Context only: the response says nothing about what weight local hierarchy tiers carry; it records amendments to the Annex B definition and a possible future guidance note on identifying settlements in plans. That is consistent with tiers mattering through plan identification, but it does not qualify SH1. Watch item: any later Planning Practice Guidance on settlement identification.
- **S5 - silent (context only).** "sub-paragraph (1)(j)(i) of the policy already requires that it be ‘well-related’ to an existing settlement, but further clarification has been added that this means ‘physically’ well-related." (Q39 response, p.33). This explains the draft-to-final wording change that put "physically" into S5(1)(j)(i); it gives no view on what "physically well-related" means. Our four competing readings (physical only, physical not visual, settlement form, accessibility or character read in) are all readings of the final word "physically", so the response does not resolve or narrow them. At most it is drafting-intent context that a party could cite against reading accessibility or character into the test.
- **TR - not applicable (different test).** "the Framework glossary defines a “reasonable walking distance” as 800 metres or approximately 10 minutes’ walking time. The definition also recognises that local circumstances may affect the practicality of walking this distance, including where physical barriers or other constraints discourage pedestrian access." (Q133/137/138 response, p.93). This explains the Annex B glossary term used only for the well-connected-station tests (S5(1)(h), L3(2)(c), GB7(1)(h), here in the GB3 station context). It does not cover TR3 or the GB7(1)(g)(iii) village-edge access test that our TR proposition is about, so it takes no position on route quality versus distance there. Even for the station tests, the final glossary text itself lists "route availability and quality" as a reason the 800 metres can fall.
- **TR - agrees.** "around 400 metres, or around five minutes’ walk, unless topography, route availability and quality or physical barriers would prevent or discourage walking up to that distance." (Q161 response, p.109, HC5 context). Expressly names route quality as able to defeat a walking distance.
- **A2 - agrees.** "It now makes clear that the policy can apply to parts of development plan policies to address concerns that entire policies could be given limited weight based on partial inconsistency. In addition, inconsistency is to be assessed based on material differences" (Q192 response, p.132). Direct support for "cuts only the offending clause".
- **SUP - silent.** "These make clear that substantial weight should be given to the benefits of providing additional homes, including accommodation to meet the needs of particular groups." (Q62 response, p.40, on policy HO7) and "meeting the need for homes is already an important benefit that should be considered as set out in other parts of the Framework." (Q189 response, p.130, on HE6/HE4 public benefits). Context only: both passages explain drafting of the housing-benefit weight (HO7, particular-group needs) and the heritage public-benefits list; neither addresses housing land supply shortfall, the triggers it opens, or whether supply can decide a case. Substantial weight to housing benefits applies with or without a shortfall, which is consistent with SUP (supply opens the gate; the benefit weight is a separate input to the balance), not a qualification of it.
- **DIV - agrees (respondents' view).** "Other respondents raised concerns about subjectivity, noting the potential for differing interpretations either to justify development or to strengthen protection." (Q188/189 response, p.129). Consultees anticipated divergent heritage method; the government's answer was clarity of wording, not a method change.

## Leads

- Planning Advisory Service (PAS) training and resources on viability assessment (pp.26 and 97): first evidence of a promised officer training programme on the 2026 Framework.
- National model conditions to be developed with the Planning Inspectorate "before the end of the year" (Q29, p.27).
- Revised transport planning practice guidance on the vision-led approach and the Connectivity Tool in rural areas (Q150/152).
- Possible planning guidance on identifying settlements in plans (Q35) and on strategic Green Belt reviews (Q132).
- Public Sector Equality Duty (PSED) assessment published alongside.
- Design and Placemaking planning practice guidance final version promised in the autumn (Q148 area).
