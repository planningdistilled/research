# The New NPPF: What the August 2026 Rewrite Means for You

## Summary

A plain-English overview of the August 2026 Framework for lay and small-developer readers. It explains that S3-S5 replace the old paragraph 11 "tilted balance" with a permanent presumption in favour of suitably located development, so contested cases turn more on whether a site is suitably located than on five-year housing land supply figures. It covers stations and "medium development", L2 (substantial weight to extra homes on underused plots), stronger protection for pubs, and substantial weight against intentional unauthorised development. On plan currency, it says local plan policies that sit awkwardly against national decision-making policies generally get very limited weight unless the plan was examined against the new Framework.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Stop relying on old paragraph numbers (paragraph 11, paragraph 84); check every citation against the new policy codes.
- Argue the case on whether the site is suitably located (S3-S5), not on 5YHLS figures.
- Give very limited weight to local plan policies that conflict with national decision-making policies unless the plan was examined against the 2026 Framework.
- Give substantial weight against development where the evidence shows the unauthorised work was intentional.
- Apply the new Framework to applications and appeals already in the system, with weight depending on the case.

## Positions against our propositions

- **SUP: agrees.** "this shifts a good deal of the argument in contested cases toward whether a site is suitably located in the first place, rather than toward technical disputes over five year housing land supply figures" (Section "A permanent presumption, not a tilted balance"). Supports our reading that supply no longer decides the case; location (the S4/S5 route) does.
- **A2: silent (restates policy).** "Local Plan policies that sit awkwardly against the new national decision-making policies are, in general, now to be given very limited weight, unless the plan in question was examined and adopted against this new Framework." (Section "When does it apply, and to what?"). A lay paraphrase of Annex A paragraph 2 itself, including its own carve-out for plans examined against the 2026 Framework. It does not address whether the cut falls on the whole policy or only the offending clause, nor which kinds of policy keep weight, so it neither supports nor contradicts our reading.
- **S5: silent (checked).** "this presumption applies more broadly across suitable locations, both within and beyond settlement boundaries" (Section "A permanent presumption, not a tilted balance"). A loose lay paraphrase of S3-S5, not a position on how S5 works: it says the presumption covers *suitable* locations beyond settlement boundaries and that the argument turns on "whether a site is suitably located in the first place", which fits our reading that the S5(1) category decides the case outside settlements. "Settlement boundaries" is imprecise wording (local boundary rather than Annex B "settlement"), not an argued view on SH2; it does not address S5(4) or S5(1)(j).
