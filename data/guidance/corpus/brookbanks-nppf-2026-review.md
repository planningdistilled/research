## Summary

A long review by Brookbanks, a multi-disciplinary engineering, transport and planning consultancy, with sections from its planning, cost, technical, sustainability and transport directors. The page body is rendered by script, so curl returned only the title; the text was read through WebFetch and is not held locally. It quotes the Annex B "settlement" definition in full, including the exclusion of villages that lie within and are defined as part of the Green Belt, and explains S3 as sending decision-makers to S4 or S5 by location. It predicts that the main appeal battleground will be how settlements are defined (inconsistent maps, outdated plans) and S5(1)(j), "where speculative housing and logistics developments may find most success". On transport it notes the Connectivity Tool's raised status (to be used "alongside other relevant quantitative and qualitative evidence"), TR3's five principles, and the unchanged "severe" test in TR6.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Decide first whether a site is inside a settlement (S4) or outside (S5), using the Annex B definition, which excludes washed-over Green Belt villages.
- Use the Department for Transport Connectivity Tool together with other quantitative and qualitative evidence when assessing location and connectivity (TR3).
- Apply the HDT (Housing Delivery Test) below-75% trigger as deemed unmet need for S5(1)(j).
- Apply the Golden Rules (GB8) to major Green Belt housing; give substantial weight to compliance.
