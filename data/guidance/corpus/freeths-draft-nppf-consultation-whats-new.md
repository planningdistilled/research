## Summary

A Freeths summary of the December 2025 consultation draft of the National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF). The date comes from the page's datePublished metadata, 2026-02-11. It describes the move to coded policies and the much more frequent use of "substantial weight". It says draft S4 and S5 replace paragraph 11d and "reframe the tilted balance". Under draft S5, "only" listed forms of development should be approved outside settlements. These include development near stations in the top 60 Travel to Work Areas (TTWAs) and development meeting an evidenced unmet need. For housing, unmet need is defined by a missing five-year supply or a Housing Delivery Test (HDT) result below 75%. The note also quotes the draft implementation wording: plan policies "in any way inconsistent" with national decision-making policies get very limited weight. The final version changed this to "materially inconsistent". The note is about the draft, so where the draft differs from the final text it is only background.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Under the draft, approve development within settlements unless adverse effects substantially outweigh the benefits (the listed impacts are not a closed list).
- Approve "only" listed categories outside settlements, under the same balance.
- Expect developers to argue "evidenced unmet need" for narrow types of development (care, self-build, specific employment).
- Expect plan policies that duplicate or modify national decision-making policies to lose weight.

## Positions against our propositions

- **S5, agrees.** "This sets out that ‘only’ certain forms of development should be approved outside of settlements" (section "Development & balance", draft text). This supports our view that the S5(1) category is the whole case outside settlements.
- **SUP, agrees.** "For housing this is defined by a local planning authority being unable to demonstrate a five-year housing supply or scoring lower than 75% on the Housing Delivery Test and is therefore similar in effect to existing policy." (section "Development & balance", on draft S5(1)(j)). A supply shortfall is what opens the S5(1)(j) route. The note does not say the shortfall decides the case.

## Leads

- Draft Annex A wording ("in any way inconsistent ... except where ... examined and adopted against this Framework") against the final "materially inconsistent". The change bears on how far Annex A para 2 cuts plan policies (A2).
- Draft E2.2 on unmet need and market signals: check whether it survived into the final E2/E3.
