# Green Belt is now Grey Belt - LGBC & CPRE report

## Summary

A campaign report by the London Green Belt Council (LGBC) and CPRE Hertfordshire on the effect of the December 2024 grey belt policy on the London Metropolitan Green Belt. It counts appeals: ten of twelve London Green Belt appeals from February to December 2025 were allowed on grey belt grounds (83%, against a historic rate of about 40%), and cites Marrons research for the Land Promotion and Development Federation (LPDF) finding 84% of Green Belt appeals allowed February to September 2025. It argues the grey belt definition, by excluding purposes (c) countryside encroachment and (e) urban regeneration, lets high-quality countryside be classed grey belt, illustrated by the Hemel Hempstead (Leighton Buzzard Road) appeal. It quotes the December 2025 draft definition (policy GB2, Annex B) and recommends that Green Belt be released only after very special circumstances have been proven against all five purposes. It is about the 2024 regime and the draft, not the final August 2026 text, and gives no officer instruction.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Nothing addressed to officers directly; recommendations are to government.
- Recommends Green Belt release only after very special circumstances have been proven, considering all five purposes in draft GB2.
- Recommends brownfield-first and that Brownfield Land Registers be kept up to date.

## Positions against our propositions

- **METH: agrees.** "From February to December 2025 there were twelve London Green Belt planning appeals. Ten were granted permission on grey belt grounds (83% where normally only 40% have been successful)." (Key findings bullet list). An appeal-counting method like ours, used to show how grey belt is applied in practice. Sample of 12; compares against a 10-year baseline we do not have. December 2024 regime.
- **METH: agrees.** "This showed that 84% of Green Belt appeals were being approved compared with 40%, as an average, over the last ten years." (Introduction, paragraph on Marrons research for LPDF). A second appeal-count study (developer-commissioned) reaching the same result; confirms that sector actors on both sides treat appeal outcome rates as evidence.
- **GB: agrees.** "The developer’s counsel argued, amongst other issues, that the site was grey belt, due to its definition specifically exempting encroachment from being a permitted reason for refusal (NPPF Glossary), and the Inspector agreed with the appellant and allowed the appeal." (Hertfordshire section, Hemel Hempstead (Leighton Buzzard Road) example). Supports grey belt being won where encroachment is the main harm. This was a town edge, not a village edge.
- **GB: silent (campaign characterisation, not a contrary finding).** "In December 2024, the Government updated the National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF) introducing the grey belt policy that allows development in the Green Belt with little or no restriction." (Executive Summary bullet list). Rhetorical description of the policy's permissiveness under the December 2024 regime; it does not analyse whether passing the grey belt test amounts to approval, nor village-edge outcomes or very special circumstances (VSC). The report's own figures (2 of 12 London appeals refused) and its note that the Golden Rules apply to grey belt schemes are consistent with our finding that passing the grey belt test is not approval. Not a disagreement.
