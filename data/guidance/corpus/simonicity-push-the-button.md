## Summary

A blog post by Simon Ricketts, a partner at the planning law firm Town Legal, written two weeks after the August 2026 National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF) was published. It lists 21 "buttons" a scheme promoter should try to press: the presumption in S3 to S5, and the policies that give "substantial weight" to, or "support" for, particular benefits (for example HO7 homes, E2 business land, L2 effective use of land, GB8 Golden Rules, DP3 outstanding design, HE6 to HE9 heritage). Most of the post simply reproduces the policy text. Its own comment is short. Outside settlements, getting into the S5(1) list (categories a to j) is "vital". In the Green Belt, the scheme must also fall within one of the GB7(1) categories (a to h) so that it is not inappropriate, and grey belt development then gets the same strong presumption.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Apply S3: use S4 inside settlements and S5 outside them. Where a scheme accords with an up-to-date plan and the national policies, approve it without delay.
- Outside settlements, check first whether the scheme falls within S5(1) categories a to j. Ricketts treats this as decisive.
- In the Green Belt, check whether the scheme falls within a GB7(1) category. If it does, the S5 presumption applies.
- List every policy that gives substantial weight or support, because the scheme may engage several of them. Failing to engage one "may in some cases count against you in the planning balance".

## Positions against our propositions

- **S5, agrees.** "For developments outside settlements, falling within the a to j list is vital." (body, after the S5 extract). This supports our view that the S5(1) category is the whole case outside settlements.
- **GB, agrees (restating policy).** "If your land is green belt the development proposed must not be “inappropriate development” and must therefore fall within one of the a to h criteria in policy GB7 1 – so grey belt development, for instance, also qualifies for this strong pro-development presumption." (body, after the S5 extract). This is a summary of how GB7 feeds the S5 presumption, not a view on outcomes. It is consistent with our point that passing GB7 is not approval: what the scheme gets is a presumption (approve unless the adverse effects substantially outweigh the benefits), and the balance still has to be struck. It says nothing about where grey belt is won or about very special circumstances (VSC).
