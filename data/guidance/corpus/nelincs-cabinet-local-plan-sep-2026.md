## Summary

This is a 282-page Cabinet pack: a short covering report with the Pre-Submission Draft Local Plan (the Regulation 19 version) attached. The covering report briefs members on risk. The council cannot show a five-year housing land supply against the standard method figure of 621 homes a year, and it is seeing speculative applications on unallocated sites. The only mention of the August 2026 National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF) is one "policy misalignment" risk paragraph. It says the new Framework "provides an even stronger emphasis on housing delivery and approving schemes in sustainable locations without delay" and that it "is being considered". The report does not analyse S3 to S5, Annex A or Annex B. The attached plan is still written against the December 2024 NPPF (it cites old paragraph numbers). It includes a four-tier settlement hierarchy (Draft Strategic Policy 3) that rests on a points-based Settlement Accessibility Assessment (2023).

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Treat the new NPPF as a risk to be tracked through the local plan risk assessment, not yet as a reason to change the plan.
- Understand that without a five-year supply "national planning policy takes precedence over local policy", and that inspectors are more likely to overturn refusals of speculative schemes.
- Press on to Regulation 19 consultation and submission by 31 December 2026 under the legacy (transitional) arrangements, so that the plan gains weight and supply can be restored.
- Use the settlement hierarchy as "the framework for the Council's decisions on the location and scale of development". Development should be "commensurate with a settlement's position in the settlement hierarchy".

## Positions against our propositions

- **SUP (agrees, weakly; restates 2024 policy):** "Not meeting the five-year supply means national planning policy takes precedence over local policy and there is an effective ‘presumption in favour of sustainable development’ meaning even if local decisions refuse such applications, a Planning Inspector is more likely to overturn such refusals. This does not mean all speculative applications will happen and the Council has been successful in a recent appeal. However, the bar is very much higher" (covering report, Risks, para 2.3). This is a plain-language risk briefing for councillors on the December 2024 tilted balance (para 2.2 ties the shortfall to "the changes to the NPPF in December 2024"). It is not an analysis of the August 2026 Framework or of S3 to S5. Read in full, it fits our reading: a shortfall makes refusal harder to defend ("more likely", "the bar is very much higher"), but it does not decide every case, and the council has won an appeal while in shortfall. "Takes precedence" is a loose lay summary of the tilted balance, not a different legal test. It is not a genuine qualification of SUP, and our analysis does not need to change.

## Leads

- Settlement Accessibility Assessment (2023) and Green Wedges Methodology and Evaluation (2023), North East Lincolnshire evidence base. These are points-based accessibility scoring and could be compared with our TR3 route-quality approach.
- The "recent appeal" the council won during its supply shortfall (not named in the report).
