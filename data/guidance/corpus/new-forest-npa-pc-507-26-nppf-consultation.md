## Summary

A planning committee report by the Interim Head of Planning & Place (David Illsley) briefing members on the December 2025 draft National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF) and recommending the main points of the National Park Authority's consultation response. It explains the new split between plan-making policies and national decision-making policies (NDMPs). It tells members that local policies inconsistent with the NDMPs will "immediately be given very limited weight", and sets out how National Park protection moves from old paragraphs 189 and 190 to draft N4. The points of concern are: "substantial" replacing "great" weight; the loss of the "highest status of protection" wording; a weaker major-development test; the deleted reference to the National Parks Circular; and draft S5, which it says could allow significant development in remote, unsustainable places because adverse effects are judged only against national policies. It asks that plans submitted by December 2026 under the current system not be out of date on adoption.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Read the revised Framework, NDMPs included, "as a whole", and apply station-led and density policies "judiciously" in a National Park, checking against all NDMPs, including landscape protection.
- Expect local policies inconsistent with NDMPs to get very limited weight under draft Annex A, which threatens plans being prepared now.
- Watch draft S5 categories (reuse of buildings, previously developed land, "limited" infilling in groups of houses) for landscape harm, because draft S5 does not refer to the development plan.

## Positions against our propositions

- **A2, not applicable (restates the draft).** Section 3.5 (Annex A bullet, p.6) only summarises the December 2025 draft Annex A: "day of its publication. Development plan policies which are “in any way”" inconsistent with the national decision-making policies "should be given very limited weight". Its concern is the effect on local plans being prepared now, not whether a whole policy or only the inconsistent part loses weight, so it takes no stance on our clause-only reading. The final August 2026 text reads "(or parts of those policies) which are materially inconsistent", which supports A2.
- **S5, not applicable (checked).** It criticises the December 2025 draft S5 wording, asking that the adverse-effects limb refer to "the adopted development plan in addition to" the national decision-making policies (section 4.2 box, S5 bullet, p.8). This is a consultation drafting point about the draft text. It says nothing about whether the S5(1) category decides the case, about S5(4), or about S5(1)(j), so it does not qualify our S5 proposition.
