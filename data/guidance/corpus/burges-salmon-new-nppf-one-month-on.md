## Summary

A short law-firm note (authors Sarah Sutherland and Matthew Tucker) that reads the first month of Planning Inspectorate (PINS) appeal decisions under the August 2026 National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF) to show how inspectors are applying it. It covers the procedural question of whether inspectors go back to the parties for comments on the new Framework (three appeals: 6005664, 6008314, 6007184), and two Vale of White Horse inquiry decisions on Policy S5(1)(j) (development outside settlements meeting an evidenced unmet need): 6005108 and 6005809. In 6005108 the inspector gave only moderate weight to conflict with local plan policies because S5(1)(j) "undermines the thrust" of them. In 6005809 the inspector found compliance with S5(1)(j)(i) and gave substantial weight to 249 homes and 35% affordable housing. Of all the sources in this batch, this is the one whose method is closest to ours: it uses appeal decisions as evidence of how the Framework is applied.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Expect inspectors to apply the new Framework at once. Whether they re-consult the parties depends on whether changed policies could benefit a party.
- Do not assume an earlier dismissed appeal on a similar scheme settles the matter. Revisions to the Framework and grey belt can change the material considerations.
- Treat S5 as an important material consideration in the overall planning balance. Where S5(1)(j) is met, conflict with local spatial policies may carry reduced (moderate) weight.
- Weigh housing delivered within five years, and policy-compliant affordable housing, substantially in favour where S5(1)(j) is complied with.

## Positions against our propositions

- **METH, agrees.** "we are now seeing appeal decisions published which refer to the new NPPF and grapple with its policies" (opening paragraph). The note uses appeal decisions as the evidence of how the Framework is applied, as we do, though it uses five hand-picked cases rather than coded counts.
- **METH, agrees (consistency principle; not a critique of our method).** Burges Salmon paraphrases inspector 6007184: "although it is important to determine similar cases in a similar manner, the material considerations differed in each appeal following the revisions to the NPPF and the introduction of grey belt land. The Inspector therefore determines the appeal on its own merits, and on the evidence before him." (third bullet, appeal 6007184, para 5 as reported; these are the firm's words, not a direct quote from the decision). This restates the settled rule that earlier appeals are material for consistency but not binding precedent. It says nothing against counting or coding decisions. If anything it backs our choice to use only decisions made under the August 2026 NPPF, because it treats pre-revision decisions as weak guides once grey belt and the new policies apply. The "will not be universal" remark (first bullet) is about whether inspectors re-consult the parties on the new Framework, not about method. No change to our analysis is needed. The only caution is the usual one: outcome rates describe patterns, and each case still turns on its own evidence.
- **A2, qualifies.** "He notes that Policy S5(1)(j) undermines the thrust of relevant local plan policies and so only moderate weight is given to the conflict between the appeal scheme and those policies" (appeal 6005108, paras 137-139 as reported). The note only reports this inspector's finding; it does not argue for a general rule. The weight cut comes from S5(1)(j) as a national material consideration in the balance, not expressly from Annex A para 2, and the note does not name which local policies were affected. It is one counter-example of the kind our pattern 9 already counts (about 13 cut, about 17 kept: "some inspectors still cut spatial-policy weight"), and 6005108 is already in our dataset. No change to A2 needed.
- **S5, agrees.** "He summarises S5 as defining categories of development which should be approved unless the benefits of doing so would be substantially outweighed by any adverse effects" (appeal 6005108) and "Although this meets rather than exceeds the local policy requirement, it weighs substantially in favour of the scheme which complies with S5(1)(j)." (appeal 6005809, para 80). Both cases turn on whether the scheme fits the S5(1)(j) category.
