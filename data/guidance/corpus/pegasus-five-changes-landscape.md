# 2026 NPPF: Five Key Changes for Landscape Planning (Pegasus Group, Andy Cook)

## Summary

A landscape-planning briefing on the August 2026 Framework. It says the natural environment policy now uses the words "landscape character" (the 2024 text said "intrinsic character and beauty of the countryside"), but makes no matching general reference to visual amenity. It stresses that landscape effects and visual effects (as in Landscape and Visual Impact Assessment, LVIA) must be kept apart. It sets out Policy N4 on protected landscapes and their setting (including tranquillity and dark skies), N3 on trees, the Annex B glossary definitions of ancient woodland and veteran trees, and green infrastructure (DP3, N1, N2). On Green Belt, it notes the footnote 7 qualification has gone from the grey belt definition, that the Annex E assessment method is now in the Framework, and that Green Belt purposes must be kept separate from landscape character.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Assess landscape character expressly under the natural environment policy, and keep it apart from visual effects.
- For National Parks, National Landscapes and the Broads (N4): give substantial weight to natural beauty, refuse major development unless exceptional circumstances apply, and consider effects on their setting, tranquillity and dark skies.
- Treat trees as part of the design from the start (N3), and use the Annex B definitions of ancient and veteran trees and ancient woodland.
- Do not apply the old footnote 7 test when identifying grey belt; apply those protections through the relevant policies elsewhere.
- Do not mix up Green Belt purposes with landscape and visual harm.
