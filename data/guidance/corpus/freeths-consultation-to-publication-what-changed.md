## Summary

A blog post from the Freeths planning consultancy team comparing the final August 2026 National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF) with the December 2025 consultation draft. It says the Framework applies to live applications from publication, and that development plan policies "materially inconsistent" with national decision-making policies get very limited weight (Annex A para 2). It calls S4 and S5 the biggest change in years. It notes that S4's refusal threshold is now "substantial adverse impact", and that S5(1)(h) now covers stations in the top 80 Travel to Work Areas (TTWAs), up from 60. It flags uncertainty over how the 800m "reasonable walking distance" is measured. It covers the new Green Belt station category GB7(1)(h), the Golden Rules viability route (GB8(3)), the simpler grey belt definition and lower station densities (L3). It concludes that the Framework is "clearly pro-development" and that its policies will be tested at appeal and in the courts.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Apply the new Framework to every live application, including those submitted under the December 2024 version.
- Give very limited weight to development plan policies that are materially inconsistent with the national decision-making policies.
- Treat S4 refusals as needing a "substantial adverse impact", which is a higher bar than "unacceptable impact".
- For S5(1)(h), check that the station is well-connected (Annex B: four trains or trams an hour overall, or two in one direction) and that the site is within 800m or about a 10-minute walk. The measuring points are not defined, and some flexibility is expected.
- Read S5(1)(j) "unmet need" as covering employment as well as housing. Housing Delivery Test (HDT) results below 75% may engage it.
- Treat GB7(1)(h) station schemes as not inappropriate in the Green Belt, with no very special circumstances (VSC) needed. Major schemes must also meet the GB8 Golden Rules.
- Do not trade design quality for support in principle.

## Positions against our propositions

- **S5, agrees.** "Arguably, policies S4 and, in particular S5 are the biggest change in national planning policy in recent years." and "The policy has the potential to bring forward sites that may previously have been considered unacceptable." (section "Development & balance"). This supports our view that the S5 category drives the case outside settlements.
- **SUP, agrees.** "thus potentially engaging the tilted balance under S5(1)(j)" (section "Development & balance", on HDT results below 75%). Under-delivery is framed as something that potentially opens the S5(1)(j) route, not as something that decides the case.
- **TR, not applicable (different test).** "Annex B defines “reasonable walking distance” as being within 800m, or around a 10-minute walk, of a well-connected station. However, the NPPF does not specify how that distance should be measured." (section "Development & balance", on S5(1)(h)). This restates the Annex B definition for the station categories (S5(1)(h), GB7(1)(h)) and flags that the measuring points are undefined. It says nothing about TR3 or GB7(1)(g)(iii), or about whether route quality or distance decides those tests, so it neither agrees nor disagrees with TR. It does not show any decision or reasoning that would make our TR analysis change. (Re-checked 2 Oct 2026; earlier coding as "qualifies" withdrawn.)
- **GB, qualifies.** "As a result, where the relevant policy tests are met, planning permission should be granted without the need to demonstrate very special circumstances." (section "Green Belt", on GB7(1)(h)). On a closer reading this is not a disagreement. The sentence is about the Green Belt test only: a GB7(1)(h) station scheme is not inappropriate, so very special circumstances (VSC) are not needed. Permission is conditional on "the relevant policy tests" being met, with GB8 added for major schemes, and nothing says heritage, design, access or the S5 category stop applying. The shorthand "permission should be granted" is loose. It could be read as treating GB7 as a green light, which is the reading our GB proposition ("passing GB7 is not approval") warns against. But it is a summary of the policy, not a contrary view. Our analysis does not need to change.

## Leads

- Freeths predicts that measuring the 800m walking distance (from the site edge or centre, from a platform or the entrance) "will be tested at appeal". Check our decisions DB for S5(1)(h) and GB7(1)(h) cases on the measuring point.
- The Design and Placemaking Planning Practice Guidance (PPG) was not final when the NPPF was published. Check whether it has been published since.
- The Housing Delivery Test 2024 and 2025 results were published with the Framework.
