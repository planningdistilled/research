## Summary

A short news report of the housing and planning minister's (Matthew Pennycook's) letter to English councils on the August 2026 National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF). It lists the headline changes: the Framework now sets out "more clearly the respective roles of different types of plans and what needs to be considered in decision-making", a wider "default yes" around well-connected stations (top 80 travel-to-work areas by Gross Value Added, up from 60), density and parking flexibility, a new category of sites of around 1,500 homes, limits on local quantitative standards, and the statutory-consultee reforms. It gives no guidance on how to apply any specific policy test.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Nothing operational. It signals that the Framework now separates what plans do from what decisions must consider (transport, pollution, heritage, protected landscapes, safety of women and girls).
- Local quantitative standards in plans should be limited to matters where local variation is justified.
- Note the statutory-consultee changes (Sport England narrowed; Gardens Trust and Theatres Trust moved to notification).

None. Despite the headline, the article does not say how plan policies and national decision-making policies interact, so it is silent on A2 and SH1.
