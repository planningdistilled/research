## Summary

A heritage consultancy's explainer, published on the day the August 2026 NPPF came out, on Chapter 20 (policies HE1-HE10). It explains that "less than substantial harm" has gone. HE5 now asks whether an effect is positive, no effect, harm, substantial harm or total loss, and defines substantial harm as seriously affecting a key element of significance. HE6 replaces "great weight" with "substantial weight" for conserving designated assets, and any harm is still "a matter of considerable importance and weight". Harm is weighed against public benefits, and HE6 now names examples. It stresses that the effect on significance, not the scale of development, is what is assessed, and that Annex C names the Heritage Impact Assessment.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Identify significance through archaeological, architectural, artistic and historic interest, including the contribution of setting.
- Link the proposed change directly to that significance, and classify the effect using the HE5 categories.
- Apply the HE6 balance (designated assets) or HE7 balanced judgement (non-designated assets) where harm occurs; support positive effects.
- Use HE8 (World Heritage Sites), HE9 (Conservation Areas) and HE10 (archaeology, loss) where relevant.
- Do not simply swap old paragraph numbers into reports; use the new terminology.
