# The new 20% housing land supply buffer: what it means from July 2026 (Eddisons)

## Summary

A property consultancy note, published 22 July 2026, before the August 2026 Framework. It explains the transitional rule in paragraph 78 of the December 2024 NPPF: from 1 July 2026 a council whose housing requirement was adopted in the last five years, under an older Framework, and is 80% or less of local housing need must add a 20% buffer to its five-year housing land supply (in effect six years). Falling short engages the presumption in favour of sustainable development (the "tilted balance"). It runs alongside the Housing Delivery Test. The note sees a "window of opportunity" for landowners but says a well-argued case is still needed and that protections for designated land and assets still apply. It does not address the August 2026 policy codes.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Check three things for the buffer: when the requirement was adopted, which Framework it was set under, and how it compares with standard-method need.
- Expect closer scrutiny of housing land supply statements from July 2026.
- Weigh benefits against harm properly even where the tilted balance applies; supply shortfall does not remove protections for designated land and heritage.

## Positions against our propositions

- **SUP (agrees):** "None of this removes the need for a well-argued planning case. Tilted balance planning still requires the benefits of a scheme to be weighed properly against any harm, and protections for designated land and assets continue to apply regardless of an authority's supply position." (section "What does this mean for landowners and developers?"). Supports our reading that a shortfall opens the gate but does not decide the case.

## Leads

- Note the source pre-dates the August 2026 Framework and cites December 2024 paragraph 78; check whether the August 2026 Framework kept the 80% / 20% buffer rule (and under which code) before relying on it.
- It speaks of "Stronger prospects on appeal" in buffered authorities: our decisions database could test whether buffer-triggered shortfalls change appeal outcomes.
