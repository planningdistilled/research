## Summary

A long, text-led comparison of the December 2024 and August 2026 National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF), mapping 461 sub-clauses from 81 of the old paragraphs and counting phrase occurrences. It explains citation by policy code, the bar on using plan-making policies in decisions, and that the annexes are now policy. It describes the presumption as a routing question: S4 inside settlements, a closed S5(1) list outside with S5(4) "exceptional circumstances", and S5(5) taking Green Belt to GB6 to GB8. It notes the Annex B settlement definition excludes villages washed over by the Green Belt outright, while hamlets are excluded unless the plan defines them as settlements. On Annex A paragraph 2 it says the clause, not the whole policy, is the unit, and that age alone does not reduce weight. It also flags open questions, including whether Annex E binds grey belt assessments in decisions.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Cite policy code and clause (for example GB7(1)(a), S5(4), Annex A(2)), not paragraph numbers.
- Route first: S4 inside, S5 outside, Green Belt to GB6/GB7/GB8; check Annex B before choosing the route, and read the plan to see whether a hamlet is defined as a settlement.
- Weigh Annex A(2) clause by clause; a materially inconsistent part gets very limited weight, the rest is weighed on its own terms; age alone is no reason to reduce weight.
- Re-source "safe and suitable access" objections to TR3, TR4 and TR6 (there is no direct successor to December 2024 paragraph 115(b)).
- Treat reasonable walking distance as about 800 metres, or about 10 minutes' walk where route quality, topography or barriers discourage walking.
- Follow Annex E in grey belt assessments as a precaution.

## Positions against our propositions

- SH2 (agrees): "Villages "which lie within and are defined as part of the Green Belt in the development plan" are excluded outright. Only the second exclusion is absolute, so read the plan before advising that a hamlet takes the S5 route." ("The presumption is now a map to a test")
- SH1 (agrees): "Hamlets and scattered groups of houses outside predominantly built-up areas are excluded "unless specifically defined as a settlement in the development plan"." (same section). The local plan feeds the Annex B question, consistent with SH1(b).
- S5 (agrees): "Outside, S5(1) is a closed list: "Only certain forms of development should be approved outside settlements", 10 limbs, a to j, each carrying the same substantially-outweighed standard." (same section)
- A2 (agrees): "so a materially inconsistent part should receive very limited weight while Annex A(2) does not itself determine the weight of the remainder, which is assessed on its own terms." ("Annex A: what weight your development plan now carries"); also "Age alone is expressly not a reason to reduce weight, and that is the whole of it."
- TR (silent): "Reasonable walking distance is defined per policy: for S5, L3, and GB7, around 800 metres from a well-connected station, or around 10 minutes' walk time where topography, route availability and quality, or physical barriers would prevent or discourage walking that far." ("Five numbers to re-check", Density). This restates the Annex B glossary for the station-proximity tests (S5(1)(h), L3(2)(c), GB7(1)(h)) in a density discussion. It is not about TR3 or GB7(1)(g)(iii) access, and takes no view on whether route quality or distance decides. If anything, the glossary letting route quality replace the 800 m figure fits TR.
- SUP (agrees): "The words of the December 2024 11(d) trigger have gone, with no identified successor." (paragraph 11 mapping). Supply no longer switches on a tilted balance.
- GB (agrees): "where development would not be inappropriate under HC8 or GB7, the S5 approval expectation returns, applying S5(2)." (S5(5) discussion). Passing GB7 leads to a balance, not approval.
- GB (silent; related method point, not a stance): "Whether an applicant assessing grey belt under GB7(1)(g)(i) must follow Annex E is unresolved on the face of the text, and I flag rather than resolve it." ("The annexes are policy now"). This is an open question about which assessment method applies to the GB7(1)(g)(i) purposes test. It says nothing about where grey belt is won, about very special circumstances (VSC), or about whether passing GB7 means approval, so it does not qualify GB. Its working advice is to grade against Annex E "as a precaution rather than a concession". Useful as a lead: check whether inspectors in the decisions database cite Annex E when assessing grey belt.
