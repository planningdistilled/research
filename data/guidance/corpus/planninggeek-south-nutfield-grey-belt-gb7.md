## Summary

A news analysis of appeal 6009966 (Chedworth, Coopers Hill Road, South Nutfield, Tandridge; permission in principle (PIP) for up to five homes; dismissed 18 September 2026). The land was agreed to be grey belt and the unmet-need and wider-purposes limbs passed, but the site failed the GB7(1)(g)(iii) sustainable-location requirement: the village edge was about 800 metres away and the lanes had no continuous footways or street lighting, so pedestrians and cyclists would share the carriageway. Once GB7 failed the scheme was inappropriate development, and the housing supply shortfall (given substantial weight) did not amount to very special circumstances (VSC). It contrasts this with Hurst Green, which passed GB7 but failed on odour.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Do not stop at the grey-belt definition; work through every limb of the GB7 route, which operate cumulatively.
- Test walking, cycling and public-transport conditions on the ground rather than by distance alone.
- A sustainable location can be decided at PIP stage; it goes to suitability of location.
- If a GB7 criterion fails, go on to inappropriate-development harm (substantial weight), openness and VSC.
- Treat a previous appeal decision on the same site as an important material consideration where circumstances have not changed.

## Positions against our propositions

- TR (agrees): "The edge of South Nutfield was around 800 metres away, with many services a similar distance further on. The walking and cycling environment was the real difficulty." and "Coopers Hill Road and Kings Cross Lane did not provide continuous footways or street lighting. Pedestrians and cyclists would have to share the carriageway with motor traffic." (section "Why the location failed Policy GB7"); also "Test walking, cycling and public-transport conditions on the ground rather than by distance alone" (section "What should applicants check?"). Route quality, and walking in the carriageway, decided GB7(1)(g)(iii). The note lists lighting alongside footways, so it does not test our "lighting does not cure it" point.
- GB (agrees): "A site can therefore be grey belt and still fail to benefit from the policy exception if one of the remaining criteria is not met." (section "Grey-belt status was not the disputed point"); and "Those benefits did not clearly outweigh the Green Belt harm, loss of openness, countryside encroachment, character harm and unsustainable travel pattern. Very special circumstances were therefore not demonstrated." (section "Housing need was not enough to amount to very special circumstances").
- SUP (agrees): "The shortfall in housing land supply gave substantial weight to the benefit of providing up to five homes." (same section) - weight given, appeal still dismissed.
