## Summary

A news briefing from Out-Law, Pinsent Masons' legal news service, on the August 2026 National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF). It describes a general presumption for plan-compliant development (S3), applied more specifically within settlements (S4) and outside them where criteria are met (S5). This includes homes within reasonable walking distance (800m) of well-connected stations, and development meeting an evidenced unmet need, including where there is no five-year housing land supply or the Housing Delivery Test (HDT) result is below 75%. It says that where the presumption applies, local planning authorities (LPAs) must approve unless the benefits are substantially outweighed, assessed against the NPPF "not any local planning policies". It notes the shift from "significant" to "substantial" weight for renewables, repowering and water infrastructure. A partner, Emma Barkas, advises applicants to address the new NPPF in committee addenda or updated application documents.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Approve development that falls within S4, or within an S5 category, unless the benefits are substantially outweighed by adverse effects, assessed against the national decision-making policies.
- Give substantial weight to the energy-security, economic and net zero benefits of renewables. Give substantial weight to the benefit of reusing an established site for repowering, and to listed water-infrastructure benefits.
- Make sure reports on applications heading to committee address the new NPPF, for example through an update addendum.

## Positions against our propositions

- **A2, silent (restates S4/S5 wording; checked 2026-10-02).** "Where the presumption applies, local planning authorities would be obliged to approve the proposed development “unless the benefits of doing so would be substantially outweighed by any adverse effects” – something which they would have to assess with reference to the NPPF, not any local planning policies." (paragraph on the presumption outside settlements). This is a paraphrase of the S4(1) and S5(1) text, which says the balance is "assessed against the national decision-making policies in this Framework". It describes the inside of the presumption balance. It does not discuss Annex A para 2, the weight of plan policies that conflict with the Framework, or the section 38(6) development-plan judgment that sits around the presumption. So it is not a stance against A2. One point for our own wording: A2 is about how much weight plan policies keep under Annex A para 2. It should not be read as saying plan policies count inside the S4/S5 "substantially outweighed" balance itself.
- **SUP, agrees.** "The presumption would also, subject to certain caveats, apply outside settlement boundaries where development would address an evidenced unmet need – including where a local planning authority cannot demonstrate a five-year housing land supply" (same paragraph). A supply shortfall opens the S5 route, "subject to certain caveats". The briefing does not treat it as deciding the case.
- **TR, silent.** "the presumption outside of settlement boundaries will apply where the development is proposed within 800 metres of train, underground or tram stations" (paragraph beginning "Broadly speaking"). This is a hedged shorthand summary of the station category ("Broadly speaking, while there are further criteria to satisfy"). The preceding paragraph quotes the policy wording "within reasonable walking distance of a well-connected station". The briefing restates the policy and does not say how walking distance is measured or whether route quality matters. It takes no position on TR3 or GB7(1)(g)(iii), so it does not qualify our TR proposition.
