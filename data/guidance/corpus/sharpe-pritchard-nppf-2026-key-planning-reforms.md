# NPPF 2026: Key Planning Reforms for LPAs and Developers (Sharpe Pritchard, Rebecca Stewart)

## Summary

A first-look briefing from a firm that mostly advises local planning authorities (LPAs). It explains that the National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF) presumption now turns on whether a site is inside a settlement (policy S4) or outside one (policy S5). Outside settlements only the listed S5 categories are supported, and anything else needs "exceptional circumstances". It reminds LPAs that section 38(6) of the Planning and Compulsory Purchase Act 2004 still applies, and that Annex A gives very limited weight to plan policies "to the extent" they are materially inconsistent with the Framework. It also covers station-led Green Belt release in plans (GB3), station densities (L3), substantial weight for intensification, and shorter local plan periods (PM2).

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Apply the 2026 Framework immediately in decisions.
- Decide first whether the site is inside or outside a settlement; only "(some) villages" count.
- Outside settlements, check the S5 list; if the scheme fits no category, approve only in exceptional circumstances.
- Keep determining applications under section 38(6): development plan first, NPPF as a key material consideration.
- Where plan policies conflict with the Framework, show due regard to Annex A (very limited weight to the inconsistent extent).
- Give substantial weight to schemes that add homes or floorspace within settlements by building upwards or within plots (L3), and show that weight was given.
- Consider whether a new plan is needed where local policies clearly conflict.

## Positions against our propositions

- **A2 (agrees):** "to the extent development plan policies are materially inconsistent with the NPPF 2026, they should be given very limited weight unless they have been examined and adopted, or made, against the NPPF 2026" (section 1, "Implications for our clients"). "To the extent" supports the reading that only the inconsistent part loses weight. It does not say which kinds of policy keep full weight.
- **S5 (agrees):** "Where an out-of-settlement development does not fall within one of those categories, it should only be approved in exceptional circumstances." (section 1). This supports falling outside every category (S5(4)) being close to fatal.
- **SUP (agrees):** "Developments that address evidenced unmet need (i.e. poor housing land supply and poor housing delivery) are also supported where certain criteria are met." (section 1). A supply shortfall opens a route, but only subject to criteria.
- **SH2 (agrees, implicit):** "inside or outside a settlement (e.g. cities, towns and (some) villages)" (section 1). Says that not every village is an NPPF settlement. It does not name the Green Belt washed-over exclusion.
