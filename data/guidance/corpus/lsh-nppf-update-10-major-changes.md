# NPPF Update: 10 Major Changes Every Planner Must Know

## Summary

A practitioner overview (Viewpoint, 4 February 2026) of the December 2025 consultation draft, not the August 2026 final text. It explains the move to coded policies split into plan-making and national decision-making policies, and says local policies inconsistent with national decision-making policies are expected to get "very limited weight". It describes S3 as replacing old paragraph 11, S4 as approval within a settlement boundary unless negative effects substantially outweigh benefits, and S5 as restricting development outside settlements, singling out S5(j) (evidenced unmet need, well related to an existing settlement) and the station route within 800 metres. It also notes the station route can run in the Green Belt under draft GB7(h) if the GB8 golden rules are met.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Treat local plan policies that are inconsistent with national decision-making policies as carrying very limited weight (draft-stage reading).
- Within a settlement boundary, approve unless negative effects substantially outweigh benefits (draft S4).
- Outside settlements, look to the S5 categories, especially (j) evidenced unmet need well related to an existing settlement, and the 800-metre station route.
- Expect the Department for Transport (DfT) Connectivity Tool to play a growing role in testing sustainable locations.

## Positions against our propositions

- **A2: silent.** "where local plan policies are inconsistent with national decision‑making policies, the local policies are expected to be given very limited weight in decisions" (Section 2, "Plan-Making Policies and Decision-Making Policies"). Checked against the source: this restates the December 2025 draft's general approach (that the split into national decision-making policies means inconsistent local policies get very limited weight). It is about the draft, not the August 2026 text, and it does not discuss Annex A para 2, whether weight is cut for the whole policy or only the inconsistent clause, or heritage, design, access or spatial-preference policies. It is a general summary, not a position on how granular the reduction is, so it neither supports nor qualifies A2.

## Leads

- The DfT Connectivity Tool as evidence of a sustainable location (TR3): check how inspectors treat it against route quality.
- The draft treated the S4/S5 split as a settlement-boundary question; compare with the final Annex B definition (washed-over villages excluded).
