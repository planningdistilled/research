# What the grey-belt policy is really doing to parts of our countryside

## Summary

A short CPRE Kent blog post by David Mairs relaying the London Green Belt Council and CPRE Hertfordshire report 'Green Belt is now grey belt'. It explains the December 2024 grey belt definition and reports that 83 per cent of London Metropolitan Green Belt appeals between February and December 2025 were allowed on grey belt grounds, over twice the ten-year historic rate. It argues Green Belt loss is unnecessary given 1.4 million unbuilt permissions and brownfield capacity. It concerns the December 2024 Framework, not the August 2026 one, and gives no instruction on applying policy.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Nothing addressed to officers; it signposts the London Green Belt Council and CPRE report.

## Positions against our propositions

- **METH: agrees.** "‘Green Belt is now grey belt’ uncovers that 83 per cent of planning appeals on sites in the London Metropolitan Green Belt between February and December 2025 were permitted specifically on grey-belt grounds." (Fourth paragraph). The campaign sector uses the same evidence base as us (counting appeal outcomes) to show how the Framework is applied in practice. Note the regime is the December 2024 NPPF and the sample is small (12 appeals in the source report).
- **GB: agrees.** "This is more than twice the proportion that were granted over the previous 10 years on any basis." (Fifth paragraph). Supports the view that grey belt is usually won at appeal. Pre-August 2026 regime; says nothing on the village-edge point or very special circumstances.
