## Summary

Lichfields explains the final station policy for development outside settlement boundaries. It has four limbs: the station is in the top 80 travel-to-work areas by Gross Value Added; it is "well connected" (four trains or trams an hour overall, or two in one direction, or a reasonable prospect of that); the site is physically well-related to the station or the settlement and within reasonable walking distance; and density is at least 35 dph, or 45 dph at twice the frequency. It reads "reasonable walking distance" as "around 800m" and says only the part of a site within 800m qualifies. It notes that 64% of councils already accept walking distances over 800m, and that no unmet need or five-year supply shortfall has to be shown. Lichfields estimates capacity for 535,000–570,000 homes, much of it in the Green Belt (73% in the West Midlands).

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Test the four limbs (travel-to-work area, frequency, well-related plus walking distance, density).
- Treat about 800m as the walking-distance measure, and apply the policy and density test only to the part of the site within 800m.
- Do not require a housing land supply shortfall or unmet need for this route.

## Positions against our propositions

- **TR – not applicable (different test).** Lichfields restates the Framework's own definition for the station routes (GB7(1)(h) and S5(1)(h)): "The framework clarifies that ... this is a distance of ‘around 800m’. It further asserts that only the area of the site that is within 800m of an eligible station will meet the policy criteria and density definition" (section "Clearer definition of ‘reasonable walking distance’"). This is the station-route distance limb, not the TR3 or GB7(1)(g)(iii) access tests that our TR covers. Our patterns analysis (finding 13) already applies "around 800m" literally on the station routes. Lichfields says nothing about route quality, so it neither supports nor challenges TR.
- **S5 – not applicable.** The quote "The development is physically well-related to the station or the settlement and within reasonable walking distance of the station." (four-limb list) is verbatim, but it restates the station-route limb of S5, not S5(1)(j), the unmet-need route whose "physically well-related" wording (to an existing settlement) our competing readings concern. Lichfields does not interpret the phrase or offer a reading, so it neither supports nor qualifies our S5 analysis.
- **SUP – silent (context only).** Lichfields restates the policy that the station route is a separate rules-based route: "There is no requirement to demonstrate ‘unmet need’ or the absence of a five year housing land supply." (after the four-limb list). This describes a different test that does not depend on supply. It says nothing about what weight a shortfall carries where supply is relevant, so it does not qualify SUP. It is a reminder that SUP applies to the supply route, not to every route outside a settlement.
