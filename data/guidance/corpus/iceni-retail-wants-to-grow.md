## Summary
A short opinion piece by Craig Stewart (Senior Planner, Iceni) arguing that the August 2026 National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF) puts economic growth back on the agenda but leaves retail policy "broadly unchanged". It says town-centre-first retail policy was shaped to stop out-of-centre growth and is now out of step with demand for retail warehouse space. It uses draft London Plan Policy PV3 as an example of treating retail parks as sites to intensify rather than invest in. It gives no guidance on how decision-makers should apply any specific 2026 policy code.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do
- Nothing directly. Implies decision-makers should weigh access to facilities "in the right locations" rather than retaining retail floorspace at all costs.
- Tells applicants to show both commercial demand and contribution to wider placemaking objectives.

## Positions against our propositions
- None. Silent on settlement hierarchy, Green Belt, transport, S5, housing supply, Annex A and appeal evidence.
