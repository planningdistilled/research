# The revised NPPF: navigating the new “rules-based planning system”

## Summary

Web version of the PDF coded as cornerstone-revised-nppf-rules-based-planning; same text, dated 20 August 2026 on the page. Five Cornerstone barristers (Dehon KC, Townsend KC, Williams KC, Lovatt, Sirica) comment on the final August 2026 NPPF. They say Annex A gives 'very limited weight' only to plan policies 'materially inconsistent' with the national decision-making policies, narrower than the draft, and that weight remains for the decision-maker. They describe S4/S5 as a locational presumption with a 'reverse presumption' (S5(4)) against development outside the countryside categories. They list five likely 'battlegrounds': a non-exhaustive settlement definition and out-of-date boundaries; split sites under S3(2); two meanings of 'evidenced unmet need' (narrow in GB7(1)(g), not limited in S5(1)(j)); scale and 'physically well-related' as matters of judgement; and how to measure 'reasonable walking distance'. On the Green Belt they note GB6 is unchanged, the new station category GB7(1)(h), the 'minimised' openness wording in GB7(1)(f), and that heritage harm is no longer part of the grey belt definition but is assessed separately.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Check every relevant development plan policy, however recent, for 'material' inconsistency with the national decision-making policies; mere inconsistency is not enough for very limited weight.
- Remember weight is still for the decision-maker; Annex A guides discretion but does not remove it (contrast N2(3) on biodiversity net gain, which says 'no weight').
- Outside settlements, first place the scheme in an S5(1)(a)-(j) category or in appropriate Green Belt development (S5(5)); otherwise S5(4) needs 'exceptional circumstances'.
- For split sites apply both S4 and S5 (S3(2)).
- Treat scale and 'physically well-related' in S5(1)(h) and (j) as planning judgements open to reasonable disagreement.
- Settle how 'reasonable walking distance' is measured (crow-flies or walking route, how much 'around' allows, when to use 'around 10 minutes' walk')).
- In the Green Belt, inappropriate development still needs very special circumstances (GB6(2)); grid connections can be appropriate under GB7(1)(f), but solar, wind and battery generation stays inappropriate.
- Assess heritage and other harms separately after deciding whether land is grey belt.

## Positions against our propositions

- **S5 – agrees** (web article para 9, under heading 'The new presumption'): "there is a sting in its tail: a new reverse presumption against any development in the countryside which does not fall within its pre-ordained categories" Treats falling outside every S5(1) category as triggering a reverse presumption (S5(4)), matching our view that the category is the whole case outside settlements.
- **S5 – agrees** (web article para 12(4), under heading 'How big is too big?'): "This necessarily entails an exercise of planning judgement and is self-evidently a question on which reasonable people may reasonably disagree (and likely will). The same is true in respect of the requirement, found in the same countryside categories, that sites be" The sentence continues: sites be 'physically well-related' to settlements or stations. Supports our point that S5(1)(j) 'physically well-related' (and scale) has competing readings.
- **S5 – qualifies** (web article para 12(3), under heading 'EUN (Evidenced Unmet Need)'): "However, in the presumption policies (S5(1)(j)) it is expressly not limited to these circumstances. Does that mean that an evidenced unmet need for, say, affordable housing or older/disabled persons housing would be sufficient to bring a residential proposal which included such housing into the countryside category?" Adds a reading we do not state: the S5(1)(j) gate may open on unmet need for particular housing types (affordable, older or disabled persons), not only on a five-year supply or Housing Delivery Test failure. Also bears on SUP: supply shortfall may not be the only gate.
- **SH2 – silent** (web article para 12(1), under heading 'Whose line is it anyway?'): "The definition of settlement includes areas defined as a settlement in a development plan, but the definition is not exhaustive. What happens, say, where the site falls outside of a settlement boundary which is out of date, and no longer reflects reality on the ground?" Recoded from 'qualifies' after checking. The point is about edge sites outside an out-of-date settlement boundary, where the open 'includes' wording in Annex B lets a place count as a settlement on the ground. It does not touch washed-over villages. Annex B separately and expressly excludes "villages which lie within and are defined as part of the Green Belt in the development plan", and an inclusive definition cannot override an express exclusion. The article does not mention washed-over villages, so it is no argument against SH2. It is relevant to inset villages with stale boundaries (S4 vs S5 at the edge).
- **TR – silent** (web article para 12(5), under heading 'One person's reasonable walking distance'): "The NPPF has defined reasonable walking distances, but in doing so has left significant scope for disagreement. Is the 800m measured as the crow-flies, or actual walking distances?" This is about the Annex B 'reasonable walking distance' used only for the well-connected station categories (S5(1)(h), L3, GB7(1)(h)), not TR3 or GB7(1)(g)(iii). It raises open questions without answering them, and does not say distance outweighs route quality. Annex B itself already brings in 'route availability and quality' through the 'around 10 minutes' walk' alternative, and asking whether to use actual walking distances points towards the route, not away from it. Not a position against TR.
- **A2 – agrees** (web article para 7, under heading 'Implementation'): "mere inconsistency is not sufficient to engage the NPPF’s deemed weighting" The published Annex A needs 'material' inconsistency, narrower than the draft's 'in any way'; consistent with our view that Annex A para 2 bites narrowly.
- **A2 – qualifies** (web article para 7, under heading 'Implementation'): "as the courts have repeatedly told us, weight is ultimately a matter for the decision maker, who is entitled (subject to public law constraints) to depart from policy, even where that policy is expressed in directive terms" Adds that Annex A's 'very limited weight' is itself not binding on the decision-maker and development plan policies keep section 38(6) primacy; expects litigation.
- **GB – agrees** (web article para 19, under heading 'Green Belt and Grey Belt'): "in determining whether land is Grey Belt, it no longer appears necessary to consider (for example) whether there might be a strong reason for refusal in respect of heritage. However, those considerations will now form part of a separate assessment under other policies in the new NPPF." Supports 'passing GB7 is not approval': heritage and other harms are assessed separately after the grey belt question.
