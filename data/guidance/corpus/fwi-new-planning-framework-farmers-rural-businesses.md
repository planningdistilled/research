## Summary

A Farmers Weekly feature (Suzie Horne, dated 15 April 2026 on the page; listed elsewhere as 18 April) on the **draft** National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF), mostly giving the views of consultant Hannah Moule (The Rural Planning Co). She says that under the draft the NPPF "could trump the local plan" where the plan does not support a use, so refusals on grounds of planning principle are worth revisiting, though refusals on technical grounds are not. She suggests "within" a settlement can mean immediately adjacent, that well-connected sites outside a former settlement boundary may get the tilted balance, and that smaller villages may now count as settlements. She notes hamlets are still excluded, while the Country Land and Business Association (CLA) wants clarity on what "settlement" and "built-up area" mean. The CLA also lobbies for better training of planning officers on rural issues.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Give "substantial weight" to economic benefits of rural business growth and to farm modernisation benefits.
- (Consultant's reading of the draft) Treat national policy as taking precedence over a conflicting local plan on matters of principle.
- (Consultant's reading) Treat sites immediately adjacent to, or well connected with, a settlement as potentially "within" it for the tilted balance.
- Do not apply the town-centre sequential test to small-scale rural offices or other small-scale rural development.
- Outside settlements, look for previously developed land and sites "physically well-related to existing development".

## Positions against our propositions

- **A2 (qualifies)**: "A fundamental change in the proposed NPPF, as drafted, is that it sets out that where there is conflict with the local development plan, the NPPF should take precedence." and "This is a major change and basically means that where a local plan doesn’t support something, if the NPPF does, then the NPPF could trump the local plan" (section "Change of emphasis"). This describes the draft's precedence mechanism, not the final August 2026 text, and it works conflict by conflict: national policy prevails only where the plan conflicts with it. It does not say whole plans or all spatial policies lose weight, and it does not address clause-by-clause severance. Its exception, that refusals "on technical grounds such as unsafe highways or flooding risk" are unaffected, fits our reading that access and similar policies keep full weight. The tension with A2 is narrower: the consultant expects refusals on planning principle (a plan "did not support a particular type of development") to be worth revisiting, which presses on our claim that spatial-preference policies keep full weight. That is a practitioner's expectation about the draft, not a reading of Annex A para 2.
- **S5 (qualifies)**: "“Within” can also mean immediately adjacent to a settlement, suggests Hannah." and "If a site is outside of a previous settlement boundary and well connected to the settlement, then there are more times where the tilted balance will apply." (section "Residential development"). This is a consultant's suggestion about the draft, not the final text. It stretches "within a settlement" (the S4 route) to adjacent and well-connected land, so for edge sites the S5(1) category would not be the whole case. That moves the S4/S5 line we draw rather than reading S5(1)(j) "physically well-related" directly. It matches the council reading in our data (adjacency treated as well-related), which inspectors mostly reject in favour of settlement form and severance. No change to our analysis; low weight.
- **SH1 (agrees)**: "Smaller villages – which may have previously been counted out of the titled balance consideration – may under the new framework be considered settlements in planning terms" (section "More potential for smaller villages?"). This fits our reading that the Annex B settlement definition, not the local tier, sets the route. It is about the draft, and the agreement is by inference.
- **DIV (silent)**: "While generally very positive – and I look forward to seeing the final draft – it will still take time for the changes to filter down through the LPAs and planning officers. It also won’t make any change to local objectors." (section "More potential for smaller villages?"). On checking, this is not a stance on DIV. It is a consultant's general forecast, made about the April 2026 draft, that councils will be slow to absorb a change she expects to be more permissive. It compares councils with the new policy over time, not councils with inspectors on the same tests, and gives no evidence about decisions. It neither supports nor contradicts our finding that councils pass what inspectors fail. At most it is a lead on officer training lag.

## Leads

- CLA lobbying for "improved training of planning officers on rural issues and agricultural matters". Ask the CLA whether it runs or funds officer training.
- CLA (Shannon Fuller) draft-consultation response asking for clarity on "settlement" and "built-up area" (relevant to SH2).
- The Rural Planning Co (Hannah Moule): any post-August 2026 update on whether the final text kept the "precedence" wording.
