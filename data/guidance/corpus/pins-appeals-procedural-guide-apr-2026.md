# Planning appeals: procedural guide (applications dated on or after 1 April 2026)

## Summary
This Planning Inspectorate (PINS) guide covers appeals on applications made on or after 1 April 2026. Most section 78 appeals now follow the "part 1" (expedited) written representations procedure, under which "the appellant is not able to submit evidence at appeal not previously considered by the LPA" and interested people cannot comment at appeal stage. New evidence may be accepted only after a material change in circumstances, such as "A material and relevant change in development plan policy and/or national planning policy (including emerging policies)", a court judgment, or "A relevant decision is made on another application or appeal". It has no instruction specific to the 2026 NPPF. It still tells parties to cite the Framework by "quoting their paragraph numbers", which the August 2026 Framework no longer has (it uses policy codes). It was last updated on 16 July 2026, before the Framework was published.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do
- Councils: send the application, the decision notice, committee minutes, the officer report and the appeal questionnaire. In part 1 appeals the inspector considers only these.
- All parties: tell PINS in writing, as soon as possible, about new legislation or national policy that they consider material (for example the 17 August 2026 NPPF, for appeals in progress).
- Do not explain national policy in statements. Point to the relevant parts by paragraph number, which is now out of date for the 2026 Framework.
- Statements of common ground should identify relevant appeal decisions on the site or neighbouring sites.
- Declare any use of artificial intelligence (AI) (sections 9.2.11, 9.3.14, 10.16 and 11.20).

## Positions against our propositions
- **DIV, silent (method note only).** "In the part 1 procedure, the appellant is not able to submit evidence at appeal not previously considered by the LPA when they determined the application." (Section 7.3.1.) This is a procedural rule. It says nothing on whether councils and inspectors read the tests differently, so it neither supports nor limits DIV. It matters only for our method. In part 1 appeals the inspector mostly has the evidence the council had, so divergence there is a cleaner sign of differing policy readings. Even so, sections 7.3.4 and 7.3.5 let new material in after a change in national policy (such as the 17 August 2026 NPPF) or another relevant appeal decision. Part 2, hearing and inquiry cases may diverge because of new evidence. See Leads.
- **METH, agrees.** "A relevant decision is made on another application or appeal" (Section 7.3.4, listed as a material change in circumstances). PINS treats other appeal decisions as material enough to justify new evidence. This supports using appeal decisions as evidence of how policy is applied, though only for decisions relevant to the case.

## Leads

- Whether PINS updates the "quoting their paragraph numbers" instruction for the policy-code Framework.
- Our 971 decisions: mark which are part 1 appeals (applications dated on or after 1 April 2026), so that DIV can be compared on the same evidence.
