## Summary

A pre-publication briefing (June 2026) for rural landowners, written before the August 2026 National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF). It covers the December 2024 grey belt changes, the move to new-style Local Plans, the National Scheme of Delegation from 31 October 2026, the draft "rules-based" NPPF (a presumption for development within settlements and near stations), stricter written-representation appeals for applications made since 1 April 2026, local government reorganisation, and Biodiversity Net Gain (BNG) exemptions. It frames grey belt and housing shortfall as opportunities for landowners to act quickly. It gives no guidance on how the final 2026 tests are applied.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Under the National Scheme of Delegation, officers will decide more applications. Only prescribed larger or sensitive cases go to committee, which the author expects to make decisions more policy-led and less political.
- Appeals by written representations will generally allow no new information, so the application as submitted must stand on its own.
- (Addressed to landowners) promote land quickly while grey belt policy lasts and while delays in Local Plans may leave a housing shortfall.

## Positions against our propositions

- **SUP – agrees (gate half only).** "any delays in the Local Plan process may mean longer periods of housing shortfall, potentially triggering the presumption in favour of sustainable development and providing opportunities for speculative planning applications." (section 6, "Local government reorganisation"). It treats a shortfall as what opens the presumption to speculative applications. That matches the "opens the gate" half of our proposition. It says nothing about whether the shortfall decides the case, and it was written under the December 2024 Framework.

## Leads

- Strutt & Parker, "What the National Scheme of Delegation means for planning decisions": relevant to how officers' delegated powers change from 31 October 2026.
- New appeals procedure for applications submitted since 1 April 2026 (no new evidence at written-representation appeals). This may affect how appeal decisions compare with council decisions (DIV).
