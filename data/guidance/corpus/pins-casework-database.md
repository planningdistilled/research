# Planning Inspectorate Casework Database

## Summary
This is the official dataset (an Excel spreadsheet of 22.6 MB, file "Casework_Database_-_2609.xlsx") of Planning Inspectorate (PINS) casework decided over a rolling 5 years, plus anonymised older data covering 5 to 10 years ago. It was last updated on 1 October 2026; from September 2026 it adds the Office for National Statistics (ONS) local planning authority code in column D. The page gives no guidance on the NPPF. It is relevant to our method because it gives PINS's own cautions about reading outcome patterns from the data. Casework is allocated by availability, geography, level and expertise, which "may produce apparent patterns of outcome that are in fact artefacts of the way work is allocated". The counts may not match the Official Statistics, and the page lists known quality problems: the number-of-residences field and the major/minor housing flags (December 2024), missing agent names after March 2022, and blank inspector names.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do
- Treat each case as decided "on their individual merits, and on the basis of the evidence submitted". No allowed or dismissed targets are set.
- Do not use the data to challenge which inspector was appointed.
- Use the residences and major/minor housing fields with caution, and report errors to statistics@planninginspectorate.gov.uk.

## Positions against our propositions
- **METH, qualifies.** "This may produce apparent patterns of outcome that are in fact artefacts of the way work is allocated and do not provide evidence as to an Inspector’s fitness for or approach to a particular case" (section "Please Note"). PINS warns that outcome patterns can come from how cases are allocated, especially per inspector and with small numbers ("some dealing with as few as one which would give a result of 100% allowed or 100% dismissed"). Our outcome rates and decision routes are fine at policy-test level. Any per-inspector or per-area breakdown needs this caveat and minimum sample sizes.
