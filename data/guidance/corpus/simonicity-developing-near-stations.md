## Summary

A follow-up to "Push The Button". It sets out the August 2026 NPPF policies that encourage development near "well-connected stations": S5(1)(h), the matching Green Belt category GB7(1)(h) (which adds compliance with the GB8 Golden Rules for major development), and the L3 minimum densities (35, or 45, dwellings per hectare). It quotes the Annex B definitions. "Reasonable walking distance" is around 800 metres, or around a 10-minute walk where topography, route availability and quality, or physical barriers would discourage walking. A "well-connected station" is in a top-80 Travel to Work Area by Gross Value Added (GVA) and has at least four trains or trams an hour, or two an hour in one direction. Ricketts says these policies trump "materially inconsistent" plan policies, which Annex A paragraph 2 reduces to "very limited weight". He lists open questions: wriggle room in the 800m / 10-minute measure, no official list of the top-80 Travel to Work Areas, and how to read timetables.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- For residential or mixed-use schemes outside settlements near a station, test S5(1)(h)(i) to (iv): reasonable walking distance, physically well-related to the station or its settlement, infrastructure capacity, and no prejudice to long-term comprehensive development.
- In the Green Belt, apply GB7(1)(h). For major development, also require compliance with GB8.
- Where only part of a site lies within the reasonable walking distance, apply the station policies only to that part (Annex B).
- Apply the L3(2)(c) minimum densities to qualifying schemes and refuse schemes that do not use land efficiently (L3(4)).
- Give development plan policies that are materially inconsistent with these policies very limited weight (Annex A paragraph 2).

## Positions against our propositions

- **TR, silent (different test).** The only related line is "Any possible wriggle room in that 800m /10 minutes’ walking distance" (list of remaining uncertainties near the end). Ricketts lists it as an open question and gives no reading of it. He does not say whether distance or route quality governs. It concerns the Annex B "reasonable walking distance" used by the station tests (S5(1)(h), GB7(1)(h), L3), not TR3 or GB7(1)(g)(iii). The Annex B definition he quotes already uses route availability and quality as the trigger for the 10-minute alternative, so the post adds nothing beyond restating policy. It neither supports nor qualifies our TR proposition.
- **A2, silent (restates policy only).** "trumping any “materially inconsistent” development plan policies, which now have “very limited weight” (Annex A, paragraph 2)" (opening paragraph). This is the only mention of plan policies or Annex A in the post, and it is a one-line summary of what Annex A paragraph 2 says. Ricketts does not discuss which plan policies count as "materially inconsistent", whether the whole policy or only the conflicting clause loses weight, or spatial-strategy, settlement-boundary, heritage or design policies. It neither supports nor contradicts our reading that Annex A cuts only the offending clause. Checked 2 Oct 2026; an earlier "qualifies" coding read more into the sentence than it says.
