# Sevenoaks grey belt appeal: traveller site wins 3-year reprieve

## Summary

Reports an enforcement appeal (decision 3378336, Inspector T C King, 28 Sep 2026) at West Kingsdown, Sevenoaks, in which land in the Kent Downs National Landscape near Ancient Woodland was accepted as grey belt. The August 2026 grey-belt definition no longer excludes land protected by asset policies. The article stresses that grey-belt status is not approval: the asset policies still apply to the development, and ordinary housing at this remote site would have failed the GB7 sustainable-location test. A three-year personal permission was granted only because traveller policy HO12 allows some flexibility on location, unmet traveller need was substantial, and the family's circumstances (Article 8, best interests of the child) weighed in favour. Intentional unauthorised development was given substantial weight against.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Treat grey-belt classification (the contribution to Green Belt purposes a, b and d in GB2) as a separate question from whether the development is acceptable.
- Apply National Landscape and Ancient Woodland policies to the development itself even where the land is grey belt.
- Do not treat a site that is remote from services, reached by narrow lanes, as meeting the GB7 sustainable-location test for ordinary housing.
- For traveller sites, apply HO12: some sites may be appropriate in locations not well related to settlements; weigh local provision, need and personal circumstances.
- Give substantial weight to intentional unauthorised development, including in enforcement appeals.

## Positions against our propositions

- **GB (grey belt / Green Belt): agrees.** "That finding matters, but it was not a planning free pass." (section "Why the Sevenoaks grey belt appeal changed under the new NPPF"). Grey-belt status of the land did not amount to approval; matches 'passing GB7 is not approval'.
- **GB (grey belt / Green Belt): agrees.** "Inspector King said ordinary residential development would not have satisfied GB7’s sustainability requirement." (section "General housing would still have failed the GB7 test"). A remote grey-belt site fails GB7 for general housing; supports 'grey belt is usually won at a village edge'.
- **GB (grey belt / Green Belt): qualifies.** "The Inspector therefore applied some flexibility because this was a traveller proposal rather than general housing." (section "General housing would still have failed the GB7 test"). A narrow exception to our grey-belt pattern: under HO12 the GB7 location test is applied flexibly to traveller sites, so a remote site that would fail GB7 for ordinary housing can pass and win permission, here only for three years and personal to the family. This is a report of the Inspector's reasoning, not a critique of our approach. patterns.md #16 already records a traveller GB7 pass on the GTAA (Gypsy and Traveller Accommodation Assessment), but #15 (village edge) and #18 do not say that the locational flexibility is specific to travellers.
