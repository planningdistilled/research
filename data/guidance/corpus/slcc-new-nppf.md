## Summary

This is a short news and briefing note for clerks, posted after the 17 August 2026 publication. It is billed as "twelve things local councils should know", although ten bullets appear on the page. Most points are general: it is the biggest rewrite in a decade, most measures take immediate effect, and some policies once set locally are now national. The one decision-making point is a simplified reading of S4 and S5. It says the key test is whether a proposal is inside or outside "the built-up area": inside, approve unless "substantially outweighed by adverse effects"; outside, "only certain kinds of development are allowed at all". It announces webinars on 24 September and 9 December 2026, and an article in The Clerk.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Treat the inside/outside question as the first test when commenting on applications.
- Inside the built-up area, expect approval unless the adverse effects substantially outweigh the benefits.
- Outside, check whether the proposal falls within one of the permitted kinds of development.

## Positions against our propositions

- **S5 (agrees):** "Outside a built-up area, only certain kinds of development are allowed at all." (list item "A key test is whether a proposal is inside or outside the built-up area"). This matches our reading that outside settlements the S5(1) category is the whole case.
- **SH2 (silent):** The note never mentions the Green Belt, washed-over villages, Annex B or "settlement"; its only spatial wording is a one-line paraphrase of S4/S5 ("A key test is whether a proposal is inside or outside the built-up area. Inside the built-up area, a proposal should be approved unless the benefits of doing so would be substantially outweighed by adverse effects."). "Built-up area" is lay shorthand in a ten-bullet news item, not a reading of the Annex B definition, so it neither supports nor contradicts SH2. Adversarial check (2 Oct 2026): quote verbatim, but downgraded from "qualifies" to "silent". Worth noting only as an example of clerk-level guidance that, taken literally, could lead parish councils to treat a washed-over village as "inside".
