## Summary
Savills' publication-day news release on the August 2026 National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF). It lists changes from the December 2025 draft: the station "default yes" widened to the top 80 Travel to Work Areas, reasonable walking distance to a station "confirmed as 800m", minimum density of 35-45 dwellings per hectare, viability flexibility, pub protection, and the separation of plan-making from national decision-making policies, which apply alongside the development plan from 17 August 2026. It reports the latest Housing Delivery Test (HDT) results: 31% of authorities now in the presumption. It expects heavy emphasis on settlement boundaries, with opportunities within and on the edge of settlements including grey belt, and warns that existing local plan policies inconsistent with the Framework risk being given limited weight.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do
- Apply national decision-making policies alongside the development plan from 17 August 2026.
- Measure "reasonable walking distance" to a qualifying station as 800m for the station policy.
- Expect settlement-boundary definition to drive the within/edge-of-settlement analysis, with grey belt opportunities subject to the Golden Rules.
- Give less weight to local Biodiversity Net Gain (BNG) policies above the 10% minimum.
- Expect older plan policies that conflict with the Framework to get limited weight.

## Positions against our propositions
- GB (agrees): "with opportunities for new development both within and on edge of settlements. This includes locations that meet the broader definition of Grey Belt land" (paragraph beginning "Savills anticipates significant emphasis"). Consistent with grey belt being won at the settlement edge.
- A2 (silent): "there is a risk that some will be given limited weight in decision-making where inconsistent with the Framework" (paragraph beginning "Fundamentally there is strong incentive"). A one-line, general restatement of the Annex A para 2 consistency test, given as a reason to update plans promptly. It does not say whether the whole policy or only the inconsistent part loses weight ("where inconsistent" fits a clause-level reading), and it does not address heritage, design, access or spatial policies. It neither supports nor qualifies A2.
- TR (not applicable): "with reasonable walking distance from the station confirmed as 800m" (paragraph beginning "The new NPPF includes stronger in-principle support"). This only restates the Framework's definition for the station "default yes" policy. That is a different test from TR3 and GB7(1)(g)(iii), and Savills says nothing about how route quality and distance compare in those tests. It is not a stance against TR. (Checked 2026-10-02.)
