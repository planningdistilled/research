## Summary

Ricketts argues that the July 2026 draft London Plan, a spatial development strategy (SDS), does not meet the August 2026 NPPF's plan-making tests. The draft plans for 558,000 homes over ten years against a government need figure of 84,884 a year, and gives no evidence of the "stringent efforts" with other strategic authorities that PM14(2) requires (footnote 20 applies this to the Mayor of London). He explains why the 2026 Framework applies: Annex A paragraphs 5, 7 and 8 contain no transitional provision for SDSs. He also notes PM6, which bars plan policies that duplicate or are inconsistent with the national decision-making policies. The post is about plan-making and housing numbers, not about deciding applications.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Plan-makers: prepare strategic development strategies in line with the 2026 Framework from 17 August 2026. There is no transitional protection for SDSs part-way through preparation.
- Meet objectively assessed need, or show evidence of stringent efforts, through cooperation, to meet it elsewhere (S1, HO1, HO2, PM10, PM11 and PM14(2)).
- Do not include plan policies that duplicate or are inconsistent with the national decision-making policies (PM6(c)).

## Positions against our propositions

None. The post is about plan-making for spatial development strategies. It does not address how plan policies are weighed in decisions under Annex A paragraph 2, or any of our other propositions.
