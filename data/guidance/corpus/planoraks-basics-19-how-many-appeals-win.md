## Summary

A 2024 explainer on planning appeal success rates, written before the August 2026 Framework existed, so it gives no guidance on applying it. It says about 30% of appeals succeed overall, but the rate varies sharply by procedure (written representations 29%, hearings 43%, inquiries 54% since 2010) and scheme size (45% for major housing). It cites a Lichfields study: appeals against refusals that followed an officer recommendation to refuse succeeded 40% of the time; refusals made against an officer recommendation to approve succeeded 65% of the time. It warns that inspectors' approval records vary and that inspectors are "not algorithms", so prospects above 65% are rarely advisable. It is relevant mainly as a critique of reading appeal outcome rates too literally.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Do not treat the headline appeal success rate as a forecast: break it down by procedure, scheme size and decision route.
- Expect refusals made against officer advice to fare worse at appeal (65% allowed).
- Advise on prospects cautiously; individual inspectors differ.

## Positions against our propositions

- **METH (qualifies):** "Short answer - how many appeals succeed? Around 30%. But that’s a number which doesn’t tell even close to the full story…" and "Planning Inspectors are, in the end, human beings [Surely not!, Ed.]. Not algorithms. Not planning robots. A site or a scheme which strikes you one way may strike them completely differently." and "That’s before we even get into the approval records of individual planning inspectors which can vary starkly." Supports using appeal outcomes as evidence, but warns that aggregate rates hide procedure, scale and inspector effects and cannot predict a single case.
- **DIV (silent; adjacent data only):** "But appeals against refusals made against officer recommendations to approve succeeded 65% of the time." (Lichfields data, 50+ home appeals, 2017, before the August 2026 Framework). This measures whole-appeal outcomes by decision route (members overturning officers), not findings on individual policy tests, so it neither supports nor contradicts our DIV claim that on the same tests councils pass what inspectors fail. Useful context only: members' refusals against officer advice often fail at appeal (inspectors side with officers), and the 35% dismissed cannot be broken down by test.
