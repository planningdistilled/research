# NPPF is the 'pro development' pivot the UK needs (Pegasus Group, Paul Burrell)

## Summary

A promoter-side overview by Pegasus Group's Head of Planning. It says plan policies that are materially inconsistent with the Framework, and were adopted under an earlier version, get very limited weight. Its headline points are: substantial weight for new homes where there is a need; grey belt kept, with the footnote 7 test removed; "far stronger support" for development inside, outside and on the edge of settlements (where there is a need); and station-led growth. On Green Belt, it says land near well-connected stations is now one of the exceptions, so openness "need not be considered". It names villages "surrounded or washed over by Green Belt" with a well-connected station as likely opportunities, while saying major housing schemes must still pass the policy tests and the Golden Rules. It sets out the 800m / 10-minute walking distance and the top 80 Travel to Work Areas by Gross Value Added (GVA).

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Give very limited weight to materially inconsistent plan policies adopted under an earlier Framework.
- Give substantial weight to new homes where there is a need.
- Treat development near a well-connected station (within about 800m / 10 minutes, with the service-frequency test) as a Green Belt exception, without an openness assessment.
- Still apply the other policy tests and the Golden Rules (GB8) to major housing in such places.

## Positions against our propositions

- **GB — silent (restates policy).** "Development on such land can now also be regarded as one of the limited exceptions to development in the Green Belt, meaning the impacts of such development on the openness of the Green Belt need not be considered." ('A much clearer path to development next to Well Connected Stations' section). Restates policy: the well-connected station exception (GB7(1)(h)) is a Green Belt route separate from grey belt. This does not conflict with our GB proposition, which says grey belt is *usually* the winning route and does not claim it is the only one. Our analysis already covers the station route in patterns.md item 13: no appeal has yet passed GB7(1)(h) on a contested station. Coded silent on GB; the note is useful context, not a disagreement.
- **GB — agrees.** "major development proposals for housing in such locations will still need to satisfy the relevant policy tests and comply with the “Golden Rules.”" ('A much clearer path to development next to Well Connected Stations' section). Matches 'passing GB7 is not approval': falling within an exception still leaves the other tests and the Golden Rules.
- **SH2 — agrees.** "Green Belt land around villages on the edge of main conurbations that are surrounded or washed over by Green Belt but have a well-connected train station" ('A much clearer path to development next to Well Connected Stations' section). Treats development at washed-over villages as a Green Belt matter (the GB7 station exception), not as development inside a settlement under S4. That fits SH2, though it does not discuss the Annex B definition.
- **SUP — silent.** "There is now far stronger support for development within settlements and outside / on the edge of settlements (where there is a need)" ('Key points' list). A one-line headline summary of the new Framework, with no reasoning behind it. "Need" here is general housing need, not a land supply shortfall, and the article does not discuss supply, the tilted balance or how much weight need carries in the final balance. It does not engage SUP.
