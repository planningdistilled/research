## Summary
Publication-day reaction by Karen Charles (Boyer) to the August 2026 National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF). It notes the new structure with policy numbers and a separation of plan-making and decision-making policies, and says the NPPF is gaining weight in decisions "not just where development plan documents are out-of-date". It welcomes the new policy for development outside settlements (S5), naming previously developed land, limited infilling within groups of houses and evidenced unmet need where there is no five-year housing land supply (5YHLS) or a Housing Delivery Test (HDT) score below 75%. It summarises the station policy, lower station densities (35/40 dwellings per hectare), Spatial Development Strategies, grey belt with the "golden rules", and the clarification that renewables in the Green Belt are inappropriate and need very special circumstances (VSC).

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do
- Treat the NPPF as of growing weight in decisions, not only when the plan is out of date.
- Recognise S5 categories (previously developed land, infilling within groups of houses, evidenced unmet need) as routes for development outside settlement boundaries.
- Treat renewable energy in the Green Belt as inappropriate development needing VSC.
- Apply lower minimum densities near stations, with exemptions for smaller sites.

## Positions against our propositions
- SUP (agrees, gate half only): "support for development that would address an evidenced unmet need including where the authority cannot demonstrate a five year housing land supply or scores below 75% in the most recent Housing Delivery Test" (paragraph "Redevelopment of previously developed land"). Frames a supply shortfall as the trigger for an S5 category of support, which is our "opens the gate". It does not say the shortfall decides the case, and it omits the S5(1)(j) "physically well-related" and infrastructure conditions.
- S5 (silent; checked 2026-10-02): the station paragraph ("Supports of residential and mixed-use development") says the NPPF supports development within reasonable walking distance of a station "where it would be physically well-related to an existing settlement and be of a scale which can be supported by existing and proposed infrastructure". This is a loose paraphrase of S5(1)(h), whose own test is "physically well-related to the station or the settlement within which the station is located"; it is not about S5(1)(j), and it does not discuss what "physically well-related" means or how S5 categories are applied. A summarising slip, not a position on our proposition. Note only as a misquotation risk if anyone relies on it.
