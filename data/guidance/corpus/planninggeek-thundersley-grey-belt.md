## Summary

A news analysis of appeal 6007184 (land south of Daws Heath Road, Thundersley, Castle Point), allowed on 14 September 2026 for up to 58 homes with 50% affordable housing under the August 2026 NPPF. A larger version had been dismissed in July 2024. The Inspector treated the arrival of grey-belt policy and the smaller site as material changes. The Inspector did not adopt the council's broad Green Belt parcel scores; on its own boundaries (road, ancient woodland, existing Thundersley housing, riding centre) the site was found to be grey belt. Purpose (b) was found not engaged by a town-and-village gap. With 0.91 years' supply, Housing Delivery Test (HDT) results of 29% and 11%, and Golden Rules met, the scheme was not inappropriate and was allowed. Biodiversity net gain (BNG) above the 10% minimum got significant weight.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Assess grey belt on the actual site and its boundaries; do not import a broad parcel score from a strategic Green Belt review (consistent with Annex E's call for small assessment areas).
- Apply purpose (b) to neighbouring towns only, not a town and a village.
- Work through every GB7(1)(g) limb: not undermining remaining Green Belt purposes, evidenced unmet need, sustainable location, Golden Rules.
- Treat a policy change since an earlier dismissal as a material change; an earlier appeal does not fix the result.
- Give no weight to claimed economic harm without substantive evidence; give weight to BNG beyond the statutory minimum.
