## Summary

Annex E of the August 2026 National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF) sets the method for Green Belt assessments and for identifying grey belt land. It tells authorities to judge each assessment area's contribution to Green Belt purposes (a) sprawl of large built-up areas, (b) merging of towns and (d) setting of historic towns, with "strong", "moderate" and "weak or none" illustrative features. Critically for village-edge sites, it states that villages are not large built-up areas (purpose a), that purpose b concerns towns not villages, and that purpose d concerns historic towns not villages. It is written for plan-making assessments but is the same criteria decision-makers use to decide whether a site is grey belt.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Assess all Green Belt in the plan area, in units small enough to show variation, subdividing to find grey belt (para 1(a)).
- Score each area's contribution to purposes (a), (b) and (d) using the illustrative features (paras 2-5).
- Not treat villages as large built-up areas for purpose (a); not treat gaps between villages as engaging purpose (b); not treat villages as historic towns for purpose (d).
- Check whether release would fundamentally undermine the five purposes taken together across the plan area (para 1(c)).

## Positions against our propositions

- **GB - agrees.** "Villages should not be considered large built-up areas." (para 3, Purpose A). With "This purpose relates to the merging of towns[footnote 77], not villages." (para 4, Purpose B) and "This purpose relates to historic towns, not villages." (para 5, Purpose D). Land at a village edge will usually make a weak or no contribution to purposes (a), (b) and (d), which explains why grey belt is usually won at a village edge.
