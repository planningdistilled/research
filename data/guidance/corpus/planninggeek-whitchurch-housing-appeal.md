## Summary

A news analysis of appeal 6006893 (115 homes, 40% affordable, plus employment space and a pub, east of Bere Hill, Whitchurch, Basingstoke and Deane; dismissed 15 September 2026 after an inquiry). The August 2026 NPPF was published after the inquiry sat and the parties commented on it. The housing supply was 2.2 to 3.1 years and housing got substantial weight, and the Inspector found the scheme within the S5 route (evidenced unmet need, sufficiently related to Whitchurch, scale capable of accommodation). Landscape, character and design harm, including to the setting of the North Wessex Downs National Landscape, substantially outweighed the benefits. Benefits whose delivery was uncertain (pub, employment space) got reduced weight.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Treat a substantial supply shortfall as carrying substantial weight but not as converting the balance into permission.
- Once within S5, weigh benefits against adverse effects on the "substantially outweigh" test.
- Reduce the weight of benefits whose delivery is not secured (operator, market demand, triggers).
- Carry real, scheme-specific harms (layout, house types, materials, landscape) into the balance.

## Positions against our propositions

- S5 (agrees): "The Inspector found that the development addressed evidenced unmet housing need, was sufficiently related to Whitchurch and was of a scale that could in principle be accommodated. Those findings brought the proposal within the decision-making route in Policy S5 of the August 2026 Framework." followed by "That was not the end of the exercise." (section "What the Whitchurch housing appeal says about Policy S5"). The S5(1)(j) category framed the case; it uses "sufficiently related" rather than the policy's "physically well-related".
- SUP (agrees): "The shortfall mattered a great deal. What it did not do was convert the balance into an automatic permission" (same section).
