## Summary

Chapter 13 sets Green Belt policy in the August 2026 National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF). The plan-making policies (GB1 to GB5) include GB4(1)(b): villages kept in the Green Belt because their open character matters "should not be identified as ‘settlements’" in the plan's spatial strategy. The decision-making policies say development is inappropriate unless it fits a GB7 category (GB6(1)). Inappropriate development needs very special circumstances (VSC), with "substantial weight" on Green Belt harm (GB6(2)). GB7 lists what is not inappropriate, including limited infilling in Green Belt villages (c), and grey belt development (g), where all four tests must be met: not undermining purposes, evidenced unmet need, "a sustainable location, with particular reference to policy TR3", and the Golden Rules for major housing. GB8 sets the Golden Rules (affordable housing uplift, infrastructure, green space) with "substantial weight" on compliance.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Start by asking whether the proposal fits a GB7 category; if not, it is inappropriate (GB6(1)).
- For inappropriate development, give substantial weight to Green Belt harm (including openness) and require other considerations to "clearly outweigh" all harm (GB6(2)).
- For grey belt (GB7(1)(g)), check all four limbs. Unmet need for housing means no five-year supply or a Housing Delivery Test result below 75% (footnote 41). Location sustainability is judged "with particular reference to policy TR3".
- For major housing in the Green Belt, require Golden Rules compliance and give it substantial weight (GB8(2)). Viability departures are allowed only in three listed cases (GB8(3)).
- Limited infilling in villages lying within the Green Belt is not inappropriate (GB7(1)(c)).

## Positions against our propositions

- **SH2, agrees.** Quote: "Such villages should not be identified as ‘settlements’ for the purpose of the spatial strategy in the development plan" (GB4(1)(b)). This is a plan-making rule; the decision-making exclusion is in Annex B.
- **GB, agrees.** Housing need is not listed as a VSC factor; only renewables get a named VSC example. And GB harm carries substantial weight. Quote: "In making this assessment, substantial weight should be given to the harm to the Green Belt which would be caused, including harm to its openness." (GB6(2).) Note: the text does not say housing supply can never be VSC. Our "never produced VSC at appeal" is an empirical claim that the policy neither makes nor rules out.
- **GB, silent (restates policy; checked 2 Oct 2026, not a qualification).** GB7(1) takes away only Green Belt harm and the VSC requirement. It says nothing about the overall planning balance. Other Framework and development-plan policies (heritage, design, S5 location, access) still apply, which fits "passing GB7 is not approval". Any tilt via S5(5) belongs to Chapter 4, not this text. Quote: "The following categories of development are not inappropriate in the Green Belt, and therefore should not be regarded as harmful to the Green Belt or be required to demonstrate very special circumstances" (GB7(1)).
- **TR, silent.** The text only sets the grey-belt access limb and points to TR3. It says nothing about whether route quality or distance decides, so it neither supports nor qualifies TR, which already treats TR3 as the test. Quote: "The development would be in a sustainable location, with particular reference to policy TR3 of this Framework" (GB7(1)(g)(iii); footnote 42 swaps in HO12 for traveller sites). Contrast GB7(1)(h)(i), which does use a distance test ("reasonable walking distance of a well-connected station").

## Leads

- Annex E (Green Belt assessments) for how grey belt is identified in plan-making.
- GB7(1)(c) "limited infilling in villages lying within the Green Belt" is the main route for small schemes in washed-over villages, now that the S5 list does not apply there.
- Footnote 43: the Golden Rules do not apply to land released through plans adopted before 12 December 2024.
