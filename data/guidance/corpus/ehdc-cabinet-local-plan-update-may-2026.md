## Summary

This Cabinet report recommends dropping the district Local Plan under the legacy system and moving to the new plan-making system. The reasons are local government reorganisation and the December 2026 deadline for legacy submissions. One of the stated reasons is the draft National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF). The report quotes the draft's provision that plan policies "in any way inconsistent" with national decision-making policies get "very limited weight". It warns members that even "positively framed local policies" in a plan prepared now could attract very limited weight before or after submission. The report does not discuss settlement, Green Belt or transport tests. It also notes that the council has no five-year housing land supply but has approved housing "where appropriate taking account of the tilted balance".

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Treat the draft NPPF's weight provision (draft Annex A) as a reason not to progress a district plan that could be out of date on adoption.
- Move plan-making into the new system and prepare for unitary-wide plans (Mid Hampshire and South East Hampshire, vesting day 1 April 2028).
- Keep supporting neighbourhood planning and development management during the transition, because the system should be "genuinely plan-led".

## Positions against our propositions

- **A2 (silent; about the draft, not a disagreement):** The report quotes the December 2025 draft: "“Development plan policies which are in any way inconsistent with the national decision-making policies in this Framework should be given very limited weight, except where they have been examined and adopted against this Framework”" (para 4.11, page 6). It then says this "introduces a clear risk that policies prepared under the existing framework, including positively framed local policies, could attract very limited weight prior to, or even following, submission." (para 4.11, continuing on page 7). This is a plan-making risk warning about the draft's "in any way inconsistent" wording. It does not say whether the whole policy or only the inconsistent part loses weight, and it does not say that consistent policies lose weight. "Positively framed" means policies the council wrote in a positive form that might still conflict with the new national policies. The final Annex A paragraph 2 says "Development plan policies (or parts of those policies) which are materially inconsistent". So the report does not take a position against our clause-level A2 reading of the final text. At most it shows how a council read the draft's broader wording.

## Leads

- East Hampshire's published response to the December 2025 NPPF consultation (footnote 3).
- Advice from Local Plan Counsel at the 27 April 2026 meeting (para 4.38).
- Any East Hampshire committee report after 17 August 2026 that revisits the weight point under the final "parts of those policies" wording.
