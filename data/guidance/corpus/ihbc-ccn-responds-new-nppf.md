## Summary

The IHBC newsblog post (dated 28/08/2026) relays the County Councils Network's on-the-day response to the final August 2026 NPPF. CCN says some of its concerns were met, and that government has suggested some policies "will not operate in isolation". It says it is unclear how that will work in practice. It still expects national policy to override local plans and leave councils unable to refuse development unconnected to settlements. It also expects the default "yes" near stations to change the character of villages. The post gives no practical guidance on how to apply the Framework.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

Nothing directly. It flags that CCN would be "analysing these policies in the coming days" and engaging ministers.

## Positions against our propositions

- **S5 - qualifies (low weight; checked 2 Oct 2026).** "Even where a Local Plan is in place, in many instances national policy will override this and mean that councils are unable to refuse development in locations that are not connected to existing settlements, resulting in homes being built in a piecemeal and unplanned way across the countryside." (Cllr Andrew Husband, quoted in the post; verbatim against the local text). This is a campaign body's prediction on publication day, not a reading of the S5 tests. It names no policy code, says nothing about the S5(1) categories, S5(4) or S5(1)(j), and is not based on any decisions. It does expect the final Framework to be more permissive outside settlements than our coding shows: falling outside every S5(1) category is almost always fatal, and S5(1)(j) requires the site to be physically well-related. But it does not contradict our claim that the category decides the case. Read it as a contrary expectation to check against later appeal data, not as disagreement with our method. No change to our analysis.
- **A2 - silent (tension only).** The same quote predicts that national policy will "override" adopted Local Plans "in many instances" so councils cannot refuse development unconnected to settlements. It does not mention Annex A para 2 or how far an inconsistent plan policy loses weight, and it names no route (S5 categories, the presumption, land supply or plan inconsistency). So it does not engage our point that Annex A para 2 cuts only the offending clause. At most it is a publication-day political prediction that sits in tension with our finding that spatial-preference policies keep weight. It is not coded as disagreeing with A2.
