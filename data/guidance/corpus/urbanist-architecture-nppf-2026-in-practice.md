## Summary

A long practice guide by Ufuk Bahar, founder of Urbanist Architecture, a London architecture and planning practice that specialises in Green Belt and grey belt schemes (co-author of "Green Light to Green Belt Developments"). It reads the August 2026 National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF) as a gateway system: identify the exact decision route (S4 inside a settlement, an S5 category outside, GB7/GB8 in Green Belt, station-led), then supply the evidence each gateway needs. It stresses that Annex B excludes washed-over Green Belt villages from "settlement", that S5 does not override Green Belt, that outside the S5 categories approval needs exceptional circumstances, and that a Housing Delivery Test (HDT) failure or supply shortfall cannot rescue a missed gateway. On station sites it says an 800-metre catchment does not prove access and the actual walking route must be audited. It claims a growing number of grey belt refusals have been allowed at appeal, with success rates higher for major schemes, and calls appeal decisions "illustrative ... rather than binding precedent". It calls for councils to retrain officers and committees and to structure decision reports around the national route.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Identify the national decision route first (S4, S5 category, GB7/GB8, station), then the development-plan position, the evidence for each gateway and any harm left after mitigation, in a consistent report structure.
- Do not assume a site is in a settlement from proximity; check the Annex B definition, including the washed-over Green Belt village exclusion.
- In Green Belt, use GB7/GB8; the positive S4/S5 balance applies only after a finding of not inappropriate.
- Treat a supply shortfall or HDT below 75% as opening the S5(1)(j) route only; settlement, infrastructure, Green Belt and other tests still apply.
- For station sites, measure the real walking route (gradients, crossings, lighting, personal safety); a straight-line radius is screening only.
- Cut weight only for materially inconsistent policy (or part), by asking whether it would lead to a materially different decision; age alone does not count.
- Before refusing, consider conditions and obligations; refusals should name the policy, the specific harm and why mitigation fails.
- Retrain officers and committees.

## Positions against our propositions

- **SH2 (agrees)** — "Villages which lie within, and are defined as part of, the Green Belt are also excluded for this purpose." and "Where the answer is unclear, build the application around the S5 or Green Belt route that may apply instead." (section 2, "How does the presumption in favour of sustainable development now work?")
- **S5 (agrees)** — "Outside those categories, approval requires exceptional circumstances and benefits that substantially outweigh the adverse effects, including harm to the countryside and sustainable patterns of movement." (section 2). It also asks for an implementation review of how "physically well-related" is being read, consistent with S5(1)(j) having competing readings: "That review should examine how “materially inconsistent”, “reasonable prospect”, “physically well-related” and “reasonable walking distance” are being interpreted" (closing "My view" section).
- **SUP (agrees)** — "A HDT result below 75% does not make every local policy out of date or establish that a particular site is suitable." (section on housing delivery) and "a missed gateway cannot be rescued later by pointing only to housing need." (section 2)
- **GB (agrees)** — "S5 does not override Green Belt or Local Green Space policy." (section 2); "If the Green Belt applies, use GB7 and GB8 rather than relying on the general unmet-need route." ("What should applicants, landowners and local authorities do now?"); "It should nevertheless be treated as a demanding route, not as discounted Green Belt consent." (section 5)
- **GB (silent on location; side note)** — "Success rates for Grey Belt appeals remain considerably stronger for major developments than for smaller schemes" (section 5). This is an unquantified, unsourced claim about scheme size, not site location; it neither narrows nor contradicts our village-edge finding (the two can both be true), so it is not coded as qualifying GB. Possible follow-up: check grey belt allow rates by major/minor in the decisions DB.
- **TR (agrees)** — "A timetable and an 800-metre catchment do not prove that a site is genuinely accessible." and "A straight-line radius may help with initial screening, but it is not a final accessibility assessment." (section 8, station growth and transport). It lists lighting among the things to assess but does not say lighting cures a poor route.
- **A2 (agrees)** — "The relevant question is not whether a local policy differs from the national wording, but whether it would lead to a materially different decision." (section 1) and "Asking an inspector to discount an entire plan is weaker than showing why a specific policy produces a result inconsistent with the national rules." (appeals section)
- **DIV (qualifies; consistent with our grey-belt exception, not a disagreement)** — "A growing number of Grey Belt applications initially refused by local planning authorities have subsequently been allowed by the Planning Inspectorate where the evidence demonstrated that the site and scheme satisfied the relevant policy tests." (section 5). This describes councils refusing grey belt that inspectors allow. Our own analysis already records grey belt as the reversed case (patterns.md section F: inspectors 23 pass / 2 fail, councils 7 / 5, "Reversed", driven by members regrading purpose (a)); DIV's claim of councils passing what inspectors fail is confined to route quality, triggers and heritage method. So the source matches our grey-belt finding rather than contradicting DIV. It is also an unquantified practitioner claim with a built-in selection effect (appeals only see refusals; council approvals are never tested by an inspector). The related line "Unless officers apply that discipline consistently, applicants will continue to look to appeal for the clarity and judgement that should have been provided at application stage." (decision-making section) is a general complaint about officer consistency, not about any specific test.
- **METH (agrees)** — "Our analysis of Grey Belt appeal decisions since the route was introduced in December 2024 shows inspectors working through each limb in sequence rather than reaching a general conclusion about housing need. I expect the same discipline under the renumbered tests, and appeal decisions remain illustrative of that approach rather than binding precedent." (appeals section). It does its own analysis of appeal decisions to read how inspectors apply the tests. The "not binding precedent" caveat is the standard legal position and matches how we use the decisions: as evidence of how the Framework is applied, not as binding authority. It does not criticise counting outcomes or the empirical approach.
