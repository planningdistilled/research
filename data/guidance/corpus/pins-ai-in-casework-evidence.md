# Use of artificial intelligence in casework evidence

## Summary
This Planning Inspectorate (PINS) guidance (last updated 29 September 2026; the substantive change was on 23 September 2026) requires anyone submitting material to declare deliberate, substantive use of artificial intelligence (AI). That includes using AI to draft or substantially rewrite text, to "produce a summary or analysis", or to generate images. It sets a "golden rule" that the person submitting is responsible for accuracy. It gives a separate instruction to councils: a council using AI to summarise representations must still send the original documents, and must say that it used AI. Improper use may count as unreasonable behaviour, which can lead to an award of costs. It says nothing about the 2026 NPPF, but it applies directly if our coded-decision analysis, or representations drafted with AI help, are put to an inspector.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do
- Councils (as decision-makers in the appeal chain): flag any AI use in case management or decision-making to PINS, and never send only AI summaries of representations in the appeal questionnaire.
- Professionals: follow their professional body's code. Each expert gives their own declaration; a single "catch all" declaration is not enough.
- Declarations should say what AI was used for, which parts of the document it affected, what checks were made, and that the author takes responsibility. Routine spelling and grammar tools need no declaration.

## Positions against our propositions
- **METH, not applicable (procedural only).** "It is your responsibility to ensure that the information generated is accurate and appropriate." (Introduction, the "golden rule".) The guidance says nothing about counting appeal outcomes, the weight of appeal decisions as precedent, or empirical analysis of decisions, so it takes no stance on our method. It is a submission-procedure rule. If AI was used to "produce a summary or analysis" in material sent to the Planning Inspectorate (PINS), the submission must declare it. The declaration says which sections are affected and what checks were made, and the example agent declaration confirms that quotes are "genuine, unedited extracts". This is a compliance step for any representation citing our analysis, not a challenge to the analysis.

## Leads
- Section 15 of the PINS planning appeals procedural guide (general obligations on submitted documents).
- The example agent declaration ("All quotes that have been included are genuine, unedited extracts from the original source documents") as a template for any representation that cites our decision analysis.
- Whether any council has published an internal AI-use protocol for officer reports. That would be a lead on how officers are trained.
