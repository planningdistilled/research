## Summary

This is the government's statutory heritage adviser responding to the draft NPPF. The live URL returned 403. The PDF was retrieved from a Wayback Machine snapshot taken on 12 March 2026, and the letter is dated 27 February 2026. Historic England welcomes the structured heritage-effects method (draft HE5) and the greater prominence of non-designated assets. It opposes the "approve unless substantially outweighed" wording in S4 and S5 as undermining the plan-led system. It warns that replacing "great weight" with "substantial weight" in HE6 may weaken heritage protection and sit uneasily with the statutory "special regard" duty. It says S5's categories, especially the unmet-need route (criterion j), are drawn so widely they could allow large development in sensitive places. It also says S5's terms ("reasonable walking distance", "high level of connectivity", "physically well-related") are vague and will be read inconsistently. It offers to help update the Planning Practice Guidance (PPG).

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

The response lobbies government. It asks that:
- heritage be named in S4(2)(a)(ii) and S5(4) as a reason that can outweigh benefits;
- "substantially outweighed" be softened to "clearly outweighed", or development be limited to "appropriate" proposals;
- the S5 terms be defined;
- "use consistent with the conservation of the asset" replace optimum viable use;
- grey belt exclude land whose openness contributes to heritage significance;
- the PPG be updated on heritage method.

## Positions against our propositions

- **S5 - agrees.** "In addition, Policy S5 1) h) contains multiple, vague criteria (e.g. 'reasonable walking distance', 'high level of connectivity', and 'physically well-related')... All these terms leave room for inconsistent interpretation, and need definition." (Q40, p.27; the source uses curly quotes and the passage runs over PDF line breaks). This supports our point that "physically well-related" has competing readings. It refers to draft criterion h (stations), and the lettering differs from the final S5(1)(j).
- **DIV - agrees.** Policy S5 "will be difficult for applicants and decision-makers to interpret, and therefore for it to be applied consistently and effectively." (Q38, p.26). This predicts that decision-makers will read S5 inconsistently. It is consistent with our finding that councils and inspectors split on the same tests, but it says nothing about which way they split.

## Leads

- The response cites a recent Court of Appeal decision on the duty to treat preserving the setting of a listed building as desirable (Q190, around line 3069 of the text). Identify the case as a heritage-method lead (DIV).
- The government response to Q189 says the "great" to "substantial" weight change does not reduce protection. Compare it with Historic England's concern.
