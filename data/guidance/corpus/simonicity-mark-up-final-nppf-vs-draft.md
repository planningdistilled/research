## Summary

A same-day blog post by Simon Ricketts of Town Legal, a planning law firm. It links to a Town Legal tracked-change comparison of the December 2025 draft and the August 2026 National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF), prepared by Archie Hunter and offered as "an indicative guide". It stresses that the policies take immediate effect for decision-making. It names two main substantive changes. First, standardised viability inputs were dropped, and DM5 now restates when viability assessment is appropriate. Second, the well-connected-station policy changed: there is a wider definition of "well-connected", a "reasonable walking distance" of around 800 metres or around 10 minutes, and minimum densities of 35 or 45 dwellings per hectare (dph). The rest of the post quotes the government's consultation response on viability, section 73/106A (varying permissions and planning obligations), model conditions, standard section 106 agreements, neighbourhood plans, specialist housing needs, commuted sums, the Housing Delivery Test (HDT) and Permission in Principle. It gives no guidance on settlements, S4/S5, Green Belt or the presumption.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Get to grips with the new Framework "in detail, without delay", because its policies "take immediate effect in terms of decision-making".
- Use the Town Legal redline to see where wording changed between draft and final, treating it as indicative only.
- Use the Connectivity Tool alongside other quantitative or qualitative evidence when assessing connectivity (quoting the Framework).

## Positions against our propositions

- None. The post takes no position on any of our propositions. Its only touch-point is the second main-change bullet, which quotes the Framework's own definition of "reasonable walking distance" for the well-connected-station policies ("around 800 metres, or around 10 minutes' walk time if topography, route availability and quality or physical barriers would prevent or discourage walking from up to 800 metres away"). That is a restatement of policy, offered without comment, and it concerns the station-proximity test (S5(1)(h), L3, GB7(1)(h)), not TR3 or GB7(1)(g)(iii). Earlier coding as "TR (qualifies)" was withdrawn on adversarial check (2 Oct 2026). Point for our own analysis, not a source stance: for the station policies the Framework itself makes distance the default and route quality a modifier.
