## Summary

A short consultancy blog on the December 2025 draft NPPF and neighbourhood plans. It says neighbourhood plans stay part of the statutory development plan but are reframed as "positive delivery tools". They should allocate land, focus on local matters such as design and infrastructure, and avoid repeating national or higher-level policy. It reports that the housing protection is "largely unchanged": a neighbourhood plan made within the last five years with allocations meeting its housing requirement still protects against conflicting proposals even when the presumption would otherwise apply. It is about the draft and does not cite final policy codes.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- (Aimed at parish councils) Draft neighbourhood plans tightly, keep them proportionate, plan positively for housing and support strategic policies.
- Avoid duplicating national policy in neighbourhood plans.
- Expect proposals conflicting with a recent, allocating neighbourhood plan to be refused even where the presumption applies.

## Positions against our propositions

- **SUP - agrees (weak; restates policy).** Adds no exception to our proposition. (paragraph beginning "In housing terms, the position is largely unchanged"). This is consistent with SUP: the shortfall opens the gate (the presumption applies) but does not decide the case, here because conflict with a recent, allocating neighbourhood plan weighs against the scheme.
