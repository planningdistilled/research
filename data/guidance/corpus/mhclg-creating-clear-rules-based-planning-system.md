## Summary

This is the government's policy paper published with the new NPPF on 17 August 2026. It says the decision-making policies, now split from the plan-making policies, take effect "from today". It says they are meant to make development management "more certain, consistent and streamlined" and to "avoid unjustified local deviation from national policy in local plans". It lists the changes made since the December 2025 consultation. These include widening the "default yes" for development around well-connected stations from the top 60 to the top 80 Travel to Work Areas (TTWAs), support for higher densities, a category of strategic sites of around 1,500 homes, stronger policy on Protected Landscapes and on keeping facilities such as pubs, and dropping "fast food outlets". It also covers the statutory consultee reforms and the Housing Delivery Test (HDT) results for 2024 and 2025, and it promises updated Planning Practice Guidance (PPG).

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Apply the new national decision-making policies immediately, from 17 August 2026.
- Use the 2025 HDT results for decision-making.
- Expect updated PPG and further support for council capacity "in the coming months".
- Note that the station "default yes" now covers stations in the top 80 TTWAs by Gross Value Added (GVA).

## Positions against our propositions

- None coded. The paper states aims (consistency, less local deviation) but gives no reading on settlement tiers, walking routes, S5 categories, Green Belt, supply or Annex A weight. It says nothing about appeal evidence either.
