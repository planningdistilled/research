## Summary

A short case note by Landmark Chambers on the Aston Clinton appeal (6008253, inspector Zoe Raygen, 24 Sep 2026), where Matthew Fraser acted for the successful appellant, Rectory Homes, for up to 66 homes. The note reports that the inspector found S5(1)(j) "physically well-related" not met, even though the site adjoins the settlement boundary, is accessed from within the village and is near services, solely because of limited harm to character, appearance and the village's setting. The inspector nonetheless allowed the appeal under S5(4) exceptional circumstances, with the benefits, "principally addressing the significant HLS [housing land supply] shortfall", substantially outweighing the harm, and the same benefits gave the "clear justification" needed under DP3(3). The note also reports that HO13(4) (consistency with an emerging strategic allocation) did not defeat a lower-density scheme on part of a draft allocation.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- S5(1)(j) "physically well-related" can be failed on character and setting harm alone, even for a site adjoining the settlement boundary.
- Failing every S5(1) category is not automatically fatal: S5(4) exceptional circumstances can be met where benefits "substantially outweigh" harm.
- The same benefits can supply the "clear justification" that stops DP3(3) requiring refusal.
- HO13(4) inconsistency with a draft strategic allocation (plan not yet submitted) may be given little force where the appeal site is a small part of it.

## Positions against our propositions

- **S5 - qualifies.** "applying policy S5(4), exceptional circumstances justified approval of the proposal despite it not falling within any of the S5(1) categories" (case note, para 3). A live exception to "falling outside every category is almost always fatal". Our case file PINS-6008253 already records this as a rare S5(4) pass.
- **S5 - agrees (competing readings of S5(1)(j)).** "The Inspector reached this conclusion solely based on "limited harm" caused by the proposal to the character and appearance of the area and to the setting and context of the village" (case note, para 2). Confirms the character-and-setting reading of "physically well-related", one of the readings we list.
- **SUP - qualifies.** "the benefits of the proposal (principally addressing the significant HLS shortfall) would "substantially outweigh" the adverse effects" (case note, para 3). This is a short case report, not an argument against our proposition. It reports one decision and makes no general claim about how much weight supply carries. It is still a real counter-example to "never decides": on counsel's account, the supply benefit tipped the S5(4) exceptional-circumstances balance and the DP3(3) "clear justification". The 25% affordable housing (HO8, substantial weight) and the HO13 emerging allocation in our case file PINS-6008253 (DL 41, 73-77, 82) were also benefits, so supply was not the only one. Our wording should read "rarely decides" rather than "never", and this case should be cited as a counter-example.
