## Summary

A long comparison of the December 2024 and August 2026 National Planning Policy Framework. It explains that paragraph 11 and the "tilted balance" are replaced by S3 (routing), S4 (within settlements) and S5 (outside settlements), so the decision now starts from location; five-year supply failure and a Housing Delivery Test below 75% become "evidenced unmet need" for S5(1)(j). It covers Annex A (materially inconsistent policy, or part of a policy, gets very limited weight; age alone does not reduce weight), the Annex B settlement definition (some Green Belt villages excluded), the recast Green Belt chapter (GB1 to GB8, grey-belt classification separate from acceptability), heritage terminology (no "less than substantial harm") and transition rules. It ends with a step-by-step checklist and cautions, including that 2024-era decisions need their policy context.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Confirm which Framework applies to the decision and, separately, to any plan in preparation.
- Establish settlement status under the current definition; apply S4 or the exact S5 category, splitting cross-boundary sites.
- Keep Green Belt and Local Green Space separate from the ordinary S5 route.
- Identify materially inconsistent local policies (or parts of policies) and check the Annex A exception; do not downgrade policies simply for age.
- Identify every express refusal instruction and whether its exception is met.
- Use the current weight words rather than importing 2024 phrasing; do not build a numerical scale of weight words.
- Treat grey-belt classification and acceptability as separate questions.
- Read older decisions in their own policy context.

## Positions against our propositions

- **SUP (agrees):** "The August 2026 NPPF starts somewhere different: location." (section "Paragraph 11 has gone: S3, S4 and S5 now control the route") — supply failure now only supplies S5(1)(j) need rather than engaging a tilted balance.
- **SH2 (agrees):** "Most hamlets and scattered groups of houses are excluded unless the development plan identifies them as settlements, and some Green Belt villages are excluded." (section "Settlement status, plots and density now carry more weight").
- **S5 (agrees):** "A proposal outside the listed categories needs exceptional circumstances." (section "Paragraph 11 has gone").
- **A2 (agrees):** "It says that a development plan policy, or part of a policy, which is materially inconsistent with a national decision-making policy should receive very limited weight." and "Other local policies do not lose weight simply because they are old." (section "The development plan still comes first") — supports a clause-level, inconsistency-based cut.
- **GB (agrees):** "Classification and acceptability are separate questions." (section "Green Belt and grey belt have been recast").
- **METH (agrees):** "Older decisions remain useful but need their policy context, because an inspector or court applying the 2024 wording was not deciding under the 2026 architecture" (section "Important cautions when comparing the two Frameworks") — supports restricting evidence to decisions under the August 2026 text.
