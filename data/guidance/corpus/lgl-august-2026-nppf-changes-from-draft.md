## Summary

This feature by Burges Salmon lawyers, published in Local Government Lawyer on 9 September 2026, compares the final August 2026 NPPF with the December 2025 consultation draft. It covers implementation: under Annex A paragraph 2 (the article calls it "Annex 1"), plan policies "or parts of those policies" that are materially inconsistent with the Framework get very limited weight, a narrower test than the draft's "in any way inconsistent". It explains the S3 to S5 structure: S4 applies within a settlement and S5 outside one, and split sites are assessed part by part. It covers the station policy (S5(1)(h) and GB7(1)(h)) with the Annex B "reasonable walking distance" definition, and the S5 unmet-need route. It also covers the softened Golden Rules in GB8 (15 percentage points above the local affordable housing requirement, capped at 50%, with viability exceptions), medium development and strategic sites, DM3 on consultees and DM6 on conditions. It expects early disputes over transitional arrangements, over which plan policies keep full weight, and over the walking-distance definition.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Give plan policies, or parts of them, that are "materially inconsistent" with the national decision-making policies very limited weight. Expect debate about which policies keep full weight.
- Apply S4 within a settlement and S5 outside one. Split sites are assessed part by part. Settlement boundaries and site composition become central judgements.
- Near well-connected stations, recognise that the local planning authority's scope to resist is "materially narrowed". Apply the Annex B walking-distance definition only to the part of the site that falls within it.
- Treat S5 unmet need as a route that is not limited to housing (it links to E2(2) on employment land).
- Under DM3(1)(d), consult only where necessary and do not hold decisions beyond statutory deadlines, except in the stated cases.
- Use national model conditions unless there are strong reasons not to, and avoid pre-commencement conditions (DM6).

## Positions against our propositions

- **A2 (agrees):** "development plan policies, or parts of those policies, which are materially inconsistent with the national decision-making policies in the new NPPF should be given very limited weight". The words "or parts of those policies" support our reading that only the offending clause is cut. The article adds that this is "still likely to give rise to early debate about which existing local plan policies can properly continue to carry full weight", so it does not confirm that heritage, design and spatial-preference policies keep full weight. (Implementation)
- **TR (silent; restates policy):** It quotes the Annex B definition verbatim, "around 800 metres, or around 10 minutes’ walk time if topography, route availability and quality or physical barriers would prevent or discourage walking from up to 800 metres away.", but it offers no view on whether distance or route quality decides. Its remark that "the interpretation and application of this will be subject to legal challenge" follows the Glossary note on counting only the parts of a site within the distance. It is not a critique of route-quality reasoning. The definition itself does make about 800 metres the starting point, with route quality switching the test to walk time. That is a point about the policy text, not about this source's stance. (Default ‘Yes’ around train stations)
- **S5 (agrees):** "residential and mixed-use development outside settlements can be approved where it is within reasonable walking distance of a well-connected station" and "Policy S5 contains a broader route for development outside settlements where it would meet an evidenced unmet need". These treat the S5(1) categories as the gateways for development outside settlements, which is consistent with our view that the category is the case. (Default ‘Yes’ around train stations; Unmet need)
- **SH1 (agrees, weakly):** "S3 directs decision-makers to apply S4 where development is within a settlement and S5 where development is outside a settlement" and "This is likely to make settlement boundaries, site composition and the treatment of split sites increasingly important in planning judgement." This supports the idea that the settlement question, not any local tier, decides the route. The article does not mention local hierarchy tiers. (Decision-making structure)
