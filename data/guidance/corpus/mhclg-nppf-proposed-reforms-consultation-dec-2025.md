## Summary

This is the government's December 2025 consultation document, which explains the draft restructured National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF) policy by policy. The council committee reports elsewhere in this corpus responded to it. It is about the draft, not the final August 2026 text, so some wording changed. For example, the final Annex A says "materially inconsistent", while this draft proposed "very little weight" for any inconsistent policy. It explains that the old paragraph 11(d) "tilt" is replaced by S4 (support within settlements, subject to listed exceptions) and S5 (a restricted list of acceptable development outside settlements). It says plans should define settlement boundaries or criteria to support S4/S5. It explains that the S5 unmet-need category reproduces the old presumption but adds a "well-related to an existing settlement" proviso. It says development that is not inappropriate in the Green Belt should be approved unless the benefits are substantially outweighed. It justifies non-statutory national policy partly by an appeal outcome statistic: 80% of major residential appeals on grey belt land have been allowed.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Treat the NPPF as a material consideration from the day the final version is published. In the draft, give inconsistent development plan policies "very little weight" unless they were examined and adopted against the new Framework.
- Within settlements, support development in principle subject to the S4 exceptions (allocated land, valuable undeveloped land, and "should be refused" policies elsewhere in the Framework).
- Outside settlements, accept only the listed S5 categories. Where meeting an evidenced unmet need (for example, no five-year housing land supply), require the site to be well-related to an existing settlement.
- Limit station-area development to land physically well-related to the station and within reasonable walking distance.
- For development that is not inappropriate in the Green Belt, approve unless the benefits would be substantially outweighed by adverse effects.
- Use the Connectivity Tool when assessing connectivity under TR3.

## Positions against our propositions

- **SH1, agrees.** "It emphasises the importance of identifying settlement boundaries, or clear criteria for identifying settlements, to support the proposed approach to decision-making on development within and outside settlements (policies S4 and S5)." (section "S2: Producing a spatial strategy"). Local plan settlement definitions are presented as feeding the S4/S5 route, not as carrying separate weight.
- **S5, agrees.** "this policy restricts the types of development that are considered acceptable outside settlements, in order to prevent unsustainable patterns of growth and conserve rural character." (section "S5: Principle of development outside settlements"). The listed category is the gateway outside settlements.
- **SUP, agrees.** "development of other sorts which would meet an evidenced unmet need (for example in instances where there is not a five-year housing land supply)" together with "although it adds the proviso that in such circumstances development should be well-related to an existing settlement (unless the nature of the use would make this inappropriate), to guard against development being badly-located." (section "S5: Principle of development outside settlements"). A shortfall opens an S5 category, but location still has to pass, which fits "opens the gate but never decides". This is the draft's explanation of what became S5(1)(j).
- **GB, silent (restates draft policy; checked against our pattern 18).** "where development is ‘not inappropriate’ in the Green Belt, the same approach should be taken – i.e. of approving development unless the benefits would be substantially outweighed by any adverse effects." (section "S5: Principle of development outside settlements"). Checked: no conflict, so this is not a qualification. The passage describes the draft S5 final-paragraph balance for Green Belt development that is not inappropriate: approve unless the benefits are substantially outweighed. That is a starting point that still needs a balance, not approval. Our pattern 18 already applies this balance: GB7 passes still lost through the S5(2) "should be refused" triggers. The source restates draft policy and does not take a position on our claim (silent).
- **GB, agrees.** "Since the current Framework was updated in December 2024, an unprecedented 80% of major residential appeals located on grey belt land have been approved" (section "The case for non-statutory national policy"). This is consistent with grey belt usually being won at appeal.
- **A2, silent (checked 2 Oct 2026; earlier coded "disagrees").** "we are proposing that development plan policies should be afforded very little weight where inconsistency arises, except where they have been examined and adopted against the new Framework." (section "Transitional arrangements", "For the purposes of decision-making"); also "requiring that any inconsistent local policies are immediately given very limited weight." (section "The case for non-statutory national policy"). This is the December 2025 draft. It sets how much weight inconsistent policies get ("very little"), but it does not say whether that cuts the whole policy or only the inconsistent part. It also says nothing about heritage, design or spatial policies. The same section says that where plan and Framework policies are consistent "this would be straightforward", so policies with no conflict keep their weight. Reading it as cutting the whole policy, with no carve-out, goes beyond the text. The final Annex A uses "materially inconsistent" instead (see the Landmark slides entry). No change to A2 is needed.
- **METH, agrees.** "Since the current Framework was updated in December 2024, an unprecedented 80% of major residential appeals located on grey belt land have been approved" (section "The case for non-statutory national policy"). The government itself uses appeal outcome rates as evidence of how national policy works in practice, which is our method.

## Leads

- The source of the government's "80% of major residential appeals on grey belt" figure (probably a Planning Inspectorate (PINS) or MHCLG analysis). A comparable figure from our database under the August 2026 NPPF would be a direct test.
- Consultation questions 35 (definition of settlements), 37 to 39 (S4/S5) and 152 (TR3 and the Connectivity Tool). The government response (August 2026) summarises respondents' views on these, including any objection to treating washed-over Green Belt villages as settlements.
- Final Annex A wording ("materially inconsistent") compared with this draft's "very little weight". This matters for A2.
