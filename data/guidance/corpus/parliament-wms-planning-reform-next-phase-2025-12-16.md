## Summary

The Housing Minister's Written Ministerial Statement (WMS) launching the draft Framework. It says statutory National Development Management Policies (NDMPs) will not be pursued for now; instead the Framework's decision-making policies are designed to make development management "more certain, consistent and streamlined" and to "effectively override conflicting policies in local plans from day one". It lists the headline reforms: a permanent presumption in favour of suitably located development, "default yes" around stations including in the Green Belt, densification, small and medium sites, and funding for planning capacity (including recruiting about 1,400 planning officers). It is a political statement of intent, not a guide to applying specific tests.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Expect national decision-making policies to override conflicting local plan policies from the day the Framework is published.
- Treat suitable urban land as acceptable by default and apply the revised presumption to direct development to appropriate locations.
- Give "in principle" support to suitable housing near well-connected stations, including in the Green Belt.

## Positions against our propositions

- **A2 — silent (downgraded from "qualifies" on adversarial check, 2 Oct 2026).** The quote is verbatim ("A revised National Planning Policy Framework" section: "the government is proposing that the new national decision-making policies effectively override conflicting policies in local plans from day one"), but it is a one-line political statement about the December 2025 consultation draft ("proposing"), not about the final August 2026 Annex A para 2. It says nothing about whether a whole policy or only its conflicting part loses weight, and nothing about heritage, design, access or spatial-preference policies. "Override conflicting policies" is consistent with our reading that only the conflicting part is displaced. Context only; no change to A2.
