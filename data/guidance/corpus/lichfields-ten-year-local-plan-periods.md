## Summary

Matthew Spry (Lichfields) criticises the August 2026 National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF) for cutting the minimum local plan horizon from 15 years to "no less than 10 years from the point of adoption" (Policy PM2). He notes 69% of consultation respondents opposed the change and traces the history back to PPG12 (Planning Policy Guidance 12, 1999). He argues the effective period in which a plan's new allocations shape delivery is only about years 6 to 10, so strategic sites (1,500+ homes, Policy HO4) contribute little within the plan period and local planning authorities (LPAs) have less incentive to allocate them. Spatial Development Strategies (SDSs, now 25-year horizons) are offered by Government as the answer, but he says they cannot allocate sites and depend on untested governance. This is a plan-making piece; it says nothing about how decision-takers apply the Framework to individual applications.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Plan-makers: use Policy PM2(1)(a)(iii) to show how the plan reflects longer-term expectations beyond the 10-year period, especially for large-scale sites.
- Expect examining inspectors to ask whether a plan aligns with the longer-term SDS picture rather than only the first decade's need.
- Because delivery risk is concentrated in years 6 to 10, bring better deliverability evidence or allocate more land than the requirement as a buffer.

## Positions against our propositions

Silent on all of SH1, SH2, TR, S5, SUP, GB, A2, DIV and METH. It concerns plan periods, not decision-taking.
