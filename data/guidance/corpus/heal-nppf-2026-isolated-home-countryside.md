# The New NPPF 2026 Explained – Can You Build An Isolated Home In The Countryside?

## Summary

Explains that under S5 an isolated new home outside settlements or groups of houses should not normally be approved unless it meets one of the circumstances in HO11: an essential rural worker need, securing a heritage asset's future, reuse of a redundant building, subdivision of an existing dwelling, or exceptional design quality. The exceptional-design route is described as a very high bar. It says self-build status, land ownership and personal connections are not a separate justification. It advises first checking whether the site is genuinely isolated, because land next to a recognisable group of houses may fall under other S5 routes, including limited infill or evidenced unmet need.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Refuse isolated new homes in the countryside unless one of the HO11 circumstances applies.
- Give no separate weight to self-build status, land ownership or personal connection to the area.
- Treat the exceptional-design route as a very high threshold (beyond ordinary good design).
- First decide whether the site is genuinely isolated; if it adjoins a group of houses, consider the other S5 routes (limited infill, evidenced unmet need).

## Positions against our propositions

- **S5: agrees.** "Importantly, self-build status, land ownership or personal connections to an area do not provide a separate justification for an isolated countryside dwelling." (Body, fifth paragraph). Supports our reading that outside settlements the category is the whole case: personal circumstances cannot substitute for an S5/HO11 category.
- **S5: silent (restates policy).** "Land adjoining a recognisable group of houses may potentially fall under other provisions within Policy S5, including limited infill or development addressing evidenced unmet need." (Body, sixth paragraph). This is a hedged signpost, not a reading of S5(1)(j). "Limited infill" is S5(1)(e) (limited infilling within groups of houses), and the point follows from S5(3), which defines isolated homes as those outside settlements or groups of houses. The source does not say a group of houses is a "settlement", and it does not address the S5(1)(j)(i) "physically well-related to an existing settlement" test. It is consistent with our view that the S5(1) category decides the case, and takes no side on the competing readings of (j). (Checked 2 Oct 2026: quote verbatim; an earlier "qualifies" coding was withdrawn.)

- Check appeals on S5(1)(j) for sites next to groups of houses, as against villages.
