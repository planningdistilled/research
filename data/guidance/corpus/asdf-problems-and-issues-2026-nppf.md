## Summary

A long policy blog post extending the author's March 2026 response to the December 2025 consultation draft of the National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF). Most of it is about viability, affordable housing obligations and housing-delivery models, not decision-taking. Section two, "Point Two", is the part relevant to us: it describes the draft S2–S5 presumption as a shift to a rules-based system within settlements, with S5 applying only in "carved-in" categories outside them. It predicts that the line between inside and outside "settlements" will generate contested applications and appeals, and that weighing "substantially outweigh" will stay a local discretionary judgment. It is written about the draft, so policy wording may differ from the August 2026 text.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Expect settlement-boundary questions to drive applications and appeals under S4/S5.
- Expect greenfield sites outside every S5 "carved-in" category to be restricted.
- Note that under draft Annex A, local plan policies inconsistent with the Framework get "very limited weight".
- Mostly policy critique (viability, affordable housing, spatial development strategies) rather than practical instruction.

## Positions against our propositions

- **S5 — agrees.** "Beyond these ‘carved-in’ categories, approval should only be granted if exceptional circumstances are deemed to apply" and "They could also restrict development on greenfield sites not specifically covered by the S5 stated ‘carved-in’ exceptions." (Section 2, Point Two). This supports our view that falling outside every S5(1) category is close to fatal (draft wording).
- **SH1 — agrees (indirect).** "The separate treatment of land in and outside of areas defined as ‘settlements’ (policy S2a) combined with the importance accorded by policies S4 and S5 to the demarcation of settlement boundaries, however, could lead to a new series of contested applications and appeals." (Point Two). The route depends on the defined-settlement question, not on local tier labels; it does not discuss local tiers directly.
- **METH — silent.** The post never discusses appeal decisions as evidence, counting outcomes or precedent. Its line "a judgment whichwill continue to be subject to local discretionary decision" (Point Two; the typo is in the original) is about the draft presumption: conflicts with affordable-housing or design policy will still turn on the "substantially outweigh" balance. That restates how the policy works. It does not criticise empirical coding, and our method already codes the weighing (harm/weight findings) rather than treating outcomes as rules. (An earlier pass coded this as "qualifies"; adversarial check on 2026-10-02 found it does not hold.)
