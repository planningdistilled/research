# Planning reform in focus: Highlights from our draft NPPF roundtable with hgh Consulting

## Summary

Short write-up of a Cornerstone and hgh Consulting roundtable (development industry delegates) on the decision-making policies in the draft NPPF. It describes the shift from a process-based presumption to a location-based one (S4 within settlements, S5 for certain forms outside). It lists likely battlegrounds: whether a place is a settlement (hamlet or village), whether settlement boundaries are definitive, the S5 categories, reasonable walking distance (and why not cycling), and whether 'evidenced unmet need' for housing is limited to five-year supply and Housing Delivery Test failure. It also flags the reverse presumption (S5(4)), minimum densities, heritage wording and the loss of 'valued landscapes'.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Decide first whether the place is a settlement at all (hamlet versus village) and whether the site is within it; do not assume plan boundaries are definitive.
- Test whether the proposal falls in an S5 'acceptable in principle' category; otherwise the reverse presumption S5(4) applies.
- For 'evidenced unmet need', ask whether housing need is limited to the five-year supply and Housing Delivery Test triggers.

## Positions against our propositions

- **SH2 – silent** (Key insights, 'The new presumption: likely battlegrounds'): "Is it a settlement (hamlet vs village)? Is the site within the settlement (settlement boundaries appear not to be definitive)?" Lists open questions on the draft Framework (consultation stage, not the August 2026 text). It takes no position on what counts as a settlement and says nothing about Green Belt or washed-over villages, so it neither supports nor qualifies SH2. It does confirm that settlement status and boundaries were expected to be disputed. (Re-coded from 'qualifies' after adversarial check, 2 Oct 2026.)
- **S5 – agrees** (Key insights, 'The reverse presumption (S5(4))'): "The reverse presumption (S5(4)) – a new restriction on promoting development outside of settlements which are not “acceptable in principle”." Recognises S5(4) as a restriction on development outside the categories. Draft stage.
- **S5 – silent** (Key insights, 'The new presumption: likely battlegrounds'): "Is there “evidenced unmet need” (and, for housing, is this restricted to demonstrating a lack of 5yrHLS and <75%HDT?)" An open question from a roundtable on the consultation draft, not a position. It asks whether the need limb of the S5(1)(j) gate is limited to the supply and delivery triggers, which our pattern 1 (SUP) already covers (shortfall or HDT below 75% makes need automatic; PINS-6005809 ¶57 "including, but not limited to"). It says nothing on whether the S5(1) category decides the case, on S5(4) being fatal, or on "physically well-related". No conflict. Draft stage.
