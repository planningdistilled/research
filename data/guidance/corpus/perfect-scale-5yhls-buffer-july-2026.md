## Summary

A research briefing from a data consultancy about the December 2024 NPPF, not the August 2026 Framework. It explains the paragraph 78(c) rule that from 1 July 2026 adds a 20% buffer to the five-year housing land supply (5YHLS) requirement in seven London boroughs, making it more likely the "tilted balance" (paragraph 11(d)) applies. It uses the firm's dataset of about 2,900 small-site (1-9 home) decisions to show borough refusal rates and argues the buffer will shift marginal, officer-delegated decisions. It is explicit that the tilt does not force approvals but raises the cost of refusing. Only indirectly relevant to the 2026 Framework.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Aimed at applicants: time validation so determination falls after 1 July 2026 in the seven boroughs; check the latest Annual Position Statement before relying on a planning balance.
- Notes that small-site refusals are mostly delegated, so the case officer's reading of the planning balance does most of the work.
- Notes section 62A designation (Epping Forest, June 2026) when over 10% of major decisions are overturned at appeal.

## Positions against our propositions

- **SUP — agrees.** "This does not force approvals. It raises the cost of refusing, most visibly at the marginal scheme." (section "Why the trigger matters for refusal defensibility"). A supply shortfall shifts the balance but does not decide the case.
- **METH — agrees (analogous approach).** "For applicants weighing whether to push a marginal scheme before 1 July, those three numbers together are the empirical version of the question the buffer raises in policy terms." (section "What the data shows for the seven boroughs", closing paragraph). Uses counted decision outcomes as evidence of how policy plays out, as we do, though with council decisions rather than appeals.
