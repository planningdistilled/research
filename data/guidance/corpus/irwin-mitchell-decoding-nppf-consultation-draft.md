## Summary

An overview by Irwin Mitchell (author Anna Tranter, solicitor) of the December 2025 draft NPPF. It explains the split into plan-making policies and National Decision-Making Policies (NDMPs), and says local plans that conflict with NDMPs will carry "very limited weight", while flagging that how conflicts are reconciled is uncertain. It describes the change from the "tilted balance" to a permanent "presumption in favour of suitably located development", quoting the Government's aim of a rules-based approach that says what is acceptable in principle in each location. It also covers higher densities near well-connected stations (including in the Green Belt), support for small and medium-sized enterprise (SME) builders on 1–2.5 hectare sites, and grey belt: sites meeting the Golden Rules would no longer need to show very special circumstances (VSC). About the draft only.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Not addressed to officers directly; it tells developers to check whether forthcoming proposals fit the new location-based approach.
- Treat local plan policies that conflict with national decision-making policies as carrying very limited weight (draft wording).
- Expect interpretation and transitional questions about how existing plans and NDMPs are reconciled.

## Positions against our propositions

- **A2 — silent.** It says "Local plans which conflict with NDMPs will carry “very limited weight” (meaning that such local plans will be treated as having very little influence on decision-making) once the revised NPPF is effective" (section "Structural re-organisation and the introduction of NDMPs"). This is a one-line summary of the December 2025 *draft's* proposed rule on conflict with National Decision-Making Policies (NDMPs). It is not about Annex A para 2 of the August 2026 text, and it does not address whether weight is lost for the whole plan or only for the conflicting policy or clause. "Local plans" is loose shorthand, and the author goes straight on to call reconciliation an open question ("how will inconsistencies and tensions between NDMPs and existing local plans be reconciled/ addressed?"). So it neither supports nor challenges our clause-only reading. (On adversarial check, 2 Oct 2026, this was recoded from "qualifies".)
- **S5 — agrees.** Quotes the Government: "want to make clear what forms of development are acceptable in principle in different locations as part of creating a more rules-based approach to development" (section "Sustainable Development: From tilted balance to permanent presumption"). Supports our reading that location category decides the case outside settlements.
- **GB — silent (restates draft policy).** "Sites within Grey Belt land that comply with the Golden Rules (also introduced in December 2024) no longer have to demonstrate that they meet the “very special circumstances” test" (section "Green and Grey Belt Flexibility"). This restates the December 2024 / draft rule that compliant grey belt development is not inappropriate, so very special circumstances (VSC) are not needed. It goes on "and where the benefits ... are compelling – providing a clearer scope for development", so it does not claim that passing the grey belt test means approval. It says nothing on where grey belt is won, on housing supply as VSC, or on what follows after GB7. It is about the draft only. Not a qualification of our proposition.
