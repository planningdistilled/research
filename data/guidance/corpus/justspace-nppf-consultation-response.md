## Summary

An eight-page response by Just Space, a London network of grass-roots community groups, to the December 2025 draft National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF). It is not guidance on applying the final August 2026 Framework; it attacks the draft's structure. Its central objection is that draft policy PM6 (plans must not restate or modify national decision-making policies, NDMPs) read with draft Annex A(2) (plan policies "in any way inconsistent" with NDMPs get "very little weight") would end local plan-making. It also objects to the S4 presumption within settlements as an "impossibly high bar" for refusal and asks for tighter readings of HO7 (housing need), DM5 (viability), HE5/HE6 (heritage harm thresholds and "public benefits") and HC6 (community facilities).

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- (Asks government, not officers) Keep an even planning balance rather than an S4 presumption; if kept, test adverse effects against the development plan as well as the NDMPs.
- Treat housing that does not meet an evidenced local need (affordability, unit size) as carrying no weight under HO7.
- Do not count private housing, or benefits already required for policy compliance, as "public benefits" outweighing heritage harm under HE6.
- Set the threshold for "substantial harm" to heritage relatively lower.

## Positions against our propositions

- **A2 - silent (checked 2 Oct 2026; earlier "disagrees" coding withdrawn).** The quote is verbatim (p. 2): read with draft PM6, draft Annex A(2) giving "very little weight" to plan policies "which are in any way inconsistent with NDMPs" "effectively announces the end of the local plan, local knowledge of need and local decision-making." This is a constitutional and political objection to the December 2025 draft: the NPPF becoming an unexamined national development plan, against the statutory primacy of local plans. It does not say whether a whole policy or only the inconsistent clause loses weight, and it says nothing about the final August 2026 text or how inspectors apply it. So it does not engage A2's clause-versus-whole-policy finding. At most it is context: the draft wording worried campaigners that whole plans would be sidelined, which appeal practice so far has not borne out.
