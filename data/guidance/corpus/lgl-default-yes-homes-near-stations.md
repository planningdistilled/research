## Summary

News report of publication day (17 August 2026). It reproduces the Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government's (MHCLG's) list of main changes: a "more explicit presumption in favour of development in sustainable locations, including development within settlements", a "default yes" and minimum densities around well-connected stations, 40% accessible homes on major schemes, support for data centres and AI Growth Zones, and a new "medium" development category. Its most useful content for us is the County Councils Network (CCN) reaction: it fears national policy will override adopted local plans and stop councils refusing development "not connected to existing settlements". A Browne Jacobson partner calls it the biggest rewrite since 2012.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Nothing directly; it is a news report.
- Implicitly (CCN): expect national decision-making policies to prevail over local plan spatial policies in many cases, and expect the station "default yes" to bite in villages.

## Positions against our propositions

- **A2 – silent (tension only, checked 2 Oct 2026).** The County Councils Network (CCN) does not discuss Annex A para 2 or how much weight an inconsistent plan policy keeps. Its statement is a publication-day political forecast that national decision-making policy will win out over adopted plans on location: "in many instances national policy will override this and mean that councils are unable to refuse development in locations that are not connected to existing settlements" (CCN statement, Cllr Andrew Husband). In the same statement the CCN concedes that the government has said some policies "will not operate in isolation" and that "it is unclear how this will work in practice". That is a prediction about outcomes under the new location policies (S4/S5 and the presumption), not a reading of the consistency test. It does not contradict our finding that only the inconsistent clause loses weight. At most it is a contextual tension to watch against appeal outcomes.
- **S5 – qualifies (tension, not a reading of S5).** Same quote. The CCN predicts on publication day that national policy will in many cases leave councils unable to refuse development "not connected to existing settlements". It does not cite or interpret S5, name any S5(1) category, or say that development outside every category succeeds; it is a lobby group's political forecast in the same breath as its station "default yes" concern. It is consistent with S5(1) categories (for example S5(1)(j) on a broad reading) letting some unconnected sites through, which our proposition already allows for. It is not evidence against our finding that falling outside every S5(1) category is almost always fatal at appeal; at most it is a prediction our 971 decisions can test.
