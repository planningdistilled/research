# The Rural ROAR: Green Not Grey

## Summary

A campaign site, founded by Effingham Parish Council (resolution adopted 26 August 2026), for a 'Rural Alliance for Planning Change'. It argues that under the August 2026 NPPF grey belt is defined only by Green Belt purposes A, B and D, which Annex E says concern large built-up areas and towns, not villages, so open countryside around villages is a 'rural blind spot' and purpose C (encroachment) is not tested. It cites a Guildford Green Belt Assessment (551 of 575 areas did not strongly contribute to A, B or D), a Buckinghamshire assessment (83% of the study area grey after removal of footnote 7) and October 2025 research finding 16 of the first 20 major residential grey belt appeals allowed. It asks for grey belt to be suspended for villages, for parish call-in and veto powers, and for Neighbourhood Plan protection, and is careful to say these are proposed reforms, not current law. It gives a parish-level audit method (a parcel-by-parcel grey belt and purpose C audit) rather than officer guidance.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Asks local planning authorities, pending reform, to record grey belt findings as contested.
- Asks parishes to audit, parcel by parcel, which grey belt purposes were tested and where purpose C would change the conclusion.
- States the current law accurately: decisions remain with the local planning authority; a made Neighbourhood Plan may be outweighed by material considerations; purpose C is not part of the grey belt test.

## Positions against our propositions

- **GB: agrees.** "This is the rural blind spot: in the grey-belt test, the green fields around villages appear not to matter." (Home page, campaign introduction). Supports our finding that grey belt is usually won at a village edge, explaining why: the purposes test towns, not villages.
- **GB: agrees.** "Open countryside beyond a defined village settlement boundary can therefore be recast as low-contribution land without testing the purpose it performs most directly." (Charter grievance 03). Same point, framed against village settlement boundaries.
- **METH: agrees.** "Research published in October 2025 found that 16 of the first 20 major residential grey-belt appeals—schemes of ten homes or more—were allowed." (Home page, evidence panel). Uses appeal outcome counts as evidence of how the policy is applied, as we do. Pre-August 2026 decisions; small sample.
