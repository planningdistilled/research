## Summary

A developer guide to grey belt eligibility written before the August 2026 National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF): it cites December 2024 paragraphs 154 and 155, not GB7(1)(g), so it describes the earlier Framework. It frames four tests (grey belt status, not undermining the remaining Green Belt, unmet housing need, sustainable location) plus the Golden Rules. It reports early appeal patterns (citing a Lichfields figure of about 36% of grey belt housing appeals allowed by March 2025, and the Marrons tracker). It warns that these are observed patterns, not statistics. It says the sustainable-location test is the most common failure, judged on the totality of accessibility and service frequency, and that grey belt status is not a guarantee of approval.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Mainly addressed to developers; it describes how inspectors and officers read each test.
- Read open fields on the urban edge as still serving purpose (a), preventing sprawl.
- Judge sustainable location on overall accessibility, including bus frequency and destinations, not distance alone.
- Keep heritage and ecology considerations in play after grey belt status is found.

## Positions against our propositions

- GB (agrees): "It is also not a guarantee of approval." ("What grey belt is, and what it is not"); also "Grey belt eligibility does not switch off other planning considerations." December 2024 Framework.
- TR (silent; checked 2026-10-02, earlier "qualifies" withdrawn): "Inspectors look at the totality of accessibility rather than a single factor — a site with weaker bus access but strong walkable amenities can pass; a site reliant entirely on private car use rarely does." (Test 4: Sustainable location); also "Distance, frequency, and connectivity matter. A 400m walk to a hourly bus is not the same as a 400m walk to a 15-minute service." (desktop screen, Test 4). This is about public transport frequency and the mix of nearby amenities under the December 2024 grey belt sustainable-location test, written as a developer's desktop-screening guide. It never mentions footways, lighting, walking in the carriageway or route quality, so it neither supports nor undercuts our claim that route quality decides. Where it overlaps with us, it agrees that distance alone does not decide. "No single factor" is a general framing, not a finding against the walking-route test.
- METH (agrees): "These are observed patterns, not statistics. Treat them as a directional signal until a meaningful body of determinations builds up." ("What approval data tells us so far"). This is a caveat about its own thin early sample under the December 2024 Framework, not a critique of the method. It implies that outcome patterns become reliable once "a meaningful body of determinations" exists, which is what our 971-decision set is meant to be. It still reminds us to report small-cell rates (single routes or tests with few decisions) as directional only.
- METH (agrees): "Lichfields published an analysis in March 2025 showing approximately 36% of grey belt housing appeals allowed at that point." (same section). An appeal outcome rate used as evidence.
