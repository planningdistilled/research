## Summary

A 71-minute walkthrough of the August 2026 National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF), recorded the day after publication by a panel of planning King's Counsel (KC) and barristers. The local copy is YouTube's **auto-generated captions** (speech recognition, unedited), with timestamps. Quotes are verbatim from the captions, so transcription errors are kept (for example "Monk Hill" for Monkhill and "HTT" for HDT). Charles Banner KC covers chapter 4: settlements under Annex B exclude hamlets and Green Belt villages; S4 applies inside settlements; outside them S5(1) lists qualifying categories, and S5(4) allows approval only in exceptional circumstances. He also argues S5(1)(j) "evidenced unmet need" is "not limited to" housing-supply shortfall, and S5(2) reverses Monkhill. Other panellists cover viability, climate and water, housing, employment, Green Belt (GB4 washed-over villages, GB7(1)(h) stations, GB8 Golden Rules), natural environment and heritage (HE5/HE6).

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Check the Annex B definition first: hamlets and villages in the Green Belt are not "settlements", so expect village-or-hamlet disputes in place of the old town-or-village ones.
- Inside settlements apply S4; its S4(2) list of likely-outweighing harms is indicative only (the word "likely").
- Outside settlements, find an S5(1) category; if none applies, S5(4) gives a strong tilt against (possible tension with section 38(6) of the Planning and Compulsory Purchase Act 2004, mis-cited on the show as the Town and Country Planning Act).
- Treat a five-year supply shortfall or a Housing Delivery Test (HDT) result below 75% as one gateway into S5(1)(j), not the only one: evidenced unmet need for affordable or family housing, hotels and so on may also qualify.
- Under S5(2), only Framework policies that direct refusal are show-stoppers; weight-only policies (for example non-major development in National Landscapes) go into the balance.
- GB4: only villages not classed as settlements in the spatial strategy should be washed over by Green Belt.

## Positions against our propositions

- **SH2 — agrees.** "villages in the green belt are excluded from the definition" ([09:50], Charles Banner KC on S2/Annex B). Related point on plan-making: "it's only villages which are not classified as settlements for the purposes of the spatial strategy which should be included in the green belt, i.e. washed over with it." ([50:17], on GB4).
- **SH1 — agrees (indirect).** "Settlements are as defined in um in Annex B, it's important to note they exclude hamlets." ([08:48]). The S4/S5 route turns on the Annex B definition, not on local labels.
- **S5 — agrees.** "S5. um four indicates that development should only be approved in exceptional circumstances where the benefits substantially outweigh the adverse effects." ([14:07]).
- **S5 — silent (different limb of (j)).** "It seems to me that in principle those kinds of arguments are open for somebody to make given the wording of S5J subject in particular to evidence substantiating the need for those kinds of developments." ([17:22]). This is about the "evidenced unmet need" gateway of S5(1)(j) (affordable or family housing, hotels, beyond a supply or HDT shortfall), not the "physically well-related" limb whose competing readings our S5 proposition concerns. It does not conflict with S5; it widens how (j) can be entered (see SUP below), and he does not address how "physically well-related" should be read.
- **SUP — agrees (gate only).** "clearly as before a a sub 5-year supply or sub 75% HDT will trigger uh the new tilted balance" ([16:16]). This restates the policy that a shortfall opens the gate. His point that it "isn't the only way to get through" ([16:16]) is about other ways into S5(1)(j) (see S5 above), not about how much weight a shortfall carries. It does not cut against SUP, which does not say supply is the only gate. He says nothing on whether a shortfall decides the case.
