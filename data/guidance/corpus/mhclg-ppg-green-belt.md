# Green Belt (Planning Practice Guidance)

## Summary
This is the official grey belt and Green Belt guidance. It was last updated on 27 February 2025 and still cites the December 2024 NPPF (paragraphs 147, 148, 155, 110, 115 and footnotes 7 and 55); it has not been updated for the 2026 policy codes GB1-GB8. It explains how to judge whether land "strongly contributes" to Green Belt purposes (a) checking sprawl, (b) stopping towns merging and (d) the setting of historic towns, saying that purposes (a), (b) and (d) concern large built-up areas, towns and historic towns, not villages. It confirms that grey belt status and "not inappropriate" status do not by themselves mean approval, and that grey belt land in a location that is not, and cannot be made, sustainable is inappropriate. It is the only official guidance on grey belt, so councils and inspectors working under GB7 are mapping 2024 paragraph references onto the 2026 codes themselves.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do
- Where no Green Belt assessment covers the site, consider evidence on (1) strong contribution to purposes (a), (b) or (d); (2) whether footnote 7 policies (protected areas and assets) give a strong reason to refuse; (3) whether development would fundamentally undermine the purposes of the remaining Green Belt across the plan area.
- Do not treat villages as "large built up areas" (purpose a), and apply purpose (b) to towns only and purpose (d) to historic towns only.
- Make sure grey belt development is in a sustainable location, judged on local context and the specific site; consider ways to maximise sustainable transport.
- Even where development is not inappropriate, apply section 38(6) of the Planning and Compulsory Purchase Act 2004 (decide in line with the development plan unless material considerations indicate otherwise) and the wider Framework.
- Openness: weigh spatial and visual effects, how long the development lasts and whether it can be reversed, and how much activity it generates. Where development is not inappropriate, harm to openness carries no "substantial weight" (December 2024 footnote 55).

## Positions against our propositions
- **GB, agrees.** "Where a development is not inappropriate in the Green Belt, this does not itself remove the land from the Green Belt nor require development proposals to be approved." (Paragraph 010, Reference ID 64-010-20250225.) This supports our point that passing the grey belt test (now GB7) is not approval.
- **GB, agrees.** "Villages should not be considered large built up areas." (Paragraph 005, Purpose A.) Land at a village edge therefore rarely contributes strongly to purposes (a) or (b), which is consistent with our finding that grey belt is usually won at a village edge.
- **TR, silent.** "where grey belt land is not in a location that is or can be made sustainable, development on this land is inappropriate." (Paragraph 011, Reference ID 64-011-20250225.) This restates the policy test (2024 Framework paragraph 155, now GB7). It adds that sustainability is "determined in light of local context and site or development-specific considerations", with regard to opportunities to maximise sustainable transport. It says nothing about route quality versus distance, carriageway walking or lighting. Its point that a location "can be made" sustainable fits with TR: a route improved so that people no longer walk in the carriageway would pass on TR's own test. So this is not a qualification, and our analysis does not need to change. (Rechecked 2 Oct 2026; earlier coded "qualifies".)
