## Summary

This is the official text of Chapter 6 of the August 2026 National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF): plan-making policies on housing need, requirements, land supply and site mix (HO1-HO6) and decision-making policies HO7-HO13. The key decision-making policy for us is HO7, which gives "substantial weight" to the benefits of homes that meet evidenced needs; it is framed around need, not around whether the council has a five-year housing land supply. HO10 (rural and community-led exception sites outside settlements) uses the phrase "adjoin or be physically well-related to a settlement", the same "physically well-related" wording as S5(1)(j). HO11 lists the only circumstances for isolated homes in the countryside, and HO12 allows traveller sites "not well-related to existing settlements" in some cases.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Give substantial weight to the benefits of homes that meet the community's evidenced accommodation needs, including the needs of particular groups (HO7).
- Require affordable housing to meet or exceed up-to-date plan requirements, on site unless off-site or a commuted sum is robustly justified (HO8).
- Support rural exception sites and community-led schemes outside settlements where they adjoin or are physically well-related to a settlement, are no larger than 1 hectare or 5% of the settlement, and include affordable housing (HO10).
- Support isolated homes in the countryside only in the five listed circumstances (HO11).
- When assessing need for traveller sites under S5 and GB7, take into account existing local provision and personal circumstances (HO12(2)).
- Consider shorter commencement conditions to speed build-out (HO13(2)).

## Positions against our propositions

- **SUP - silent (policy text, not a stance).** "In applying the policies in this Framework, substantial weight should be given to the benefits of providing homes which will contribute towards meeting the evidenced accommodation needs of the community" (HO7(1)). This is the Framework's own wording, not a commentator's view, and HO7 says nothing about land supply or a shortfall either way. Our reading is that the weight attaches to evidenced need rather than to a supply shortfall, so it would still apply where a five-year supply exists. That is our inference from the text and the source does not say it. It fits SUP and does not qualify it. Adversarial check, 2 Oct 2026: quote verbatim (local text line 266); stance recoded from "qualifies" to "silent".
- **S5 - not applicable (policy text, different test).** "Adjoin or be physically well-related to a settlement" (HO10(2)(a), exception sites). This is the Framework's own wording for a different policy, not a view on S5(1)(j). Our own textual inference is that listing "adjoin" and "physically well-related" as alternatives suggests the second can be met without adjoining. That is a drafting analogue for one of the competing S5(1)(j) readings, not a source disagreeing with us. Treat it as a lead, not a position.
- **S5 - silent (policy text, not a stance).** "locations not well-related to existing settlements may be appropriate" (HO12(1)(a), traveller sites). This is the Framework's own locational policy for traveller sites, not a commentator's view. It does not say the S5(1) category is not decisive or that falling outside every category can be overcome; HO12(2) itself routes traveller need through "policies S5 and GB7", so traveller sites still go through the S5 framework. It is relevant context for traveller cases only and does not qualify S5. Adversarial check, 2 Oct 2026: quote verbatim (local text line 348); stance recoded from "qualifies" to "silent".
