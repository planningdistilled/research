## Summary

Civic Voice, the national body for civic societies, answers the December 2025 consultation on the draft National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF). The PDF is headed "The Response of Civic Voice (Draft): 16-02-2026", although the file name is dated 13 February. It opposes national decision-making policies that would override local plans. It says the draft's "principle plus exceptions" format will lead to "multiple subjective interpretations and complex balancing", and calls the Annex A transitional arrangements a "dangerous precedent" that could make existing plans irrelevant. On the draft policies it describes S5 (development outside settlements) as a presumption against development with set categories of exception. It says the S5(1)(j) unmet-need route should be a last resort. It asks for footnote-7-style heritage protection to stay within grey belt and objects to the HE6 change from "great weight" to "substantial weight". It says nothing about how officers are trained.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

The document lobbies government. It does not instruct officers. It asks that:
- national decision-making policies be read "as an addition to" the development plan, which would remain the starting point (Q2);
- S4(1) (development within settlements) be assessed "against the development plan and the national decision-making policies" (Q37);
- an independent inquiry into the causes of under-delivery happen before the S5(1)(j) unmet-need route is used, with interim masterplans guiding which sites are released (Q39.3);
- land not be classed as grey belt where Green Belt status protects the setting of heritage assets or recreational land (Q145);
- the decision-making policies apply in a council's area only once a spatial development strategy is adopted or its plan is more than five years old (Q192).

## Positions against our propositions

- **S5 - agrees.** "Outside of settlements there is no presumption in favour of development, but development may still be permitted if it falls within a series of predefined categories" (section 2.3, Q2, p.4; the quote runs over a PDF line break). This reads S5 as category-gated, which is our reading. The comment is on the draft.
- **S5 - silent (checked, not a qualification).** "Clause S5ji permits edge of settlement development if there is under 75% of the planned housing delivery numbers in a LPA area." (Q39.3, p.18; the quote runs over a PDF line break). This restates the draft S5(1)(j)(i) route and then lobbies government to commission an independent inquiry into under-delivery and to encourage interim masterplans before the route is used. That is a policy-design request about the draft, not a view on how decision-makers apply S5. It does not touch our points that the category decides the case, that S5(4) is almost always fatal, or that "physically well-related" has competing readings.
- **A2 - not applicable (no stance).** "The arrangements provide a precedent that enables any government to come into power and make all existing development plans irrelevant and out-dated on policy grounds." (Q192.1, p.56). This is a policy objection to the draft transitional arrangements: it calls them a "dangerous precedent" that "in effect, scraps the local plan", and asks for a phased start (Q192.2). It does not say how the final Annex A para 2 deals with an inconsistent plan policy, whether by cutting only the clause or by setting aside the whole plan. So it neither supports nor contradicts our reading. It is a worry about the draft, not evidence of how the policy is applied.
