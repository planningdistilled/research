## Summary

This is the official design chapter of the August 2026 National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF). DP3 is the decision-making policy: proposals must respond to their context and reflect relevant aspects of seven principles (liveability, climate, nature, movement, built form, public space, identity). DP3(3) says proposals should be refused if, without clear justification, they conflict with the context principle, relevant principles or explicit local design standards, and that substantial weight should be given to compliance with relevant development plan policies when assessing design quality. The Design and Placemaking planning practice guidance (PPG) fills the gap where there are no local codes.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Test whether the proposal responds to the history, character and features of the site and setting (DP3(1)).
- Apply the seven principles as relevant to the nature and location of the development (DP3(2)).
- Refuse proposals that conflict, without clear justification, with DP3(1), relevant DP3(2) principles or explicit plan design standards (DP3(3)).
- Give substantial weight to compliance with relevant development plan design policies (DP3(3)) and to outstanding or innovative design consistent with surroundings (DP3(5)).
- Use the Design and Placemaking PPG where local design guidance is absent (DP3(4)); use design review and keep approved quality through to completion (DP4).

## Positions against our propositions

- **A2 - agrees.** "Substantial weight should be given to compliance with relevant development plan policies when assessing the design quality of proposals." (DP3(3)). Together with refusal for conflict "with any explicit design standards set out in the development plan (including those in locally-specific policies, guides, codes or masterplans)" (DP3(3)), this supports our reading that local design policies keep full weight under the new Framework.

## Leads

- Design and Placemaking planning practice guidance (draft published with the consultation; final promised "in the autumn" 2026).
- DP3(2)(d) "Movement": "provide good connections to the wider settlement (or those nearby), and prioritise walking, wheeling, cycling" - a design-side hook for walking-route quality arguments (TR).
