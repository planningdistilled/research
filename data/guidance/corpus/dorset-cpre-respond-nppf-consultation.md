# Have your say on the future of the countryside and respond to the NPPF

## Summary

A Dorset CPRE post encouraging the public to respond to the December 2025 draft NPPF consultation (closing 10 March 2026), pointing to National CPRE's suggested answers and to the branch's own response on chapters 4, 10, 18 and 19 and viability. Its main substantive point is that draft policy S5 J (now S5(1)(j)) creates a presumption in favour of development outside settlement boundaries where a council cannot show a five-year housing land supply. It notes Dorset's target rising from 1,793 to 3,246 homes a year and a 2.53-year supply. It concerns the draft only.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Nothing addressed to officers; it urges the public to respond to the consultation.

## Positions against our propositions

- **SUP: qualifies.** "Of concern is Policy S5 J which creates a presumption in favour of development for proposals outside settlement boundaries where authorities are unable to demonstrate a 5 year housing land supply." (Section 'Draft Policy S5 J gives excessive weight to unmet five year housing land supply'). A campaign prompt to answer the December 2025 draft consultation, not an analysis of decisions. Its account of the mechanism (a shortfall brings S5(1)(j) into play for sites outside settlement boundaries) fits our "opens the gate" finding. The 'at the mercy of' line is a rhetorical prediction that supply will be decisive, with no evidence on how cases are then decided. It does not engage with the 'physically well-related' limb, heritage, triggers or S6, where our appeal evidence shows the cases turn, and inspector permit rates are flat across supply bands. Not a genuine disagreement; no change to SUP.
- **S5: silent.** "will fail the housing delivery test leaving them at the mercy of policy S5 J" (Same section). A campaign prediction about the December 2025 draft, not a reading of S5(1): it does not discuss which category decides a case, S5(4), or how 'physically well-related' should be read. Its only real point is that a supply shortfall will drive outcomes, which is coded under SUP above, not a separate qualification of S5.
