## Summary

A short law-firm briefing on the December 2025 consultation draft of the National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF), written for retirement and later-living developers. It notes the draft drops numbered paragraphs for thematic policies split between plan-making and decision-making, and that the statutory rule (decide in accordance with the development plan unless material considerations indicate otherwise) is unchanged. On decisions it describes a clearer presumption in favour of development inside existing settlements, and outside settlements only for listed categories, citing allocated sites and development meeting an unmet evidenced need that is "well related to an existing settlement". It stresses that "substantial weight" goes to benefits meeting evidenced local needs, so evidence of need is decisive for the sector. It is about the draft, not the August 2026 text.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Aimed at developers rather than officers: evidence need carefully, because substantial weight attaches only to "evidenced needs".
- Treat the presumption as strongest inside existing settlements; outside them, look for a specific category (allocation; unmet evidenced need plus well related to a settlement; near well-connected stations).
- Engage early in plan-making so that plans allocate sites for older people's housing (draft policy HO5; 40% M4(2)/M4(3) accessible-homes requirement).

## Positions against our propositions

- **S5 — agrees (weak).** Describes the outside-settlement route as a set of specific categories: "Outside settlements, certain forms of development can also benefit from that presumption, most notable examples for the later living sector being development which is allocated for that purpose in the development plan and development which would address an unmet evidenced need and would be well related to an existing settlement." (Decision taking section.) Consistent with our reading that outside settlements the category is the whole case; it does not discuss what happens when no category fits, nor competing readings of "well related" (S5(1)(j)). Draft text.
