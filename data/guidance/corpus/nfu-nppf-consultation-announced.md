## Summary

The National Farmers' Union (NFU) reports its response to the December 2025 draft National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF) consultation, which closed 10 March 2026. The page was first published 17 December 2025 and updated 13 March 2026. It summarises the draft as reframing the "Principle of Development" so that councils would give a "default 'yes'" to many agricultural and horticultural developments in rural areas, along with biodiversity net gain (BNG) easements, protection for agricultural land and support for diversification, energy and on-farm reservoirs. It says horticulture and poultry face "unnecessary barriers" and raises concern about how policies will be interpreted locally.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Nothing directly (lobbying on the draft). By implication: apply the principle-of-development categories as a default "yes" for agricultural and horticultural development in rural areas.

## Positions against our propositions

- **S5 (agrees)**: the draft would mean "local planning authorities would be expected to give a default ‘yes’ to many agricultural and horticultural developments in rural areas, except in exceptional circumstances" (body, para 3). This fits our reading that outside settlements the S5(1) category decides the case. It is about the draft and about agriculture, not housing.
- **DIV (silent; checked 2026-10-02)**: the sentence "We also have concerns about how policies will be interpreted at a local level, particularly alongside statutory environmental regulations." (section "Unnecessary barriers") is lobbying on the December 2025 draft. It worries that councils will apply agricultural and BNG policy restrictively at the pre-application or application stage. It does not compare council decisions with inspector decisions on the same tests, so it neither supports nor qualifies DIV. It gives no evidence.
