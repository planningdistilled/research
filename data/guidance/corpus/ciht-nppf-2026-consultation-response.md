## Summary

The Chartered Institution of Highways & Transportation (CIHT) responds to the December 2025 draft NPPF from a transport-professional view. Its main worry is that the draft lets small and modest housing schemes go to car-dependent places, especially where there is no five-year housing land supply. It argues that "reasonable walking distance" (draft S5(1)(h), and draft GB7(1)(h)(i), which became GB7(1)(g)(iii) in the final text) must be judged on route quality, safety and convenience, not distance alone, with explicit regard to night-time safety for women and girls. It wants TR3(1)(a) applied to development of any size, not only schemes generating "a significant amount of movement", and wants the Department for Transport (DfT) Connectivity Tool and Manual for Streets signposted throughout. It is a consultation response on the draft, not guidance on the final Framework, but its walking-route reasoning matches how inspectors now apply TR3.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Judge "reasonable walking distance" on the route's quality, directness, footways, lighting, crossings and perceived safety (including after dark), not just a figure such as 800m.
- Test GB7 walking routes for safety, inclusivity and quality as well as distance; use rail frequency and the DfT Connectivity Tool to judge site suitability.
- (Reform request on the draft, not an instruction on the final text) Strengthen the draft S5(1)(j) tests so decision-makers can take the nearby settlement's range of facilities into account, not just how close the site is.
- Apply the TR3 aim of limiting the need to travel and offering genuine choice of transport modes regardless of scheme size; consider the cumulative, whole-life car trips from small schemes.
- Do not let substantial weight for housing (HO7) automatically override location where sites are remote from services.
- Treat Manual for Streets as a key reference in decisions.

## Positions against our propositions

- **TR - agrees.** Reasonable walking distance "should not only be based on distance – e.g. within 800m but must also take account" "of the quality of such a route and its perceived level of safety and convenience." (PDF p. 9, answer to Q39 on S5(1)(h)).
- **TR - agrees.** Asks to strengthen "GB7(1.h.i) by ensuring that walking routes are not only acceptable in terms of" distance but also safety, inclusivity and quality (PDF p. 14, answer to Q136 on GB6/GB7). Draft numbering; the final equivalent is GB7(1)(g)(iii).
- **TR - agrees (consultation lobbying on the draft, not guidance on the final text).** Treats lighting and after-dark safety as part of route quality alongside footways: "Unsafe or poorly lit routes undermine the practical" viability of active and public transport choices (PDF p. 9, before Q35), and "Routes which are attractive to users are direct, with footways, lighting and accessible" road crossings (p. 9, answer on S5(1)(h)). Lighting is listed as one component with footways, not a substitute for them, so this supports route quality over distance and does not contradict our point that lighting does not cure walking in the carriageway. Not a qualification of TR.
- **S5 - silent (re-coded from "qualifies" on check).** On draft S5(1)(j): "supply. In such cases the site’s proximity to an existing settlement may be of little" value if it does not have a reasonable range of facilities, and "The tests set out for any site to meet criterion S5.1(j.ii) should be strengthened so that the decision maker can take the provision of local facilities and services into account" (PDF p. 10, answer to Q39). This asks for the draft wording to be changed; it is not a reading of the published "physically well-related" limb, and if anything implies the draft text did not already let facilities count. It takes no view on how S5(1)(j) should be read as published, so it neither supports nor narrows our point that the limb has competing readings (the accessibility reading comes from inspectors, e.g. PINS-6011872 and PINS-6007158, not from this source).
- **SUP - qualifies.** A policy worry about the December 2025 draft, not a reading of how decisions are made: "there are ways around this, most notably when there is a shortfall in" housing land supply, which "could result" in car-dependent small schemes (PDF p. 1), and "Automatically giving substantial weight to any" benefits "when there is a lack of supply does not give the decision maker sufficient flexibility" (PDF p. 11, answer to Q62 on HO7). It agrees that a shortfall opens the gate, and fears the weight could become automatic. It cites no decisions and does not say a shortfall decides cases under the final Framework, so it does not contradict SUP. Appeal practice so far (e.g. Tandridge) has found car-dependent rural sites fail despite severe shortfalls, which is the outcome CIHT wanted.
