# National Planning Policy Framework – What are the key changes? (Anthony Collins Solicitors, Michael Clohessy)

## Summary

A short list of headline changes published the day after the Framework. It notes the move to policy codes in place of paragraph numbers, and the new national development management policies. It says plan policies that are inconsistent with those policies get very limited weight. Other points: a "permanent" presumption within settlement boundaries (S4), a "default yes" near well-connected stations, the footnote 7 policies removed from the grey belt test, protection of key community assets, farm support (E4), 40% M4(2) accessible homes on major sites (M4(2) is the Building Regulations accessible and adaptable standard), and clean energy and water policies (W1 to W4). It gives little detailed guidance on how to apply the policies.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Get to know the changes at once: they apply to decisions from 17 August 2026.
- Treat local plan policies inconsistent with the national development management policies as having very limited weight.
- Leave the old footnote 7 protections out of the grey belt test; deal with them in the wider decision instead.

## Positions against our propositions

- **A2 (silent; checked 2 Oct 2026, claimed "disagrees" does not hold):** "Appendix 1 of the new NPPF provides that existing local plan policies that are inconsistent with the development management policies in the NPPF are to be given very limited weight." (headline bullets; verbatim in the local text). This is a one-line summary of Annex A, not an argument about how far the cut reaches. It leaves out "or parts of those policies" and "materially", and calls the annex "Appendix 1", but it does not say whole policies lose weight where only one clause conflicts. It also says nothing about heritage, design, access or spatial-preference policies keeping their weight. Loose shorthand, not a conflict with our clause-only reading; no change to our analysis.

## Leads

- The firm promised follow-up articles on individual changes (anthonycollins.com blog, planning tag).
- Note: the briefing describes S4 as applying "within settlement boundaries". The Annex B definition of a settlement is wider than a local boundary and excludes washed-over villages; the briefing's shorthand misses both points.
