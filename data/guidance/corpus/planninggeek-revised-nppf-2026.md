# Revised NPPF published: what it means for you

## Summary

Day-one overview of the August 2026 NPPF. It now separates plan-making from national decision-making policy and uses policy codes. Policies S3 to S6 replace the paragraph 11(d) tilted balance, and there is a national definition of settlement. Under Annex A, materially inconsistent policies, or parts of policies, get very limited weight, but policies do not lose weight just for being old. A shortfall in five-year supply, or a Housing Delivery Test result below 75%, now evidences unmet need under S5 and the grey-belt policy rather than tilting the balance. The article also covers the station policy (800 metres or a 10-minute walk where route quality discourages walking), Annex D supply changes, DM1 planning statements, medium development and S6 neighbourhood-plan protection.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Ask whether each development plan policy (or part) is materially inconsistent with a national decision-making policy before reducing its weight under Annex A; age alone is not enough.
- Use S3 to S6 rather than the old tilted balance; decide whether the site is within a settlement (S4) or outside (S5).
- Use the national settlement definition, which plans can apply by boundaries or criteria, and which covers allocated or permitted land.
- Treat a five-year supply shortfall or a Housing Delivery Test result below 75% as evidence of unmet need under S5 and the grey-belt policy, not as an automatic tilt.
- For grey belt, still apply the other Green Belt requirements and the Golden Rules for major development.
- Check Planning Practice Guidance against Annex D while it still cites old paragraphs.

## Positions against our propositions

- **A2 (Annex A para 2): agrees.** "But Annex A now says that an existing development plan policy, or part of a policy, which is materially inconsistent with a national decision-making policy should be given very limited weight." (section "Annex A could immediately change the weight of local policies"). Supports clause-level cutting ('or part of a policy').
- **A2 (Annex A para 2): agrees.** "Other older policies should not lose weight simply because they pre-date this NPPF." (section "Annex A could immediately change the weight of local policies"). Policies that are not inconsistent keep their weight.
- **SUP (housing supply): agrees.** "A failure to demonstrate the required five year housing land supply, or Housing Delivery Test performance below 75%, can amount to evidence of unmet housing need under S5 rather than switching on the old paragraph 11 balance." (section "The tilted balance has been replaced"). A shortfall opens a gate (S5 unmet-need limb) and does not decide the case.
- **GB (grey belt / Green Belt): agrees.** "That does not make Green Belt housing automatic: the other Green Belt policy requirements still need to be satisfied and major development may have to meet the Golden Rules." (section "Green Belt, grey belt and stations"). Passing the unmet-need limb or grey belt is not approval.
- **SH1 (settlement hierarchy): agrees.** "Plans can identify settlements by boundaries or by criteria." (section "There is now a national definition of a settlement"). Local plans feed the national settlement question (our SH1(b)); the article gives the local tier no separate weight. Silent on washed-over Green Belt villages (SH2).
- **TR (walking route): agrees.** "but the Framework also uses around a 10-minute walk where topography, route availability or quality, or physical barriers would prevent or discourage walking from up to 800 metres." (section "More housing around well-connected stations"). In the station policy, the quality of the route can override the distance figure. This is analogous support for 'route quality decides, not distance' (not TR3 itself).
