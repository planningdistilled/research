The article explains the new S3 to S5 decision structure: S4 applies within a settlement and S5 outside one, and split sites are assessed part by part. It covers the station route (S5(1)(h) and GB7(1)(h)) with the Annex B "reasonable walking distance" definition, and the S5 "evidenced unmet need" route. It also covers the softened GB8 Golden Rules and Annex A paragraph 2 (which it calls "Annex 1") on materially inconsistent plan policies. It expects early disputes over which plan policies keep full weight and over the walking-distance definition.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Give plan policies, or parts of them, that are materially inconsistent with the national decision-making policies very limited weight. Expect debate about which policies keep full weight.
- Apply S4 within a settlement and S5 outside it. Assess split sites part by part. Settlement boundaries become central.
- Near well-connected stations, recognise that the scope to resist is "materially narrowed". Apply the walking-distance definition only to the part of the site that falls within it.
- Treat S5 unmet need as a route not confined to housing.
- Apply GB8 Golden Rules affordable housing at 15 percentage points above the highest local policy requirement, capped at 50%. Viability exceptions apply only in GB8(3)(a) to (c).

## Positions against our propositions

- **A2 (agrees):** "development plan policies, or parts of those policies, which are materially inconsistent with the national decision-making policies in the new NPPF should be given very limited weight". "Parts of those policies" supports our reading that only the offending clause is cut. The authors add that this is "still likely to give rise to early debate about which existing local plan policies can properly continue to carry full weight". (Implementation)
- **TR (silent):** The article quotes the Annex B definition of "reasonable walking distance" word for word ("around 800 metres, or around 10 minutes’ walk time if topography, route availability and quality or physical barriers would prevent or discourage walking from up to 800 metres away.") and says only that its interpretation "is likely" to be "subject to legal challenge". That restates policy and gives no view of its own. The definition governs the station route (S5(1)(h), L3, GB7(1)(h)), not TR3 or GB7(1)(g)(iii), and the article says nothing about those tests or about route quality against distance. It does not qualify TR. (Default ‘Yes’ around train stations)
- **S5 (agrees):** "residential and mixed-use development outside settlements can be approved where it is within reasonable walking distance of a well-connected station" and "Policy S5 contains a broader route for development outside settlements where it would meet an evidenced unmet need". Both treat the S5(1) categories as the gateways. (Default ‘Yes’ around train stations; Unmet need)
- **SH1 (agrees, weakly):** "S3 directs decision-makers to apply S4 where development is within a settlement and S5 where development is outside a settlement". The route turns on the settlement question. The article does not mention local hierarchy tiers. (Decision-making structure)
