## Summary

A 39-slide deck from Landmark Chambers' second webinar on the August 2026 National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF), given by Matthew Reed KC, Matthew Dale-Harris and Eleanor Bennett on 24 September 2026. It covers healthy communities (HC1-HC8), design (DP1-DP4), housing delivery and density (HO7, S4, S5, L3), transport (TR1-TR8) and the new pollution chapter (P1-P6). The most consequential material for us is Reed KC's housing section: he says S5(1)(j) will "likely override all other settlement-based constraint policies in existing LPs [local plans]" because of Annex A, and cites the Kingston Bagpuize appeal (6005809, 14 Sep 2026), where a 5.36-year supply did not stop affordable-housing need engaging S5(1)(j) and the appeal was allowed. He also cites the Whitchurch appeal (6006893) as proof that the presumptions do not prevent refusal on landscape harm. The transport section maps TR3 to 2024 paragraphs 109(b)-(f) and 110 and notes forthcoming guidance on the Department for Transport (DfT) Connectivity Tool, but says nothing on walking-route quality.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Treat S4 and S5 as location-based presumptions, not an overarching tilted balance as under the 2024 NPPF.
- Treat S5(1)(j) as the key route for housing outside settlements; "unmet need" includes, but is not limited to, a five-year housing land supply (5YHLS) shortfall or a Housing Delivery Test (HDT) result under 75%; affordable-housing need on the register can engage it even with a 5YHLS.
- Local planning authority (LPA) side: temper the weight given to affordable need where a scheme is mainly market housing, and scale it to the size of the affordable shortfall.
- Remember the presumptions do not prevent refusal (Whitchurch, landscape and visual harm despite a 2,000-3,000 dwelling shortfall).
- Grey belt housing: there must be evidenced unmet need for the type of development; under footnote 41 need is measured by the Annex D 5YHLS against a plan requirement under five years old, even if the standard method shows a shortfall.
- Apply HO7 substantial weight to housing benefits, based on needs assessments "and other relevant evidence".
- Density: within settlements residential schemes should increase density; 35 dwellings per hectare (dph) minimum within walking distance of a well-connected station, 45 dph at double the service frequency; refuse schemes that do not use land efficiently (L3.4) (Halstead 6007121 and Great Witley 6010196 dismissed on density).
- Design: refusal under DP3 only where a proposal departs "without clear justification"; substantial weight to compliance with design policies.
- Transport: TR3 now triggered by proposals that "could generate a significant amount of movement" and refers to the Connectivity Tool; guidance on the tool, on vision-led transport and on TR6 impact assessment is expected.

## Positions against our propositions

- **A2 - disagrees.** "this provision will be likely to override all other settlement-based constraint policies in existing LPs: because of Annex A and fact that be inconsistent." (slide "Mechanisms", Reed KC). Reads Annex A as removing the weight of local spatial-constraint policies wholesale, where we say only the offending clause is cut and spatial-preference policies keep full weight.
- **SUP - agrees.** "5.36 years’ supply but affordable housing need with this engaged S5j (2,904 on the Council’s housing register) and appeal allowed. So – as apparent – confirmed that other needs beyond 5 HLS engages S5j" (slide "Mechanisms"). Not a qualification: our patterns.md finding 1 already cites this appeal (PINS-6005809 ¶57, "including, but not limited to") as a best case for the gate, so a marginal compliant supply plus a housing register opening S5(1)(j) is within our analysis. The slide says nothing on whether need decided the outcome, and the next slide ("Contd.") says the weight of affordable need is tempered by tenure mix and the extent of the shortfall. Only the one-line SUP summary is narrower than the full finding ("unmet need", not just a supply shortfall).
- **SUP - agrees.** "-But these presumptive policies not prevent refusal: proposal for mixed use where 2-3000 dwelling shortfall refused because of landscape and visual impacts: Whitchurch, 606893." (slide "Contd."). Supply opens the gate but did not decide the case.
- **S5 - agrees.** "S5.1.j – a key provision in practice: replacing old need based presumption rendering policies out of date." (slide "Mechanisms"); also "S5 – outside settlement, categories of development attracting tilted balance" (slide "Housing delivery"). Supports reading S5 by category; weak support only, it does not discuss S5(4).
- **GB - silent (restates policy).** "note – must be an evidenced unmet need for the type of development proposed." and "need is only to be calculated on the basis of the 5-year housing land supply calculation under Annex D" (slide "Cont.", grey belt). A restatement of the Framework's own grey-belt condition and footnote 41, which our analysis already applies (patterns.md, fn41 "evidenced unmet need"); it says nothing on where grey belt is won, on very special circumstances, or on whether passing GB7 means approval, so it takes no stance on our proposition. The point that an up-to-date plan requirement can mask a standard-method shortfall is worth noting as a lead.
- **METH - agrees.** "The application of this, only some at the moment: -Halstead Lane, Halstead, ref 6007121: dismissed for low density (13dph) not meeting targets." (slide "Contd.", density). Counsel uses individual appeal decisions as the evidence of how new policies are applied, the same evidence base we use.
