## Summary

A trade-press feature on the August 2026 National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF) with comment from Boyer, LandTech, AECOM, The Environmental Dimension Partnership (EDP) and Killian Garvey KC (Kings Chambers). It says the Framework raises the weight of national policy in decisions: where a development plan is inconsistent with a national decision-making policy (NDMP), the NDMP "takes primacy" and the plan gets very limited weight, although the statutory duty to decide in accordance with the plan is unchanged. It explains S4 (within settlements) and S5 (outside), and flags the S5(1)(j) "evidenced unmet need" limb as a likely headache because it can be any kind of unmet need, notably affordable housing. It reports that 93 authorities scored below 75% on the Housing Delivery Test (HDT), and that an inspector used S5 within 48 hours to allow 110 homes outside Maldon's settlement boundary. Design quality is said to carry more weight.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Treat NDMPs as taking primacy over inconsistent development-plan policy and give that plan policy very limited weight, while still starting from the statutory development-plan duty.
- Read S5(1)(j) unmet need as not limited to five-year supply or HDT; expect affordable-housing need arguments and wait for appeal decisions to define how far it goes.
- Give more weight to design quality and context.

## Positions against our propositions

- **A2 (silent; consistent):** "if the development plan is saying something different and inconsistent with the NDMP, the NDMP takes primacy, and you give very limited weight to the development plan." (Killian Garvey KC, quoted from a spoken webinar summary). This restates the Framework's general rule that plan policy inconsistent with a national decision-making policy loses weight; "the development plan" here is loose shorthand for the inconsistent content, and the passage does not address whether only the offending clause or the whole policy is cut, nor whether heritage, design or access policies keep full weight. Garvey also stresses the statutory duty to decide in accordance with the plan is unchanged. Not a disagreement with A2.
- **SUP (silent):** The panel on S4/S5 says that "Garvey says this may allow many planning applications to cite an unmet need, especially on affordable housing provision, as almost all local authorities have a shortfall when it comes to such homes." This is about which needs can engage S5(1)(j), so it widens what can open the gate. It says nothing about whether a supply or other shortfall then decides the case, so it does not qualify SUP. It is coded under S5 below. (Adversarial check, 2 Oct 2026.)
- **S5 (qualifies):** "It can be any type of unmet need, notionally; how far this can be taken is something that will be played out in appeal decisions." (Garvey). Adds that the unmet-need part of S5(1)(j), not only "physically well-related", has competing readings.
- **METH (agrees):** same quote as above: the reach of the S5(1)(j) limb "will be played out in appeal decisions", treating appeal decisions as where application of the Framework is settled.
