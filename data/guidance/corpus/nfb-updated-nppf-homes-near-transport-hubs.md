## Summary

A short press release from the National Federation of Builders (NFB), the trade body for small and medium-sized (SME) builders, reacting to the National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF) published on 17 August 2026. It describes the station-led policy as a "default 'yes'" to homes within "an appropriate walking distance" of well-connected rail, Underground and tram stations, backed by minimum housing densities, and notes that councils can proceed without some statutory consultee input. Its chief executive welcomes the policy and urges tall buildings and reopened lines; its policy director asks for a "medium sized site" category (10 to 49 homes). It gives no guidance on how decision-makers should apply any test.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Nothing directly. It is lobbying aimed at government, not decision-makers.
- Implicitly frames the station policy as a distance test ("appropriate walking distance"), with no mention of route quality.
- Notes that councils have "greater scope to proceed without seeking input from certain public bodies where specialist advice is not considered necessary".

- **TR (silent; reclassified from "qualifies" on adversarial check, 2026-10-02)**: "the reforms propose a default ‘yes’ to residential developments within an appropriate walking distance of well-connected railway, underground and tram stations" (body, para 3).
