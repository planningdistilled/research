## Summary

Sebastian Tibenham (Pegasus Group) summarises Green Belt policies GB1-GB8 in the final August 2026 National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF). The grey belt definition no longer depends on the old footnote 7 constraints and is fixed to previously developed land or land not strongly contributing to purposes A, B and D; Annex E now holds the former Planning Practice Guidance (PPG) criteria, under which "the purposes do not relate to villages". GB7 adds an exception near well-connected stations (top 80 Travel to Work Areas (TTWAs), at least 4 services an hour), counting only the parts of a site within about 800 metres or 10 minutes' walk, subject to route considerations. GB6 keeps very special circumstances (VSC) for inappropriate development, and GB8 keeps the Golden Rules with limited scope for site-specific viability.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Decide grey belt status on purposes A, B and D only (Annex E criteria); do not fold in footnote 7 constraints, but still weigh them at the application stage.
- For grey belt housing under GB7, check identified need (for example a five-year housing land supply or Housing Delivery Test failure), sustainable location, no undermining of the wider Green Belt, and the Golden Rules.
- For the station exception, apply only the part of the site within about 800 metres / 10 minutes' walk, with the route considered.
- Treat everything outside GB7 as inappropriate; VSC must clearly outweigh harm that carries substantial weight.
- Allow site-specific viability only for previously developed land with unforeseen costs, strategic sites (typically 1,500+ homes) or a wholly different development model.

## Positions against our propositions

- **GB, agrees.** Grey belt status is assessed against purposes that do not relate to villages, which supports our finding that grey belt is usually won at a village edge. Quote: "The criteria remain unchanged from that previously set out in the PPG, and as before, the purposes do not relate to villages." (Section "Assessing a Site’s Contribution to the Purposes A, B, and D".)
- **GB, agrees.** Very special circumstances remain a high bar. Quote: "Passing this bar still requires one to overcome the substantial weight afforded to the harm to the Green Belt." (Section "Policy GB6".)
- **GB, agrees.** Meeting the grey belt definition is not approval; other constraints still bite. Quote: "This does not mean to suggest that such policy restrictions would not impact on the ability for a site to be allocated or successfully promoted through a planning application." (Section "Definition of Grey Belt Land".)
- **SUP, agrees.** A supply or delivery shortfall is framed as one gate condition for grey belt among several, not the decider. Quote: "There is an identified need – e.g. failure of the housing delivery test or 5-year land supply test" (Section "Policy GB7", first bullet.)
- **TR, not applicable (restates policy on a different test).** The 800 metres / 10 minutes figure is Pegasus restating the Glossary definition of a "well-connected station" for the station exception, GB7(1)(h), with route considerations as the Glossary's own modifier and only the in-distance part of a site counting. Our TR proposition covers TR3 and GB7(1)(g)(iii), not the station route; we treat (h) separately (patterns.md finding 13), where the distance and service thresholds are applied literally, which is consistent with this. No disagreement with our method. Quote: "800m/10 minutes (subject to considerations of the route i.e. topography/barriers, etc). Notably, only the parts a respective site that fall within this definition can be included when applying Policy GB7." (Section "Policy GB7", well-connected station bullets.)
