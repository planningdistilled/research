## Summary

Marc Timlin and Joe Raper (Turley) compare the draft and final heritage chapter (Chapter 20) of the August 2026 National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF). "Less than substantial harm" has gone; harm is now graded as total loss, substantial harm or (other) harm (HE5, HE6). HE4(2) requires proposals to avoid or minimise harm, and HE5 makes applicants state whether the effect is positive, neutral, harmful (with the degree) or total loss. Substantial harm to non-designated heritage assets is now possible in policy terms. The Framework now says expressly that it does not displace the statutory duties in the Planning (Listed Buildings and Conservation Areas) Act 1990. The grey belt definition no longer refers to the old footnote 7, but heritage remains a significant test through Chapter 20 and the statutory duties. They expect Planning Practice Guidance (PPG) updates will be needed to interpret the new harm categories.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Grade harm as total loss, substantial harm or (other) harm; stop using "less than substantial harm".
- Require heritage statements to assess significance (including setting) and to classify the effect under HE5(a)-(d).
- Judge impact by effect on significance, not by scale of development.
- Keep applying the statutory special-regard duties; the NPPF does not displace them.
- For grey belt cases, do not treat heritage as part of the grey belt test, but still apply Chapter 20, the statutory duties and possibly purpose (d).
- Support proposals with positive effects on significance (HE6-HE9).

## Positions against our propositions

- **A2, agrees.** Heritage policy and the statutory duties keep their force under the new Framework, consistent with our reading that heritage policies keep full weight. Quote: "For the first time, the revised NPPF expressly confirms that it does not displace the statutory obligations placed on decision-makers by this and other legislation." (Section "Alignment with legislation and established heritage practice".)
- **GB, agrees.** Passing the grey belt definition does not clear heritage; it is still a significant test, consistent with "passing GB7 is not approval". Quote: "However, this remains a significant policy test through other NPPF policy (Chapter 20) and relevant statutory duties and may be considered as part of the purpose (d) analysis." (Section "Grey belt and heritage considerations".)
