## Summary

A news analysis of the Aston Clinton appeal (ref 6008253, Inspector Zoe Raygen, decided 24 Sep 2026, up to 66 homes, Buckinghamshire). The inspector found the site very close to the village and accessible, but held it "not physically well related" because of harm to the village's setting and the landscape, so S5(1)(j) was not met. She then allowed the appeal under S5(4) exceptional circumstances, giving substantial weight to housing in an area with a substantial five-year supply shortfall and to affordable housing, and gave only limited weight to conflict with spatial-strategy policies. The article contrasts this with Buntingford (6008238), where a site across the A10 was found well-related, and concludes that "physically well related" is not a measurement exercise. It expressly says the decision is not a binding precedent.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Do not treat proximity and good access as automatically satisfying "physically well related"; landscape, settlement form and setting can count.
- Treat failure under S5(1)(j) as moving the case to S5(4), not as automatic refusal; apply S5(1)(j) and S5(4) sequentially.
- Weigh a substantial supply shortfall heavily within the S5(4) balance (as the inspector did), while keeping full weight on landscape harm.
- Give limited weight to an emerging Regulation 19 allocation; require evidence (masterplan, infrastructure strategy) before finding prejudice to a wider allocation.
- Treat individual appeal decisions as indications, not binding precedent.

## Positions against our propositions

- **S5 (qualifies):** "proximity and good access do not automatically make a site physically well related, and failure under S5(1)(j) does not necessarily mean refusal." (introduction) — an S5(4) pass on supply and affordable housing; our proposition already says "almost always" fatal, and our DB codes 6008253 as an S5(4) pass, so it is a counter-example rather than a contradiction. Also supports the "competing readings" limb: "The contrast suggests that “physically well related” is not simply a measurement exercise" (section "Why the Aston Clinton appeal matters after Buntingford").
- **SUP (silent; checked 2 Oct 2026):** "The Inspector gave substantial weight to delivery of 66 homes in an authority with a substantial housing supply shortfall" (section "S5(4) provided the route to approval") — verbatim, but this is a report of the inspector's weighing, not a position on supply. The shortfall raised the weight of the housing benefit and cut spatial-strategy weight, yet the case still turned on the S5(4) benefits-versus-harm balance (housing plus affordable housing plus moderate-weight benefits) with landscape harm kept at full weight. That is consistent with "opens the gate but never decides"; the article does not claim the shortfall alone decided the appeal. The case is relevant to S5 (an S5(4) pass), already coded above.
- **A2 (qualifies):** "Raygen therefore gave only limited weight to conflict with the spatial strategy policies, while continuing to give full weight to the identified landscape harm." (section "A substantial housing shortfall changed the weight") — quote verified verbatim. The article reports the inspector's reasoning without endorsing or criticising it as a reading of the Framework; it does not argue that Annex A para 2 (or anything else in the Aug-2026 NPPF) allows spatial-strategy weight to be cut for under-supply. The cut was not an Annex A para 2 inconsistency finding at all but a 2024-style supply-based reduction, which our analysis already records as a counter-example (consistency review and reference-cases flag 6008253 ¶39). Landscape weight kept at full, consistent with A2. Counter-example already captured; no change to A2 needed.
- **METH (agrees):** "Nor should the Aston Clinton appeal be treated as creating a binding precedent. It is an individual appeal decision on its own evidence. But as an early application of the August 2026 NPPF, it gives practitioners a useful indication of how S5(1)(j) and S5(4) can operate sequentially." (section "Why the Aston Clinton appeal matters after Buntingford") — the not-binding caveat is a standard one that our method shares (we treat decisions as evidence of application, not precedent); the article itself uses a single appeal as an indication of how the policies operate, which is our approach. It does not critique counting outcomes or empirical analysis of appeals. Adversarial check 2026-10-02: originally coded "qualifies", recoded "agrees".
