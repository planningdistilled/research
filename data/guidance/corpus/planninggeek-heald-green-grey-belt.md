## Summary

A news analysis of appeal 6005877 (land off Bolshaw Farm Lane, Heald Green; permission in principle for one to six homes; allowed 11 September 2026, Cheshire East part only). Undeveloped land adjoining the built-up edge and enclosed by development was found to be grey belt; the unmet-need limb passed on a supply shortfall and the location was sustainable on the actual pedestrian links and services, read with NPPF TR3. Older Local Plan Green Belt policy PG3 got very limited weight under Annex A because it did not reflect the GB7 exceptions. The article stresses that age alone does not reduce a policy's weight; the actual inconsistency must be identified.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Assess grey belt against purposes (a), (b) and (d); undeveloped land can qualify.
- Under Annex A, reduce weight only where a plan policy (or part of one) is materially inconsistent with national policy; not because of its age.
- Judge sustainable location on the actual connections and facilities (TR3), not on local distance thresholds or adjacency alone.
- Check GB8 applicability for major housing; check administrative boundaries of the red line.

## Positions against our propositions

- A2 (agrees): "Other development plan policies should not receive reduced weight simply because they were adopted before the Framework was published." and "The practitioner lesson is to identify the actual inconsistency. Age alone is not the argument." (section "Why the older local policy carried less weight"); also "That does not mean PG3 ceased to be part of the development plan, or that every older Green Belt policy can be ignored." (same section).
- TR (agrees): "Not every facility was within the distances mentioned in the supporting text to local policy SD2. Nevertheless, the Inspector found that residents would have reasonable access to shops, education, public transport and other services." and "This was a judgment about the actual connections and facilities available. It was not a conclusion that all land beside a settlement is automatically a sustainable location." (section "The connection to Heald Green mattered"). Actual route and access quality, not distance, decided.
- GB (agrees): "The site adjoins the built-up area and benefits from good pedestrian infrastructure connecting it to some everyday services and facilities." (same section). Grey belt won at a settlement edge with good footway links.
- SUP (agrees): "The council’s most recent evidence showed a shortfall in deliverable housing sites. The Inspector accepted that this established an evidenced unmet need for housing" (section "Grey belt was only the first part of the assessment"). The shortfall opened the GB7 gate.
