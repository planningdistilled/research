## Summary

A promoter-optimistic article (Dan Hewett) calling S5 of the August 2026 National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF) "a belter". It lists the S5 categories (rural business, reuse of buildings, infrastructure, nature, limited infilling in groups of houses, station-led housing, unmet housing need) and urges landowners to revisit sites previously refused or discounted. It reads unmet need as engaged where the council lacks a five-year supply or scores below 75% in the Housing Delivery Test. It predicts councils will struggle to absorb the change and may cling to local policies. It does not discuss S5(4) or how the balance works once a category is met.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Little direct instruction; it tells landowners that older conflicting local policies should carry reduced weight and that previous council refusals will be easier to challenge.
- Re-appraise sites outside settlement boundaries in villages, hamlets or clusters under S5(1)(e).

## Positions against our propositions

- DIV (silent; recoded from "disagrees" on adversarial check): "This is big. It is likely to be too much for local authorities to comprehend in the short term. Only time will tell whether they try to cling to their own locally adopted planning policies, regardless of whether they align with the new national ones." (paragraph following the S5 category list, before "Stronger Policy Support for Farm Diversification and Rural Enterprise"). A speculative prediction made two weeks after publication about councils giving weight to older local policies over S5 in principle-of-development terms. It offers no decisions or evidence and does not address how councils and inspectors apply the same tests (route quality, triggers, heritage method), so it does not contradict DIV. If anything it bears on A2 (weight of inconsistent plan policies), though it does not say how much of a conflicting policy loses weight.
- SUP (silent; restates policy): "For many landowners with land on the edge of villages, market towns and larger settlements, this could present a significant opportunity to revisit sites that may have been previously discounted for development." ("Housing Land Supply Shortfalls Could Create New Development Opportunities"). The section restates S5(1)(j): a five-year supply shortfall or a Housing Delivery Test result below 75% engages it, and it then lists its limits (physically well-related, a scale that infrastructure can accommodate, appropriate to location). It treats a shortfall as a reason to revisit sites, which is consistent with "opens the gate". It says nothing about how much weight supply carries in the balance or whether it decides cases, so it takes no stance against SUP. (Checked 2 Oct 2026: an earlier note said it omitted the scale and infrastructure limits, but it does list them.)
- S5 (silent): "For landowners controlling land within villages, hamlets or clusters of existing development, sites that have historically fallen outside settlement boundaries may now warrant a fresh appraisal." (limited infilling section). Restates S5(1)(e) limited infilling as a reason to re-look at sites; it takes no position on whether the category decides the case or on S5(4), and implicitly accepts that fitting a category is the route in. Not a qualification of our proposition (checked 2026-10-02).
