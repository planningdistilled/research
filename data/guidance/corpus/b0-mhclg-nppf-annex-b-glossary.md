## Summary

Annex B holds the definitions that decide which route a proposal takes under the August 2026 National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF). "Settlement" covers cities, towns, villages and other predominantly built-up areas, plus allocated or permitted land, and areas the development plan defines as settlements. It excludes hamlets and scattered groups of houses unless the plan names them, and it excludes villages which lie within and are defined as part of the Green Belt. "Grey belt" is Green Belt land that is previously developed and/or does not strongly contribute to purposes (a), (b) or (d) in GB2. "Reasonable walking distance" is about 800 metres, or about 10 minutes' walk where topography, route quality or barriers discourage walking; it applies only to the station-led policies. "Well-connected station" means a station in a top-80 Travel to Work Area (TTWA) with at least four trains or trams an hour. "Connectivity" is access to jobs, services and facilities by sustainable modes, and the Connectivity Tool "provides a means of assessing this".

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Decide "settlement" status by built-up form and by the development plan's own boundaries "or equivalent terms", not by a settlement's local status label.
- Treat a hamlet as outside settlements unless the development plan defines it as a settlement.
- Treat a village that lies within and is defined as part of the Green Belt as not a settlement, whatever its size or services.
- Measure reasonable walking distance as about 800 metres, switching to about 10 minutes' walk time where the route discourages walking. Apply only the part of a site inside that distance for S5(1)(h), L3(2)(c) and GB7(1)(h).
- Assess grey belt against GB2 purposes (a), (b) and (d) only.
- Count only means of transport that are "efficient, safe and accessible" as sustainable transport modes.

## Positions against our propositions

- **SH1, agrees.** Settlement status comes from built-up form and from the plan's definitions; no hierarchy tier is mentioned. A tier counts only through the plan's settlement definitions, which is our route (b). Quote: "This includes areas defined as a settlement in the development plan (whether using defined settlement boundaries or equivalent terms, or criteria for identifying settlement extents where boundaries have yet to be defined)." (Annex B, "Settlement".)
- **SH2, agrees.** Quote: "For the purpose of this Framework they also exclude villages which lie within and are defined as part of the Green Belt in the development plan." (Annex B, "Settlement".)
- **TR, not applicable (different test).** This definition is expressly limited to "policies S5, L3, GB7 (relating to land around well-connected stations)" and to HC5. It does not define the test under TR3 or GB7(1)(g)(iii), which is what our TR proposition is about, so it neither supports nor qualifies TR. Context only: even for the station test, the glossary lets route quality and barriers change an 800-metre distance into a 10-minute walking-time test. That fits the general idea that route quality matters, but it is no authority on TR3. Quote: "this should be considered to be around 800 metres, or around 10 minutes’ walk time if topography, route availability and quality or physical barriers would prevent or discourage walking from up to 800 metres away." (Annex B, "Reasonable walking distance".)
- **TR, agrees (in part).** Safety is part of the definition of a sustainable mode, which supports treating an unsafe route (such as walking in the carriageway) as failing. Quote: "Any efficient, safe and accessible means of transport with overall low impact on the environment, including walking, wheeling, cycling and public transport." (Annex B, "Sustainable transport modes".)
