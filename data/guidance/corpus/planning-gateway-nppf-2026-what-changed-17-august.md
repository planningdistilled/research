## Summary

A practitioner guide on what the August 2026 National Planning Policy Framework means for applications already submitted. It says the national decision-making policies apply immediately to undetermined applications, the development plan remains the statutory starting point, and materially inconsistent plan policies get very limited weight (not every older policy merely because of age). It explains the S3 to S5 route, the conditional "default yes" around well-connected stations (insisting on mapped walking routes rather than an 800 m circle), intensification and curtilage rules, DM1/DM2 and Annex C validation, and the 2025 Housing Delivery Test consequences. It gives a checklist for live applications.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Take the revised Framework into account on every undetermined application; agree the scope of any NPPF addendum.
- Audit every development-plan policy relied on by either side for material inconsistency.
- Settle settlement status and the S3 to S5 route early.
- For station-led cases, map the actual walking route (crossings, gradients, severance, entrances), not a radius; evidence service frequency and travel-to-work area.
- Use the 2025 HDT result; treat it as one input, not a trump card.
- Tie local validation requirements to a specific plan policy and keep them proportionate (DM2, Annex C).

## Positions against our propositions

- **A2 (agrees):** "it does not say that every older policy automatically becomes weak merely because of age." (section "2. The development plan remains the legal starting point") — supports a targeted, inconsistency-based cut; it does not address clause-level cutting or which policy types keep weight.
- **SUP (agrees):** "The delivery test is one input to the decision route; it does not erase site-specific constraints or the need to satisfy other national decision-making policies." (section "7. The 2025 Housing Delivery Test can alter the decision balance").
- **TR (agrees, by analogy):** "Map the walkable route, crossings, gradients, severance and entrances. Then verify the station and service-frequency definitions. A straight-line circle is an initial screening tool, not the policy evidence." (box "Do not use an 800-metre radius alone") — about the station "reasonable walking distance" test, not TR3 or GB7(1)(g)(iii), but the same route-quality-over-distance principle.
