## Summary

A law firm's overview of the August 2026 National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF). It explains that development plan policies "materially inconsistent" with the national decision-making policies (NDMPs) get "very limited weight" under Annex A, unless examined against the 2026 Framework, and says the Framework does not define "materially inconsistent", which it expects to be argued and possibly litigated. It summarises S4 (strong presumption inside settlements) and S5 (categories outside settlements, including unmet housing need with a physically well-related site of appropriate scale, and station-area sites), and lists other policies (N4 protected landscapes, HO5, DP4, TR2, HC1, P5, CC2/CC3).

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Give very limited weight to plan policies that are materially inconsistent with the NDMPs (except plans examined under the 2026 Framework).
- Distinguish sites inside settlements (S4) from those outside (S5, category-based).
- Expect developers to use Annex A to challenge restrictive local policies, including on settlement growth and design.

## Positions against our propositions

- **A2 (silent)**: "For developers, promoters and landowners, Annex A creates new opportunities to challenge reliance on restrictive local policies. This could be particularly relevant in areas such as housing delivery, green belt/grey belt assessments, settlement growth, design requirements and other development management matters now addressed through NDMPs." and "Notably, the NPPF doesn't provide a comprehensive definition of “materially inconsistent”, so this could be fruitful ground for planning argument and maybe even litigation." (section "Plan-making and decision-taking separated"). Checked 2 Oct 2026: this is a forecast of where developers will argue, not a view on how the weight test applies. It does not say whether a whole policy or only the inconsistent clause loses weight, and it does not say design or spatial policies will lose weight. Its expectation of challenges, and that "some of their policy toolkit is weakened", fits our own count (about 13 cut, about 17 kept). Not a disagreement with A2.
- **S5 (agrees)**: "Policy S5, concerning development outside settlements, provides categories where the same strong presumption in favour of development applies." and "One of these is where the development will address unmet housing need and is physically well-related to the relevant settlement in question as well as at an appropriate scale." (section on S1-S6). It confirms the category-based structure; it does not discuss what happens outside every category or the competing readings of "physically well-related".
