# Grey Belt: 10-Caravan Site Wins Enforcement Appeal

## Summary

Reports enforcement appeal APP/P0119/C/26/3378284 (Inspector R Merrett, 4 Sep 2026) at Pomphrey Hill, Mangotsfield, South Gloucestershire. An earlier three-home permission had lapsed because a contamination condition precedent was not met in time. On ground (a), previously developed Green Belt land contained by housing, recreation, roads and planting was found to be grey belt. South Gloucestershire's 3.93-year housing land supply established unmet need, and the location was sustainable. The touring pitch was not counted as a permanent home, so the scheme was below the major-development threshold and the GB8 Golden Rules did not apply. The article says grey-belt classification did not by itself make the development acceptable.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- On an enforcement ground (a) appeal, assess the development against the current development plan and the August 2026 NPPF at the date of decision.
- Treat grey-belt classification as only the first step; work through each GB7 test (unmet need, sustainable location, not fundamentally undermining remaining Green Belt purposes).
- Use a housing land supply shortfall as evidence of unmet need under the grey-belt policy.
- Before applying the GB8 Golden Rules, check whether the scheme is actually major housing development (10 or more homes or 0.5 hectares or more), looking at the nature of each unit rather than a headcount.
- Do not equate a mistaken belief that a permission was extant with intentional unauthorised development.

## Positions against our propositions

- **GB (grey belt / Green Belt): agrees.** "That classification did not itself make the development acceptable. The Inspector still had to work through the policy tests for development on grey belt land." (section "How grey belt became decisive"). Grey belt is a gate, not approval.
- **GB (grey belt / Green Belt): agrees.** "Although Mangotsfield formed part of the wider Bristol built-up area, the site was contained by existing residential and recreational development, roads and planting." (section "How grey belt became decisive"). Grey belt won on contained previously developed land at the edge of a built-up area; consistent with 'usually won at a (village/urban) edge'.
- **SUP (housing supply): agrees.** "That meant unmet housing need was established for the purposes of the national grey belt policy." (section "South Gloucestershire had a 3.93-year housing land supply"). The shortfall satisfied one gate test; the outcome still turned on the location, Green Belt function and other tests.
