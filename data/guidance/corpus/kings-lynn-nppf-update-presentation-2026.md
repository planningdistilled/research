## Summary

A short slide deck given to the council's Local Plan Task Group on 10 February 2026 by a Principal Planner (Planning Policy), while the draft National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF) was out for consultation (closing 10 March 2026). It explains what the NPPF is, why it is being rewritten (clearer, "more rules-based", national decision-making policies) and the main features of the draft. It notes "clarity re principles of development inside/ outside settlements" and the integration of the presumption with location (spatial) policies, but gives no detail on the settlement tests, Green Belt, transport or housing land supply. Its most decision-relevant line is on National Development Management Policies (NDMPs): local plan policies "in any way inconsistent" with the national policies should get "very limited weight". It is not about the final August 2026 text; the "S5(4)" coverage suggested in the lead is not present.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Treat the NPPF as an important consideration alongside the Local Plan when determining applications.
- Expect standardised national decision-making policies to replace similar Local Plan policies; future Local Plans to focus on land allocations.
- Give very limited weight to development plan policies (Local Plan or neighbourhood plan) that are "in any way inconsistent" with the national decision-making policies once in place.
- Expect clearer rules on development inside and outside settlements and a simplified presumption in favour of sustainable development.
- Note Planning and Infrastructure Act 2025 changes (committee reform, delegation, fees).

## Positions against our propositions

- **A2 — silent (restates draft wording; checked 2 Oct 2026).** "Development plan policies (including those contained within the Local Plan or a neighbourhood plan) which are in any way inconsistent with the national decision-making policies in the new NPPF, once in place, should be given very limited weight." (slide "National Development Management Policies (NDMPs) – Impact on Decision making"; line breaks removed). The slide is a bullet list of the aims of the consultation-draft NDMPs, reproduced for members in Jan-Feb 2026 before the August 2026 Framework was published. It adds no officer interpretation and does not address whether the whole policy or only the inconsistent part loses weight, or whether heritage, design or access policies keep their weight. It is useful context only: the draft wording ("in any way inconsistent ... very limited weight") was broader than Annex A paragraph 2 as published. It is not a disagreement with our reading of the published text.
