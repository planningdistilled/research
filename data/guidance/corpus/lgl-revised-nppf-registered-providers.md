## Summary

A law-firm summary of the August 2026 National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF) for registered providers (housing associations). It lists the headline changes: the station "default yes" within 800 metres in the top 80 Travel to Work Areas, a new "medium" development category, 40% accessible homes on major sites, density of 35-45 dwellings per hectare, viability changes, and restrictions on local policies that conflict with national rules. It notes that on-site affordable housing on medium sites survived, and expects planners to place "significant emphasis" on defining settlement boundaries because development is supported within and on the edge of settlements. It gives little practical guidance on applying the decision-making policies.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Expect settlement-boundary definitions to matter more, because they decide which route (within or outside a settlement) applies.
- Give limited weight to existing local plan policies where they are inconsistent with the Framework.
- Keep on-site affordable housing requirements on medium-sized sites.

## Positions against our propositions

- **A2 (agrees):** "without them being updated to match the NPPF some will be given limited weight in decision-making where they are inconsistent with the Framework." (penultimate paragraph). Only "some" policies lose weight, and only where inconsistent, which fits our narrow reading; but the article does not say whether the cut is clause by clause.
