# CPRE responds to summer 2026 NPPF announcement (CPRE, the countryside charity)

Note: cpre.org.uk returns 403 to direct download; text read via WebFetch only, so quotes could not be checked against a local copy.

## Summary

A same-day press reaction by CPRE (the Campaign to Protect Rural England) chief executive Roger Mortlock, issued before the Framework text was out. It attacks the announced "default yes" to development near train stations, including in the Green Belt, as a rigid one-size-fits-all rule that ignores station capacity and risks car dependency. It also criticises fast-tracking of data centres and calls for mayors and Strategic Authorities to set their own housing targets. It promises a full response later. It gives no guidance on applying the Framework.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Nothing directly. Its implied point for decision-makers: a station's actual capacity and infrastructure matter, not only proximity to it.

## Positions against our propositions

- **TR (silent; checked 2 Oct 2026, was coded "qualifies"):** "A 'default yes' to development near any train station, regardless of its capacity, could see thousands of homes built in places with no prospect of extra trains or other essential infrastructure." (Roger Mortlock quote, para 2; wording confirmed by a second WebFetch, no local copy). This is a campaign critique of the announced policy, issued before the Framework text was published. It argues about what the policy should be (station capacity, car dependency), not about how TR3 or GB7(1)(g)(iii) are applied. It says nothing on walking-route quality versus distance, so it neither supports nor qualifies TR. No change to our analysis. If a decision ever turns on rail-service capacity at a station, this is background on the campaign view only.
