## Summary

The December 2025 consultation draft of the National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF), superseded by the final Framework published 17 August 2026. It already has the S3 to S5 locational presumption (S4 inside settlements, S5 outside, with a closed list of acceptable categories) and the Annex A implementation rule. Comparing it with the final text shows three changes that matter to our propositions: (1) the Annex B "settlement" definition had **no** carve-out for villages washed over by the Green Belt (the final adds "For the purpose of this Framework they also exclude villages which lie within and are defined as part of the Green Belt in the development plan"); (2) S5(1)(j)(i) said only "well related to an existing settlement" (the final says "physically well-related"); (3) Annex A para 2 cut whole policies "in any way inconsistent" (the final says "policies (or parts of those policies) which are materially inconsistent"). The draft also had no glossary definition of "reasonable walking distance"; the final adds around 800 metres, or around 10 minutes' walk where "topography, route availability and quality or physical barriers" discourage walking.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Route every decision through S3: S4 within settlements, S5 outside, and approve plan- and Framework-compliant proposals without delay.
- Outside settlements, approve only the S5(1) categories (a) to (j); anything else only "in exceptional circumstances" (S5(4)).
- In the Green Belt, disapply S5 and use GB6, GB7, GB8 (S5(5)).
- Under S5(1)(j), a five-year supply shortfall or Housing Delivery Test below 75% counts as evidenced unmet need, but the site must still be well related to an existing settlement and of a scale infrastructure can take.
- Give "very limited weight" to development plan policies "in any way inconsistent" with national decision-making policies (Annex A para 2).
- Use the Connectivity Tool "alongside other relevant evidence" in TR3 connectivity assessments.

## Positions against our propositions

- **SH1 — agrees (partly).** Annex B, PDF p.113: "This includes areas defined as a settlement in the development plan (whether using defined settlement boundaries or equivalent terms, or criteria for identifying settlement extents)." Local plan definitions feed the settlement question; there is no national hierarchy tier.
- **SH2 — not applicable (superseded draft; no stance on the final text).** Annex B, PDF p.113: "Settlement: Includes cities, towns, villages and other predominantly built-up areas, including land which is allocated or has permission for development". The draft definition has no washed-over Green Belt village exclusion, so under the draft such a village could have counted as an S4 settlement (though S4(2)(c) still pointed to the national refusal policies). This does not dispute SH2: SH2 rests on the August 2026 final text, which added "For the purpose of this Framework they also exclude villages which lie within and are defined as part of the Green Belt in the development plan". The draft-to-final comparison shows the carve-out was a deliberate change, which supports SH2 rather than opposing it.
- **S5 — agrees.** S5(4), printed p.25: "Any other development proposals which do not fall within one of the categories set out above should only be approved in exceptional circumstances, where the benefits of the proposal would substantially outweigh the adverse effects".
- **S5 — no stance (drafting history only).** S5(1)(j)(i), printed p.25: "be well related to an existing settlement29 (unless the nature of the development would make this inappropriate)". This is superseded consultation text, not a view on how the final test applies. The draft had no "physically"; the final added it. That is useful background on the "physically well-related" wording, but the draft says nothing about which reading of the final (j) is right, so it neither qualifies nor contradicts S5.
- **SUP — agrees.** S5(1)(j), printed p.25: "Development which would address an evidenced unmet need (including, but not limited to, development proposals involving the provision of housing where the local planning authority cannot demonstrate a five year supply of deliverable housing sites". Supply shortfall only opens category (j); location and scale tests still apply.
- **A2 — not applicable (superseded draft; context only).** Annex A para 2, printed p.98 (PDF p.100): "Development plan policies which are in any way inconsistent with the national decision-making policies in this Framework should be given very limited weight". This is consultation wording, not a view on how the August 2026 Framework applies, so it does not disagree with our reading. The draft spoke of whole policies "in any way inconsistent"; the final changed this to "policies (or parts of those policies) which are materially inconsistent", which supports our clause-only reading. Useful only as draft-to-final evidence that the change was deliberate.
