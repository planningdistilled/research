## Summary

A same-day note by James Firth of Ceres Property, a property and planning adviser, summarising the August 2026 National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF). The site blocked curl (HTTP 403), so the text was read through WebFetch and is not held locally. It describes the new national decision-making policies (NDMPs), the "default yes" for homes near well-connected stations (top 80 Travel to Work Areas, service frequency, around 800 metres or around 10 minutes' walk where route quality or barriers discourage walking), the new medium-site category, and Housing Delivery Test (HDT) consequences, including deemed unmet need for S5(1)(j) below 75%. On implementation it repeats Annex A: plan policies, or parts of policies, materially inconsistent with the NDMPs get very limited weight. It is a factual summary with little interpretation.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Apply the new Framework at once to applications.
- For station sites, apply the well-connected station test and the reasonable walking distance definition, shortening the catchment where route quality or barriers discourage walking.
- Apply HDT consequences: action plan below 95%, 20% buffer below 85%, deemed unmet need for S5(1)(j) below 75%.
- Give very limited weight to materially inconsistent plan policies or parts of them.
