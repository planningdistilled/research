# New NPPF closes policy void for energy developers and renewables (Pegasus Group, Chris Calvert)

## Summary

A launch-day briefing for the energy sector. It says renewable and low-carbon schemes now get "substantial" (no longer "significant") weight, that applicants need not show overall need (Policy W3), and that repowering and life-extension of existing sites also get substantial weight. It argues that local plan policies that conflict with national energy policy carry limited weight, and that most local plans are probably out of date for energy. It warns that councils may misread national deployment targets (such as Clean Power 2030) as caps rather than floors. It also covers changes outside the Framework: Nationally Significant Infrastructure Project (NSIP) pre-application consultation removed, mayoral call-in powers, and Historic England advice. It says little about Green Belt.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Give substantial weight to renewable and low-carbon energy benefits, and to repowering and life-extension.
- Do not ask for evidence of overall need for renewable energy (W3).
- Do not limit renewables to areas a local plan identifies as suitable.
- Do not treat national deployment targets as caps on permissions.
- Give limited weight to local plan energy policies that conflict with the Framework.

## Positions against our propositions

- **A2 — silent (checked 2026-10-02; earlier coded "qualifies").** The article says "the direction for decision makers to consider substantial benefits against any harm (and in the context of local plan policies that are most likely out of date) should result in more planning consents granted" ('Substantial weight given to renewable and low-carbon energy schemes' section), and its key takeaways say "Out-of-date or conflicting local plan policies carry limited weight against national energy strategy". The first is a passing aside from a promoter, guessing that local energy policies are probably out of date. The second ties reduced weight to the policies that conflict with national policy. Neither discusses Annex A para 2, whether the cut applies clause by clause or to whole policies, or the weight of heritage, design, access or spatial policies. So it does not take a position on A2. If anything, "conflicting ... policies carry limited weight" fits our reading that only the offending policy loses weight.
