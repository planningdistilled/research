## Summary

A short guide from AGORA Architects, a Norfolk practice (RIBA, Royal Institute of British Architects, chartered), marked "Policy update — August 2026" with no exact date. It explains that the August 2026 National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF) replaces paragraph numbers with policy codes (old paragraphs 84, 139 and 11 become HO11, DP3 and S3-S5). It describes S3 as pointing to S4 (inside settlements) and S5 (outside), and stresses that S5 categories each carry their own tests and the proposal "must still be acceptable in the round". It says the old "tilted balance" belongs to the previous Framework and that a housing supply shortfall "does not, on its own, grant permission for any proposal". It treats DP3(5) design weight as part of the balance, not a way to establish the principle of development.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Assess proposals against the August 2026 Framework codes, the development plan and site circumstances; no policy gives an automatic right to permission.
- Use S4 inside settlements and S5 outside; check each S5 category's own tests.
- Do not treat a supply shortfall as granting permission by itself.
- Treat DP3(5) outstanding design as weight in the balance, not a route to principle; HO11(1)(e) is a demanding test.
