## Summary

MHCLG's equality analysis under the Public Sector Equality Duty (PSED, section 149 of the Equality Act 2010) for the August 2026 National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF). It is not guidance to officers, but it is the department's own account of what the main decision-making policies are meant to do: a "permanent presumption in favour of suitably located development" that sets out what is acceptable in principle in different locations (S3 to S5), "default yes" around well-connected stations, densification, grey belt and Green Belt clarifications, and the transport chapter (TR1 to TR5). Its most useful passage for us explains that the new glossary definition of "reasonable walking distance" (used in L3, GB7 and S5 for station proximity) deliberately lets route availability, route quality and physical barriers count, partly because older and disabled people find walking harder. It also states that the actual effect of the Framework will depend on how it is applied locally and the weight given to individual policies.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Nothing directly; it is an impact assessment, not practice guidance.
- Implicitly: read S5 as approving "certain forms of development outside of settlements" (a category-based route), not as a general permission.
- When judging "reasonable walking distance" to a station (L3, GB7, S5), consider route availability, route quality and physical barriers, not distance alone.
- Treat traveller sites the same as bricks-and-mortar housing under GB7(1)(h) and the station-related Green Belt policy; traveller sites are exempt from L3 minimum densities.
- Note the document cites "Policy SD5" for station "in principle" support (page 8), which appears to be a stale or erroneous policy code.

## Positions against our propositions

- **TR (agrees)**: "Partly to account for the fact that some groups (such as older and disabled people) may face greater challenges with walking, the new definition of ‘reasonable walking distance’ explicitly allows for route availability, quality and physical barriers to be considered." (page 22, Making effective use of land, para 28). Supports route quality over raw distance, though the passage concerns station distance under L3/GB7/S5 rather than TR3 generally.
- **S5 (agrees)**: "Policy S5 seeks to approve certain forms of development outside of settlements where sustainable" (page 8, Delivering a sufficient supply of homes / plan-making and spatial strategy, para 1); and the presumption "makes clear what forms of development are acceptable in principle in different locations as part of creating a more rules-based approach to development." (page 2). Consistent with S5 being category-driven.
- **METH (agrees, indirect)**: "it is through the application of the NPPF that any impact will be realised and the weight afforded to individual policies may result in a different on-the-ground impact from that set out in this assessment." (page 2, introduction). The department itself says outcomes depend on application, which supports studying decisions empirically.

## Leads

- Promised Planning Practice Guidance (PPG) on assessing traveller needs and traveller site design.
- MHCLG says it will monitor the reforms with "new metrics that are being created" (section 3.2): a possible official counterpart to our decision-coding.
- The reference to "reducing the risk of authorities applying Green Belt policies in differential ways" (page 25) is a government acknowledgement of inconsistent local application (traveller sites only); loosely relevant to DIV.
