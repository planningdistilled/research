## Summary

Strutt & Parker's affordable housing broker comments on rural affordable housing after the August 2026 National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF) and the first wave of the £39 billion Social and Affordable Homes Programme (SAHP). It notes that decision-makers must now give substantial weight to accommodation that meets local community needs, and that rural exception sites and support for village vitality are kept. The main argument is practical: Registered Providers (RPs) often will not take two to four Section 106 homes on small rural schemes. The August 2026 NPPF keeps on-site provision as the starting point but allows off-site provision or a commuted sum where robustly justified. It does not go into the S5 tests for where rural housing may go.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Give substantial weight to the benefits of housing that meets local community needs.
- Councils should aim to maximise affordable housing actually delivered, not to secure units on every site. On very small rural schemes, a commuted sum may beat insisting on on-site units that no RP will buy.
- Expect developers to show a documented RP marketing exercise before departing from on-site provision.

## Positions against our propositions

None. It mentions rural exception sites only in passing and is silent on the S5 route tests, settlement status, walking routes, land supply, Green Belt, Annex A and method.

## Leads

- Government consultation on standardised planning agreements for medium-sized sites, including affordable housing and commuted-sum mechanisms.
- SAHP prospectus from Homes England (rural and community-led priorities, flexible grant rates).
