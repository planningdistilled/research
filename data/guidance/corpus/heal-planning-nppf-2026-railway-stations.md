## Summary

Short explainer (author Mathilda Peters, 14 September 2026) on the August 2026 National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF) station route for homes outside settlements (S5(1)(h)) and in the Green Belt (GB7(1)(h)). The site blocked direct download (HTTP 403), so the text was read only through WebFetch and the quotes below are as WebFetch returned them, not machine-checked against a local copy. It says reasonable walking distance is normally about 800 metres, but that the actual pedestrian route, topography and barriers should be considered rather than a straight-line measure. It notes station qualification rules (location, service frequency), minimum densities, and that Green Belt policy still applies.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Measure walking distance along the actual pedestrian route, not as the crow flies; take account of topography and physical barriers.
- Treat about 800 metres as the normal benchmark.
- Check the station meets the location and service-frequency requirements.
- Apply Green Belt policy separately where the site is in the Green Belt.
- Continue to weigh design, highways, amenity, heritage, flood risk and infrastructure.
