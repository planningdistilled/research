# SLCC's response to the NPPF consultation (Society of Local Council Clerks)

## Summary

The news page announces SLCC's response (prepared with the National Association of Local Councils, NALC) to the draft National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF) consultation that closed on 10 March 2026; the linked response letter (10 March 2026) was also retrieved and is the main local copy (the news page is saved as slcc-response-to-nppf-consultation.txt). The response answers the consultation questions. Points relevant to applying the Framework: Annex B's settlement definition is welcome but "town", "village" and "settlement" still need defining because the gap complicates grey belt and Green Belt decisions; the policy on development outside settlements (S5) is unclear; it asks that plan policies lose weight only where conflict with the Framework is substantial (a drafting request, not a reading of the final text); it disagrees with the change in heritage policy HE6 from "great weight" to "substantial weight". It also says grey belt has caused speculative applications that undermine adopted plans.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Nothing directly; it asks government for clearer definitions and drafting.
- It asks that development within settlements (S4) not be treated as "a green light": design, heritage, natural and community assets must still carry their NPPF weight.
- It asks that parish councils in neighbouring areas be consulted on applications that affect them.

## Positions against our propositions

- **SH2 (silent; recoded from "qualifies" on adversarial check)**: on the consultation draft, it asks for "town", "village" and "settlement" to be defined because the gap complicates grey belt and Green Belt decisions. It does not discuss washed-over villages or say whether such a village can be an Annex B settlement. It is a general call for clearer drafting, not a position against SH2. "like 'town,' 'village,' and 'settlement’, which complicates the application of grey belt and green belt" (answer to Question 35, settlements definition; same point at Question 3)
- **S5 (agrees)**: supports our view that the outside-settlements categories are open to competing readings. "We feel that the drafting of this policy could be clearer – as it complicates the application of grey" (answer to Question 39, categories of development outside settlements)
- **A2 (not applicable; checked 2 Oct 2026)**: a consultation ask on the draft's weight wording, not a reading of the final Annex A text. It asks government to let recently adopted policies take primacy and to give limited weight only where conflict is substantial. That is broadly in the same spirit as our view that only the offending part loses weight, but it does not address the clause-level cut or which policy types keep full weight, so it neither qualifies nor confirms A2. "We support the need for a pragmatic approach to handling tensions between old-style local plans and the new framework, suggesting that more recently adopted policies should take primacy and that only policies with substantial conflict should be given limited weight." (answer to Question 5, weight terminology); the grep-checked fragment is "that only policies with substantial conflict should be given limited weight."
