## Summary

James Cox (Lichfields) reviews the December 2025 consultation draft of the National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF) for town centres. The draft keeps the sequential and retail impact tests, gives "substantial weight" to proposals supporting a centre's vitality (draft TC2), drops "primary shopping areas" for a wider "town centre", extends edge-of-centre to 300 metres, and appears to bring back disaggregation (draft TC3). The hot food takeaway restriction moves to draft HC5 with an undefined "reasonable" walking distance. Outside settlements, draft S5 recognises roadside facilities as acceptable in principle, subject to draft TR5, but says nothing about the retail and food elements. This is commentary on the draft, not the final text.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Expect a wider sequential search area (whole town centre, 300-metre edge-of-centre).
- Under draft TC3, consider whether a proposal could be split across several smaller in-centre sites.
- Treat roadside facilities outside settlements as acceptable in principle under draft S5 if draft TR5 is met; ancillary retail and food uses still need testing.

## Positions against our propositions

- **S5, agrees (draft only).** Treats S5 as a list of categories that are acceptable in principle outside settlements, which is consistent with our reading that the S5(1) category decides the principle. Quote: "Draft Policy S5 explicitly recognises such facilities as acceptable in principle outside settlement boundaries, subject to meeting the requirements of draft Policy TR5." (Section "Roadside facilities".)

## Leads

- Check whether the final S5(1) keeps a roadside-facilities category and how TR5 was worded in August 2026.
- Hot food takeaway policy (draft HC5): what counts as a "reasonable" walking distance to schools; a possible parallel to TR3 route-quality arguments.
