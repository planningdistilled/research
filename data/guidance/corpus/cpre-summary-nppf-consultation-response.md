# A summary of CPRE's response to the NPPF consultation (CPRE)

Note: cpre.org.uk returns 403 to direct download; text read via WebFetch only.

## Summary

CPRE's summary of its formal response to the 2025-26 draft Framework consultation. It asks the government to keep real Green Belt protection, put brownfield first and drop speculative "grey belt" development, saying grey belt is being used to release greenfield land, wildlife sites and good farmland. It opposes the draft's station-proximity Green Belt policies (draft GB3 and GB7h), especially around isolated rural stations without services. It uses appeal outcome counts as evidence: over 140 major greenfield schemes allowed on appeal in 2020-22, and around 100 (40%) of appeals where the valued landscapes policy was applied approved. It criticises the standard method for inflating targets and five-year supply figures.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Nothing directly; it is a consultation response. It implies station proximity alone should not justify rural development where the station lacks services.

## Positions against our propositions

- **METH (agrees):** "Planning inspectors have approved approximately 100 (or 40%) of all appeals for developments where the policy was applied since it was introduced in 2012." (valued landscapes section). CPRE itself treats appeal outcome counts and rates as evidence of how a national policy works in practice, the same kind of approach as ours.
- **TR (not applicable; checked 2 Oct 2026):** "Policies allowing more development simply because land is near a station (GB3 and GB7h) would lead to suburban sprawl, ribbon development, and higher car dependency, especially around isolated rural stations lacking services." (section 3, "Development near rural railway stations threatens openness and increases car use"). This objects to the consultation draft's station-proximity Green Belt release policies (draft GB3 and GB7h) and says CPRE supports development near urban stations on brownfield land only. It says nothing about walking routes, footways or distance, so it neither agrees with nor qualifies our point that route quality, not distance, decides TR3 and GB7(1)(g)(iii). "Lacking services" refers to the station's surroundings, not a test for access. Campaign position on a draft, not guidance on applying the adopted Framework.
- **GB (agrees, indirect):** "CPRE evidence shows the grey belt policy is being used to release greenfield land, including local wildlife sites and farmland with high quality soils, and not degraded sites as promised." (grey belt section). Consistent with grey belt being won mainly on greenfield edge sites.
- **SUP (silent):** "CPRE has major concerns with draft Policy S5, which relies heavily on a stock‑based formula (the so-called 'standard method') that creates inflated local housing targets and five‑year housing land supply calculations." (housing targets section). A consultation critique of the draft standard method and how targets and supply figures are set; it says nothing about the presumption, the tilted balance or what weight a shortfall carries once found, so it neither supports nor qualifies SUP. At most it implies shortfalls are found more often than they should be (how often the gate opens), not whether a shortfall decides the case.
