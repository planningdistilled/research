## Summary

The Local Government Association (LGA) briefing for councils on the August 2026 National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF). It explains the national decision-making policies (NDMPs), which took effect on 17 August 2026, and tells councils to review Annex A. It covers: the bar on local plans duplicating NDMPs (PM6); the move of the presumption to a location test (S3 to S6) with the split-site rule (S3(2)); the S5(1) categories; the policies that disapply the tilted balance; station-led growth and the 800-metre walking distance; Green Belt changes (GB3(4), GB4, GB7, GB8); heritage (HE6(3)); and the 2025 Housing Delivery Test (HDT). It ends by saying the Planning Advisory Service (PAS) will run in-person workshops and webinars, "starting with the implications for development management". It is the councils' own summary, so it is a direct source on how members and officers are being briefed. In places it reads the policy differently from the text (GB4, and Annex A paragraph 2). The direct fetch returned 403; the local copy was taken through a reader proxy (r.jina.ai) on 2 October 2026.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Review Annex A (Implementation). Give development plan policies that are "materially inconsistent" with NDMPs "very limited weight"; other policies keep their weight.
- Apply S4 inside, and S5 outside, "defined settlement boundaries". On sites that straddle a boundary, apply S4 and S5 to each part before reaching an overall view (S3(2)).
- Outside settlements, apply the tilted balance only when the scheme falls in an S5(1) category. Under S5(1)(j) (evidenced unmet need, including no 5-year supply or HDT below 75 per cent), check that the site is still "physically well-related to an existing settlement".
- Treat "reasonable walking distance" as about 800 metres or a 10-minute walk, reduced where route quality or barriers make walking harder.
- Refuse where the Framework says development "should be refused". "Great weight" policies do not stop development by themselves.
- Treat conflict with a neighbourhood plan adopted in the last 5 years that has housing allocations (S6) as likely to justify refusal.
- Give harm to a designated heritage asset "considerable importance and weight" (HE6(3)).

## Positions against our propositions

- SH2 (silent; misstates GB4): "Villages (as opposed to hamlets) should not be considered Green Belt if they are identified as a settlement. These terms remain undefined." (section 8, "Village protections (Policy GB4)"). This is a plan-making summary of GB4, not a view on the decision route, so it takes no stance on SH2. It never mentions Annex B, S4/S5 or how to treat a washed-over village in a decision. But it gets GB4(1)(b) backwards. GB4(1)(b) tells plans to include villages in the Green Belt where their openness matters, and says such villages "should not be identified as 'settlements' for the purpose of the spatial strategy in the development plan". The LGA has the causation the other way round (identified as a settlement, therefore not Green Belt). "These terms remain undefined" is fair for "village" and "hamlet", which the Framework does not define. It does not touch Annex B's definition of "settlement", which excludes villages "which lie within and are defined as part of the Green Belt in the development plan". Our SH2 rests on that definition, so the analysis does not change. The risk is in briefings: a councillor relying on this line could read local "settlement" status as taking a washed-over village out of the Green Belt.
- A2 (agrees, restating policy): "Existing development plan policies that are found to be "materially inconsistent" with the new national NDMPs must immediately be downgraded by decision-makers and given "very limited weight". This allows some pre-existing, non-conflicting local plan policies to maintain a greater shelf-life but national policy takes precedence in cases of material conflict." (section 1, "Primacy over inconsistent local policies (Annex A, Paragraph 2)"). This is a summary of Annex A paragraph 2, not an argument about how to apply it. It agrees that non-conflicting policies keep their weight and that national policy wins only where there is material conflict. It drops Annex A's "(or parts of those policies)", so it does not say whether the cut works clause by clause, but it does not argue for whole-policy discounting either. It is silent on heritage, design, access and spatial-preference policies.
- S5 (agrees): "Only certain forms of development outside defined settlement boundaries are permitted but the "default yes" tilted balance still applies if a project triggers specific categories listed under Policy S5(1)" (section 3, "Presumption outside settlements"). This supports our view that the S5(1) category decides the case outside settlements. It does not mention the competing readings of S5(1)(j) "physically well-related".
- SUP (agrees): "This explicitly includes scenarios where councils lack a 5-year housing supply or fail the housing delivery test (<75 per cent), but is "not limited to" that" and "The site must still be physically well-related to an existing settlement and compatible with local infrastructure." (section 3, S5(1)(j)). A supply shortfall opens the S5(1)(j) gate, but the site still has to pass the location tests. That matches our reading.
- TR (agrees): "A "reasonable walking distance" is formally defined as around 800 metres (or a 10-minute walk), but can be reduced where topography, route availability, quality, or physical barriers make walking harder." (section 4, "Walking distance defined (Glossary - Annex B & Policy S5)"). It says route quality can override the distance figure. This concerns the S5(1)(h) station category, not TR3 directly, and it does not mention walking in the carriageway or street lighting.

## Leads

- The PAS development management workshops and webinars on the new Framework, which this briefing says are coming. Find dates, slides and recordings (the task brief mentions October 2026 roadshows and a November "development boundaries" deep dive).
- PAS analysis of the 2025 HDT: 33 per cent of authorities below 75 per cent, so "evidenced unmet need" applies under S5(1)(j) and the Green Belt provisions in a third of councils (section 13 table). Check whether Stratford-on-Avon is one.
- The S3(2) split-site rule ("This prevents developers from using a minor urban connection to pull a large rural field into a permissive planning category.") applies to edge-of-village sites. Check our decisions database for S3(2) findings.
- The S6 neighbourhood-plan safeguard (plan adopted within the last 5 years, with housing allocations) may matter for areas with recent neighbourhood plans.
- The statutory consultee reform response (Active Travel England threshold raised from 150 to 250 homes; National Highways only where a transport assessment is required) affects TR3 evidence on small village schemes.
