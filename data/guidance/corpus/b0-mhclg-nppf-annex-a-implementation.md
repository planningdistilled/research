## Summary

Annex A sets the transitional rules for the August 2026 National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF). For decisions, the Framework is a material consideration from the day it was published (17 August 2026), with no grace period. Development plan policies "or parts of those policies" that are materially inconsistent with the national decision-making policies get "very limited weight", unless they were examined and adopted against this Framework. Other policies are not to lose weight just because they are older. Para 3 is a new limit on S5(1)(j): where a council has a five-year supply and passes the Housing Delivery Test (HDT) at over 75%, a standard-method housing need figure above the adopted requirement is not "unmet need" for five years after adoption. Paras 4 to 8 set which Framework applies to plans in preparation, and para 10 lists the Written Ministerial Statements (WMS) now absorbed or superseded.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Apply the Framework to every decision from 17 August 2026.
- Test each development plan policy, clause by clause, against the national decision-making policies. Give the inconsistent parts very limited weight and leave the rest at full weight.
- Do not downgrade consistent policies because of their age.
- Do not treat a gap between local housing need and the adopted requirement as unmet need under S5(1)(j) within five years of adoption, if the five-year supply and HDT are both met.
- Stop relying on the listed WMSs (for example the December 2024 traveller policy and the 2015 Green Belt unauthorised development statement) as freestanding policy.

## Positions against our propositions

- **A2, agrees.** Quote: "Development plan policies (or parts of those policies) which are materially inconsistent with national decision-making policies in this Framework should be given very limited weight." (Annex A para 2.) Also: "Other development plan policies[footnote 67] should not be given reduced weight simply because they were adopted prior to the publication of this Framework." (Annex A para 2.) It adds one exception we do not state: inconsistent policies examined and adopted against this Framework keep full weight. Footnote 67 says this includes made neighbourhood plans.
- **SUP, silent (policy restatement; relevant to S5 rather than SUP).** Para 3 applies only where the council *has* a five-year supply and passes the HDT at over 75%, so it says nothing about what a supply shortfall does. It sets a condition on a different input to the S5(1)(j) gate: a gap between standard-method need and the adopted requirement is not "evidenced unmet need" in the five years after adoption. This fits SUP (with no shortfall, a need gap alone does not open the gate) and is a detail to add to the S5 note on S5(1)(j), not a disagreement with our method. Quote (second half of the para): "if the most up-to-date local housing need figure (calculated using the standard method set out in Annex D) is greater than the housing requirement set out in adopted development plan policies, this should not be considered as evidence of unmet need, for a period of 5 years from the date of the plan’s adoption." (Annex A para 3.)

## Leads

- Annex A para 3 is a test for S5(1)(j) "evidenced unmet need" and it is not in the December 2024 Framework. Check how appeal decisions in the decisions DB handle unmet need where a five-year supply exists.
- Annex D (Housing calculations and supply) for the standard method and five-year supply mechanics.
- Neighbourhood plans submitted under Regulation 15 on or before 17 August 2026 are prepared under the December 2024 Framework (para 6).
