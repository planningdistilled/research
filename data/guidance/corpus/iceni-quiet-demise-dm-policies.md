# The quiet demise of local development management plan policies (Iceni Projects, Oliver Ricketts)

## Summary

Iceni argues that local plans now at examination under the 2024 Framework's transitional arrangements (57 being examined and over 100 more expected) may be found sound, yet their development management policies may then be "rendered effectively useless" by Annex A(2) of the 2026 Framework. Annex A(2) gives very limited weight to plan policies (or parts of them) that are materially inconsistent with the national decision-making policies. Its example is a Regulation 19 plan that tightly limits development outside settlement boundaries to allocated sites. Iceni calls this "entirely at odds" with the new Framework, citing the well-connected station routes in S5 and GB7. A second example is employment policy that limits commercial development to allocated sites, which conflicts with E2.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Apply Annex A(2) when weighing even recently adopted plans: very limited weight for policies, or parts of them, that are materially inconsistent with the Framework's decision-making policies.
- Expect restrictive settlement-boundary and allocation-only policies to be challenged as inconsistent with S5, GB7 and E2.

## Positions against our propositions

- **A2 — qualifies.** "Evidently the restrictive nature of policy is entirely at odds with the intent of the new NPPF, not least including the much reported ‘well-connected train station’ through Policies S5 (Principle of development outside settlements) and GB7 (Development which is not inappropriate in the Green Belt)." (Main text, discussion of the Regulation 19 example). This is an opinion on an unnamed draft (Regulation 19) plan, not on weight given at appeal, and it speaks of the Framework's "intent" without doing an Annex A(2) weighing. The policy it targets limits development outside settlement boundaries to allocated sites only. That blanket bar is itself the clause that conflicts with the S5(1) categories and GB7, so our A2 would cut it too. Iceni does not say the rest of a spatial strategy, or heritage, design or access policies, lose weight. It is in tension with our wording that spatial-preference policies keep full weight, but it does not contradict A2 directly. Iceni does no clause-by-clause analysis.
- **A2 — qualifies.** "This leaves a difficult situation where many policies could be found sound when considered against the 2024 NPPF, but might then be rendered effectively useless through the provisions of the 2026 NPPF." (Main text, paragraph after the Annex A(2) quote). A forward-looking warning about plans examined against the 2024 Framework, not an account of how decision-makers apply Annex A(2). Iceni quotes Annex A(2) with its "(or parts of those policies)" wording and does not argue that whole policies, rather than offending parts, lose weight. The real tension with A2 is the spatial-policy point in the bullet above; this sentence only predicts that many policies may lose practical force, with no decisions cited.
