## Summary

A land-promotion platform's hub page on the August 2026 National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF), with two linked pages also saved: "NPPF 2026: what changed" and "Sustainable development and what it decides". It explains the switch from paragraph numbers to policy codes, the S3/S4/S5 presumption, grey belt (Annex B, GB7(1)(g), Annex E), the GB8 Golden Rules and L3 station densities. It says its own automated site scoring is run against these policy codes. The task brief said this page claims there is "not yet a settled body of appeal decisions on the key definitions"; that wording is **not** on the hub page or on the three linked guides fetched (searched for "settled" and "appeal"), so that claim is unverified. The pages are internally inconsistent: the "what changed" guide uses the new "substantially outweighed" balance, while the sustainable-development page still quotes the old "significantly and demonstrably" wording.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Re-cite every policy reference to the August 2026 codes; an appraisal under the December 2024 Framework needs re-running, not renumbering.
- Read the five-year housing land supply and Housing Delivery Test figures from the council's current published position.
- Build any grey belt case from the Annex B definition and the GB2 purposes, and put GB8 Golden Rules costs in the viability model from the start.
- Test sites against L3 station distance (about 800 metres) and density.

## Positions against our propositions

- **SUP — agrees.** "The supply position is still the practical gateway to the presumption" (changes-explained guide, "Housing land supply and delivery: HO3 with Annex D"). It is a gateway, consistent with our reading.
- **SUP — agrees (checked; earlier coded "qualifies").** "it is the mechanism through which most greenfield edge-of-settlement consents are won at appeal." (sustainable-development page, "The presumption in favour of sustainable development"). "It" here means the S3 presumption, not the shortfall alone. The same sentence says it converts the shortfall into positive weight only "for a site that is otherwise sustainably located". The page also says wins come from "the presumption combined with a walkable relationship to existing services and quantified benefits" and that losses turn on "location and landscape" ("How sustainability decides a site in practice"). So the presumption is a necessary route, not the deciding factor, which matches "opens the gate, never decides". It is a promoter's emphasis, not a conflict with our reading.
- **TR — qualifies (distance-first framing, no engagement with route quality).** "Distance to a settlement and to everyday services is the practical test of sustainability." (sustainable-development page, "Sustainable location and travel patterns"). This is a general summary for landowners of the transport chapter (TR1-TR3), and its "Evidence expected" list is distance-led ("Accessibility audit with walking and cycling distances to services"). It does not discuss route quality, footways, walking in the carriageway or lighting, and cites no decisions, so it does not argue against our finding; it simply puts distance first where we find route quality decides. Checked 2026-10-02: quote verbatim (line 50 of the local text); downgraded from "disagrees".
- **GB — agrees.** "Sustainability arguments do not override Green Belt policy, but the grey belt route makes them decisive" (sustainable-development page, "Green Belt, grey belt and the Golden Rules").
- **METH — agrees.** "The pattern in appeal decisions is consistent." (sustainable-development page, "How sustainability decides a site in practice"). It too relies on appeal-decision patterns, but gives no dataset, count or citation.
