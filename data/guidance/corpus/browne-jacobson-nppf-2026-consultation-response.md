## Summary

Browne Jacobson's formal response to the December 2025 consultation draft of the National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF), written by partners Ben Standing and Will Thomas. The landing page sets out the case for Spatial Development Strategies (SDSs), which it says benefit developers, council planners and communities, and links to answers by chapter. On achieving sustainable development (Questions 36-38) it "strongly agrees" with the revised presumption. It finds the within/outside-settlement split easier to understand than the old tilted balance, and supports S5 as "a very clear test". On decision-making (DM1-DM5) it backs proportionate information requirements and suggests regulations for validation. It asks for clarity on how far DM3 expects councils to suggest improvements to applications, and supports limits on viability assessments while keeping DM5(2). This is about the draft. Nothing in it sets out how the final text should be applied.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Act as facilitators of development rather than gatekeepers (DM3). Do not delay decisions when statutory consultees respond slowly.
- Spend officer time helping well-designed schemes through, not reworking poor ones.
- Ask only for the minimum necessary information on non-major applications.
- Allow site-specific viability assessment only when circumstances have materially changed since the plan (DM5(2)).

## Positions against our propositions

- **S5, agrees.** "Policy S5 restricts development outside settlements to specified acceptable types" and "The policy sets out a very clear test that supports housing delivery in sustainable locations outside of town centres" (sub-page "Achieving sustainable development", summary of S5 and answer to Question 38; draft text). This supports our view that the S5 category decides the case outside settlements.
