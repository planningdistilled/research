## Summary

Reports MHCLG's rebuttal of press claims (led by the shadow housing secretary) that the revised NPPF makes it easier to get retrospective permission for unauthorised Traveller sites. MHCLG says the Framework now tells councils to give intentional unauthorised development "substantial weight" on retrospective applications, keeps environmental, flooding and transport policies applying to Traveller sites, and folds the separate Traveller policy into the main Framework so that such applications are "judged consistently alongside other planning applications".

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Give intentional unauthorised development substantial weight against retrospective applications: "councils should give this substantial weight when deciding retrospective planning applications" (MHCLG statement).
- Judge Traveller site applications against the same national decision-making policies (environment, flooding, transport) as other development.
- Use existing enforcement powers, including injunctions.
