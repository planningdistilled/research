## Summary

Turley's overview of the December 2025 consultation draft of the National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF). For decisions, the presumption in favour of sustainable development is split between sites within settlements and those outside, with a new glossary definition of "settlement" that Turley paraphrases as cities, towns and villages but not hamlets; it predicts debate and possibly litigation over the village/hamlet line. Outside settlements there is "defined" scope for the presumption, and otherwise a strengthened presumption against. It reads draft Annex A paragraph 2 as giving national decision-making policies (NDMPs) precedence over existing development management (DM) policies, even in up-to-date plans. It also lists policies that now attract "substantial weight" (including HO7 housing benefits and HE6 heritage) and the "limited circumstances" where refusal is expected (for example poor design under DP3).

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Decide whether a site is within or outside a "settlement" using the new glossary definition, and route the presumption accordingly.
- Outside settlements, apply the presumption only within the defined categories; otherwise expect a strong presumption against.
- Give "very limited weight" to development plan policies inconsistent with the NDMPs unless examined against the new Framework.
- Give "substantial weight" to the listed objectives (housing benefits HO7, heritage HE6, and others).
- Refuse in the listed "limited circumstances" (town-centre impact, inefficient land use, poor design, flood risk, biodiversity harm).

## Positions against our propositions

- **SH2, silent (draft-era summary, not a contrary view).** Summarises the December 2025 consultation-draft glossary definition of "settlement" "in essence" as cities, towns and villages but not hamlets. It says nothing about villages washed over by the Green Belt and does not reason about them, so it neither supports nor contradicts SH2. The final Annex B (Aug 2026) expressly excludes villages "which lie within and are defined as part of the Green Belt". Earlier coding as "disagrees" withdrawn after adversarial check. Quote: "In essence cities, towns and villages are settlements; hamlets are not." (Section "Presumption".)
- **S5, agrees.** Outside settlements, failing the defined categories brings a stronger presumption against development. Quote: "where they don’t there is a strengthening of the presumption against" (Section "Presumption".)
- **A2, silent (restates the draft).** Paraphrases draft Annex A paragraph 2: NDMPs prevail over DM policies that are "in any way inconsistent", even in up-to-date plans. It is commenting on the December 2025 consultation draft and says nothing on whether the whole policy or only the inconsistent clause loses weight, or on heritage, design, access or spatial-preference policies. Our proposition is about how the final wording is applied, which this does not contradict. Quote: "the NDMPs are to take precedence over existing DM policies in (even up to date) development plans." (Section "Development management".)
