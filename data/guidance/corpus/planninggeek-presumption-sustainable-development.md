## Summary

An explainer page (updated 17 September 2026) on how the August 2026 National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF) replaced the old paragraph 11 "tilted balance" with policies S3 to S6. It frames the central question as whether a site is within a settlement (S4, strong support) or outside one (S5, support only for listed categories plus a harder exceptional-circumstances route). It explains the Annex B settlement definition, notes the special qualification for villages washed over by the Green Belt, and says S5(1)(j) unmet-need housing must also be physically well-related to a settlement. It treats a five-year housing land supply (5YHLS) shortfall or a Housing Delivery Test (HDT) result below 75% as evidence of unmet need that opens a route but does not decide the case, and says Green Belt and Local Green Space keep their own tests.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Start from section 38(6) of the Planning and Compulsory Purchase Act 2004; the NPPF is a material consideration.
- Decide first whether the site is within a settlement as defined nationally (not just inside or outside a mapped boundary); hamlets and scattered groups are not normally settlements.
- Inside a settlement, apply S4: approve unless benefits are substantially outweighed by adverse effects.
- Outside, identify the S5 category; read each S5 route separately (S5(1)(j) is not a condition on S5(1)(e) infilling).
- For S5(1)(j) housing, require evidenced unmet need and a physical relationship to a settlement and capacity of infrastructure.
- If no category fits, apply the separate exceptional-circumstances route, which is materially harder.
- Assess Green Belt land under the Green Belt policies; a 5YHLS/HDT shortfall can satisfy the grey-belt unmet-need limb but the other tests and Golden Rules still apply.
- Apply the Annex A transitional protection for recently adopted plans; check S6 for recent neighbourhood plans.

## Positions against our propositions

- SH1 (agrees): "A development plan may identify a settlement using a defined boundary or an equivalent term, but it can also use criteria to identify the extent of a settlement where no boundary has been drawn." (section "What counts as a settlement?"). Local plan definitions feed the national settlement test rather than carrying separate weight; it speaks of boundaries, not hierarchy tiers.
- SH2 (agrees): "The Framework also contains a particular qualification for villages which lie within and are defined as part of the Green Belt." (section "What counts as a settlement?"). Acknowledges the washed-over-village exclusion but does not spell out its consequence.
- S5 (agrees): "The settlement-relationship test in S5(1)(j) is not a general additional condition imposed on every other S5 route." (section "Read each S5 route separately"). The category chosen frames the whole case.
- S5 (agrees; restates policy): "Falling outside the S5 list is not an automatic refusal. There is a separate exceptional-circumstances route." and "That is a materially harder test than the one applied to development which is acceptable in principle under S5." (section "What if the proposal is not one of the S5 categories?"). Restates the S5(4) exceptional-circumstances route from the policy text and itself calls it materially harder; it makes no claim about how often the route succeeds, so it is consistent with "almost always fatal" (which already assumes the route exists) rather than qualifying it.
- SUP (agrees): "Evidence of housing need is therefore important, but it is not enough on its own." (section "S5 development outside settlements"); also "A shortage of housing land and poor housing delivery can still be very powerful, but they now operate principally as evidence of unmet need within the new policy structure rather than switching on the old paragraph 11(d) balance." (section "How this replaced the tilted balance").
- GB (agrees): "That does not mean permission automatically follows: the other Green Belt tests still have to be satisfied" (section "Green Belt and Local Green Space are different").
