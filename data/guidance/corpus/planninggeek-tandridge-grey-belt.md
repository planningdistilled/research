## Summary

A news analysis of appeal 6010313 (Branford Wells / South View Barns, Brickhouse Lane, Newchapel, Tandridge), dismissed on 4 September 2026 under the August 2026 NPPF. The scheme was five homes in a converted agricultural building. The Inspector accepted the land was grey belt and that Tandridge had only 1.97 years' housing land supply. But the site is away from settlements and services, on a 40mph lane with no footways or street lighting, so residents would depend on cars, failing the sustainable-location limb read with TR3. Further harms were openness, moderate harm to a Grade II listed farmhouse's setting, character and poor gardens. A genuine prior-approval fallback for five homes got only limited weight because it would cause less harm. The author notes an internal tension: the Inspector found the building-reuse exception GB7(1)(b) met but still treated the scheme as inappropriate.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Treat grey-belt status and a housing shortfall as only the start; each GB7(1)(g) limb, including sustainable location, must be met separately.
- On TR3, ask whether the actual routes and services give future residents a genuine, usable alternative for everyday journeys, not merely whether a bus stop or village exists nearby.
- Judge a fallback by comparing land affected, layout and harm, not just the number of dwellings.
- Consider the setting of nearby listed buildings even where the converted building is not listed.
- Treat building-reuse and grey-belt as alternative GB7 categories, not two hurdles.

## Positions against our propositions

- **SUP - agrees.** "It is not permission simply because the land qualifies as grey belt and the council is short of housing." (section "Grey belt is only the beginning of the test"). A 1.97-year supply did not save a car-dependent site.
- **GB - agrees.** "Grey-belt status and a serious housing shortage can support a planning case, but they do not remove the other policy requirements" (section "The Planning Geek takeaway"). Once the scheme was found inappropriate, the 1.97-year supply did not produce very special circumstances.
- **TR - agrees.** Route reality, not proximity, decides: "It is whether the routes and services offer future residents a genuine, usable alternative for their everyday journeys." The facts were: "Brickhouse Lane has a 40mph speed limit, no footways and no street lighting." (section "A rural lane with no realistic transport choice").
