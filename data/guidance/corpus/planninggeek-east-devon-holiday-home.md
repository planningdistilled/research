# East Devon holiday home wins unrestricted use under new NPPF

## Summary

Reports appeal 6009619 (Inspector S Sharp, 29 Sep 2026) removing a holiday-occupancy restriction at Cosy Cow Shed, Dalwood. The site was outside the settlement, so S5 applied rather than S4, and it conflicted with East Devon spatial and transport policies, including the settlement hierarchy. The property was previously developed land under S5(1)(d), which expressly covers a change of use to residential. None of the national refusal triggers (L3, DP3, TR6, F6, F7, N2, N6, HE6) were engaged. TR3 did not apply because one dwelling does not generate significant movement. The Inspector walked the unlit route, which had no footway, and found it suitable in clement weather and daylight. With a 3.5-year supply giving only minor weight, the Framework, as a 'material consideration of critical importance', outweighed the development-plan conflict.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- Decide first whether the site is inside or outside a settlement (S4 or S5).
- Test the Framework definition of previously developed land, including its exclusions, before relying on S5(1)(d).
- Check every national decision-making policy that directs refusal (S5(2)) before the balance.
- Apply TR3 only where development generates a significant amount of movement in absolute and relative terms; keep that separate from local-plan transport conflict.
- Walk the route to services and record its conditions (lighting, footways, gradient).
- Follow the sequence: lawful use, settlement status, previously developed land, S5 route, national refusal policies, then the balance.

## Positions against our propositions

- **SH1 (settlement hierarchy): agrees.** "That mattered twice: first under East Devon’s settlement strategy, and again when the Inspector decided whether the proposal fell under NPPF Policy S4 for development within settlements or S5 for development outside them." (section "Why the East Devon holiday home was constrained by location"). The local settlement hierarchy fed development-plan accordance and, separately, the S4/S5 route: our SH1 (a) and (b).
- **S5 (S5 categories): agrees.** "Once the proposal qualified under S5 and did not breach a refusal-triggering national policy, the identified harms did not substantially outweigh the benefits." (section "Housing benefit was modest but still positive"). The S5(1)(d) category decided the case.
- **SUP (housing supply): agrees.** "Against that background, adding an unrestricted home was a benefit, but the Inspector gave it only minor positive weight because the numerical contribution was so small." (section "Housing benefit was modest but still positive"). A 3.5-year supply did not decide the case.
- **TR (walking route): silent (scope note, not a qualification).** "Sharp found that the change involving one dwelling would not generate a significant amount of movement in absolute terms." (section "One dwelling was not significant movement under TR3"). This is about whether TR3 is engaged at all, which is TR3's own wording, not about how route quality is weighed once it is. It does not dispute that route quality, not distance, decides. Our analysis already records the threshold and that inspectors read it in context: in Green Belt 1 to 9 homes have been held to be "significant movement … in this context" (patterns.md, PINS-6010313 ¶13; PINS-6009966 ¶27). The contrast with this single holiday-let conversion outside the Green Belt is worth noting, but it is not a conflict.
- **TR (walking route): silent (on route quality).** "Sharp considered the walk suitable in clement weather and daylight." (section "The Inspector walked the route to Dalwood"). This does not temper the carriageway fail fact. TR3 was never engaged because one dwelling is not significant movement, so no route-quality test was applied. The suitability finding was conditional on daylight and good weather. The article also reports that residents would drive "because of darkness or poor weather", that "the location made genuine travel choice difficult", and that the proposal conflicted with local transport policy. Read that way, the unlit, footway-less route counted against the site, not for it. This is a secondary report, and the decision letter has not been checked.
- **A2 (Annex A para 2): silent.** "The development plan remained the statutory starting point. What changed was the weight and effect of national policy as a material consideration in this particular decision." (section "Local-plan conflict did not disappear"). The article never mentions Annex A, inconsistency with the Framework, or reduced weight for any plan policy. It describes the ordinary statutory balance, in which the Framework as a material consideration outweighed a development-plan conflict. That is a different test from A2's clause-cutting under Annex A para 2. It is consistent with A2 (the plan conflict stood) but does not address it.
