## Summary

A short chambers news item (18 September 2026) on a week-long inquiry in which Michael Rhimes, as sole counsel, defended Basingstoke and Deane Borough Council against a non-determination appeal for 115 homes at Whitchurch. The council had no five-year housing land supply, and only two of eight putative reasons for refusal (landscape and design) survived to the inquiry. The inspector found the scheme a "significantly urbanising and strident intervention into the countryside" (decision letter paragraph 26), criticised the failure to evolve the design in response to consultees including the Design Review Panel (DL/43), and gave less than the HC4 "substantial weight" to a proposed pub because of real doubts over its deliverability (DL/72). Overall the benefits were substantially outweighed by the harms and the appeal was dismissed despite the supply shortfall (DL/79). ## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- A council without a five-year supply can still defend refusals at inquiry on landscape and design grounds if the harm is strong.
- Narrow the case: drop weak reasons for refusal and run the strongest (here two of eight).
- Record how a scheme did or did not respond to consultee and Design Review Panel advice; an inspector may weigh a failure to evolve against it.
- Test the deliverability of claimed community benefits (such as a pub) before giving them HC4 "substantial weight".
