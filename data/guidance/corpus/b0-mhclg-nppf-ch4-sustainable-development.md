## Summary

Chapter 4 holds the presumption in favour of sustainable development in its new form. S3 sends proposals inside a "settlement" (Annex B) to S4 and those outside to S5. A proposal that accords with both an up-to-date plan and the national policies is approved without delay. S4 approves development within settlements unless the benefits are "substantially outweighed" by adverse effects. S5(1) lists the only forms of development to approve outside settlements, including (j) development meeting an evidenced unmet need (expressly including a housing land shortfall) that is "physically well-related to an existing settlement". S5(4) allows anything outside the list only in exceptional circumstances where benefits substantially outweigh harm. S5(5) takes the Green Belt and Local Green Space out of S5 altogether. S6 protects recent neighbourhood plans that allocate for their housing requirement.

## What it tells officers/decision-makers to do

- First decide whether the site is inside or outside a settlement (Annex B), then apply S4 or S5; split part-in, part-out sites (S3(2)).
- Inside settlements: approve unless substantially outweighed. The S4(2) list sets out typical refusal cases.
- Outside settlements: identify which S5(1) category, if any, the proposal fits. If it fits none, require exceptional circumstances with benefits substantially outweighing harm to countryside character and sustainable movement (S5(4)).
- For S5(1)(j) housing: check for evidenced unmet need (no five-year supply or a Housing Delivery Test result below 75%), physical relationship to a settlement, and infrastructure capacity. Footnote 28 asks whether a site beyond an unbuilt allocation would still be suitable if the allocation does not proceed.
- Refuse isolated homes except under HO11 (S5(3)).
- In the Green Belt, use GB6 to GB8, not S5; if not inappropriate, approve unless substantially outweighed (S5(5)).
- For housing proposals that conflict with a neighbourhood plan made in the last five years that allocates for its requirement, treat the benefits as likely to be substantially outweighed (S6).

## Positions against our propositions

- **SH1, agrees.** The route turns on the Annex B definition, which the plan's settlement list and boundaries feed into; no hierarchy tier is given weight. Quote: "Settlements within the development plan area (applying the definition in the glossary at Annex B), whether existing or proposed, and their boundaries" (S2(1)(a)). And: "Policy S4 in this Framework should be applied when considering development proposals within settlements" (S3(1)(a)).
- **SH2, agrees (refines the label; checked 2 Oct 2026).** This is the Framework's own text, not commentary, and it supports SH2 rather than qualifying it. It does not address whether a washed-over village is an Annex B settlement (that is Annex B's job). What it adds is precision on our "S5/Green Belt" shorthand: Green Belt land is taken out of S5 entirely and goes to GB6 to GB8, so the S5(1) list and S5(4) do not apply; the S5 balance (with S5(2)) returns only if the proposal is found not inappropriate under GB7. Quote: "This policy does not apply to development proposals in the Green Belt or on land designated as Local Green Space, which should instead be determined in accordance with policies HC8, GB6, GB7 and/or GB8 (as appropriate)." (S5(5).) Wording point for our notes: say "the Green Belt route (GB6 to GB8, via S5(5))" rather than "S5/Green Belt".
- **S5, agrees.** Quote: "Development proposals which do not fall within one of the categories set out in this policy should only be approved in exceptional circumstances, where the benefits of the proposal would substantially outweigh the adverse effects, including to the character of the countryside and in relation to promoting sustainable patterns of movement." (S5(4).) S5(1)(j)(i) is not defined further, apart from footnote 28, which leaves the competing readings open.
- **SUP, agrees.** Shortfall qualifies a proposal for S5(1)(j), but the proposal must also be physically well-related and of an acceptable scale, and the "unless substantially outweighed" balance still applies. Quote: "Be physically well-related to an existing settlement[footnote 28] (unless the nature of the development would make this inappropriate) and be of a scale which can be accommodated taking into account the existing or proposed availability of infrastructure" (S5(1)(j)(i)).
- **GB, agrees.** This is the Framework text, not commentary. Passing GB7 does not mean approval: it brings in the S5(5) balance, so approval is still conditional on adverse effects (including the S5(2) "should be refused" triggers) not substantially outweighing the benefits. Our pattern 18 already codes this tilted balance and finds that half of such appeals were still lost through those triggers. Quote: "However, where development would not be inappropriate in these locations (through the application of policies HC8 and GB7), proposals should be approved unless the benefits of doing so would be substantially outweighed by any adverse effects, when assessed against the national decision-making policies in this Framework, and applying paragraph 2 of this policy." (S5(5).)
